# Denosumab: From Osteoporosis to Severe Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Denosumab is a RANKL-inhibiting monoclonal antibody, originally used to treat osteoporosis (including postmenopausal and drug-induced osteoporosis).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy** (top-ranked prediction), but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific direction.
Among the other top predictions, only **dermatitis** has any supporting material (1 Phase 1 mechanistic study and several case reports, mostly describing skin adverse events).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, denosumab is a human monoclonal antibody that binds RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor κB ligand) and thereby inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. Its efficacy in osteoporosis is established.

For severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, the link is speculative. RANKL/OPG signalling has been discussed in vascular and inflammatory pathways, which could in theory be relevant to diabetic microvascular disease. However, none of the data compiled for this report supports this link, and the prediction rests on the model score alone.

The second-ranked prediction, diabetic retinopathy (score 99.23%), has two indirect publications. One is a real-world cohort study with a meta-analysis suggesting denosumab may be associated with a lower incidence of type 2 diabetes and fewer foot ulcers and deaths compared with bisphosphonates. The other is a fracture-risk assessment study in type 2 diabetes. Neither tests denosumab against retinopathy, so both are hypothesis-generating at best.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in the interaction screening. The ones listed are all rated Moderate, and include:
  - Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone, methylprednisolone
  - Radiopharmaceuticals and related agents: ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane (I-131), tositumomab (with and without I-131)
  - Immunomodulators and antimetabolites: levamisole, trimetrexate, mercaptopurine, tacrolimus, dupilumab
  - Adrenocorticotropic agents: corticotropin, tetracosactide
- **Cutaneous adverse events**: The dermatitis literature reports AGEP, DRESS, lichenoid eruptions, erythema nodosum and vasculitis associated with denosumab, which is relevant to any repurposing toward skin conditions.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions rest almost entirely on model scores. There are no clinical trials or direct literature for severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, and the evidence level is L5. The only signal found (dermatitis) is a Phase 1 mechanistic study plus case reports that mostly describe harm rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a plausible mechanistic rationale for RANKL inhibition in diabetic retinopathy
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the two Singapore registrations
- Preclinical or observational studies that directly examine denosumab and retinopathy outcomes
- A review of whether the ocular-outcome cohort data (cataract, AMD) offer any relevant safety or efficacy signals
