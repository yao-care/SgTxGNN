# Deferiprone: From Transfusional Iron Overload in Thalassaemia to Hepatic Porphyria

## One-Sentence Summary

Deferiprone is an oral iron chelator, originally used to treat iron overload (hemosiderosis) in beta-thalassaemia and related haemoglobin disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatic porphyria**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one case-level report and one animal study), so the evidence is indirect and preliminary.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, deferiprone is an oral iron-selective chelator. Its efficacy in removing iron in transfusional iron overload is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to porphyrias where iron plays a role.

In porphyria cutanea tarda, excess hepatic iron is thought to worsen porphyrin accumulation by inhibiting uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase. Lowering labile iron could therefore reduce disease activity. Phlebotomy, which also depletes iron, is an effective treatment in this setting.

The supporting studies are indirect. One is a mouse model of porphyria cutanea tarda, and the other is a case-level report in congenital erythropoietic porphyria. Neither is randomised, and the subtypes studied may not match "hepatic porphyria" as a whole.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen found 100 recorded interactions. The entries listed are all rated Moderate. They mainly involve polyvalent metal-containing products, which may bind the chelator:
- Calcium salts (phosphate, acetate, carbonate, citrate, gluconate, lactate, glubionate)
- Magnesium salts (oxide, carbonate, chloride, citrate, gluconate, hydroxide, sulfate)
- Aluminium-containing antacids (aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate)
- Iron, kaolin, attapulgite and sucralfate

Please refer to the package insert for other safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is supported only by one mouse study and one case-level report in specific porphyria subtypes, with no registered clinical trials. This is not enough to move toward clinical use, and the package insert safety data are still missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (currently blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data, to support the link between iron chelation and porphyria
- Clarification of which porphyria subtypes are relevant (for example porphyria cutanea tarda versus congenital erythropoietic porphyria)
- Prospective clinical data in humans, such as a pilot study or a systematic review of existing case experience
- A review of the interaction profile and dosing separation from metal-containing products
