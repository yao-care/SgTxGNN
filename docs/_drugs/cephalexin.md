## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the input. Cephalexin is a first-generation cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It is active against *Staphylococcus aureus* and streptococci, which are found in ear infections.

Chronic suppurative middle ear disease is often polymicrobial. It commonly involves *Pseudomonas* and anaerobes, and cephalexin has limited coverage of these organisms. One retrospective study of 69 children with chronic suppurative otitis media (Brook, 1994) found mixed aerobic and anaerobic flora in 54% of children. It also found beta-lactamase-producing bacteria in 58% of children.

Because the original indications are not stated, it is unclear whether this is true repurposing or simply an on-label bacterial infection use. The score is best read as graph proximity to other otitis media terms, not as proof of benefit in chronic disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a high model score and indirect evidence. No registered trial exists for this indication. Only one record (a 1981 study) tests cephalexin in chronic otitis media. The other 19 records are microbiology, pharmacokinetic or review papers, or concern other antibiotics. Cephalexin also has limited coverage of typical chronic-disease pathogens (*Pseudomonas*, anaerobes). The package-insert safety data is missing, and that gap blocks progression to safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA source
- Approved indications and mechanism of action data, to clarify whether this is true repurposing
- Full-text review of PMID 6798820 and PMID 6361325
- Current guideline check on antibiotic choice for chronic suppurative otitis media
- Review of formulation and route, because the only direct evidence involves ear drops and no otic cephalexin product appears in the Singapore registrations
