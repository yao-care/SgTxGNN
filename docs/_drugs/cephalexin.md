# Cephalexin: From Bacterial Infections (Including Otitis Media) to Chronic Otitis Media

## One-Sentence Summary

Cephalexin is a first-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as otitis media, streptococcal and staphylococcal infections, streptococcal pneumonia, bacterial arthritis and prostatitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **chronic otitis media**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **8 publications**, mostly bacteriology surveys and reviews, with no direct trial of cephalexin in the chronic form.
This is largely an extension of an existing use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, cephalexin is a first-generation cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Its efficacy in bacterial otitis media, a labelled use, is established, so an antibacterial link to chronic otitis media is plausible.

There are important limits. Chronic suppurative otitis media often involves *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and anaerobes, which cephalexin covers poorly. The available literature is mostly bacteriology, susceptibility surveys, and reviews of other antibiotics. The disease is also closely related to the existing otitis media indication, so the high model score partly reflects ontology proximity rather than new biology.

Other predicted indications were also reviewed. Suppurative otitis media (98.53%) and middle ear disease (98.52%) have older comparative trials (L3), but these cover acute disease and the categories overlap with the existing label. The remaining predictions (otosalpingitis, non-suppurative and allergic otitis media, cholesterol granuloma, hyperamylasemia, polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, congenital analbuminemia) have no evidence and no plausible mechanism, and are likely model artifacts.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Chronic otitis media has no registered trials and no direct cephalexin trial in the chronic form. The literature is mainly bacteriology and reviews of other drugs, and cephalexin has limited coverage of the Gram-negative organisms that dominate chronic suppurative disease. The indication also largely overlaps with the existing otitis media use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences
- Current susceptibility data for chronic otitis media pathogens, especially *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and anaerobes
- A direct comparative study of cephalexin in chronic otitis media, or confirmation that the existing label already covers the intended use
- Consideration of suppurative otitis media as an alternative research question (L3, acute-disease evidence only)
