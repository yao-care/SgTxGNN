# Rasagiline: From Parkinson Disease to PLA2G6-Associated Neurodegeneration

## One-Sentence Summary

Rasagiline is a monoamine oxidase type B (MAO-B) inhibitor, originally used to treat Parkinson disease and related parkinsonian syndromes.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration** (score 99.7%), but **no clinical trials or publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The prediction rests on model output alone, so the evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, rasagiline is a selective, irreversible MAO-B inhibitor. It reduces dopamine breakdown in the brain, and its efficacy in Parkinson disease is established.

PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration includes PARK14 dystonia-parkinsonism, so its clinical picture overlaps with parkinsonian phenotypes. This overlap probably explains the graph-based prediction. MAO-B inhibition could in principle ease dopaminergic symptoms. It would not be expected to change the underlying lipid-metabolism and iron-accumulation pathology. The link therefore remains speculative.

**Other predicted indications worth noting** (all from the same model run):
- **Lewy body dementia** (score 98.8%, Evidence Level L4): biologically plausible because it shares alpha-synuclein pathology with Parkinson disease. The supporting literature is mostly preclinical, review-type, or about adjacent conditions such as Alzheimer's disease. A Phase II trial in mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease (PMID 33614888) is indirect evidence only. No trial in Lewy body dementia itself was identified. This is the most evidence-supported prediction, and it is framed as a research question.
- **Retinal dystrophy** (score 98.8%, Evidence Level L4): a single mouse study in retinitis pigmentosa (PMID 29372592) reported delayed retinal degeneration. No human data were found.
- **Paralysis agitans, juvenile, of Hunt** (score 99.2%): a juvenile parkinsonism syndrome very close to the approved indication. The high score probably reflects ontology proximity rather than independent evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration prediction is supported only by the model score, with no trials or publications. MAO-B inhibition is not expected to affect the underlying disease pathology. Among the other predictions, Lewy body dementia and retinal dystrophy are the more plausible research questions. Both have only indirect, preclinical or adjacent-disease evidence (Level L4).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Disease-specific preclinical or clinical evidence for PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration, if this indication is pursued
- For Lewy body dementia: a dedicated trial or pilot study addressing cognitive and neuropsychiatric endpoints and tolerability
- For retinal dystrophy: replication in additional animal models and an assessment of ocular exposure and safety
