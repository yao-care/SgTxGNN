## Alemtuzumab: From Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis to Hepatic Infarction
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Alemtuzumab is an anti-CD52 antibody that depletes lymphocytes, originally used to treat relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **hepatic infarction**, but this has **no clinical trials and no publications** behind it.
Among the other candidates, **syndrome with combined immunodeficiency** has the most support (**7 clinical trials** and **8 publications**). There, alemtuzumab acts as a transplant conditioning component rather than a direct treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, alemtuzumab is an anti-CD52 monoclonal antibody that depletes T and B lymphocytes. This is the basis of its use in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, where it resets an overactive immune system.

**Hepatic infarction (rank 1):** No mechanistic rationale is evident. Lymphocyte depletion has no known role in ischaemic hepatic necrosis. The high score is a computational prediction only, with no supporting trials or literature. The other top-ranked candidates, such as urothelial carcinoma subtypes and peliosis hepatis, likewise lack a clear mechanism and any clinical evidence.

**Combined immunodeficiency (rank 2):** This is the best-supported candidate. Alemtuzumab depletes host lymphocytes and is used in reduced-intensity or serotherapy conditioning before allogeneic stem cell transplantation. This helps engraftment and reduces graft-versus-host disease in primary immunodeficiencies such as SCID, CD40L deficiency, IPEX, XIAP deficiency and chronic granulomatous disease. It is a conditioning component, not a direct treatment of the immunodeficiency.

**Hemophagocytic syndromes (ranks 9-10):** The rationale is biologically plausible, because HLH is driven by hyperactivated T cells that alemtuzumab depletes. Evidence is limited to a case report in lupus-associated HLH and general reviews.

**Hepatic veno-occlusive disease (rank 3):** This is a complication of transplant conditioning, not a disease alemtuzumab would treat. The literature mentions alemtuzumab only as part of conditioning regimens in which the condition occurs as an adverse outcome, so there is a risk of harm rather than benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. The major-level ones in the displayed results are **deferiprone** and **samarium (153Sm) lexidronam**. Moderate-level interactions include:
  - corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone)
  - acetylsalicylic acid
  - anticoagulants (heparin, argatroban, lepirudin)
  - radiopharmaceuticals and radioimmunotherapies (ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab, iobenguane I-131, strontium-89)
  - palifermin
- **Literature signals:** Alemtuzumab carries a documented risk of infection and immunosuppression (PMID 15590388). EBV-related lymphoproliferative complications have been reported (PMID 18502831). Late-onset hepatic veno-occlusive disease has been seen in conditioning regimens that include it.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, hepatic infarction, rests on a computational score alone. It has no mechanistic rationale, trials or literature. The most evidence exists for combined immunodeficiency, but it comes from small, early-phase trials and single-centre series. Alemtuzumab is only one conditioning component in these, so this is a research question rather than a ready repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For combined immunodeficiency, a study that isolates alemtuzumab's contribution to transplant outcomes, plus a review of infection and EBV-related risks
- For hemophagocytic syndromes, a review of the indirect evidence and further clinical data
- Confirmation of the approved indication and route compatibility for the Singapore licence
