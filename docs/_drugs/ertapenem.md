# Ertapenem: From Bacterial Pneumonia to Bacterial Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ertapenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem antibiotic, originally used for bacterial pneumonia (including streptococcal pneumonia) and infections caused by *Peptostreptococcus*.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bacterial arthritis**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no registered clinical trials** and **10 publications** (mostly case reports, small cohorts and reviews) for this indication.
Evidence is therefore limited, and the more actionable signals lie in other predicted indications (see the end of this report).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ertapenem is a carbapenem (a beta-lactam class) that is active against many Enterobacterales, anaerobes and streptococci. Its efficacy in respiratory and other bacterial infections is established.

These organisms are also common causes of bone and joint infections, so the biological plausibility of use in bacterial arthritis is high. The supporting reports, however, are mostly individual cases (for example *Klebsiella pneumoniae* septic wrist arthritis and *Citrobacter koseri* septic arthritis with osteomyelitis treated with ertapenem) and small cohorts. They do not amount to controlled evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions** (from the knowledge graph, not label text): epilepsy, kidney disease, pyoureter, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 38 interactions were recorded. The only one rated Major is **bupropion**. Moderate interactions are with **picosulfuric acid**, **polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes)** and **sodium sulfate**. The remaining listed interactions have no assigned severity level.
- **Other consideration**: Ertapenem is highly protein-bound, and low serum albumin may reduce drug exposure.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For bacterial arthritis, the biological plausibility is high, but the evidence is limited to case reports, small cohorts and reviews, with no registered trials. This corresponds to evidence level L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies (ideally randomised trials) of ertapenem in bacterial or septic arthritis and bone and joint infection
- The package-insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently unavailable), which are required before safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Approved indication text for the four Singapore licences, to confirm current label coverage

**Other predicted indications of note:**
- **Urinary tract infection (L1, Proceed with Guardrails):** Several completed Phase 3 trials exist, including a randomised comparison with ceftriaxone in complicated UTI (NCT01014013, n=271) and a combined analysis of two double-blind ertapenem versus ceftriaxone trials (PMID 15150185). This is largely an established use rather than a novel repurposing. Guardrails should cover antimicrobial stewardship and resistance, and the label status should be verified.
- **Staphylococcus aureus infection (L2, Research Question):** Cefazolin plus ertapenem is being tested in a Phase 2 trial for MSSA bacteremia (NCT04886284, recruiting, n=60), supported by salvage-therapy case series. Ertapenem would be an add-on partner, not monotherapy.
- **Hyperamylasemia, polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, congenital analbuminemia, toxocariasis, blood group incompatibility, *Ureaplasma* urethritis and gonococcal urethritis:** No plausible mechanism or clinical support was found. These are likely model artifacts, and all are Hold.
