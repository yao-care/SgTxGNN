# Abemaciclib: From Breast Cancer to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Abemaciclib is an oral CDK4/6 inhibitor marketed in Singapore as an anticancer drug. The Singapore licence records compiled for this report do not state an approved indication, but the published literature consistently describes its use in HR+/HER2- breast cancer. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid arthritis**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one observational, one preclinical), so evidence is limited to a research-question level.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, abemaciclib is a CDK4/6 inhibitor that blocks cell-cycle progression. Its efficacy in HR+/HER2- breast cancer is established in the literature.

Rheumatoid arthritis is driven partly by overproliferation and defective apoptosis of fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLSs). Inhibiting CDK4/6 could plausibly reduce this proliferation and modulate immune cell activity. A preclinical nanoplatform study loaded with abemaciclib reported inducing cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis in FLSs.

However, the direction of effect in humans is unclear. An observational study in breast cancer patients on CDK4/6 inhibitors looked at pre-existing and newly emerging immune-mediated diseases. These may include both new onset and flares, so it cannot show that the drug treats autoimmune disease. CDK4/6 inhibitors may also trigger autoimmune reactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is supported only by one observational report with an unclear direction of effect and one preclinical nanoplatform study. No clinical trials exist, and the evidence level is L4. Autoimmune reactions are also a possible concern with this drug class. This is best treated as a research question rather than a candidate for clinical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and a clearer mechanistic link to RA
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA
- In vivo efficacy data in RA animal models, using abemaciclib itself rather than a nanoplatform formulation
- Analysis of whether CDK4/6 inhibitor exposure triggers or worsens autoimmune disease
- A safety plan covering cardiovascular risk, drug interactions, and long-term tolerability in a non-oncology population

---

**Other predicted indications (not evaluated in detail):** The data also include amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, which has preclinical evidence that abemaciclib reduces TDP-43 accumulation and is a "Research Question" at L4. Heart disease is not supported, since the literature describes cardiotoxicity rather than benefit. Hyperthyroidism, multiple endocrine neoplasia, and several rare syndromes have little or no supporting evidence and are rated Hold.
