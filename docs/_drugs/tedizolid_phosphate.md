# Tedizolid Phosphate: From Acute Bacterial Skin Infections to Post-Infectious Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tedizolid phosphate is an oxazolidinone antibacterial. Its registered trial program targets acute Gram-positive infections such as skin and soft tissue infection and ventilated pneumonia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **post-infectious syndrome**, but only **1 clinical trial** of uncertain relevance and **0 publications** are linked to this prediction, so the evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the source record. Based on the mechanistic assessment attached to this prediction, tedizolid is an oxazolidinone that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 50S ribosomal subunit. It acts against active Gram-positive infection.

A post-infectious syndrome is defined by immune-mediated or sequelae processes that continue after the pathogen has cleared. Tedizolid's antibacterial mechanism has no clear link to these processes. The connection is indirect and probably reflects the two conditions sitting close together in the bacterial-infection part of the knowledge graph rather than a shared biological pathway. This prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not as a mechanistically supported new use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only linked trial tests tedizolid in acute MRSA infection, not in a post-infectious condition, and there is no literature. The mechanism does not plausibly address immune-mediated or sequelae processes, and "post-infectious syndrome" is a non-specific category. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough to justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- The HSA-approved indication text, to confirm what counts as the original indication
- Verification of the registered condition in NCT01967225
- A precise clinical definition of "post-infectious syndrome" and a plausible mechanistic hypothesis
- A route-compatibility assessment against the target condition
