## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, fluorometholone is a synthetic corticosteroid that acts on the glucocorticoid receptor (NR3C1). Its anti-inflammatory activity is established in ocular inflammation, so a mechanistic link to other immune-mediated inflammatory conditions is conceivable.

Postinfectious vasculitis is immune-mediated, so a corticosteroid effect is plausible in principle. However, fluorometholone is an ophthalmic suspension with minimal systemic exposure, so it is unlikely to reach therapeutic levels in vascular tissue. The high TxGNN score is a model prediction with no supporting trials or literature.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (from knowledge graph, not label text):** herpes simplex virus keratitis, viral eye infection, fungal infection of eye, ocular tuberculosis, eye infectious disease, conjunctivitis (infective), open-angle glaucoma, ocular hypertension, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, porphyria (including inherited porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disease), G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosemia, corneal pigmentation.

These come from knowledge graph edges and should be verified against the package insert.

**Drug Interactions:** 11 potential interactions were identified, all with an unclassified severity level: doxycycline, salbutamol, cyclosporine, acetaminophen, digoxin, brimonidine, cephalexin, alendronic acid, acyclovir, diazepam and fenofibrate.

**Additional cautions for new indications:**
- Steroid use in infectious keratoconjunctivitis risks prolonging infection, raising intraocular pressure and delaying healing.
- Use in otitis externa would be a concern if the tympanic membrane is perforated.
- Steroids may risk parasite reactivation in Chagas disease.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information. The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications have not yet been obtained.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, postinfectious vasculitis, has no clinical trials or literature (evidence level L5). A topical ocular steroid with minimal systemic exposure is also unlikely to be effective for a systemic vascular condition. Any further work should focus on the ocular candidates (punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, lens-induced iridocyclitis, glaucomatocyclitic crisis), which are better supported mechanistically and could be treated as research questions.
