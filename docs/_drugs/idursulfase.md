# Idursulfase: From Mucopolysaccharidosis to Ptosis-Strabismus-Ectopic Pupils Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Idursulfase is an enzyme replacement therapy originally used to treat mucopolysaccharidosis (Hunter syndrome, MPS II).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ptosis-strabismus-ectopic pupils syndrome** with a very high score, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support this prediction, so it rests on model output alone.
The only prediction with any supporting literature is the closely related lysosomal disorder, Scheie syndrome, and that evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, idursulfase is a recombinant iduronate-2-sulfatase. It breaks down glycosaminoglycans (dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate) inside lysosomes, and this is why it is used in MPS II.

For the top-ranked prediction, **no mechanistic link could be identified**. Ptosis-strabismus-ectopic pupils syndrome is a congenital ocular and neurodevelopmental malformation with no known glycosaminoglycan accumulation. The high score is most likely a knowledge-graph artifact caused by shared ocular phenotype nodes, not a biological rationale.

The other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. These include camptodactyly-myopia-medial rectus fibrosis, congenital Horner syndrome, ptosis-vocal cord paralysis syndrome, jaw-winking syndrome, congenital entropion, epiblepharon and congenital ectropion. All are structural or neural developmental conditions unrelated to lysosomal storage, and idursulfase is not expected to correct them. The drug also does not cross the blood-brain barrier appreciably, which further weakens any neurological rationale.

The one exception is **Scheie syndrome** (rank 10, score 94.79%), the attenuated form of MPS I. It involves the same GAG degradation pathway, but the missing enzyme is alpha-L-iduronidase, which idursulfase does not replace. The appropriate therapy there is laronidase, so direct benefit from idursulfase is not expected.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered, for the top prediction or for any of the other predicted indications.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The data compiled for this report found no recorded drug interactions. The literature does note that infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions can lead to interruption of enzyme replacement therapy, and desensitization protocols have been used to restore treatment in MPS patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions have no clinical trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. They are most likely knowledge-graph artifacts based on shared ocular phenotypes. Scheie syndrome is the only biologically adjacent candidate, but the missing enzyme there is not the one idursulfase replaces, so it is better treated as a research question than a development candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert from the Singapore Health Sciences Authority (HSA), covering the approved indication, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) to support any mechanistic analysis

- For Scheie syndrome, a comparison against laronidase to show whether idursulfase adds any value
