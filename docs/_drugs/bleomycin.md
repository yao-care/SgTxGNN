# Bleomycin: From Lymphoma and Head and Neck Cancer to Cauda Equina Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Bleomycin is a cytotoxic anticancer antibiotic, originally used for Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphomas, Langerhans cell histiocytosis and head and neck cancer.
The TxGNN model ranks **cauda equina neoplasm** as its top predicted new indication, but this prediction rests on **no clinical trials** and **1 indirect publication**.
Among the other top-10 predictions, the best-supported is **reticulum cell sarcoma** (a historical term for non-Hodgkin lymphoma), with **11 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. It largely overlaps with bleomycin's existing lymphoma uses.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Bleomycin is a cytotoxic antibiotic that is generally known to cause DNA single- and double-strand breaks. Its efficacy in lymphoma is established, and this mechanism may plausibly apply to germ cell tumours.

For cauda equina neoplasm, the only linked paper describes radiation therapy for relapsed CNS germinoma after chemotherapy that included carboplatin, etoposide and bleomycin (PEB). This is indirect evidence. Bleomycin also penetrates the blood-brain and blood-spinal cord barriers poorly, which weakens the rationale for a tumour in this location.

Other predictions in the top 10 show different strengths:
- **Reticulum cell sarcoma** (rank 3, score 99.14%): this is the strongest-supported prediction. The term is historical for diffuse large B-cell/non-Hodgkin lymphoma, so it largely overlaps with bleomycin's approved lymphoma uses. This limits its repurposing novelty.
- **Adult astrocytic tumour** (rank 2, score 99.28%): older studies of bleomycin in glioma exist, but there are no controlled efficacy data.
- **Interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma** (rank 8, score 98.63%): several case reports describe remission after ABVD chemotherapy (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine).
- **Primary pulmonary lymphoma, pulmonary blastoma, small cell lung carcinoma, well-differentiated fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and astrocytoma (excluding glioblastoma)**: evidence is absent, indirect or limited to case reports. Bleomycin's pulmonary toxicity is an added concern for lung diseases.

## Safety Considerations

- **Pulmonary toxicity**: the literature linked to this report repeatedly describes bleomycin pulmonary toxicity, including interstitial pneumonitis, a risk that increases with age, and enhanced toxicity when combined with radiotherapy. This matters particularly for any lung-related use.
- **Hyperpyrexic reaction**: a case report describes fatal hyperpyrexia in a lymphoma patient.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The only one with a classified level is levofloxacin (Minor); the rest are unclassified. Examples include pantoprazole, palonosetron, lactulose, aprepitant, morphine, omeprazole, vancomycin, prednisone, hydrocortisone and ondansetron.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, cauda equina neoplasm, has no clinical trials and only one indirect paper. Poor penetration of the blood-brain and blood-spinal cord barriers further weakens the case. The best-supported prediction, reticulum cell sarcoma, is essentially non-Hodgkin lymphoma, which bleomycin already treats, so it offers little repurposing novelty. Bleomycin's contribution within multi-drug regimens also cannot be isolated.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator, as safety screening cannot proceed without them
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licence
- Mechanism of action data
- For CNS or spinal tumours: evidence on drug delivery across the blood-brain and blood-spinal cord barriers, and any direct clinical data for cauda equina neoplasm
- For lymphoma-type predictions: confirmation of how the predicted disease differs from bleomycin's existing lymphoma uses
