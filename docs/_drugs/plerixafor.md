## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Plerixafor is a CXCR4 antagonist, and it also acts on ACKR3. CXCR4/CXCL12 signalling anchors myeloma cells in the bone marrow. Blocking this axis releases cells from the marrow niche. This is how plerixafor works as a stem cell mobiliser, given together with G-CSF before autologous transplant. According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for plerixafor: "Plerixafor is an inhibitor of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor and blocks binding of its cognate ligand, stromal cell-derived factor-1α (SDF-1α). SDF-1α and CXCR4 are recognized to play a role in the trafficking and homing of human hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) to the marrow compartment." ([DailyMed: Mozobil (plerixafor) prescribing information, Sanofi-Aventis U.S. LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=0ed08d2b-5051-46b2-aa37-1d6275bf9003), label version 2025-09-22; accessed 2026-11-20.)

The original and predicted indications are closely related, since both are plasma cell disorders. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects this proximity rather than independent evidence. Plerixafor's established myeloma use is stem cell mobilisation, not treatment of the disease itself. In indolent disease, which is usually observed or treated lightly, a mobilising agent has no clear therapeutic role.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no trials or publications for this subtype, and plerixafor's role in myeloma is mobilisation rather than disease treatment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or literature evidence specific to indolent or smouldering myeloma, such as a rationale for a therapeutic role beyond mobilisation
- Full package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, and mechanism-of-action data
- A clear clinical question defining the intended use, population and dosing

**Note on other predictions:** The same analysis ranks **myeloid leukaemia** (score 0.9902) with 25 registered trials and 20 publications. These are mostly Phase 1 and Phase 1/2 studies of plerixafor as a chemosensitiser in acute myeloid leukaemia (for example NCT00512252 and NCT00906945, plus the Blood 2012 Phase 1/2 study, PMID 22308295). That evidence is graded L2 and flagged as a research question, but it lacks randomised or Phase 3 data. If the aim is to find the best-supported repurposing direction for plerixafor, this indication is a more productive area to evaluate. Note that the knowledge graph also lists leukaemia among contraindication-related conditions, which would need to be resolved first.
