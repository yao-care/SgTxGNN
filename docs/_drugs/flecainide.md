# Flecainide: From Atrial Fibrillation (Arrhythmia) to Stroke Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Flecainide is an oral class Ic antiarrhythmic (sodium channel blocker) used for atrial fibrillation (AF).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **stroke disorder**, but the link is indirect: stroke is a downstream outcome of AF rhythm control, not a separate drug target.
The pack lists **19 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. Only one trial is graded strongly relevant, and none tests flecainide alone against a stroke endpoint.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the pack. Flecainide is a class Ic antiarrhythmic that blocks sodium channels. Its established use is rhythm control in AF.

AF raises stroke risk about five-fold. The plausible chain is that keeping sinus rhythm may reduce AF-related thromboembolism. The best support is EAST-AFNET 4, a large randomized trial of early rhythm control, in which flecainide and propafenone were among the permitted sodium channel blockers. Its composite endpoint includes stroke. It tests a whole strategy (drugs plus ablation), so the effect cannot be attributed to flecainide.

The very high TxGNN score is most likely a knowledge-graph artifact of the AF–stroke association. Flecainide is also generally unsuitable for patients with structural heart disease or prior myocardial infarction, and many stroke patients have coronary disease. That limits the population where the idea could apply.

## Safety Considerations

The package insert warnings and contraindications have not been retrieved, and no drug interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

Signals from the evidence review that matter for this repurposing idea:
- **Structural heart disease and post-MI:** flecainide is contraindicated or unsuitable in these settings, and coronary disease is common in stroke patients.
- **Proarrhythmia:** clinical concern about proarrhythmic effects in patients with cardiovascular disease (PMID 38702961), and a refractory ventricular tachycardia case report (PMID 40800559).
- **Brugada pattern:** class Ic drugs can unmask it, especially in overdose (PMID 27884575).
- **Anticoagulant co-use:** stroke patients with AF usually take DOACs. Interactions between antiarrhythmics and DOACs have been studied (PMID 41152878).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The stroke prediction rests on the AF–stroke link, and the only strong trial (EAST-AFNET 4) tests a rhythm-control strategy, not flecainide. The registered trials with flecainide as the intervention either target arrhythmia rather than stroke or have not reported. The safety review is also blocked because the package insert data is missing. The pack classifies this as a research question rather than a candidate for clinical use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the HSA package insert (warnings, contraindications, interactions) and confirm the approved indication text for both licences.
- Obtain detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Wait for results from EAST-STROKE (NCT05293080) and the flecainide PFO trial (NCT05213104).
- Analyze the flecainide subgroup within EAST-AFNET 4 for stroke outcomes.
- Define a safety-screened target population (no structural heart disease or coronary disease) and a plan for DOAC co-medication.
- Note that the TxGNN entry "cerebrovascular disorder" (L3) shares the same indirect evidence. The "obsolete susceptibility to ischemic stroke" entry overlaps with stroke disorder and should be merged or dropped.
- Note that the "sick sinus syndrome 2" prediction is more likely a safety concern than an opportunity.
