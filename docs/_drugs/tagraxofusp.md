# Tagraxofusp: From Blastic Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cell Neoplasm to Esotropia

## One-Sentence Summary

Tagraxofusp is a CD123-directed diphtheria toxin fusion protein, approved for blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm (BPDCN) according to the trial records in the Evidence Pack.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **esotropia** with a very high score, but **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** support this direction.
The prediction rests on the graph model alone and has no mechanistic or clinical support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Tagraxofusp targets CD123 (the IL-3 receptor alpha chain). It delivers a diphtheria toxin payload that kills CD123-expressing cells, and this mechanism underlies its use in CD123-positive blood cancers.

Esotropia is an ocular motor disorder (inward deviation of the eye). It has no known link to CD123 expression or to the cell types this drug kills. The high TxGNN score of 0.997 is a graph-based association, not a mechanistic one, and the pack found no clinical or literature evidence for it. This prediction is not considered plausible.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The esotropia prediction has only a model score (L5), with no trials, no literature, and no plausible link between CD123 targeting and ocular motor disease. A systemic cytotoxic biologic is also unsuitable for a benign, non-life-threatening condition.

For context, the rank 2 prediction (pre-malignant neoplasm) has five early-phase trials (L3, "Research Question"). However, these trials enrol overt malignancies such as AML and myelofibrosis, so the match to pre-malignant disease is indirect.
