# Dopamine: From Hypotensive Disorder to Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Dopamine is a catecholamine vasopressor, originally used to treat hypotensive disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)**,
but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **5 publications** (none testing dopamine as a treatment) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, dopamine is an endogenous catecholamine and a precursor of norepinephrine. It acts as a vasopressor on dopaminergic and adrenergic receptors, and its efficacy in hypotensive states is established.

POTS is an autonomic disorder involving catecholamine dysregulation and abnormal neurohumoral responses to standing. The literature supports a biological link, including catecholamine abnormalities and norepinephrine-transporter mutations in familial cases. This makes a connection to a catecholamine drug plausible at the model level.

However, the retrieved papers concern other agents (bupropion, DA-9701) or are descriptive. No study tests dopamine itself as a POTS treatment. Intravenous dopamine is also impractical for a chronic outpatient condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output and an indirect biological link. No trial or paper tests dopamine for POTS, and intravenous dosing is poorly suited to a chronic outpatient condition. Other predicted indications are weaker still: multiple system atrophy, gastroduodenitis and peptic ulcer disease have only indirect L4 evidence, and the rest have none or only irrelevant literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for dopamine
- Direct clinical evidence of dopamine in POTS, plus an assessment of whether a non-intravenous route is feasible
- A review of knowledge-graph contraindications against the intended patient population
