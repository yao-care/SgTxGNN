# Thrombin: From Fibrin Sealant Hemostasis to Primary Release Disorder of Platelets

## One-Sentence Summary

Thrombin is a clotting protein used in Singapore as a component of fibrin sealant products (topical hemostasis). The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **primary release disorder of platelets**, but no clinical trials and no literature testing thrombin as a treatment for this condition were found. The evidence is **model prediction only (0 clinical trials)**, and the retrieved publications are general background on platelet biology.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Thrombin is the enzyme that converts fibrinogen to fibrin. It is also a strong platelet activator that triggers granule release. This is the likely basis for the model linking it to a platelet release (secretion) disorder.

On closer inspection, the link is weak. A platelet secretion defect would not be corrected by adding thrombin, because the problem lies in the platelet's own release machinery. Thrombin is also used as a topical or local agent, not a systemic therapy for platelet function defects. The high score appears to reflect a network association with platelet biology rather than a therapeutic rationale.

For context, one other predicted indication, **esophageal disease**, has more substantial support. It rests on a Phase 3 trial and a meta-analysis of thrombin injection for bleeding gastric varices (see the conclusion).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output alone (L5). There are no trials, and the literature describes platelet biology without showing any therapeutic role for thrombin. Mechanistically, a platelet release defect would not be repaired by exogenous thrombin, and a topical agent does not address a systemic defect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the registered products, which are currently unavailable
- Mechanism of action data
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence that thrombin improves platelet secretion or bleeding in this disorder
- A route-of-administration assessment, since the registered products are topical sealants
- Consideration of the better-supported indication, **bleeding gastric varices (under "esophageal disease")**. It is supported by a small Phase 3 trial (NCT01717612, n=62, status unknown) and a systematic review and meta-analysis. This would need to be assessed as a separate candidate restricted to variceal bleeding.
