# Propofol: From General Anaesthesia and Sedation to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Propofol is an intravenous anaesthetic and sedative that is marketed in Singapore under 6 valid registrations.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, particularly acute and refractory migraine treated in the emergency department.
Currently **3 clinical trials** (1 completed Phase 2/3) and **20 publications**, including several randomised trials and systematic reviews, support this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, propofol is a GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator. Enhanced GABAergic inhibition may dampen trigeminovascular and cortical hyperexcitability, both of which are involved in migraine.

Preclinical work (PMID 22390898) shows that propofol hemisuccinate suppresses cortical spreading depression. This is the proposed substrate of migraine aura and a possible trigger of headache pain. This supports a plausible mechanism for an abortive effect in refractory migraine.

The clinical rationale is also practical. Patients with severe migraine who fail first-line emergency treatments (NSAIDs, dopamine antagonists, triptans) have few good options, and sub-anaesthetic doses of propofol have been reported to help. Propofol is already used in hospitals under monitored conditions, which would make this use easier to introduce.

Other predicted indications were also reviewed. Migraine with brainstem aura is a possible extension, but its evidence is extrapolated from general migraine. The remaining predictions (Prinzmetal angina, Tourette syndrome, tendinitis, fibromyalgia and others) rest on incidental anaesthesia literature or on model scores alone, and are not supported as therapeutic uses.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph** (these are model-derived contraindication links, not label text): epilepsy, hypotensive disorder, respiratory failure, intracranial arteriosclerosis, inherited lipid metabolism disorder and familial hyperlipidaemia.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The ones shown are mostly of moderate severity, and one is minor (metronidazole). Examples include:
  - Opioids: morphine (including liposomal), opium
  - Antiemetics: ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron
  - Gastrointestinal agents: famotidine, loperamide, cisapride, and laxatives such as bisacodyl, lactulose, lactitol, magnesium citrate, magnesium hydroxide, castor oil, glycerin and mineral oil
  - Antibiotics: clarithromycin, levofloxacin

  Several of these (ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, cisapride, clarithromycin, levofloxacin) are agents commonly associated with QT prolongation, which is relevant in an emergency setting where antiemetics are often co-administered. Please verify this against the interaction source before relying on it.
- A case-report signal of anaesthesia-related coronary spasm was also found in the literature (Prinzmetal angina search); it is a safety signal rather than evidence of benefit.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as label-based safety data was not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 2/3 trial in paediatric migraine, several randomised trials in adults and children, and systematic reviews support the use of low-dose propofol for refractory acute migraine in the emergency department. A plausible GABAergic and cortical spreading depression mechanism adds support. However, the trials are small and the existing reviews describe the evidence as limited, so the evidence level is L2 and not enough for an unrestricted recommendation. The other predicted indications are not supported and should stay on hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications from HSA, which are currently missing and are a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Confirmation of the original approved indications for the Singapore registrations
- A confirmatory, adequately powered Phase 3 trial, with dose and setting defined
- A guardrail protocol limited to monitored hospital or emergency settings, with trained staff, airway and haemodynamic monitoring, and dose limited to sub-anaesthetic levels
- A review of the knowledge-graph disease associations (epilepsy, hypotension, respiratory failure and lipid disorders) and of concurrent QT-prolonging drugs before use
- Confirmation of whether the sub-anaesthetic migraine dose falls outside the approved labelling, and whether a new indication application would be required in Singapore
