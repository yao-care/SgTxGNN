## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Piribedil is a D2/D3 dopamine receptor agonist with alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist activity. Its use in Parkinson disease rests on stimulating dopamine receptors to compensate for lost dopaminergic signalling.

Dopamine does play a role in normal retinal physiology. However, no link has been shown between this activity and the pathogenesis of inherited retinal dystrophy. The only publication retrieved concerns syncope in Kearns-Sayre syndrome, a mitochondrial disorder that can include retinopathy, and it does not evaluate piribedil. The high TxGNN score therefore reflects a computational association, not demonstrated efficacy.

Other top-ranked predictions are of mixed plausibility:
- **Plausible (symptomatic dopaminergic therapy):** juvenile parkinsonism (Hunt type), juvenile-onset Parkinson disease 19A (flagged as a research question), and PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration. None has trials or literature specific to piribedil, and these predictions largely reflect similarity to the existing indication.
- **Weak rationale:** X-linked myopia (two forms), where dopamine modulates eye growth, but the genetic cause and the need for systemic dosing make benefit unlikely.
- **No identified rationale:** perisylvian polymicrogyria, lethal infantile mitochondrial myopathy, congenital disorder of glycosylation with defective fucosylation, and atypical glycine encephalopathy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. No clinical trials exist, the single retrieved publication does not involve piribedil, and no mechanistic link to retinal dystrophy is established. Evidence is at L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank).
- Preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking dopamine D2/D3 agonism or alpha-2 antagonism to retinal dystrophy pathology.
- If a repurposing programme is pursued, consider prioritising the parkinsonism-related candidates (juvenile parkinsonism, juvenile-onset Parkinson disease 19A), which are mechanistically more coherent. These would be framed as research questions on symptomatic management.
