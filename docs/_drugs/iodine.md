# Iodine: From Iodinated Contrast Imaging to Sjogren Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Iodine is registered in Singapore as the active component of iodinated contrast injections (Iomeron, Ultravist), and its original indication is not stated in the registration data.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Sjogren syndrome**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **association-type literature** behind this, with no evidence of therapeutic benefit.
The high score most likely reflects the biological link between iodine uptake and salivary gland or thyroid disease, not a treatment effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the registered products, iodine here is delivered as iodinated contrast media for imaging. It is not used as a treatment, so there is no established therapeutic use to extend to Sjogren syndrome.

The literature links iodine and Sjogren syndrome only through association:

- Salivary glands take up iodine through the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS).
- Radioactive iodine (131I) therapy can cause sialadenitis and sicca (dry eye and dry mouth) symptoms that resemble Sjogren syndrome.
- Sjogren syndrome often co-occurs with autoimmune thyroid disease. A 2025 in-silico study examined NIS epitopes as a possible shared target.

These links most likely explain the high graph-based score. They point to iodine as a cause of, or marker for, salivary gland dysfunction, not as a therapy. No source supports iodine treating Sjogren syndrome.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Note from the literature: radioactive iodine therapy is associated with salivary and lacrimal gland dysfunction. This is a reason for caution, not a safety profile for the registered contrast products.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on association, not therapeutic evidence. There are no clinical trials, and the literature describes iodine as a cause of salivary gland injury or a thyroid-disease link, not a treatment. The registered products are injectable contrast agents with no known route or rationale for treating Sjogren syndrome.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Preclinical or clinical evidence that iodine improves Sjogren outcomes, and a clear intended route of administration
- Consider prioritising other predictions for this drug. **Keratitis** (rank 7, L2, 5 trials including completed Phase 2 and terminated Phase 3 povidone-iodine studies) and **acne** (rank 3, L2, a Phase 2 topical povidone-iodine trial) have the strongest support. Both use topical povidone-iodine, which is not among the registered injectable products.
