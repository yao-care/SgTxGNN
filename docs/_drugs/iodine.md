# Iodine: From Iodinated Contrast Imaging to Sjögren Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Iodine is marketed in Singapore as part of iodinated contrast injections (Iomeron, Ultravist, Omnipaque).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Sjögren syndrome**, but **no clinical trials** and only **20 publications** support this, and those describe associations, adverse effects, or diagnostic use rather than treatment.
The one direction with real clinical-trial activity is **keratitis** (povidone-iodine), with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. All seven Singapore registrations are iodinated injections (iomeprol, iopamidol-type and iohexol-type contrast products), whose original role is diagnostic imaging, not treatment.

For Sjögren syndrome, the literature does not support a therapeutic link. It shows that Sjögren syndrome co-occurs with autoimmune thyroid disease, that the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS), which takes up iodine, is expressed in both thyroid and salivary glands and is discussed as a possible shared autoantigen, and that radioactive iodine can cause salivary gland inflammation resembling or complicating Sjögren syndrome. Iodine is therefore more plausibly a risk factor or a diagnostic agent (sialography contrast) than a treatment. The high model score is not backed by clinical evidence.

The more credible repurposing signal is **keratitis**. Povidone-iodine (an iodine complex) releases free iodine and has bactericidal, fungicidal, virucidal and amoebicidal activity, and it is already used in ophthalmology for antisepsis. This gives a direct mechanistic rationale for infectious corneal ulcers. Note that it concerns a topical antiseptic form, not the injectable contrast products registered in Singapore.

Other predictions (rank 2 to 10) have little or no support:
- **Acne** (93.1%): only indirect antimicrobial rationale and an uncontrolled pilot of PVP-iodine hydrogel in infective dermatoses (L4).
- **Recurrent corneal erosion** (90.7%): a single 1964 paper (L4).
- **Lacrimal gland neoplasm** (89.4%): literature mainly reports lacrimal damage caused by radioactive iodine, a safety signal (L4).
- **Lacrimal gland prolapse, vulvar inverted follicular keratosis**: no evidence.
- **Seborrheic keratosis, fetal erythroblastosis, peptic esophagitis**: literature is diagnostic or adverse-effect only (L5).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions** (3 moderate-level interactions): Iodide I-131, Iodide I-123 and lithium carbonate. Lithium combined with iodine may add to thyroid-suppressing effects.
- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph** (contraindication links in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text): epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thyroid conditions (hypothyroidism, myxedema, congenital hypothyroidism, Hashimoto thyroiditis), blood disorders (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis), respiratory conditions (bronchitis, respiratory failure, pulmonary tuberculosis), heart block, pyoureter and hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis. These need confirmation against the product labels.
- **Safety signals in the literature**: radioactive iodine causes salivary and lacrimal gland dysfunction and nasolacrimal duct obstruction, and iodinated contrast used in amniography was linked to fetal thyroid hyperplasia. These argue against a therapeutic use in Sjögren syndrome and lacrimal conditions.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction (Sjögren syndrome) is supported only by association and adverse-effect literature, with no trials, so the high model score is not backed by clinical evidence. The injectable contrast products registered in Singapore are also not the form relevant to the best-supported signal (topical povidone-iodine in infectious keratitis), which remains a research question rather than a ready candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Outcome data from NCT00516399 (terminated Phase 3), NCT00386958 and NCT03484507 for keratitis
- A decision on whether povidone-iodine, a topical ophthalmic antiseptic, is the product to evaluate, since it is distinct from the registered injectable contrast agents, plus its registration status in Singapore
- For Sjögren syndrome, any controlled evidence of therapeutic benefit, which is currently absent
