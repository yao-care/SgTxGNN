# Cimetidine: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Smouldering Systemic Mastocytosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Cimetidine is a histamine H2-receptor antagonist that suppresses gastric acid and is marketed in Singapore for acid-related conditions such as peptic ulcer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cimetidine is an H2-receptor blocker that reduces gastric acid secretion, and its efficacy in acid-related upper GI disease is well established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to mast cell disease only as symptom control.

In mastocytosis, mast cells release excess histamine, which can drive gastric acid hypersecretion and GI symptoms. Blocking H2 receptors could ease these symptoms. This is a symptomatic rationale only. Cimetidine has no known disease-modifying effect on the abnormal mast cell population, and the TxGNN score is the only support for this prediction.
