## One-Sentence Summary

Acyclovir is an antiviral drug originally used to treat herpes simplex infections, herpes zoster, chickenpox, herpes labialis, herpes simplex encephalitis and genital herpes.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but **no clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (case series on unrelated eye conditions) currently support this specific prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, acyclovir is a nucleoside analogue that is phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase and then inhibits herpesvirus DNA polymerase. Its efficacy in herpes infections is well established.

**Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis (top prediction):** Punctate epithelial keratitis can have a herpetic cause, so the link is plausible but indirect. The two retrieved papers concern microsporidial keratoconjunctivitis and corneal lipidosis in AIDS. Neither is an acyclovir-responsive condition, so the prediction rests on the model score alone.

**Common wart (best-supported prediction):** Warts are caused by HPV, which has no thymidine kinase of its own, so the classical acyclovir mechanism does not directly apply. Proposed explanations for intralesional acyclovir include inhibition of viral DNA synthesis in lesional cells and a local immune-modulating effect. Several comparative trials, including a Phase 2/3 randomised trial against cryotherapy, show clinical activity.

**Other predictions** are weakly supported:
- Post-infectious neuralgia, COVID-19 sequelae and epidemic keratoconjunctivitis have only indirect links.
- Hepatitis C cirrhosis, vulvovaginal candidiasis, eosinophilic pustular folliculitis and papular urticaria have no antiviral rationale and are likely model artefacts.
- Disease of the orbital region mostly captures herpetic eye disease, which is already an on-label use, so it is not a novel signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis)

**Rationale:**
The prediction has only a model score behind it (L5). The two retrieved papers are unrelated to acyclovir efficacy, and no trials exist. The mechanistic link is plausible only where the keratitis is herpetic, which is already an existing use.

**Alternative direction:** Common wart (intralesional acyclovir) reaches evidence level L2, with a Phase 2/3 RCT and several comparative trials. It could be considered a **research question**. The trials are small, mostly from a single region, with unknown status and varied comparators.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For common wart, results of the Phase 2/3 RCT and larger multi-centre, blinded trials
- For common wart, a decision on formulation and route, since intralesional use of the injectable powder would be outside the registered labelling
- For punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, evidence that the condition is herpetic, or abandon this prediction
