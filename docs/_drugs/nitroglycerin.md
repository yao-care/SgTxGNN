# Nitroglycerin: From Angina and Other Cardiovascular Uses to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide (NO) donor and vasodilator. It is marketed in Singapore in sublingual, spray, injectable, infusion and ointment forms, but the data compiled for this report do not include approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**, with **3 registered clinical trials** (2 directly relevant) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The evidence is mainly small studies, hemodynamic studies and reviews, and no completed Phase 2/3 randomized trial was identified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, nitroglycerin is an NO donor. It activates soluble guanylate cyclase, which raises cGMP and relaxes vascular smooth muscle. This produces vasodilation and lowers cardiac preload.

In pulmonary hypertension the same pathway can lower pulmonary vascular resistance and pulmonary artery pressure. The main concern with systemic delivery is non-selective vasodilation, which can cause hypotension. Inhaled or nebulized delivery may give relative pulmonary selectivity and limit systemic effects. Hemodynamic studies from the 1980s (for example, nine patients with chronic pulmonary hypertension) reported falls in pulmonary vascular resistance and pulmonary artery pressure. More recent work on nebulized nitroglycerin in children and neonates is consistent with this.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph drug-disease links, not label text)**: hypotensive disorder, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, methemoglobinemia, sulfhemoglobinemia, anemia, macrocytic anemia, intracerebral hemorrhage, constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy, cardiac tamponade.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified, and the first 20 retrieved are listed below.
  - Moderate: bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, dronabinol, nabilone, sapropterin, rosiglitazone.
  - Minor: hyoscyamine, atropine, scopolamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, trospium, mepenzolate, methscopolamine, propantheline, acetylsalicylic acid, omeprazole.
- **Considerations for pulmonary hypertension use**: systemic hypotension is the main risk with non-selective vasodilation, which is why inhaled delivery is being studied. Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the compiled data, so please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- The NO-cGMP mechanism is plausible for pulmonary vasodilation. Hemodynamic studies, a recent neonatal RCT and a small completed trial of nebulized nitroglycerin point in the same direction. However, no completed Phase 2/3 RCT exists, the registered trials are small, and the best-supported route (inhaled) is not registered in Singapore.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which are currently missing
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results of NCT07214129 and NCT04594629, and ideally an adequately powered randomized trial of nebulized nitroglycerin
- A route and formulation plan for inhaled use, since no inhaled product is registered
- A hemodynamic monitoring plan to manage hypotension risk
