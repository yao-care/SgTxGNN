# Nitroglycerin: From Established Cardiovascular Use to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide (NO)-donor vasodilator with five Singapore registrations, but the supplied registration data does not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **pulmonary hypertension**, mainly as a nebulized (inhaled) formulation.
Support is **13 listed clinical trials** (only 3 directly relevant, all small and none Phase 3) and **20 publications**, mostly small hemodynamic and pediatric studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is not available in this dataset. The analysis below relies on the evidence pack's repurposing rationale.

Nitroglycerin releases NO, which raises cGMP in vascular smooth muscle and causes vasodilation. In pulmonary hypertension, the appeal is that delivery by nebulizer could relax the pulmonary vessels selectively, with less systemic hypotension than intravenous dosing.

The published literature is consistent with this idea:
- **Early hemodynamic studies (1983–1984):** nitroglycerin lowered pulmonary vascular resistance in patients with chronic pulmonary hypertension, and did so in COPD-related pulmonary hypertension.
- **Inhaled nitroglycerin:** small studies in children with congenital heart disease and in adults undergoing mitral valve surgery.
- **Nebulized nitroglycerin trials:** small trials in pulmonary arterial hypertension (vasoreactivity testing) and in persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN).

There is no Phase 3 RCT and no outcome data showing lasting benefit. Most evidence shows short-term hemodynamic effects, often in the perioperative or neonatal setting.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and small nebulized-nitroglycerin studies (PAH, PPHN, pediatric congenital heart disease, cardiac surgery) point in the same direction. However, there is no Phase 3 RCT, no long-term outcome data, and no registered inhaled formulation in Singapore. The package-insert safety data has not been retrieved, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- DrugBank mechanism-of-action data
- Approved indication text for the five Singapore licences
- Confirmation of intervention arms and results for NCT04594629, NCT06107465 and NCT07214129
- A nebulized or inhaled formulation route, or a decision that the use stays within specialist perioperative or vasoreactivity-testing settings
- Efficacy data beyond acute hemodynamics, for example a randomized trial with clinical outcomes

Other predicted indications are weaker. Prinzmetal angina overlaps with nitroglycerin's established anti-anginal use and may reflect a labeling gap rather than true repurposing. The headache prediction reflects nitroglycerin's role as a headache trigger, not a treatment signal.
