## One-Sentence Summary

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin's lymphoma**,
but support is limited: **2 Phase 2 clinical trials** (neither isolates daunorubicin) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews and studies of related lymphomas) are currently available.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, daunorubicin is an anthracycline that intercalates into DNA and inhibits topoisomerase II. This is the same mechanism as doxorubicin, which is a backbone of Hodgkin lymphoma regimens such as ABVD.

Its efficacy in leukaemia is established. Leukaemia and lymphoma are both lymphohaematopoietic malignancies, and anthracyclines are well established in lymphoid cancers. This makes the prediction plausible at the drug-class level.

The caveat is that the available trials are multi-agent Phase 2 studies that do not separate daunorubicin's contribution. Reviews note that standard first-line therapy for advanced Hodgkin's lymphoma is ABVD, which uses doxorubicin rather than daunorubicin. The evidence is therefore indirect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The knowledge graph links daunorubicin to caution in neutropenia and agranulocytosis, kidney disease, liver disease, cardiomyopathy, heart conduction disease and ventricular tachycardia, sudden cardiac arrest, and infectious disease (including fungal infection). These are graph associations and need confirmation against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include cisapride, dolasetron, papaverine, deferiprone and macimorelin. Moderate-level examples include clarithromycin, levofloxacin, ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron and famotidine. Several of these relate to QT prolongation, which is relevant given the cardiac concerns.

Please also refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and anthracyclines are established in lymphoid cancers. However, no trial or publication tests daunorubicin in Hodgkin's lymphoma, and the standard regimen (ABVD) already uses doxorubicin. This is a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, including the approved indication text
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Direct evidence of daunorubicin in Hodgkin's lymphoma, such as comparative data against doxorubicin
- A cardiac and haematological safety monitoring plan, with review of the interacting drugs
