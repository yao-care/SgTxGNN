# Daunorubicin: From Its Registered Use (Indication Not Recorded) to Hodgkin's Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, registered in Singapore as an injectable product, but the local licence record lists no approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin's lymphoma**, with **50 matched clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved for this disease.
Almost all of this evidence concerns doxorubicin-based or other regimens, and none of it shows a daunorubicin-specific trial in Hodgkin's lymphoma.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for daunorubicin. Based on general anthracycline pharmacology, it acts by intercalating into DNA and inhibiting topoisomerase II. That cytotoxic mechanism is plausible in a chemosensitive lymphoma.

Doxorubicin, a close anthracycline relative, is already a standard component of Hodgkin's lymphoma regimens such as ABVD and AVD. This gives the prediction class-level support. Daunorubicin itself has no established role in Hodgkin's lymphoma.

The direct evidence is thin. One small study of liposomal daunorubicin (DaunoXome) in 19 patients with relapsed or refractory lymphoma reported one complete and two partial responses at the higher dose. It was a different formulation from the Singapore product, and the patients had relapsed or refractory lymphoma.

## Conclusion and Next Steps
