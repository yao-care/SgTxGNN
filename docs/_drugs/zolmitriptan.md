## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Structured mechanism-of-action data was not available for this report. The published literature describes zolmitriptan as a selective serotonin 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist (a triptan). It acts both peripherally and centrally. It is thought to constrict cranial blood vessels and to inhibit trigeminovascular activation, and pre-clinical work supports this central and peripheral action.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype that shares the same trigeminovascular pathophysiology as the approved indication. Mechanistically the link is therefore strong, and this likely explains the very high model score.

There is an important caveat. The retrieved literature covers general migraine treatment and triptan reviews, not brainstem aura specifically. Some triptans carry cautions in brainstem or hemiplegic aura because of theoretical vascular concerns. Subtype-specific efficacy and safety data are needed before any conclusion.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the drug-disease knowledge graph (not label text):**
  - Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia, Prinzmetal angina, Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome, hypertension, stroke, transient ischemic attack, cerebral infarction, brain ischemia, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, intracranial arteriosclerosis
  - Vascular: peripheral vascular and arterial disease, intermittent claudication, Raynaud disease, ischemic bowel disease
  - Other: familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, hemoglobinopathy, pyoureter
- **Drug Interactions:** 82 interactions are recorded.
  - Major interactions: lorcaserin, dolasetron, palonosetron, ondansetron, granisetron, dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine and sibutramine.
  - Moderate interactions: morphine (including liposomal), cimetidine and obeticholic acid.
- **Case-level signal:** A published case report describes zolmitriptan-associated myocardial infarction.

For full warnings, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link is plausible, but evidence is at the L4 level. There are no clinical trials and no publications specific to brainstem aura. The knowledge graph also flags related contraindications, including hemiplegic migraine and cerebrovascular conditions, that raise subtype-specific safety concerns. The approved migraine indication is not in question. The open question is whether the brainstem aura subtype is a safe and useful extension.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific efficacy and safety data in brainstem aura (clinical studies or case series)
- A review of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular risk for patients with brainstem or hemiplegic aura
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A drug interaction management plan, especially for serotonergic co-medications
