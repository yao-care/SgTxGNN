# Nicotinamide: From Parenteral Vitamin Supplementation to Elevated Plasma Zinc

Nicotinamide (vitamin B3) is marketed in Singapore as a component of parenteral multivitamin infusion products.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, nicotinamide is part of the vitamin B3 class and serves as an NAD+ precursor. It is supplied in Singapore as a component of multivitamin infusions.

The prediction is **not** mechanistically supported. No plausible pathway links nicotinamide to lowering plasma zinc. Elevated plasma zinc is a laboratory finding rather than a treatable disease entity. The high score (97.83%) reflects graph proximity only. The retrieved trials were matched on the keyword "zinc" and mostly study zinc or micronutrient supplementation, which is the opposite clinical question.

Other predictions in the same list have a more coherent rationale. **Werner syndrome** (rank 4) is the clearest example. WRN deficiency is associated with NAD+ depletion and impaired mitophagy, and nicotinamide feeds NAD+ through the salvage pathway. The evidence there is preclinical plus one RCT of nicotinamide *riboside*, a related but distinct NAD+ precursor (PMID 40459998). It is indirect evidence for nicotinamide and would need its own evaluation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a graph-based score. No mechanism is plausible, and elevated plasma zinc is a laboratory finding, not a disease. None of the retrieved trials or publications supports nicotinamide for this use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A defined clinical condition and a plausible mechanism linking nicotinamide to zinc handling (none is currently identified)
- Mechanism of action data for nicotinamide (from DrugBank)
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- Indication text for the two Singapore registrations
- Consider redirecting effort to **Werner syndrome** (research question only). That would require nicotinamide-specific data on NAD+ boosting and safety at the doses used, since the human RCT tested nicotinamide riboside.
