# Vigabatrin: From West Syndrome to ARX-Related X-Linked Intellectual Disability (With or Without Seizures)

## One-Sentence Summary

Vigabatrin is an antiepileptic drug originally used for West syndrome (infantile spasms) and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ARX-related X-linked intellectual disability, with or without seizures**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **indirect literature** (9 publications, mostly reviews and case reports on related genetic epilepsies) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, vigabatrin irreversibly inhibits GABA transaminase, the enzyme that breaks down GABA, and so raises brain GABA levels.

ARX variants impair the development and migration of GABAergic interneurons. They are a known cause of infantile spasms and early-onset epileptic encephalopathy. Because vigabatrin is already used for infantile spasms (West syndrome), boosting GABA is a plausible way to control the spasms and seizures seen in ARX-related disease.

There are important limits. Vigabatrin would not be expected to correct the underlying intellectual disability. No ARX-specific vigabatrin study was identified. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** kidney disease, pyoureter, congestive heart failure, peripheral nervous system disease, peripheral neuropathy, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia and scotoma (visual field defect). The scotoma flag is consistent with the known retinal and visual field toxicity of vigabatrin, which is a particular concern in young infants.
- **Drug interactions:** 65 interactions were identified. Major interactions include systemic and other corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), oxyquinoline (topical) and deferoxamine. Moderate interactions include morphine, opium, diphenoxylate, difenoxin, dronabinol, nabilone, metoclopramide, sibutramine and ethanol.
- **Case report signal:** One case report describes vigabatrin-associated MRI abnormalities with obstructive hydrocephalus in an infant with lissencephaly.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible for the seizure component, because ARX variants affect GABAergic interneurons and vigabatrin is used in infantile spasms. However, there are no clinical trials, no ARX-specific studies, and the supporting literature is indirect. The evidence remains at the research-question stage. The visual field toxicity concern in infants further argues against moving forward without more data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- ARX-specific clinical data, such as case series or registry data on vigabatrin in ARX-related epilepsy
- A retinal and visual field monitoring plan for infants and young children
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licence, to compare with the predicted use
