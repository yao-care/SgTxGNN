## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled drug record. Based on the clinical literature, amisulpride is a selective D2/D3 dopamine receptor antagonist, with 5-HT7 antagonism at higher doses. Blocking D2 receptors is the core pharmacology behind antipsychotic efficacy in schizophrenia.

Amisulpride is a long-established antipsychotic, so schizophrenia is a known, marketed use rather than a new one. The empty original-indication field appears to be a data gap in the registration record, not evidence that the drug has another primary use. The model's high score is consistent with the mechanism and the existing clinical evidence.

Other predictions in the list are weaker:
- **Psychotic disorder** (rank 5) is supported mainly by the same trials as schizophrenia, so the evidence is not independent.
- **Dysthymic disorder** (rank 8) has a plausible mechanism and literature, including double-blind comparisons with other antidepressants. Low-dose amisulpride is thought to increase dopaminergic transmission by blocking presynaptic autoreceptors. However, no registered trials are present and the key evidence dates from around 2002, so it is a research question rather than a ready candidate.
- **Anxiety disorder** (rank 10) has only indirect support, and no efficacy trials in anxiety are listed.
- The remaining predictions (retinal dystrophy, X-linked myopias, hydranencephaly, polymicrogyria) have no trials or literature and no plausible mechanistic link. These should be held.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs, of which 20 are listed in the compiled data. Several are rated **Major**: cisapride, clarithromycin, dolasetron, granisetron, levofloxacin and metoclopramide. Many of these are QT-prolonging or related agents, which is consistent with amisulpride's known QTc risk. Moderate interactions include famotidine, epinephrine, hydrocortisone, amphotericin B, and several laxatives and antidiarrhoeals (bisacodyl, castor oil, glycerin, lactitol, lactulose, loperamide, magnesium citrate and hydroxide). Metronidazole is rated Minor.
- **Other safety signals in the literature**: hyperprolactinaemia and QTc prolongation are the main concerns. Renal impairment requires dose adjustment. Amisulpride has a relatively favourable metabolic and weight profile compared with olanzapine and clozapine.

The package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Schizophrenia is supported by many Phase 3/4 trials, several randomized and double-blind, and by multiple high-tier meta-analyses (L1). This is an established antipsychotic use rather than a new indication. The guardrails are needed because the package-insert safety data is missing and the interaction list includes several major QT-related interactions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication text) and correct the empty indication record
- Add mechanism-of-action data to the drug record
- A monitoring plan covering prolactin, QTc/ECG, metabolic parameters and renal function, with a review of co-medications that have major interactions
- For dysthymic disorder (rank 8), adequately powered modern RCTs before it can move beyond a research question; anxiety disorder and the rare genetic and structural conditions should remain on hold
