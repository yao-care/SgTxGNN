# Hyaluronidase: From an Unlisted Original Indication to Esotropia

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Hyaluronidase is an enzyme that breaks down hyaluronan in tissue, and it is commonly used as an adjunct in local anaesthesia. No original indication is recorded in the Singapore licence, so a direct link between its established use and esotropia cannot be drawn.

The only supporting paper reports persistent diplopia and strabismus after cataract surgery under local (retrobulbar) anaesthesia, where hyaluronidase is a co-administered adjunct. This points to an adverse-event association, possibly anaesthetic muscle toxicity, rather than a therapeutic effect. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the literature co-occurrence of hyaluronidase and strabismus in the knowledge graph, not a treatment rationale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials, and the sole publication points to an adverse-event association rather than a treatment effect, so the prediction is not supported. The high model score alone is not sufficient grounds to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to confirm the approved indication, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any evidence of therapeutic benefit in esotropia; none currently exists
- A review of the higher-evidence candidates in the same Evidence Pack instead. Diabetic retinopathy (intravitreal hyaluronidase for vitreous haemorrhage) has two completed Phase 3 trials (NCT00198497, n=510; NCT00198510, n=750). Their efficacy results and regulatory outcome must be confirmed first, and the intravitreal route is not covered by the currently registered Singapore product.
