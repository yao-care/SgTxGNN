## Hyaluronidase: From Registered Injectable Use to Esotropia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Hyaluronidase is an enzyme injection that is registered and marketed in Singapore, although the register does not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **esotropia** (inward-turning eye), but **no clinical trials** and only **1 publication** exist for this prediction.
That publication describes strabismus as a *complication* after eye anaesthesia, not as something hyaluronidase treats, so the evidence is very weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Hyaluronidase is an enzyme that breaks down hyaluronan, a component of tissue "scaffolding". In ophthalmic practice it is used as an additive to local anaesthetic injections to help the anaesthetic spread.

The only publication linked to esotropia (PMID 16934027) reports persistent double vision and strabismus after cataract surgery under local anaesthesia. Hyaluronidase appears there as part of the anaesthetic block, and the strabismus is a possible complication of that block. It is not a condition the drug treats.

No therapeutic mechanism connecting hyaluronidase to esotropia has been established. The very high TxGNN score is not backed by clinical evidence, so this prediction should be regarded as a model artefact until shown otherwise.

## Safety Considerations

They include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone), antihistamines (promethazine, chlorpheniramine, acrivastine, loratadine, cetirizine, brompheniramine, phenyltoloxamine), local anaesthetics (lidocaine, articaine), oestrogens (ethinylestradiol, estradiol, conjugated estrogens) and aspirin. The remaining 43 were not itemised.
- **Hypersensitivity**: A 2024 review in the literature for other predicted indications notes that allergic reactions to hyaluronidase injection are described and often misdiagnosed. This is background context, not label information.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as these were not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no trials, and the only publication describes strabismus as a complication of an anaesthetic block, not a treatment effect. The prediction has no supporting mechanism or clinical evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, and the approved indication for SIN10302P
- Mechanism of action data
- Any evidence that hyaluronidase has a therapeutic role in esotropia, which currently does not exist

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other indications predicted for this drug, **diabetic retinopathy** has far stronger support. It has two completed Phase 3 trials of intravitreal hyaluronidase for vitreous haemorrhage (NCT00198497, n=510; NCT00198510, n=750) and a Phase 2 study (NCT00198471, n=10). However, outcome data were not available, and the indication was reportedly not ultimately approved. It would be a more sensible candidate for further review than esotropia, once published results are retrieved and benefit-risk is checked.
