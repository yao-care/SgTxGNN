## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Chlorpromazine is a long-established antipsychotic, and its effect in psychotic disorders is generally attributed to dopamine D2 receptor antagonism. No mechanism is identified that would make it useful for inherited retinal dystrophy.

The only retrieved publication (JAMA, 1967) describes phototoxic reactions induced by high doses of chlorpromazine and thioridazine. This points to photosensitivity and potential retinal toxicity, which argues against benefit. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects a graph-based association with ocular adverse effects rather than a therapeutic effect, and should be treated as a computational signal only.

Other predictions in the list (for example X-linked myopia, syndromic myopia, hydranencephaly and several rare genetic disorders) also have no clinical or literature support. The only one with a plausible clinical context is early-onset schizophrenia. This is an age-defined subgroup of an already approved indication, not a true repurposing target. Observational and genetic-association studies exist for it, but there are no controlled efficacy data in younger patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone. There are no registered trials, and the single publication reports a phototoxicity safety signal that argues against benefit. No mechanism links chlorpromazine to retinal dystrophy. The safety profile, including QT prolongation and blood-disorder concerns, adds further caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, to allow a mechanistic-link analysis
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Preclinical evidence of benefit in retinal degeneration models, with the retinal toxicity signal explicitly assessed
- If the team wants a more tractable lead, a separate evaluation of early-onset schizophrenia, framed as an age-specific use of an approved indication, with paediatric safety data
