# Chlorpromazine: From Psychiatric Use (Antipsychotic) to Retinal Dystrophy

## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorpromazine is a first-generation antipsychotic (phenothiazine), and the Singapore registration data do not list an approved indication text.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the source database. Chlorpromazine is generally described as a dopamine D2 antagonist that also blocks serotonin 5-HT2A, histamine H1, alpha-1 adrenergic and muscarinic receptors. This receptor profile underlies its antipsychotic use.

**We could not identify a plausible mechanistic link to inherited retinal degeneration.** The predicted condition is a genetic retinal disorder, and no known pathway connects chlorpromazine to it. Phenothiazines are actually associated with pigmentary retinopathy and lens or corneal deposits at high cumulative doses, which raises a safety concern rather than a rationale.

The high score of 0.9995 is a graph-based prediction only. It is not supported by any clinical or mechanistic evidence in this Evidence Pack.

## Safety Considerations

- **Ocular safety concern:** Phenothiazines are associated with pigmentary retinopathy and lens or corneal deposits at high cumulative doses. This is directly relevant to any use in retinal disease.
- **Drug Interactions:** No interaction records were found in the queried source.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on the TxGNN score alone. There are no clinical trials, the retrieved literature does not involve chlorpromazine, and no plausible mechanism was identified. The known ocular toxicity of phenothiazines works against the proposal.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking chlorpromazine to retinal degeneration pathways
- A review of the other predicted indications. The only one with any trial or literature support is early-onset schizophrenia (rank 10, L3), but it largely overlaps with chlorpromazine's established psychiatric use and may be an on-label extension rather than true repurposing.
