# Formoterol: From Obstructive Airway Disease to Respiratory Malformation

## One-Sentence Summary

Formoterol is a long-acting beta2-agonist bronchodilator used in inhaled products for asthma and COPD.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **respiratory malformation**, but none of the **18 retrieved clinical trials** and **10 retrieved publications** actually studies that condition.
The retrieved studies concern asthma and COPD, so this prediction is best treated as a model-only signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Formoterol is a long-acting beta2-agonist, and its efficacy in asthma and COPD is well established. Mechanistically, it relaxes airway smooth muscle to produce bronchodilation.

That bronchodilator action gives no clear basis for treating structural airway malformations. Any benefit would be symptomatic relief of coexisting obstructive airway disease, not correction of the malformation itself. The high TxGNN score is more likely a knowledge-graph proximity effect (respiratory diseases cluster together) than a true mechanistic link.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model score alone. No study in the evidence pack tests formoterol in respiratory malformation, and there is no plausible mechanism by which bronchodilation would treat a structural defect. The pack's own scoring for this indication is L4 with a Hold recommendation.

For context, the same evidence pack shows far stronger support for other predicted indications, namely obstructive lung disease (rank 4) and asthma (rank 5). Both are scored L1 with "Proceed with Guardrails", and both are essentially on-label uses. If the goal is a repurposing candidate, those are better places to focus than respiratory malformation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, which is currently missing
- The HSA package insert, to obtain warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Any clinical or preclinical evidence in respiratory malformation, or a clinical rationale for why a symptomatic bronchodilator would help in this population
- Confirmation of the original approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
