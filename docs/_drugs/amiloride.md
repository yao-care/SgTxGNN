## One-Sentence Summary

Amiloride is a potassium-sparing diuretic that blocks the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) in the distal nephron.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant hypertensive renal disease**, with a score of 0.9982.
There are currently **no clinical trials** and **no publications** supporting this specific direction, so the prediction rests on model output alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for amiloride: "Amiloride HCl is a potassium-conserving (antikaliuretic) drug that possesses weak (compared with thiazide diuretics) natriuretic, diuretic, and antihypertensive activity. These effects have been partially additive to the effects of thiazide diuretics in some clinical studies." ([DailyMed: amiloride hydrochloride prescribing information, Kesin Pharma Corporation](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=5eedadbd-9be9-b920-e063-6394a90afa68), label version 2026-11-11; accessed 2026-11-19.) From general pharmacology, amiloride blocks ENaC in the distal nephron, which reduces sodium reabsorption, and this can contribute to lowering blood pressure while sparing potassium.

Malignant hypertensive renal disease is kidney damage driven by severely elevated blood pressure. A drug that lowers blood pressure through sodium handling is mechanistically plausible here. However, no trials or publications were retrieved for this specific condition. The high score (0.9982) most likely reflects the drug's close relationship with other hypertension-related conditions, rather than direct evidence in this disease.

Other predictions (for example, pulmonary hypertension-related conditions and chronic pulmonary heart disease) were also generated by the model, but they are not the main prediction of this report and are not assessed here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-related cautions (not label text):** The data compiled for this report flag several conditions in which amiloride is generally considered unsuitable or requiring caution, including kidney disease, anuria, diabetic nephropathy, liver disease, hepatic encephalopathy, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency disease, gout, acidosis disorders, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, systemic lupus erythematosus and hyperparathyroidism. These have not been confirmed against the local package insert.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found in the interaction database. Examples at moderate level include metformin, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide), SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin), exenatide, bupropion, bisacodyl and lactulose. Minor-level examples include doxycycline, amoxicillin and cimetidine.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps
