# Ephedrine: From Allergic Rhinitis to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ephedrine is a sympathomimetic drug originally used for allergic rhinitis, vasomotor rhinitis, the common cold, bronchitis and related upper airway conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (both old or preliminary), so this prediction is largely an extension of existing use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ephedrine is an alpha/beta adrenergic agonist with indirect sympathomimetic activity. By constricting the blood vessels of the nasal mucosa, it reduces congestion.

Nasal cavity disease is closely related to the rhinitis indications ephedrine already has. The prediction therefore mostly extends existing use to a broader disease category rather than identifying a new therapeutic area. This explains the high score, but it also means the prediction adds limited new information.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph as contraindications** (these come from drug-disease relationships, not package insert text): hypertension, congestive heart failure, epilepsy, COPD, liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, hypothyroidism, open-angle glaucoma, peptic and gastric ulcer, inflammatory bowel disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, porphyria, anxiety and other neurotic disorders, and psychotic disorders including schizophreniform and schizoaffective disorder.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level ones include tramadol, amitriptyline, bupropion and methylene blue. Moderate-level ones include other sympathomimetics and beta-agonists (amphetamine, methylphenidate, salbutamol, formoterol, arformoterol, atomoxetine), beta-blockers (atenolol, acebutolol) and antidiabetic drugs (metformin, acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide).
- **Literature safety signals**: the retrieved literature includes reports of ephedrine-induced cardiopathy and ephedrine psychosis.

For package insert warnings and contraindications, please refer to the official HSA product information. These were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction scores very high, but it largely restates ephedrine's existing rhinitis use. No clinical trials are registered, and the only two publications are a 1964 report and a preliminary observation of a combination spray. The knowledge graph also flags many contraindicated conditions, including cardiovascular disease, which weighs against any expansion without clear benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm whether nasal cavity disease is already covered
- Controlled clinical evidence that separates ephedrine's effect from that of combination products
- A comparison against current standard decongestants and intranasal therapies
