# Ephedrine: From Its Established Uses to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ephedrine is a sympathomimetic drug marketed in Singapore in injectable and nasal-drop forms, but the HSA records do not state its approved indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **nasal cavity disease** (score 99.90%).
The evidence so far is thin: **16 retrieved clinical trials, none confirmed to test ephedrine**, and **8 publications**, mostly animal models or studies of related decongestants.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Ephedrine is generally known as a mixed direct and indirect sympathomimetic. Its alpha-adrenergic effect constricts blood vessels in the nasal lining, which can reduce swelling and congestion. This is general pharmacology, not something the pack confirms.

The original indication is not recorded, so the link between old and new use is inferred. Nasal congestion is a vascular-swelling problem that vasoconstrictors are expected to relieve. The literature fits this idea: dog nasal-congestion models were built to test decongestant drugs, and one clinical study compared oral and topical decongestants. That comparison used phenylpropanolamine and pseudoephedrine, which are related drugs, not ephedrine itself.

The mechanism is plausible, but direct proof for ephedrine in nasal disease has not been established in the retrieved evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, and a nasal-drop product is already registered in Singapore. However, no retrieved trial is confirmed to test ephedrine in nasal disease. The supporting literature is mainly animal models and studies of related decongestants. Safety data are missing, which blocks progression to safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap), plus the approved indications for each registration, especially the nasal drops
- Manual verification of whether ephedrine was the study drug in NCT00562120 and NCT03620513
- Mechanism of action data, for example from the DrugBank API
- A review of whether the registered nasal-drop label already covers nasal congestion, which would make this a labelled use rather than a new one
- Other predicted candidates were also reviewed. Lung disease has the next strongest evidence (L4, mouse studies only), while the remaining candidates are prediction-only and should stay on hold.
