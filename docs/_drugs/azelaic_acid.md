## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for azelaic acid is not available in the data compiled for this report. Published reviews describe it as having anti-infective and anti-inflammatory activity. It also inhibits follicular keratinization and epidermal melanogenesis (PMID 26355614).

**Demodicidosis (rank 1):** Azelaic acid is used in rosacea, where Demodex mite density is implicated. This link is speculative, as no trials or literature were provided.

**Seborrheic dermatitis (rank 7):** This is the most plausible candidate. The condition involves *Malassezia*, inflammation and sebaceous activity. Azelaic acid has anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial effects, and it is already used in the related condition rosacea. Seborrheic dermatitis often co-occurs with rosacea and acne. One case report describes managing both conditions together. However, a 1989 clinical pharmacology study found that a 20% cream did not change seborrheic skin in healthy-looking epidermis, although it improved acne lesions (PMID 2475995). This is a cautionary signal.

**Keratinization disease (rank 6):** There is indirect preclinical support. Azelaic acid reduces keratinocyte proliferation in vitro and has anticomedonal effects. This evidence concerns comedonal hyperkeratosis, not a defined keratinization disorder.

**Other predictions** (cutaneous candidiasis, familial lichen planus, elevated plasma zinc, adermatoglyphia, nail infection, tinea corporis, Beare-Stevenson syndrome) have no direct supporting evidence. Several, such as elevated plasma zinc, adermatoglyphia and Beare-Stevenson syndrome, are likely graph artifacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** dermatitis, Wilson disease, iron metabolism disease, hereditary hemochromatosis, hypopigmentation of the skin, and pigmentary dilution. The "dermatitis" entry deserves attention given the seborrheic dermatitis candidate.
- **Drug Interactions:** Three interactions were recorded (salbutamol, levothyroxine, niacin). Their severity levels are unknown.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and label contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (top-ranked prediction and overall). **Proceed with Guardrails** applies only to seborrheic dermatitis, as a research question.

**Rationale:**
- Eight of the ten predictions rest on model scores alone or on general reviews with no direct azelaic acid evidence.
- Seborrheic dermatitis is the only candidate with a trial. It is a small Phase 2 pilot (n=48) with no available results. The knowledge graph also lists dermatitis as a contraindication, and an older study found no effect on seborrheic skin.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT00408330 (efficacy and tolerability versus vehicle)
- The Singapore package insert, to confirm warnings, contraindications and the approved indication text
- Reconciliation of the knowledge-graph "dermatitis" contraindication with the seborrheic dermatitis use
- Mechanism-of-action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Interaction severity details for the three recorded drug interactions
- For demodicidosis, any clinical or in vitro data on Demodex before it is further considered
