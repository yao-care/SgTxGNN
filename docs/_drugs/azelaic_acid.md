# Azelaic Acid: From Acne and Rosacea to Demodicidosis of Sebaceous Gland

Azelaic acid is a topical agent registered in Singapore as a 20% cream (Skinoren). The literature in the pack describes its established uses as acne and rosacea.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the drug record. A published review (PMID 26355614) describes azelaic acid as having anti-infective and anti-inflammatory activity. It also inhibits follicular keratinization. These properties underlie its use in acne and rosacea, which are both diseases of the pilosebaceous unit.

Demodicidosis of the sebaceous gland is also a condition of the pilosebaceous unit. It is linked to mite colonisation and inflammation. A plausible link is therefore azelaic acid's antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory action in sebaceous follicles. However, no trial or publication in the provided data addresses this condition. The score is the highest among the ten candidates, but it has no clinical corroboration.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score but no trials or literature behind it. The mechanistic link is only plausible and has not been tested. The package insert safety data is also missing.

Other candidates for this drug have more support. Seborrheic dermatitis has one completed Phase 2 trial (NCT00408330, n=48, provisional L2) plus supporting reviews. Keratinization disease has L4 preclinical support. If the goal is to advance azelaic acid, these are better starting points than demodicidosis.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website, which are required for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any published or registered study of azelaic acid in demodicidosis, or in Demodex-related conditions such as rosacea
- A check of whether the approved indication text can be obtained for SIN07138P
