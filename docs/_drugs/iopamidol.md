## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Iopamidol is a non-ionic iodinated contrast agent. It makes blood vessels and tissues visible on X-ray and CT, and it has no established pharmacological action on coronary vasospasm.

The prediction appears to be a knowledge-graph artifact. Iopamidol is closely associated with coronary angiography, so it sits near cardiac conditions in the graph. That proximity produces a high score but does not indicate that the drug treats Prinzmetal angina.

Overall, the mechanistic link is not credible, and the prediction should not be treated as a therapeutic lead.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has only a model score behind it: no trials, no literature, and no plausible mechanism for coronary vasospasm. The score most likely comes from iopamidol's role in coronary angiography.

Other predicted indications did not change this picture:
- **Female breast carcinoma (L3):** the evidence is imaging-only. It covers CT lymphography, perfusion CT and acidoCEST MRI, plus one terminated early-phase feasibility trial with 8 participants. It shows iopamidol as a diagnostic probe, not an antitumour effect.
- **Pulmonary hypertension and hemoglobinopathy (L4):** the literature is about the safety of contrast use in these populations, not efficacy.
- **Migraine with brainstem aura, myositis fibrosa, idiopathic granulomatous myositis and fibromyalgia:** no evidence at all.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to confirm approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- If the team wants to pursue the breast cancer signal, frame it as an imaging-agent question (for example tumour pH mapping or sentinel node mapping), not as therapeutic repurposing
