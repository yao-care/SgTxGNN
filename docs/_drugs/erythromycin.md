## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic. Macrolides act on the bacterial 50S ribosomal subunit and also have anti-inflammatory effects. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to blepharitis-associated ocular surface disease.

The link to the new indication is plausible but unproven. Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is an inflammatory condition of the cornea and conjunctiva. A drug with both antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity could help, especially if lid margin bacteria contribute. The high TxGNN score is a graph-based prediction only, and no trial tests erythromycin in this condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no clinical trial supports it. The only two publications are general or unrelated to erythromycin. The registered products are oral forms, and no ocular formulation appears among the listed authorizations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Direct evidence for erythromycin in ocular surface disease. A Phase 4 trial of erythromycin ophthalmic ointment in blepharitis (NCT01478256) appeared under another prediction and could be reviewed as a lead.
- Confirmation of an ophthalmic route or formulation available in Singapore
- For comparison, lymphogranuloma venereum (L3) is the better-supported prediction for this drug and may merit review first.
