## Erythromycin: Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic originally used for acne, otitis media, streptococcal and staphylococcal infections, pneumonia, gonorrhoea, pertussis, urethritis and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a review) supporting this direction, so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic with both antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity. Its efficacy in bacterial infections, including conjunctivitis and staphylococcal infections, is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to bacterial or staphylococcal-associated ocular surface disease.

The one retrieved paper concerns chronic blepharokeratoconjunctivitis in children, a condition in which macrolides are used. This is indirect evidence. No clinical trial tests erythromycin for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis specifically, and the high model score has not yet been confirmed by clinical data.

Other predictions in the same list include several that are weaker:
- **Lymphogranuloma venereum** has the strongest mechanistic link (erythromycin is active against *Chlamydia trachomatis*). However, the evidence is review-level only, and doxycycline is first-line.
- **Necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis, polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, hyperamylasemia, postinfectious vasculitis and the broad post-infectious categories** have only historical, indirect or no supporting evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (0.9989), but there are no clinical trials and only one indirect review for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. Evidence is at L4, and no ophthalmic erythromycin product is among the Singapore registrations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical studies (ideally controlled trials) of erythromycin in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis or closely related ocular surface disease
- Mechanism of action data (MOA), including its anti-inflammatory effects on the ocular surface
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA
- Confirmation of an ophthalmic formulation or route, since the current Singapore products are oral or topical skin lotion
- Comparison with existing standard therapy for this condition
- If a more actionable candidate is wanted, consider reviewing **lymphogranuloma venereum**, which has the strongest mechanistic rationale but still lacks trial evidence and is behind doxycycline as first-line treatment
