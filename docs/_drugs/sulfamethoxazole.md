## Sulfamethoxazole: From Bacterial and Parasitic Infections to Acute Contagious Conjunctivitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sulfamethoxazole is a sulfonamide antibacterial, originally used for infections such as otitis media, nocardiosis, toxoplasmosis, pneumocystosis, shigellosis and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acute contagious conjunctivitis**, but **no clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a bacterial susceptibility study) currently support this direction.
Conjunctivitis is already among the drug's listed original indications, so this is not a true repurposing candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, sulfamethoxazole is a sulfonamide that blocks bacterial folate synthesis by inhibiting dihydropteroate synthase. It is commonly used together with trimethoprim, and its efficacy in several bacterial and protozoal infections is established.

Acute contagious conjunctivitis is usually bacterial, so an antibacterial folate-pathway mechanism is biologically plausible. Conjunctivitis is already listed as an original indication, so the prediction mostly restates existing use rather than proposing a new one.

The remaining nine predictions are weaker. Several (punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, endocardial fibroelastosis, postinfectious vasculitis, post-infectious syndrome) involve viral, immune-mediated or fibrotic processes with little antibacterial rationale. Ulcerative blepharitis and blepharoconjunctivitis are plausible because they overlap with bacterial eye infections, but they have no supporting data. Parasitic eyelid infestation links loosely to the listed myiasis and hirudiniasis indications, but it is a prediction only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, acute contagious conjunctivitis, overlaps with an existing indication and is supported only by an indirect susceptibility study, with no efficacy trials. The other nine predictions have no direct supporting trials, and several lack a plausible antibacterial mechanism. Label safety information was also unavailable, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the Singapore licences
- Clinical efficacy data for sulfamethoxazole in bacterial conjunctivitis, and a decision on whether an ocular formulation is relevant, since none is registered
- Further review of the ulcerative blepharitis and blepharoconjunctivitis predictions, which are mechanistically plausible but have no data
