# Imipramine: From Anxiety Disorder to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Imipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant, originally used for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder and nocturnal enuresis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for imipramine is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its known pharmacology, imipramine blocks the reuptake of norepinephrine and, to a lesser extent, serotonin. Boosting noradrenaline signalling in prefrontal circuits is also the principle behind atomoxetine and other non-stimulant ADHD treatments.

Imipramine's established uses (anxiety, neurotic and dysthymic disorders, bedwetting) are all neuropsychiatric conditions that respond to monoamine modulation. ADHD, often accompanied by anxiety and enuresis, shares this neurochemical basis. Reviews in the literature describe imipramine and desipramine as non-stimulant options, mainly when stimulants fail.

Stimulants and non-stimulants such as atomoxetine remain the preferred first-line options. Imipramine would be a later-line alternative.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not package-insert text)**: epilepsy; bipolar disorder and major affective disorder; kidney, liver and gallbladder disease; blood disorders (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis); benign prostatic hyperplasia; substance abuse/dependence; cardiovascular conditions (coronary artery disease, heart disease, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest); stroke and other cerebrovascular disorders; ileus and intestinal obstruction; ocular hypertension and angle-closure glaucoma; hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded, and a medication review is required. Examples of **Major** interactions include epinephrine, bupropion, lorcaserin, potassium citrate and dolasetron. **Moderate** examples include famotidine, cimetidine, clarithromycin, morphine, acetylsalicylic acid, and anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine).
- **Points of attention for this indication**: cardiac toxicity (QTc and conduction effects), anticholinergic burden, lethality in overdose, and safety in children.

The package-insert warnings and contraindications have not been compiled. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Imipramine has a plausible noradrenergic mechanism and a dedicated body of literature in ADHD, including reviews and a 2021 network meta-analysis. No registered trials exist, and the design of the primary studies is unverified, so evidence is capped at L3. Established first-line alternatives (stimulants, atomoxetine) also make it a later-line option.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Verification of the designs of the primary imipramine ADHD studies (e.g. PMID 6849467, 18304665)
- A cardiac monitoring plan (ECG) and a paediatric dosing and overdose-safety plan
- A medication-review process for the 100 recorded drug interactions
