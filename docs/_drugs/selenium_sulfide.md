## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, selenium sulfide is a topical agent with sebostatic, keratolytic and antifungal (anti-*Malassezia*) properties. Its efficacy in seborrheic dermatitis and pityriasis versicolor is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to benign keratinocytic lesions.

Inverted follicular keratosis is a benign keratinocytic lesion related to seborrheic keratosis, which is one of the drug's listed original indications. This similarity is the most likely reason the model ranks it highly. The link is a model prediction only and has not been verified.

Two further concerns apply. No trials or publications address the vulvar site. Selenium sulfide is also a known skin irritant, so tolerability on vulvar mucosa is a concern.

Other predicted indications in the same list were also reviewed:

- **Cutaneous candidiasis (score 83.86%, Evidence Level L4):** This is the only other prediction with any literature (two older narrative reviews, 1986 and 1995, on superficial *Pityrosporum* and *Candida* infections). Extending antifungal activity from *Malassezia* to *Candida* is plausible, but the support is indirect. Effective azole and polyene antifungals already exist, so the added value is low.
- **Acne (score 65.49%) and acne keloidalis nuchae (score 79.87%):** These are plausible dermatologic targets through sebostatic, keratolytic or antimicrobial effects, but nothing has been tested.
- **Remaining predictions** (acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, dermatomyositis variants, childhood interstitial lung disease, familial hydroa vacciniforme, familial lichen planus): No plausible mechanism or clinical evidence was found. These are most likely artefacts of knowledge-graph associations.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text):** The knowledge graph lists dermatitis as a contraindicated disease for this drug. This should be checked against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions:** 79 potential interactions were identified. The 20 shown in the data compiled for this report include pantoprazole, metformin, omeprazole, triamcinolone, prednisone, warfarin, simvastatin and dexamethasone. None has a severity grade, so their clinical significance is unknown. Systemic absorption from topical use is generally low, so the practical relevance is probably limited.
- **Site-specific concerns:** Selenium sulfide is an irritant. Use on vulvar mucosa, or in neonates where percutaneous absorption of selenium is a concern, would need careful assessment.

Please refer to the package insert for the remaining safety information, such as warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output alone (Evidence Level L5). There are no trials or publications for vulvar inverted follicular keratosis, and mucosal irritation is a real concern. The nearest supported direction is cutaneous candidiasis, but its evidence is indirect and existing therapies are adequate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which is currently a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Clinical or case-based evidence in inverted follicular keratosis, or evidence of direct anti-*Candida* efficacy
- A tolerability assessment for vulvar and mucosal application
- The approved indication text for the Singapore product
