# Losartan: From Approved Use (Indication Not Recorded) to Malignant Renovascular Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Losartan is an angiotensin II receptor (AT1) blocker marketed in Singapore, but the source data does not record its approved indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant renovascular hypertension**, with **0 clinical trials** and **2 publications** (one case report and one rodent-model protocol) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Losartan blocks the AT1 receptor, and angiotensin II drives both malignant and renovascular hypertension, so the mechanism is plausible. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the source record. The description above comes from the pack's rationale notes.

The link to the new indication is weak on evidence. The source data lists no original indications, so the similarity between old and new indications cannot be assessed. The only support is one case report (Takayasu's arteritis) and one rodent antihypertensive-screening protocol.

There is also a safety concern specific to this setting. ARBs carry a known risk in renovascular disease, namely bilateral renal artery stenosis and acute kidney injury. The high TxGNN score is not backed by clinical evidence for this exact condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but the evidence is one case report and one rodent-protocol paper, with no clinical trials. The renal safety concern in renovascular disease adds risk for this specific indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text and mechanism-of-action data for losartan
- Clinical studies of losartan in renovascular or malignant hypertension, including renal safety data
- A review of the other predicted indications. Chronic pulmonary heart disease is the only one that reached a "Research Question" stage. It rests on indirect COPD trials, and one experimental study reported that RAAS-based therapy was ineffective in right ventricular failure.
