## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, calcium gluconate is a calcium salt used to replace or supplement calcium, and it is listed in the knowledge graph for bone and mineral metabolism disorders.

Calcium-alkali syndrome is caused by excess intake of calcium and absorbable alkali. Calcium gluconate is therefore a potential cause of this condition, not a treatment for it. The high score (98.88%) most likely reflects a causal or adverse-effect link in the knowledge graph, not therapeutic benefit. The association is probably contraindicating, so this prediction should not be read as a repurposing opportunity.

For context, the other top-ranked predictions are also weak:
- **Primary bone dysplasia with defective bone mineralization (98.70%)**: biologically plausible because calcium supplies substrate for mineralization, but these dysplasias are usually genetic and not corrected by calcium alone. No trials or literature were found.
- **Hypophosphatemia (89.98%)**: the evidence is mostly case reports, a conference abstract and veterinary or historical items. Calcium treats the accompanying hypocalcemia, not the low phosphate itself.
- **Dyspepsia, duodenal ulcer, urolithiasis**: the literature suggests calcium may stimulate gastrin and gastric acid secretion, or promote stone formation. These signals lean toward risk.

## Safety Considerations

  - Calcium and mineral disorders: hypercalcemia disease, humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy, hyperparathyroidism, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease, sarcoidosis

  - Cardiovascular: heart disease, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, sudden cardiac arrest

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, calcium-alkali syndrome, is a condition caused by excess calcium, so the high score most likely reflects an adverse association rather than a treatment opportunity. There are no trials or literature, and the evidence is model prediction only (L5). The other predictions have only indirect, mostly case-report, animal or veterinary evidence, and several point toward risk.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the direction of the knowledge-graph association (causal or adverse-effect versus therapeutic) for calcium-alkali syndrome, and probably reclassify it as a safety signal
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Approved indication text for the three Singapore licences
- If a different candidate is pursued (for example primary bone dysplasia with defective mineralization or hypophosphatemia), targeted literature review and, where appropriate, clinical trials
