# Ceftriaxone: From Bacterial Infections to Infectious Otitis Media

## One-Sentence Summary

Ceftriaxone is a third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as pneumonia, meningitis, gonorrhoea and salmonellosis.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome**, but it has no supporting evidence. The best-supported prediction is **infectious otitis media**, with **1 clinical trial** (which does not test ceftriaxone) and **18 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Since otitis media is itself a bacterial infection, this is closer to an already-practised use than to novel repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ceftriaxone is a beta-lactam cephalosporin that kills susceptible bacteria by interfering with cell-wall synthesis. Its efficacy in bacterial infections of the respiratory tract, meninges and other sites is established.

**The top-ranked prediction is not credible.** Polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome scores highest (99.39%), but ceftriaxone has no known effect on serum viscosity or polyclonal gammopathy. No trials or literature support it. The score reflects graph-based association only.

**Infectious otitis media is the most plausible prediction.** Ceftriaxone is active against the main pathogens of acute otitis media (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*). Single-dose intramuscular use is described in the literature, including a comparison with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. Because the original indications are also bacterial infections, this is a natural extension rather than a new mechanism. Related predictions (suppurative otitis media, chronic otitis media, middle ear disease) share the same rationale, but their evidence largely overlaps and is weaker. In chronic suppurative disease, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Staphylococcus aureus* and biofilms may limit reliability.

Other predictions (hyperamylasemia, congenital analbuminemia, blood group incompatibility, premalignant haematological disease) have no mechanistic rationale. Otosalpingitis is anatomically plausible but has no evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The highest-scoring prediction (polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome) has no mechanistic or clinical support. The most plausible indication, infectious otitis media, is an already-practised antibacterial use supported mainly by older studies, reviews and prescribing data. The only registered trial does not test ceftriaxone, and no confirmed Phase 3 RCT is available. Since the required safety screening cannot be completed without label data, it cannot yet move to a Go or Proceed with Guardrails decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Mechanism of action data
- Verification of PMID 8989332 as an RCT, and a review of other ceftriaxone otitis media trials for formal evidence grading
- Confirmation of whether otitis media is already within ceftriaxone's approved Singapore indications, which would make it a label extension question rather than repurposing
- Route compatibility assessment (the current injectable-only forms versus the needs of otitis media management)
