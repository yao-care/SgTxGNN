# Tropicamide: From Ophthalmic Mydriasis to Cauda Equina Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tropicamide is a muscarinic antagonist eye drop, used to dilate the pupil for eye examinations. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Cauda Equina Syndrome**, but this is a model prediction only, with **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known pharmacology, tropicamide is a muscarinic antagonist (antimuscarinic). Its effect on pupil dilation is established, and mechanistically it might be applicable to conditions where blocking cholinergic signalling helps.

The link to cauda equina syndrome is weak. Antimuscarinics can treat the bladder dysfunction that accompanies the syndrome, but they do not treat the underlying nerve compression, which needs surgical decompression. Tropicamide is also only available as an eye drop, which gives low systemic exposure, so a class-level benefit is unlikely to carry over. The high score is most likely a graph-proximity effect and not a meaningful repurposing signal.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Two points from the prediction review are worth noting. Tropicamide causes mydriasis and can precipitate acute angle closure in eyes with narrow angles. A related ophthalmic anticholinergic (cyclopentolate) has also been reported to cause anaphylaxis in an infant (PMID 36305442), which suggests a possible hypersensitivity risk within this drug class.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on the model score alone, with no trials or literature. The ophthalmic formulation gives little systemic exposure, and established antimuscarinics already cover the plausible bladder-symptom use. The other top-ranked predictions (neurogenic bladder, irritable bowel syndrome, nasal cavity disease) are also L5 and class-level only. Those predictions are better framed as research questions than as candidates for development.
