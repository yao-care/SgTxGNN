## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, selenium is an essential trace element given by infusion, and its role in the body is well established, but the registered indication could not be confirmed from the data compiled for this report.

A plausible but unproven link exists through selenoproteins such as glutathione peroxidase and thioredoxin reductase. These enzymes counter oxidative stress and inflammation, which are believed to contribute to cholestatic liver injury. The available literature is associative: it reports hepatic copper and selenium retention in primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), poor dietary intake in PSC patients, and proteomic changes in mouse models of fibrosis. None of it tests selenium as a treatment.

The TxGNN score is very high, but it is a model output only and does not replace clinical evidence.
