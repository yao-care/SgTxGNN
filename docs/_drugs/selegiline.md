## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, selegiline is an irreversible, selective MAO-B inhibitor. At oral doses of 20 mg/day or more it also inhibits MAO-A. By slowing the breakdown of dopamine, it raises dopaminergic tone, which explains its use in Parkinson's disease.

Negative and cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia are linked to low dopamine activity in the prefrontal cortex. Antipsychotics treat positive symptoms well but have limited effect on negative symptoms. Adding a dopamine-enhancing drug alongside the antipsychotic is therefore mechanistically plausible. Selegiline may also have antioxidant effects, which relate to the oxidative-stress hypothesis of schizophrenia.

This remains a hypothesis. The only registered trial is small and early-phase, and the published studies are small with mixed results. It is worth noting that the other top-ranked predictions (rare developmental, metabolic, retinal and myopia conditions) have no plausible MAO-B-related mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from drug–disease links, not label text): multiple system atrophy, orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, psychotic disorders (including schizophreniform and schizoaffective disorder, and mental disorder in general), melanoma, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma, several cancers (duodenum, small intestine, heart, adrenal gland), and tardive dyskinesia.
  - Of particular note for this prediction, psychotic and schizoaffective disorders appear on this list, which needs careful reconciliation with the proposed use.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified, so the full list needs review. Examples of major-level interactions include bupropion, isometheptene, diethylpropion, phentermine and dexfenfluramine. Moderate-level interactions include epinephrine, metoclopramide, and many antidiabetic agents (various insulins, glimepiride, repaglinide, nateglinide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide). Serotonergic agents and sympathomimetics deserve particular attention, as does the patient's own antipsychotic and any antidepressants.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings; they were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Schizophrenia is the only predicted indication with a plausible mechanism and real studies, but the evidence is L3: one small early-phase trial without reported results, and older small studies with mixed findings. The knowledge graph also lists psychotic disorders as a contraindication, and the interaction burden is large. The other nine predictions, including the top-scored one, have no supporting trials or literature, so they remain at Hold with only model-prediction support.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Read and appraise the full results of the randomized trials and the 2023 meta-analysis for negative-symptom outcomes
- Obtain the HSA package insert to confirm warnings, contraindications and the approved indication
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data
- Resolve the psychotic-disorder contraindication flag and define an interaction-management plan for patients on antipsychotics and antidepressants
- If the evidence supports it, design a confirmatory Phase 2/3 trial of low-dose adjunctive selegiline for negative symptoms
