# Selegiline: From Parkinson's Disease to Schizophrenia

## One-Sentence Summary

Selegiline is an irreversible MAO-B inhibitor. The literature in this pack describes its oral form as approved for Parkinson's disease. The TxGNN model predicts it may help in **schizophrenia**, mainly as an add-on for negative symptoms. This is supported by **1 completed clinical trial** and **19 publications**, including 2 RCTs and a meta-analysis, but the clinical evidence is small and mixed.

> **Note on candidate selection:** The top-scoring prediction, polymicrogyria (99.15%), has no trials, no relevant literature and no plausible mechanism. Schizophrenia (rank 2) is the only prediction with real clinical evidence, so this report focuses on it. The other eight predictions are covered briefly at the end.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Selegiline irreversibly inhibits monoamine oxidase type B (MAO-B). This raises dopamine levels. Negative symptoms of schizophrenia, such as flat affect and low motivation, have been linked to low dopamine activity in the prefrontal cortex. Selegiline may also reduce oxidative stress, which is another proposed contributor to schizophrenia. The structured mechanism-of-action field is not available in the pack, so this explanation comes from the literature and the prediction rationale.

Parkinson's disease and schizophrenia are different conditions, but both involve dopamine signalling. Studies have mostly tested low-dose selegiline added to antipsychotics for negative symptoms, not as a treatment for the whole disorder. The published results are small-scale and contradictory, so they do not support a firm efficacy claim. At oral doses of 20 mg/day or more, selegiline loses MAO-B selectivity and inhibits both MAO forms.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The Singapore package insert has not yet been retrieved, and no drug-interaction records were found.

- **Dose-dependent MAO selectivity:** Per the 2023 meta-analysis abstract, selegiline's MAO-B selectivity is lost at oral doses of 20 mg/day or more.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is a plausible dopamine-based mechanism and a real body of clinical work in schizophrenia, including two RCTs and one completed trial. However, the studies are small, the effect is mixed across trials, and the one registered trial is only early phase. The Singapore package insert, which is the blocking safety gap, has also not been reviewed. The other eight predictions (polymicrogyria, congenital disorder of glycosylation, retinal dystrophy, CMT 1G, atypical glycine encephalopathy, three X-linked or syndromic myopia entries, and hydranencephaly) are L5 model predictions only. They have no plausible mechanism and no supporting evidence, and the retinal dystrophy literature hits are keyword matches that do not mention selegiline. All of these are also on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve and review the Singapore package insert for warnings, contraindications and interactions, especially MAOI-related risks with antipsychotics and antidepressants.
- Obtain outcome data from the two RCTs (PMIDs 15677608 and 17972359) and NCT00456976, and compare them with the 2023 meta-analysis.
- Obtain structured mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Confirm the approved indication on the Singapore licence for SIN08681P.
- If the schizophrenia hypothesis proceeds, define the specific use case (adjunct for negative symptoms) and confirm that the 5 mg oral tablet fits the dose used in the trials.
