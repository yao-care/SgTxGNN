# Terbutaline: From Asthma to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Terbutaline is a selective beta-2 adrenergic agonist, originally used to treat asthma, bronchitis and emphysema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, a category that largely overlaps with its existing uses.
The data compiled for this report include **15 clinical trials** (9 of them completed Phase 3 studies) and **20 publications** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, terbutaline is a selective beta-2 adrenergic agonist. It relaxes bronchial smooth muscle through the cAMP pathway, which directly matches the pathophysiology of reversible airflow obstruction.

The predicted indication overlaps with terbutaline's established uses in asthma, bronchitis and emphysema. This is therefore closer to an on-label or near-label category than true repurposing. Several large Phase 3 trials used terbutaline as the as-needed reliever comparator, which supports its efficacy and safety profile. Note that in those studies terbutaline was the active comparator and not the investigational arm.

Among the other predictions, **chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)** (score 96.81%) also has strong support, including a Phase 3 trial (NCT00421122) and several randomized studies. Anaphylaxis has only preclinical and animal evidence. The remaining predictions (respiratory malformation, Rienhoff syndrome, laryngotracheitis, bronchial neoplasm, food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis, nasolacrimal duct disease, lacrimal atrophy) have no meaningful supporting evidence and appear to be model artefacts. Their recommendation is Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and disease-related cautions (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text)**: hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, respiratory failure, hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis), pulmonary oedema, myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease, and placental abruption.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The sample reviewed includes mostly Moderate and some Minor interactions.
  - Moderate: antidiabetic drugs (acarbose, alogliptin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), epinephrine, isometheptene, famotidine, clarithromycin, loperamide, and laxative or bowel-preparation agents (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol with electrolytes).
  - Minor: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, beclomethasone, betamethasone, budesonide).
- **Guardrails from the evidence review**: monitor for tachycardia, arrhythmia, hypokalaemia and tremor, particularly in elderly patients with comorbidities.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Terbutaline's bronchodilator mechanism fits obstructive airway disease, and multiple completed Phase 3 trials plus randomized studies support its use in asthma and COPD. Because this is essentially an on-label or near-label category, the main uncertainty is about practical positioning and safety, not efficacy. In the large Phase 3 trials terbutaline was a comparator, and long-acting agents are preferred for COPD maintenance, so its role is mainly short-acting or rescue use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), since the licence records have no indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A cardiovascular and metabolic monitoring plan (heart rate and rhythm, potassium, glucose) for patients with the comorbidities listed above
- Confirmation of terbutaline's role (study drug vs comparator) in the Phase 3 trials, especially NCT00421122 in COPD
- A decision on whether inhaled formulations are needed, since only oral forms are registered locally
