# Vildagliptin: From Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus to Focal Stiff Limb Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Vildagliptin is an oral DPP-4 inhibitor originally used to treat type 2 diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **focal stiff limb syndrome**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Target data show that vildagliptin binds dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP-4), with activity also recorded against DPP-8, DPP-9 and TRPV4. Blocking DPP-4 raises active GLP-1 and GIP levels and lowers glucagon, which improves glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes.

Focal stiff limb syndrome is a rare neurological condition within the stiff-person spectrum. These disorders are usually linked to GABAergic dysfunction and autoimmunity (often anti-GAD65 antibodies). No mechanistic link between DPP-4 inhibition and this pathology is supported by the available data.

The very high score (99.88%) is a model output only. It most likely reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, such as shared links to diabetes and autoimmune-related biology, rather than demonstrated biology. A closely related prediction, classic stiff person syndrome, received the same score and also has no supporting evidence.
