## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is currently unavailable for this drug. Based on information contained in the supporting literature, rimegepant is a small-molecule, highly selective **calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist**, approved as an orally disintegrating tablet for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura, and for prevention of episodic migraine in adults.

The rationale for extending rimegepant to migraine with brainstem aura is mechanistic rather than empirical. Triptans, the older standard-of-care acute migraine treatment, act via vasoconstriction and are conventionally contraindicated in brainstem-aura and hemiplegic migraine subtypes because of theoretical stroke risk. CGRP receptor antagonists like rimegepant do not share this vasoconstrictive mechanism, which is why gepants are increasingly considered pharmacologically more suitable for these historically triptan-restricted subtypes.

However, this is a plausibility argument, not a demonstrated finding: all 14 supporting publications studied general migraine populations (with or without aura), and none report a dedicated cohort or subgroup analysis for migraine with brainstem aura specifically. The evidence therefore supports rimegepant's safety and efficacy in migraine broadly, but the brainstem-aura-specific claim remains an extrapolation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic case for rimegepant in migraine with brainstem aura is biologically plausible (non-vasoconstrictive alternative to triptans), but no clinical trial or literature has actually studied this specific subtype — the evidence base is entirely general-migraine data (L3). In addition, a Blocking data gap (TFDA/local label warnings and contraindications) currently prevents a formal S1 safety evaluation, and the drug is not registered in Singapore.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Local (TFDA-equivalent) label warnings and contraindications to complete the S1 safety screen
- Confirmed drug-drug interaction profile
- A subgroup analysis, case series, or dedicated trial specifically in migraine with brainstem aura (or familial/sporadic hemiplegic migraine) patients to validate the mechanistic hypothesis
- Formal DrugBank MOA record to support the mechanistic rationale with primary-source data
