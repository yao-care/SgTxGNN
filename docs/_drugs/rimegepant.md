# Rimegepant: From Acute Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Rimegepant is an oral CGRP receptor antagonist, originally developed and approved for the acute treatment of migraine, and also for preventive treatment of episodic migraine in some markets.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, a migraine subtype.
**No clinical trials** are registered for this subtype. **14 publications** were found, but all concern migraine in general, so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rimegepant blocks the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor. CGRP signalling is central to migraine pathophysiology. The published reviews describe rimegepant as approved elsewhere for acute treatment of migraine with or without aura and for preventive treatment of episodic migraine. The structured mechanism-of-action field is empty in the Evidence Pack, so this description comes from the literature.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype, so a mechanistic fit is plausible. However, the very high TxGNN score may largely reflect the parent condition, migraine, which rimegepant already treats. None of the retrieved publications examines the brainstem aura subtype specifically. Phase 3 evidence exists for migraine overall, but it is indirect for this subtype. Subtype-specific efficacy and safety are unconfirmed, including the cardiovascular and vascular concerns that matter for aura subtypes.

## Safety Considerations

- **Vascular and cardiac concerns**: CGRP is a potent vasodilator, and a 2026 pharmacovigilance analysis of FDA adverse event reports ([PMID 41706150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41706150/)) examined cardiac safety signals for CGRP antagonists. Safety in aura subtypes, including brainstem aura, has not been confirmed.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings, contraindications and drug interaction information. The DDI query returned no results.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible, and rimegepant is already marketed in Singapore. However, no trial or publication examines the brainstem aura subtype, and the Singapore package insert safety data is unavailable. The score likely reflects the parent migraine indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Subtype-specific efficacy and safety data for migraine with brainstem aura, including vascular and cardiovascular risk
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording in Singapore
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank

The other nine predicted indications (ranks 2-10) have no clinical or literature support. Most have no plausible mechanism, so they are not recommended for further work. One of them, Prinzmetal angina, carries a CGRP-antagonist cardiac safety signal and should be treated as a safety concern rather than an opportunity.
