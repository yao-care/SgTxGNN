# Enalaprilat: From Hypertension to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Enalaprilat is the intravenous active form of the ACE inhibitor enalapril, used for hypertension when oral therapy is not practical. The original indication is not stated in the Singapore registration data, so it is taken from general drug knowledge.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, enalaprilat is an ACE inhibitor. It lowers angiotensin II and reduces vascular tone, and its efficacy in hypertension is established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to glaucoma only through indirect routes.

A local renin-angiotensin system exists in the eye. ACE inhibition has been proposed to influence intraocular pressure and retinal ganglion cell survival, but this evidence is preclinical. Primary hereditary glaucoma is genetically driven, and there is no direct link to enalaprilat.

Enalaprilat is also given only by IV, which is not a plausible route for chronic glaucoma therapy. The prediction should be treated as a model-generated hypothesis.
