## Dactinomycin: From Ewing Sarcoma and Other Solid Tumours to Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dactinomycin is a cytotoxic antibiotic-class chemotherapy drug, originally used for Ewing sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, Wilms tumour, testicular germ cell tumours and gestational trophoblastic neoplasm.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support it, so it is a purely computational signal.
The better-supported predictions are rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes and sites, which largely extend an existing standard-of-care use rather than represent true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Dactinomycin is generally known as a DNA-intercalating agent that inhibits transcription. This produces strong cytotoxic effects in rapidly dividing cells, and it also has immunosuppressive potential.

For **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**, the mechanistic link is weak. Dactinomycin has no established role in MS, and its toxicity profile (myelosuppression, hepatic toxicity) is unfavourable for a chronic autoimmune disease that needs long-term treatment. The very high score (0.9958) is best read as a computational artefact, not a clinical lead.

The next nine predictions are more credible but need careful reading:
- **Rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes and sites** (vaginal botryoid, extrahepatic bile duct, parameningeal, prostate): dactinomycin is a core component of the VA/VAC regimens used for rhabdomyosarcoma. These are mostly site- or subtype-specific extensions of an existing use.
- **Liver sarcoma, upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm, head and neck cancer**: the supporting papers concern paediatric sarcomas (for example undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma of the liver and head and neck rhabdomyosarcoma). They do not support dactinomycin for common adult epithelial cancers such as squamous cell carcinoma.
- **T-cell leukaemia**: the only trial is a Phase 3 in adult acute lymphoblastic leukaemia whose title shows no dactinomycin arm. The two publications are in vitro drug-resistance models.

## Safety Considerations

- **Package insert warnings and contraindications**: not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert.
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication links** (not label text): liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia, leukopenia, aplastic anaemia, idiopathic aplastic anaemia, varicella zoster infection, herpes zoster, hepatic vein thrombosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Notable ones:
  - **Major**: deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam
  - **Moderate**: palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, fostamatinib, mercaptopurine, roflumilast, chloramphenicol
  - **Minor**: levofloxacin
- **Publication-based safety signals**: hepatopathy with VAC (age is a risk factor), and late effects such as tooth agenesis in childhood cancer survivors.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction (relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis) has only a model score and no trials or literature. The mechanism does not fit a chronic autoimmune disease, and the toxicity profile is unfavourable.
- The rhabdomyosarcoma-related predictions have a sound biological basis, but they mainly reflect existing standard of care. The adult head and neck and leukaemia predictions have no direct dactinomycin evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Indication text for the Singapore licence SIN10989P
- For rhabdomyosarcoma extensions, confirmation of whether the sites and subtypes are already covered by the approved rhabdomyosarcoma indication
- For any adult or non-sarcoma indication, dedicated efficacy evidence that includes dactinomycin as an investigated agent
