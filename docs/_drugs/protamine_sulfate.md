## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

It is not well supported. Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Protamine is a strongly basic, arginine-rich peptide that binds heparin electrostatically and neutralizes its anticoagulant effect. It has no known antiseizure activity and no known action on the ion channels involved in neonatal or infantile seizure syndromes.

The original use (anticoagulant reversal) and the predicted use (an early-life epilepsy syndrome) share no pathway. A TxGNN score of 0.5 is effectively a default value, so this looks like a model artifact rather than a real signal. The same holds for the other nine predictions, which have no plausible mechanistic link.

The most-studied prediction, **anaphylaxis** (one trial and 20 publications), is inverted. The literature describes protamine as a cause of anaphylaxis, not a treatment for it.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hypersensitivity (from the literature, not the package insert)**: Protamine is documented to cause anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions. Reported mechanisms include IgE/IgG-mediated and non-immune histamine pathways. Reports note higher risk in patients exposed to protamine-containing insulin (NPH), and single intravenous doses can induce protamine-specific IgE and IgG antibodies.
- **Other**: It is also described as capable of causing hypotension and pulmonary hypertension.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings, contraindications and drug interaction information. The package insert has not yet been obtained.
