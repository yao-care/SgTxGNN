## One-Sentence Summary

Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor activity and has sedative-hypnotic properties.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia (disease)**, but the supporting evidence is limited to a **retrospective observational study** and indirect literature; there are **no registered clinical trials** and no randomized trial. The evidence level is **L3**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Alprazolam is known as a benzodiazepine that acts as a positive allosteric modulator of the GABA-A receptor, an action that produces sedative and hypnotic effects. This makes a mechanistic rationale for insomnia plausible.

However, most of the supplied literature is indirect: reviews of panic disorder and anxiolytic pharmacokinetics, benzodiazepine utilisation and cost studies, a COVID-19 insomnia survey, and a zolpidem deprescribing study. Other benzodiazepines are already used for insomnia, and the risks of dependence, falls and cognitive impairment weaken the case for repurposing alprazolam specifically.

A second prediction in the list, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy (score 0.9961), rests on the model score alone. There are no trials or literature, and no clear mechanistic link. It is not the main prediction of this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but the only direct study is a retrospective observational study in a narrow population (coronary heart disease with insomnia), evidence level L3, with no randomized trial. Dependence, falls and cognitive risks, and the availability of other agents for insomnia, weigh against repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomized controlled trials of alprazolam in insomnia, compared with approved hypnotics
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the local regulator
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A risk assessment for dependence, falls and cognitive impairment, particularly in older patients
