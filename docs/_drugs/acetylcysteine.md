## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the supplied rationale, acetylcysteine is a thiol-containing reducing agent that can reduce disulfide bonds in ultra-large von Willebrand factor (VWF) multimers and so decrease their platelet-binding activity. This is a plausible mechanism for thrombotic microangiopathies such as thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA).

Its antioxidant activity may also reduce platelet activation and the prothrombotic phenotype seen in renal insufficiency. Preclinical mouse and baboon TTP models (PMID 28011677) support the VWF-reduction mechanism.

An important caveat: the Phase 3 TA-TMA trial is listed as completed, but no outcome data are provided, so efficacy cannot be confirmed from the supplied information. The evidence concentrates on thrombotic microangiopathies rather than on thrombosis in general.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is supported by a completed Phase 3 trial and an active Phase 2/3 trial in transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy, plus a mechanistic basis and preclinical TTP data. However, no trial outcome data are available, and most other trials are small or indirect, so the evidence is rated L2 rather than higher.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of the Phase 3 trial NCT03252925 to confirm efficacy
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore products
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A clear definition of the target population (TA-TMA or TTP rather than thrombosis in general)
