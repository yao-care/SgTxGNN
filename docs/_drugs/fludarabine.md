## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Fludarabine is a purine nucleoside analog that inhibits DNA synthesis and depletes lymphocytes. Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank are not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description relies on the general pharmacology summarised in the repurposing analysis.

In myeloma, fludarabine plays an enabling role rather than a direct antimyeloma one. It is used with busulfan, melphalan or cyclophosphamide as conditioning before allogeneic stem cell transplant. It is also the standard lymphodepletion backbone (with cyclophosphamide) before BCMA-directed CAR-T therapy. Preclinical work reports some direct in vitro and in vivo activity against myeloma cells.

The prediction is therefore reasonable as a supportive-therapy use, but not as evidence that fludarabine alone treats myeloma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 potential interacting drugs. Moderate-level interactions include metronidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and tinidazole. Levofloxacin is minor. Others (for example doxycycline, cimetidine, amphotericin B, omeprazole, metformin and morphine) have an unclassified severity.
- **Other safety signals from the literature**: Higher fludarabine exposure was associated with more toxicity after ide-cel CAR-T (PMID 40972962). Therapy-related MDS/AML has been reported after fludarabine combination chemotherapy (PMID 20962860).

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, which are not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Fludarabine is established practice as a conditioning and lymphodepletion agent in myeloma transplant and CAR-T therapy, supported by one or more Phase 2/3 programmes. However, the evidence is indirect and supportive rather than showing stand-alone antimyeloma efficacy. Several larger Phase 3 CAR-T trials are still ongoing, and the small completed myeloma-specific studies are limited.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications (the licence indication text is blank)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A defined scope limiting use to lymphodepletion and transplant conditioning, not stand-alone myeloma therapy
- Dose adjustment and monitoring plan for renal impairment and myelosuppression
- Review of the interaction list for concurrent medications

**Other predictions, for context:**
- **Indolent plasma cell myeloma** (evidence L1, Proceed with Guardrails): supported by completed Phase 3 conditioning trials, but not specific to indolent myeloma.
- **Myelodysplastic syndrome** (evidence L1, Proceed with Guardrails): fludarabine-based conditioning before allogeneic transplant is established practice, including randomized comparisons (PMID 31606445), but the benefit comes from the transplant platform.
- **Unclassified myelodysplastic syndrome**: research question only.
- **Hyperthyroidism, diabetic nephropathy and several rare genetic disorders**: Hold. There is no plausible mechanistic link and no supporting trials. The high scores are likely model artifacts.
