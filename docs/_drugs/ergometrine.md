## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Ergometrine is generally known as an ergot alkaloid that stimulates uterine contraction, acting on serotonergic, dopaminergic and alpha-adrenergic receptors.

None of these pathways is an established driver of hair growth disorders. The very high TxGNN score therefore comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. No trial or publication was found that links ergometrine to hypertrichosis, so the prediction cannot currently be considered reasonable on mechanistic or clinical grounds.

Other predictions in the list show the same pattern. These include Ambras-type hypertrichosis, nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, malformation syndromes and leprosy. All have no supporting studies, and several are genetic or structural conditions that a uterotonic cannot plausibly treat.

Two lower-ranked predictions have some literature:
- **Migraine disorder:** Ergot alkaloids have a class-level history in migraine. The retrieved papers are only indirect (a methysergide monograph and a postpartum cerebral angiopathy case report), and none studies ergometrine in migraine. This remains a research question only.
- **Pulmonary hypertension:** The literature points to a safety concern, because uterotonics can raise vascular resistance and are cautioned against in cardiac disease. It is not a therapeutic opportunity.
