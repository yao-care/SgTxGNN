# Gallium Nitrate: From Radiopharmaceutical/Diagnostic Use to Plasma Cell Myeloma

## One-Sentence Summary

Gallium nitrate is a gallium-based compound originally developed as an antineoplastic agent and used for cancer-related hypercalcemia. The gallium products registered in Singapore are radiopharmaceutical forms. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **plasma cell myeloma** (the highest-ranked indication with published literature), with **0 registered clinical trials** and **8 publications** currently supporting this direction, mostly older and small-scale.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, gallium nitrate inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and has antiproliferative effects. These are partly attributed to inhibition of ribonucleotide reductase and disruption of iron-dependent processes. A 1993 study also reported inhibition of protein tyrosine phosphatase, including in a colon cancer cell line.

**The top-ranked predictions (sickle cell disease variants).** The five sickle cell variants share an identical score of 98.18%. This suggests a shared parent-node signal in the knowledge graph rather than disease-specific evidence. No trials or publications were found, and a link through iron metabolism is speculative. The hereditary persistence of fetal hemoglobin variant is a milder genotype, so the rationale is weaker still.

**Plasma cell myeloma.** This is the most plausible prediction. Myeloma is characterised by osteoclast-driven bone destruction, which matches gallium nitrate's known effect on bone turnover and its history in cancer-related hypercalcemia. A pilot randomised study (1993) examined low-dose gallium nitrate for preventing osteolysis in myeloma. Two cohort reports describe prolonged survival in advanced-stage patients receiving it alongside chemotherapy. These data are old and small, with no comparison against modern myeloma therapies (bisphosphonates, proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulators). Gallium nitrate is also no longer a commonly used agent.

**Other predictions.** Colonic neoplasm has only one preclinical paper, and indolent myeloma, drug-induced osteoporosis and relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis have no direct evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 33 interactions were identified; the main ones listed below are mostly flagged for additive nephrotoxicity.
  - **Major**: amphotericin B (including lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms), the aminoglycosides kanamycin, neomycin and streptomycin, deferasirox, and iodinated contrast agents (iothalamic acid, diatrizoate, iopromide, iopamidol, iohexol, iodipamide, iodixanol)
  - **Moderate**: balsalazide, mesalazine, olsalazine, sulfasalazine, exenatide
- Animal studies identified renal toxicity as dose-limiting (PMID 3533251).

Package insert warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report; please refer to the package insert for safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (overall). Plasma cell myeloma is the only prediction worth pursuing, as a research question.

**Rationale:**
- The highest-scoring predictions (sickle cell variants) are model output only, with no trials or literature, and share an identical score that suggests a parent-node artefact.
- For plasma cell myeloma there is a plausible mechanism and limited evidence (L3), but it is old, small-scale and uncompared with current standards of care. The Singapore-registered gallium products are radiopharmaceuticals, not therapeutic gallium nitrate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the study design of the myeloma survival reports and a comparison with current myeloma regimens
- Confirmation of whether a therapeutic gallium nitrate formulation is available in Singapore, and the route compatibility
- A renal safety plan, given the major interactions with nephrotoxic agents
