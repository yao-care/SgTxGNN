# Dimethyl Fumarate: From Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis to Progressive Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dimethyl fumarate (DMF) is an oral immunomodulator used for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progressive multiple sclerosis**. The available evidence is mixed. There are **18 related clinical trials** and **19 publications**, but only a few address progressive MS directly. The one randomized placebo-controlled trial in primary progressive MS did not meet its primary endpoint.

**Note on evidence level:** The Evidence Pack describes the primary progressive MS Phase 2 trial (NCT02959658) as open-label and uncontrolled. Its registry summary and publications (PMID 34429340, 36586351) describe it as randomized and placebo-controlled. That would meet the literal L2 criterion, but the trial was negative on its primary endpoint. L3 is kept here pending review of the source records.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the record. From the supplied rationale, DMF activates the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway and has immunomodulatory and possibly neuroprotective effects. Neuroprotection is the property that matters most for progressive MS, where neurodegeneration rather than relapsing inflammation drives disability.

The pivotal DMF trials (DEFINE, CONFIRM) and the long-term ENDORSE extension were in relapsing-remitting MS. Existing MS therapies mostly target the relapsing phase and have little effect once the progressive phase begins. Progressive MS therefore remains a large unmet need, and this is why a prediction in this direction is plausible.

Clinical data in progressive MS are limited:
- A completed Phase 2 trial in primary progressive MS (NCT02959658, n=54) did not meet its primary endpoint, a reduction in CSF neurofilament light chain, during the placebo-controlled phase.
- An open-label extension (PMID 36586351) and an observational cohort in progressive MS (PMID 33996142) provide additional but weaker data.
- A Phase 3 trial in secondary progressive MS (NCT02430532) was terminated after only 58 participants.

## Safety Considerations

- **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML):** PML has been reported in DMF-treated MS patients (PMID 32808554, 36387034, 38321317).
- **Lymphopenia:** Moderate-to-severe, sustained lymphopenia is a recognised concern and is linked to PML risk (PMID 33091427).
- **Drug interactions:** No interactions were found in the queried database.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but the only randomized placebo-controlled trial in primary progressive MS did not meet its primary endpoint, and the Phase 3 trial in secondary progressive MS was terminated early with 58 participants. Package-insert safety data are also missing. The other nine predicted indications (rank 2–10) are supported by no trials and, at most, one preclinical oral squamous cell carcinoma study. They are not recommended for pursuit at this time.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Reconcile the trial description: the Evidence Pack says open-label and uncontrolled, while the registry and publications say randomized and placebo-controlled
- Review secondary endpoints and imaging results from NCT02959658 and its extension for any signal in progressive MS
- Obtain the HSA package insert to review warnings and contraindications, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Obtain detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Set a lymphocyte-count and PML monitoring plan for any progressive MS use
