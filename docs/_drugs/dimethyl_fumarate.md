# Dimethyl Fumarate: From Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis to Progressive Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dimethyl fumarate (DMF) is an oral disease-modifying therapy originally used for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progressive multiple sclerosis**,
with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction. However, the only Phase 3 trial was terminated early with a small enrollment, so clinical efficacy remains unproven.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, DMF is thought to activate the Nrf2 pathway and to have antioxidant, cytoprotective and immunomodulatory effects.

These actions overlap with the inflammatory and neurodegenerative processes in progressive MS. The drug is already established in relapsing MS, and progressive forms share the same underlying disease, which makes the TxGNN prediction biologically plausible.

Two studies support this link: a Phase 2 trial in primary progressive MS (NCT02959658) and a Phase 4 mechanistic study of indirect neuroprotection (NCT03092544). The only Phase 3 RCT (NCT02430532) was terminated early, so it cannot establish efficacy. One open-label extension study of the primary progressive MS trial notes that the placebo-controlled phase did not meet its primary endpoint (a reduction in cerebrospinal fluid neurofilament concentrations).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified, and the 20 listed in the compiled data are all rated Moderate. They include:
  - Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone
  - Immunosuppressive or cytotoxic agents: mercaptopurine, trimetrexate, dexrazoxane, levamisole
  - Radiopharmaceuticals: ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab, iobenguane (I-131)
  - Others: naltrexone, roflumilast, dupilumab
- **Literature-based safety signals**: Sustained lymphopenia is a recognized concern with DMF and is linked to rare cases of PML (incidence 0.02 per 1000 patients in one cohort).

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
DMF is marketed in Singapore and has a plausible mechanistic link to progressive MS, supported by a completed Phase 2 trial. However, the Phase 3 trial was terminated early and the Phase 2 placebo-controlled phase missed its primary endpoint, so efficacy is unproven. This should be treated as a research question, not a basis for clinical use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Health Sciences Authority (HSA), which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Published results of NCT02959658, and the reasons for termination of NCT02430532
- A lymphocyte-count and PML monitoring plan
- A larger, adequately powered randomized trial in progressive MS

**Other predicted indications** (ranks 2 to 10, such as jugular foramen schwannoma, cervical neuroblastoma and benign oral neoplasms) rest on model prediction alone or on a single preclinical oral squamous cell carcinoma study. They should be held.
