# Pitavastatin: From Familial Combined Hyperlipidaemia to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pitavastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used to treat familial combined hyperlipidaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one RCT in HIV-associated dyslipidaemia and one case report), so the evidence is indirect and weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pitavastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which lowers intracellular cholesterol and upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, increasing LDL-C clearance.

Familial combined hyperlipidaemia and familial hypercholesterolaemia are both inherited disorders of lipid metabolism, so the model's link is biologically plausible. However, HoFH patients usually carry null or defective LDL receptor variants. Statins depend on residual receptor activity, so response is expected to be limited and variable.

The only supportive item is a 2025 case report of rapid lipid lowering in autosomal recessive hypercholesterolaemia (LDLRAP1), a condition clinically similar to HoFH. This is indirect evidence only.

**Related predictions in the same analysis:**
- **Hyperlipoproteinaemia** (rank 2) is effectively an on-label use, supported by Phase 3 and Phase 4 RCTs (see below).
- **Familial hypercholesterolaemia** (rank 3) and **autosomal dominant hypercholesterolaemia** (rank 5) are supported by paediatric clinical studies in Japan and Europe.
- **HIV infection** (rank 7) concerns cardiovascular risk reduction in people with HIV (the REPRIEVE trial), not treatment of the infection.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver disease, gallbladder disease, hypothyroidism (including myxedema and congenital hypothyroidism), myopathy and other muscle diseases, familial periodic paralysis, hypotensive disorder, rhabdomyolysis, impaired renal function and injury.
- **Drug Interactions:** 86 interactions are recorded. The most important is **erythromycin (Major)**. Moderate interactions include other statins (rosuvastatin, simvastatin), cobicistat, eltrombopag, chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine, metronidazole and tinidazole, disulfiram, naltrexone, eluxadoline, zafirlukast, chloramphenicol, dapsone and ethanol. Warfarin and dicoumarol are Minor.
- For the full set of warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, the prediction score is very high, but there are no registered trials and the only supporting items are an RCT in an HIV population and a small case report. HoFH patients often have little residual LDL receptor activity, so statin response may be limited. The better-supported directions are related indications (hyperlipoproteinaemia and heterozygous FH), where pitavastatin is largely already used within its approved lipid-lowering role.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data in genetically confirmed HoFH, with stratification by LDL receptor residual activity
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA), plus the approved indication text for the two licences
- A safety plan covering myopathy, liver effects and interactions (especially erythromycin and other CYP or transporter-related drugs)
- A review of whether the more strongly supported indications (heterozygous FH, and cardiovascular prevention in HIV) are better candidates, which would likely carry a "Proceed with Guardrails" decision
