## Pitavastatin: From Familial Combined Hyperlipidaemia to Cholesterol-Ester Transfer Protein Deficiency
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Pitavastatin is a statin. Familial combined hyperlipidaemia is recorded as its original use, but the Singapore licence records compiled for this report do not state an indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **cholesterol-ester transfer protein (CETP) deficiency**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** were found for this disease, so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pitavastatin belongs to the statin class, which lowers cholesterol. CETP deficiency is also a lipid metabolism disorder. The score of 0.9998 comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

The biological rationale is weak, however. CETP deficiency is characterised by raised HDL cholesterol and is not typically a condition that needs LDL-lowering treatment. The score most likely reflects the disease's proximity to other lipid disorders in the network, not a real therapeutic need. Without mechanism data, this link cannot be assessed further, so the prediction should be treated as a computational hypothesis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but there are no trials or publications for CETP deficiency, and the mechanistic link is unclear because raising HDL is the disease feature, not LDL. The model prediction is therefore not enough to justify progressing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and a clear biological argument for why a statin would help in CETP deficiency
- Any clinical or preclinical studies that directly address CETP deficiency
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, and the approved indication text for the two Singapore licences

**Note on other predictions:** The second-ranked prediction, HIV infectious disease, has far stronger support, including a Phase 3 trial (NCT06317051) and the REPRIEVE trial publications (e.g. PMID 37486775). That evidence concerns cardiovascular prevention in people with HIV, not treatment of HIV infection itself. It could be assessed as a separate candidate.
