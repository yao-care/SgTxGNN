# Cetirizine: From Allergic Rhinitis to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Cetirizine is a second-generation H1 antihistamine, originally used for allergic rhinitis, conjunctivitis and idiopathic urticaria.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria**,
with **1 clinical trial** (a completed Phase 3 pilot study of IV cetirizine) and **18 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Cetirizine is a selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report, but this class effect is well established. In allergic urticaria, histamine released from mast cells drives the wheal-and-flare reaction, and blocking H1 receptors addresses this pathway directly.

Cetirizine is already associated with idiopathic urticaria. The predicted indication is therefore a close extension of an existing use, such as a label expansion or a new route or population (for example, an IV formulation), rather than a new mechanism. The retrieved literature also describes an IV cetirizine product approved in 2019 for acute urticaria in the US.

The evidence-level rating of L1 rests mainly on the Phase 3 label of the trial and the established efficacy of cetirizine in urticaria. The only trial is a small feasibility study, so a definitive efficacy RCT is not shown in the data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The model's knowledge graph links cetirizine to the following conditions: hypertension, kidney disease (including chronic and end-stage renal failure), liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, coronary artery disease and atherosclerosis, angle-closure glaucoma, and hyperthyroidism (including Graves disease). These should be checked against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. The main moderate-level ones involve CNS-depressant or sedating agents and opioids: bupropion, dronabinol, nabilone, loperamide, metoclopramide, morphine (including liposomal), opium, sibutramine, difenoxin and diphenoxylate.
- **Literature safety signals**: A case of cetirizine-induced cholestasis was reported (PMID 11034010). Hypersensitivity reactions to antihistamines, including urticaria and angioedema, have also been described (PMID 24169055).

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is a direct and well-understood fit, since H1 blockade addresses histamine-driven urticaria, and cetirizine is already marketed in Singapore with 20 registrations. The only trial is a small Phase 3 pilot feasibility study (n=36), so efficacy for this specific indication still relies largely on existing use and review literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently unavailable, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording on the Singapore licences
- A definitive efficacy trial, or results of the full Phase 3 study, if an IV or new-population extension is pursued
- A review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (renal, hepatic, cardiovascular, glaucoma, prostatic) against label dosing guidance
