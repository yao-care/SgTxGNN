# Cytarabine: From Acute Leukaemia to Small Cell Lung Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Cytarabine is a pyrimidine nucleoside analogue chemotherapy drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukaemias.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **small cell lung carcinoma**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no registered clinical trials** and **no small-cell-specific clinical publications** for this use. The evidence is limited to a model prediction, older non-small-cell lung cancer studies and laboratory work.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Cytarabine is, however, a well-known pyrimidine nucleoside analogue that inhibits DNA synthesis in cells in S-phase. This makes a rapidly dividing tumour such as small cell lung carcinoma a plausible target in principle.

The original indications are acute leukaemias, where cytarabine is among the most effective agents. The literature compiled here also notes that many solid tumours are insensitive to it. This is partly attributed to high levels of cytidine deaminase, which breaks the drug down, and low levels of the activating enzyme.

The retrieved literature mainly concerns **non-small cell** lung cancer. It includes phase II trials of cytarabine plus cisplatin from 1990, which reported a response rate of about 14% with significant bone marrow toxicity. A 1987 report on high-dose cytarabine in small cell lung cancer is listed, but no abstract is available. The high TxGNN score is a prediction only and does not on its own show clinical benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no clinical trials exist for small cell lung carcinoma. The only direct small-cell publication is a 1987 report without an available abstract, and the other studies are in NSCLC or in vitro. Known marrow toxicity and the reported limited activity of cytarabine in solid tumours argue for caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieval and review of the 1987 small cell lung cancer study (PMID 3030547) for response and toxicity data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Preclinical evidence in SCLC models, including enzyme expression (deoxycytidine kinase and cytidine deaminase) and nucleoside transporter levels
- A comparison against current standard SCLC therapy before any trial design

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** Nine further indications were evaluated, and all are rated Hold except neuroblastoma, which is rated Research Question. All of these have indirect or no supporting evidence.
- Primary pulmonary lymphoma (L4, 99.78%): indirect evidence from high-dose methotrexate plus cytarabine regimens in primary CNS lymphoma.
- Well-differentiated fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung (L5, 99.76%): prediction only.
- Pulmonary blastoma (L5, 99.76%): prediction only.
- Upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm (L4, 99.49%): one randomised study of high-dose cytarabine with cisplatin and 5-FU in head and neck cancer (PMID 12110494).
- Ganglioneuroblastoma (L5, 99.36%): prediction only.
- Vertebral anomalies with endocrine and T-cell dysfunction (L5, 99.32%): prediction only, likely a graph artefact.
- Retroperitoneal neoplasm (L4, 99.23%): case reports of myeloid sarcoma and lymphoma only.
- Neuroblastoma (L4, 99.19%; Research Question): preclinical sensitivity data. The three listed trials do not test cytarabine for this disease.
- CLL/SLL with somatic hypermutation (L5, 98.96%): prediction only.
