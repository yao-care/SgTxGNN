# Melitracen: From an Unrecorded Original Indication to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Melitracen is a tricyclic antidepressant, usually marketed together with flupentixol. Its original approved indication is not recorded in the Singapore data provided.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and **4 publications** (none testing melitracen for insomnia), so this remains a model prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, melitracen is a tricyclic antidepressant that is usually combined with flupentixol. Sedating and anxiolytic effects are plausible for this class, so a link to sleep disturbance is biologically coherent. This link is inferred from drug class only.

The retrieved literature does not show that melitracen improves insomnia. It shows only co-prescription and drug-utilisation patterns in insomnia populations, plus studies of other drugs or other conditions. The high score should therefore be read as a hypothesis for investigation, not as supporting evidence.

Two other predictions in the same run look more plausible than insomnia. Dysthymic disorder fits the antidepressant class, and headache disorder has some class use and one confounded clinical observation. Most remaining predictions (osteoarthritis, pseudoachondroplasia, myositis and others) appear to be graph artifacts.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model score and drug-class plausibility only. No trial or publication tests melitracen for insomnia, and the safety data needed for screening are missing.
