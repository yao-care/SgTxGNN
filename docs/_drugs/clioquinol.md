## Clioquinol: From Tinea Infections to Ectothrix Infectious Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clioquinol is a topical agent whose recorded uses in the knowledge graph are tinea pedis, tinea corporis, tinea infection and dermatophytosis of the groin and perianal area.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ectothrix infectious disease** (a dermatophyte infection of the outside of the hair shaft).
**No clinical trials and no publications** were found for this prediction, so it rests on model output and indirect reasoning only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Clioquinol is generally thought to act by chelating metals such as zinc, copper and iron, which could disrupt fungal metalloenzymes. This is not confirmed here.

Ectothrix infection is a dermatophyte infection in which spores form on the outside of the hair shaft, for example tinea capitis caused by *Microsporum* species. The score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. It is a within-class extension, not a novel repurposing signal. Note that a model score is not the same as an approved indication, and the Singapore licences have no indication text on record.

There are practical limits. Hair-shaft infections usually need systemic therapy (such as griseofulvin or terbinafine) because topical agents penetrate hair follicles poorly. Clioquinol's history of neurotoxicity (subacute myelo-optic neuropathy, SMON, linked to oral use) argues against a systemic route. The same reasoning applies, even more strongly, to the second prediction, endothrix infection, where the fungus grows inside the hair shaft.

## Safety Considerations

- **Relations recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: The knowledge graph links clioquinol with the following contraindication-type conditions: adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, capillary disease, hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia, peripheral vascular disease, intermittent vascular claudication and peripheral arterial disease.
- **Neurotoxicity history**: Oral use has been linked to SMON, which argues against any systemic development.

No drug-interaction data were found. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a high model score and its overlap with existing skin dermatophyte relations. No trial or publication studies ectothrix disease directly, so the evidence level is L5. Topical clioquinol is also unlikely to reach organisms in the hair shaft, and a systemic route is not acceptable given the SMON history.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, and the licensed indication wording
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Laboratory or clinical data showing hair-shaft or follicular penetration and activity against *Microsporum* (ectothrix) species
- A comparison with standard systemic therapy to show a role for a topical-only agent
