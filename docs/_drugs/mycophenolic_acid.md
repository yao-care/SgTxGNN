# Mycophenolic Acid: From Transplant Immunosuppression to Hemoglobinopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Mycophenolic acid is an immunosuppressant (an IMPDH inhibitor) that is marketed in Singapore as enteric-coated and delayed-release tablets.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hemoglobinopathy** (sickle cell disease and thalassemia).
**26 clinical trials** and **10 publications** were retrieved. Nearly all are stem cell transplant studies in which the drug's role is presumed rather than confirmed, and none tests it as the intervention.

**Note on evidence level:** L2 requires a completed Phase 2/3 RCT. None of the hemoglobinopathy trials is a randomized trial of mycophenolic acid, and the supporting literature is cohort studies and a pharmacokinetic model. L3 is the more defensible grade.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mycophenolic acid inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH). This depletes guanosine nucleotides and selectively suppresses T and B lymphocyte proliferation.

The link to hemoglobinopathy is **indirect**. The drug does not correct the hemoglobin defect. Its role is supportive: preventing graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) and graft rejection in allogeneic stem cell transplantation, the only curative option for severe sickle cell disease and thalassemia. It has also been used as an immunosuppression boost for mixed chimerism after thalassemia transplants (PMID 36372358).

The trials listed below are transplant studies in which mycophenolate mofetil (MMF, the prodrug of mycophenolic acid) appears to be part of the GVHD prophylaxis regimen. Most trial titles and summaries are truncated. The drug's presence in each regimen is therefore assumed except where the summary names it (NCT03924401, NCT01917708, NCT02678143). The model score reflects this transplant-support setting more than any direct disease-modifying effect.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No drug interaction records were found.

One case report in the retrieved literature (PMID 17180133) describes neonatal anemia, hydrops fetalis and an ear malformation after maternal MMF use during pregnancy. Reproductive risk deserves attention, especially since the target population includes children and young adults.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The drug's role is supportive (GVHD prophylaxis and chimerism support in transplant), not disease-modifying, and none of the retrieved trials tests it as the studied intervention. Safety data from the package insert is missing, which blocks the safety screening step.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (safety screening is blocked without them)
- Confirmation from full trial protocols that MMF is part of each regimen, and outcome data for the completed trials (NCT03263559, NCT00919503)
- Comparative data (RCT or well-controlled cohort) on MMF-containing versus alternative GVHD prophylaxis in hemoglobinopathy transplant
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A decision on whether this is a "new indication" at all, or just an established transplant-support use

For reference, rheumatoid arthritis (rank 9) is another prediction with one Phase 3 RCT (NCT00451867), but that trial was terminated with no efficacy results provided.
