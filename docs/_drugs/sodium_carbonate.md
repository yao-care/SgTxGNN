## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, sodium carbonate is an alkalinizing agent and buffer. Its pharmacological role is pH adjustment, not a disease-specific therapeutic action.

For cauda equina syndrome, no plausible mechanistic link could be identified. The condition is a compressive neurological emergency that is treated surgically, and a buffering agent has no clear role in it. The high TxGNN score (99.80%) is a model prediction only, with no trials or literature behind it.

Other predicted indications were also reviewed:

- **Sjögren syndrome (rank 9) and dry eye syndrome (rank 5):** These are the only predictions with any supporting literature. A small exploratory study tested a sodium carbonate/bicarbonate and xylitol oral spray in patients with Sjögren's syndrome. The plausible mechanism is local pH buffering and mucosal cleansing for symptom relief, not disease modification. This evidence is indirect, and it does not transfer to the ocular surface, where safety has not been established.
- **Anaphylaxis (rank 2):** The single retrieved paper is about sublingual epinephrine formulation, with sodium carbonate at most acting as a buffer or excipient. Epinephrine remains the standard of care.
- **Ventricular tachycardia (rank 6):** Sodium *bicarbonate*, a different compound, is used in certain toxicological arrhythmias. This does not support sodium carbonate.
- **Other predictions** (food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis, panuveitis, catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, and two predictions flagged obsolete in the ontology, neurogenic bladder and bundle branch block): No mechanism or evidence was identified.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions have no clinical trials, no supporting literature, and no plausible mechanism, so they are model output only (evidence level L5). The Sjögren syndrome and dry eye directions have one small, indirect oral-spray study (L4). That study supports symptom relief of dry mouth at most. Sjögren syndrome may be worth exploring as a research question, but it is not ready for a repurposing decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA), which are required before any safety screening
- Mechanism of action data for sodium carbonate
- Clarification of the original indication, since the sole license is for a combination injectable with no stated indication text
- Verification of the design, population, and results of the Sjögren's syndrome oral spray study (PMID 27813150)
- Prospective controlled studies if the Sjögren syndrome direction (symptomatic dry mouth relief) is pursued
- Ocular safety data before any consideration of dry eye syndrome
- Route compatibility assessment, since the only licensed product is an injection and the plausible uses are local (oral)
