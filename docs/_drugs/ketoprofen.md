## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Ketoprofen is a cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor. It reduces prostaglandin synthesis, which gives it analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects.

Acromesomelic dysplasia, Hunter-Thompson type is a genetic skeletal dysplasia caused by loss of function in the CDMP1/GDF5 gene, which disrupts limb growth. Ketoprofen does not act on this pathway. At most, an NSAID could ease pain symptoms, and it would not change the course of the disease.

The high score most likely reflects the disease's proximity to musculoskeletal and pain nodes in the knowledge graph, not a real disease-modifying mechanism. This prediction should be regarded as a probable model artifact.

The other nine predictions for this drug are also weak or unsupported. The exceptions are spondyloarthropathy (susceptibility) and LACC1-related juvenile arthritis, where NSAIDs offer only symptomatic relief, which is already standard care.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
