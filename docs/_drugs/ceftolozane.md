## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Ceftolozane is a beta-lactam cephalosporin, a class that blocks bacterial cell wall synthesis. It is active against Gram-negative bacilli, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

Ureaplasma species have no cell wall. Beta-lactam antibiotics are therefore expected to be intrinsically ineffective against them. The high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. In short, the prediction is **not biologically plausible** on current knowledge.

Other predicted diseases in the list are worth noting for context:
- **Xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis** is the only prediction with a mechanistically plausible link, because the organisms usually involved (such as *E. coli* and *Proteus*) fall within the Gram-negative spectrum. It ranked 4th, and the data compiled for this report classify it as a research question. Surgery is usually the main treatment, so an antibiotic would only be an adjunct.
- **Gonococcal urethritis** is plausible in principle for cephalosporins, but ceftolozane has no documented anti-gonococcal data.
- **Uterine inflammatory disease** usually involves anaerobes and atypical organisms, which ceftolozane covers poorly.
- Most of the remaining predictions (polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, hyperamylasemia, congenital analbuminemia, blood group incompatibility, premalignant haematological disease) are non-infectious conditions with no plausible antibacterial mechanism. Urogenital tuberculosis is also implausible, because *M. tuberculosis* is intrinsically resistant to most beta-lactams.
