# Erlotinib: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

> Erlotinib is an oral EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor, used mainly for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and pancreatic cancer.
> The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**, but the support is thin: **1 clinical trial** (withdrawn, never enrolled) and **2 publications** (one review and one preclinical study).
> This is best treated as a research question, not a treatment option.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on general knowledge, erlotinib inhibits the EGFR tyrosine kinase, which blocks tumour cell division and can trigger cell death in EGFR-dependent tumours. Its efficacy in EGFR-driven lung cancer is well established.

The link to Ewing sarcoma is weak. EGFR signalling has been proposed as a possible contributor in some paediatric sarcomas, but it is not an established driver of Ewing sarcoma, which is driven by the EWS-FLI1 fusion. The only support is a preclinical xenograft study of an anti-HER3 antibody (patritumab), with or without erlotinib, combined with standard chemotherapy in paediatric sarcoma models. General paediatric oncology reviews add context but no direct evidence. The high model score therefore reflects knowledge-graph proximity, not proven biology.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial was withdrawn with zero enrolment, and the support is one preclinical study plus a general review. EGFR is not an established driver of Ewing sarcoma. The 95.77% score reflects model prediction, not clinical evidence. The other nine predicted indications (ranks 2–10) are also weak, with no relevant trials, and several have no plausible EGFR mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Ewing sarcoma-specific data showing EGFR pathway dependence, such as tumour expression or cell line and xenograft sensitivity to erlotinib
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- A clinical trial that actually enrols Ewing sarcoma patients, since the only registered study was withdrawn
