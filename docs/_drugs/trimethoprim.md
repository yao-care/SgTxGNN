# Trimethoprim: From Urinary Tract Infection to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Trimethoprim is an antibacterial originally used for urinary tract infections and several other infections, including otitis media, pneumocystosis, shigellosis and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis** with a very high score, but at present **no clinical trials and no publications** support this specific prediction.
The top-ranked prediction is therefore a model signal only. Among the other predictions, "conjunctivitis" has the most literature, but it is already a labelled use of the drug.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Trimethoprim is generally known as an antibacterial that inhibits bacterial dihydrofolate reductase (a folate pathway enzyme). Its efficacy in bacterial eye and urinary infections is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to infectious surface eye disease.

Trimethoprim is already used topically (combined with polymyxin B) for bacterial conjunctivitis, so a link to ocular surface disease is plausible. However, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is often viral or toxic/inflammatory in origin, and trimethoprim has no antiviral activity. The high score (0.996) is a graph-based prediction only, and nothing in the compiled data supports it.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text):** inherited porphyria, porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disease, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosemia, and folic acid deficiency anemia.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The **major** ones are with potassium salts (potassium citrate, potassium bicarbonate, potassium chloride, potassium gluconate), which relates to the risk of raised potassium. **Moderate** interactions include metformin, pioglitazone, rosiglitazone, repaglinide, balsalazide, picosulfuric acid and sapropterin.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, rests only on a model score. It has no trials or literature. The condition is often viral or toxic, and trimethoprim has no antiviral action. The one prediction with real supporting literature, conjunctivitis (L3, Proceed with Guardrails), is already a labelled use and is not a new repurposing finding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Evidence that punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis cases with a bacterial component respond to trimethoprim, or a clear mechanistic rationale
- Mechanism of action data and the package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website
- Confirmation of whether a topical ophthalmic formulation can be supplied in Singapore, since none of the 12 registrations is an eye product
- A review of the safety associations above (blood disorders, kidney and liver disease, G6PD deficiency) and of the potassium-related interactions
