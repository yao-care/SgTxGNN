# Ipratropium: From Known Bronchodilator Use to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ipratropium is an inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilator, and its registered indication text is not recorded in the local data. TxGNN predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, which is really confirmation of an established COPD and asthma use. The direction is supported by **at least 5 completed Phase 3 trials** among 44 linked trials, and by **20 publications**, including several Cochrane reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism data from DrugBank is not available. Based on the pack's mechanistic analysis, ipratropium is a non-selective muscarinic antagonist. It blocks M1–M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle and submucosal glands. This reduces vagal cholinergic bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion, which is the pathway that drives airflow obstruction.

The prediction therefore mostly confirms a known use rather than a new one. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma are obstructive lung diseases. Ipratropium and its combinations (with salbutamol or fenoterol) have long been studied and marketed for them. The trial and literature record below matches this.

Other TxGNN predictions for this drug are much weaker. Nasal cavity disease, pharyngitis, tracheal disease and anaphylaxis have only indirect or preclinical support (L4). Acute laryngopharyngitis, papillary conjunctivitis, Rienhoff syndrome and food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis have prediction only (L5). Respiratory malformation is also L4, with only indirect support. These are not pursued here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Anticholinergic adverse effects**: urinary retention, glaucoma or mydriasis, and dry mouth. A 2025 case report describes unilateral mydriasis after ipratropium inhalation in an infant (PMID 40069469).
- **Hypersensitivity**: rare, but a case report describes severe anaphylaxis after ipratropium inhalation (PMID 8449120).
- **Drug interactions**: no interaction records were found in the query.

Package-insert warnings and contraindications are not available in the Evidence Pack. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials, large post-marketing surveillance studies and Cochrane reviews support ipratropium and its combinations in COPD and asthma. This confirms an established use rather than a new one. Guardrails are needed because the package insert is missing, and because long-acting antimuscarinics such as tiotropium are generally preferred for maintenance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved-indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm the original indication
- DrugBank mechanism-of-action data
- A monitoring plan for anticholinergic effects and hypersensitivity, especially in patients with glaucoma or urinary retention
