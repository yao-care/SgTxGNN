# Ipratropium: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ipratropium is an inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilator, originally used for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, and bronchitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, which overlaps heavily with its existing COPD and bronchial disease uses.
The data compiled for this report include **25 clinical trials** and **20 publications** for this indication, including one large completed Phase 3 trial (n=1,118).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ipratropium is a muscarinic antagonist. It blocks M3 receptor-mediated bronchoconstriction in airway smooth muscle by interrupting vagally mediated cholinergic signalling.

This matches the pathophysiology of obstructive lung disease directly. The predicted indication overlaps with the already-established COPD and bronchial disease uses. It is therefore closer to an on-label or near-label use than to true repurposing. The high TxGNN score reflects this overlap.

Other predicted indications in the data are much weaker. Nasal cavity disease is plausible and partly on-label through the rhinitis uses. Pharyngitis, tracheal disease and anaphylaxis have only indirect or preclinical support. Several others (acute laryngopharyngitis, papillary conjunctivitis, Rienhoff syndrome, food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis) have no supporting studies at all. This report focuses on the top-ranked indication.

## Safety Considerations

Package-insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

- **Disease-level cautions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: Contraindication links were recorded for angle-closure glaucoma, ocular hypertension, benign prostatic hyperplasia, bladder neck obstruction, long QT syndrome, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, hypertension, myocardial ischemia and coronary artery disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, epilepsy, and metabolic acidosis and potassium deficiency. Many of these may reflect combination products containing a beta-agonist (as in Berodual, Combivent and Duovent) rather than ipratropium alone. They should be verified against the label.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found, all at the moderate level among those shown. The main ones are other anticholinergics and antimuscarinics (atropine, hyoscyamine, scopolamine, glycopyrronium, aclidinium, trospium, propantheline, dicyclomine), plus promethazine, chlorpheniramine, eluxadoline, prucalopride and inhaled insulin. The concern is additive anticholinergic effects.
- **Reported adverse events in the literature**: Unilateral mydriasis after inhalation (infant case report) and severe anaphylaxis after inhalation (case report).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Ipratropium's anticholinergic bronchodilator mechanism fits obstructive lung disease, and the indication overlaps with its established COPD use. A large completed Phase 3 trial (n=1,118), several other Phase 3 trials, and Cochrane and narrative reviews support this. Many of the trials are indirect, so the evidence is strong for COPD but less certain for the broader label of obstructive lung disease. The safety label data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (HSA) warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Mechanism of action data (DrugBank)
- Confirmation of the exact obstructive lung disease population (COPD vs asthma, including paediatric use) against the registered indications
- Attention to ocular, urinary and cardiac cautions and to anticholinergic interactions

Other predicted indications should stay on **Hold**, or be treated as research questions: nasal cavity disease and respiratory malformation have limited or indirect evidence, and the others have little or no supporting evidence. Anaphylaxis carries a safety signal.
