# Osimertinib: From EGFR-Mutant Lung Cancer to Thrombocytopenia

## One-Sentence Summary

Osimertinib is a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor, used for EGFR-mutation-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **thrombocytopenia**, with **8 linked clinical trials** and **20 publications**. However, the evidence shows osimertinib *causing* thrombocytopenia, not treating it, so this prediction most likely reflects a drug-to-adverse-event association.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the literature, osimertinib is an oral, irreversible EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets EGFR-sensitising mutations. Its efficacy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC is established.

The prediction does not hold up mechanistically. No therapeutic mechanism links EGFR inhibition to raising platelet counts. The high score most likely comes from a knowledge-graph link between the drug and a known adverse event. Published reports describe osimertinib causing thrombocytopenia, including severe cases that required stopping the drug and rechallenging it later.

All eight matched trials are NSCLC combination or early-phase studies in which thrombocytopenia appears only as a safety outcome. No study tests osimertinib as a treatment for low platelet counts.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications were not retrieved, and no drug-interaction records were found.

The linked literature also raises these signals, which appear in the case reports, cohort studies and pharmacovigilance analyses:
- **Thrombocytopenia:** including severe cases that needed drug interruption.
- **Cardiotoxicity:** cardiomyopathy, heart failure, reduced ejection fraction and QT-related events, mostly reversible in reports.
- **Thromboembolic events:** pulmonary embolism and multi-site thrombosis, confounded by cancer-associated thrombosis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is not backed by any evidence of benefit. Every linked trial is an NSCLC study, and the literature shows osimertinib causing thrombocytopenia. This is a safety signal rather than a repurposing opportunity. The other top-ranked predictions (cardiac and thrombotic conditions and rare platelet disorders) show the same pattern, or have no evidence at all.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any prospective study testing osimertinib as a treatment for a platelet disorder. Nothing in the current evidence justifies starting one.
