## Osimertinib: From EGFR-Mutant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Thrombocytopenia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Osimertinib is an oral EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor marketed in Singapore as Tagrisso. The TxGNN model predicts it for **thrombocytopenia** with a very high score, but **no clinical trials** and only literature describing osimertinib as a **cause** of low platelets support it. This looks like an adverse-event signal rather than a therapeutic opportunity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the literature, osimertinib is a third-generation, irreversible EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor used for EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), including T790M-positive disease.

The prediction does not appear to be mechanistically reasonable. The literature describes osimertinib as a cause of thrombocytopenia: a meta-analysis of randomised trials reports haematological toxicity, and case reports describe severe thrombocytopenia, including one with rechallenge. No plausible mechanism is identified by which EGFR inhibition would raise platelet counts. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects a drug-disease association in the knowledge graph, not a therapeutic effect.

The other top predictions show the same pattern. Heart neoplasm, thrombotic disease, heart conduction disease, cardiovascular disease and heart disease are all supported mainly by osimertinib's cardiac and thrombotic adverse events, such as cardiomyopathy, heart failure, QT prolongation and thrombosis. The remaining rare platelet and haematological conditions (marcothrombocytopenia with mitral valve insufficiency, hereditary thrombocytopenia with normal platelets, transient neonatal thrombocytopenia, dense granule disease) have no trials or literature at all.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The major-level ones among those listed include clarithromycin, dolasetron, palonosetron and levofloxacin. Several moderate interactions were also listed, for example famotidine, cimetidine, dexamethasone, aprepitant, loperamide, empagliflozin and miconazole. A reported case of severe thrombocytopenia occurred when osimertinib was combined with sitagliptin.
- **Literature safety signals:** Cardiotoxicity (cardiomyopathy, heart failure, QT prolongation, atrial fibrillation), thrombotic events (pulmonary embolism, arterial thrombosis), interstitial lung disease and haematological toxicity are reported.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report; please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score for thrombocytopenia is driven by osimertinib causing the condition, not treating it. There are no supporting trials, no plausible mechanism, and the cardiac and thrombotic predictions point to risk rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Any prospective evidence that osimertinib improves platelet counts (none was found, so reconsideration is unlikely without it)
- If the interest is in the safety signals, a separate pharmacovigilance and monitoring review (platelets, cardiac function, QT) rather than a repurposing assessment
