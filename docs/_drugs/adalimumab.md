# Adalimumab: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is a TNF-alpha–blocking biologic, established in rheumatoid arthritis (RA). The TxGNN model predicts it may help **rheumatoid vasculitis**, a severe extra-articular complication of RA. Evidence is thin: **0 relevant clinical trials** (5 retrieved, none on vasculitis) and **20 publications**, mostly case reports plus one systematic review of biologics in general. Several reports also describe vasculitis appearing *during* anti-TNF therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Adalimumab is a TNF-alpha inhibitor (an anti-TNF biologic). Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to rheumatoid vasculitis.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is one of the most severe extra-articular manifestations of RA. TNF-alpha drives much of the systemic inflammation in RA, so blocking it could plausibly calm the vessel inflammation as well. The prediction is therefore biologically coherent.

The evidence cuts both ways. Direct support is limited to a systematic review of biologics in rheumatoid vasculitis and isolated case reports, including one digital vasculitis that responded to adalimumab. Vasculitis-like and lupus-like events have also been reported in patients on anti-TNF therapy. Benefit and harm are both plausible, and the current data cannot separate them.

## Safety Considerations

- **Literature-reported signals**: Vasculitis, lupus-like events, ANCA-associated disease, glomerulonephritis and antiphospholipid syndrome have all been reported during adalimumab or other anti-TNF therapy. In rheumatoid vasculitis specifically, it is hard to tell disease progression from a drug-induced paradoxical reaction.
- **Dose reduction**: One case report describes a serious flare after adalimumab was reduced in rheumatoid vasculitis.

Please refer to the package insert for formal warnings, contraindications and drug-interaction information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but the evidence for rheumatoid vasculitis is limited to a low-quality systematic review and case reports. No trial addresses the question. Reports of vasculitis appearing under anti-TNF therapy mean harm is as plausible as benefit. This is best treated as a research question, not a treatment option.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Comparative evidence against established rheumatoid vasculitis treatments such as rituximab, tocilizumab and immunosuppressants
- A controlled study or well-designed cohort, with a protocol that separates disease flare from paradoxical drug-induced vasculitis
- Infection-risk screening (TB, hepatitis B) and a safety monitoring plan for any pilot use
