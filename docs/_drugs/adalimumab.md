# Adalimumab: From Inflammatory Arthritis and Bowel Disease to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is a TNF-alpha blocking antibody, originally used for rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, hidradenitis suppurativa and non-infectious uveitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis**.
Support is weak: **1 clinical trial** (not testing adalimumab for this condition) and **20 publications**, mostly case reports, safety reports and reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Adalimumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that neutralises TNF-alpha, a central driver of inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis and its extra-articular complications. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is well established.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a serious extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis. Because it is plausibly TNF-mediated, there is a biological rationale for the prediction. One case report describes digital vasculitis in a rheumatoid arthritis patient that responded well to adalimumab.

The direction of effect is uncertain, however. Anti-TNF agents, adalimumab included, have been reported to *induce* vasculitis and autoimmune phenomena, such as leukocytoclastic vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome and ANCA-associated nephritis. A large UK registry analysis also examined lupus-like and vasculitis-like events in TNF-inhibitor-treated rheumatoid arthritis patients. Adalimumab could therefore help, have no effect, or worsen vasculitis, and only controlled studies can settle this.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples from the data compiled for this report):
- **Major**
  - Corticosteroids: betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone (including ophthalmic)
  - Deferiprone
  - Radiolabelled or antibody-based agents: ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane (I-131), tositumomab, tositumomab (I-131)
- **Moderate:** metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin
- **Minor:** zinc acetate, zinc gluconate, zinc sulfate

**Safety signals from the literature** relevant to this indication:
- Adalimumab and other anti-TNF agents have been linked to drug-induced vasculitis, lupus-like syndromes, antiphospholipid syndrome and ANCA-associated nephritis.
- Anti-TNF therapy carries a risk of tuberculosis reactivation and other infections.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (99.80%), and the TNF-driven biology is plausible. Actual evidence is limited to case reports and observational data, however, and the only registered trial does not test adalimumab for vasculitis. Reports of anti-TNF-induced vasculitis mean the direction of effect is unclear. Adalimumab is already marketed in Singapore with 17 registrations, so access is not the obstacle. The obstacle is the lack of efficacy and safety evidence in this condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or well-designed observational studies of adalimumab specifically in rheumatoid vasculitis
- A systematic review of cases where anti-TNF therapy helped versus induced vasculitis, to clarify the direction of effect
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A clear safety monitoring plan for patients with existing vasculitis or autoimmune manifestations

**Note on other predictions:** Two other predicted indications, inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis, have much stronger evidence (L1, several Phase 3 trials). They largely match adalimumab's existing approved uses (ankylosing spondylitis and axial spondyloarthritis, and polyarticular JIA), so they are closer to label alignment than true repurposing.
