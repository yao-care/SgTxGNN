# Anastrozole: From Breast Cancer (Registered Indication) to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Anastrozole is a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor registered in Singapore as an oral tablet. The registration records compiled for this report do not include the approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it is effective for **female breast carcinoma**, which is an established use rather than a true new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. From the available assessment, anastrozole is a third-generation non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor. It blocks the conversion of androgens to oestrogens and lowers circulating oestrogen in postmenopausal women. This directly targets oestrogen-driven growth in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.

The predicted indication is already an established clinical use. The high TxGNN score is consistent with both the mechanism and the clinical evidence. This is on-label use, not true repurposing.

The other nine predictions (for example neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma, monocytic and myeloid leukaemia, rhabdomyosarcoma) have no clinical trials and no supporting literature. They are computational predictions only, with no validated link to aromatase inhibition, and are not recommended for further action (Hold).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: myocardial ischaemia and coronary artery disease. These come from drug–disease links in the TxGNN knowledge graph and need confirmation against the approved label.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: one published case report describes anastrozole-induced liver injury.
- **Class effects flagged in the trials**: bone density loss and effects on blood vessel function were studied in several of the trials above.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 potential interactions were identified, all with an unspecified severity level. Examples include bupropion, omeprazole, pantoprazole, metformin, glimepiride, simvastatin, morphine, prednisone and acetylsalicylic acid. The clinical significance of each needs separate review.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Anastrozole in breast cancer is supported by multiple completed Phase 3 trials and large randomised trials (ATAC, IBIS-II, FALCON), so the evidence level is L1. The indication is established, so this is confirmation of on-label use rather than new repurposing. The remaining nine predictions have no clinical support and should stay on Hold.

- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Guardrails: restrict use to postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive disease, monitor bone density and cardiovascular risk, and manage musculoskeletal adverse effects
- Review of the contraindication signals (myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease) and the interactions listed above
