## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the general pharmacology of the drug, tolnaftate is a thiocarbamate antifungal that inhibits squalene epoxidase in dermatophytes, blocking ergosterol synthesis. It has proven efficacy in dermatophyte skin infections, and mechanistically it may apply to related fungal skin conditions.

**Ectothrix infectious disease (rank 1)** is a dermatophyte infection of the hair, closely related to the approved tinea indications. However, topical tolnaftate penetrates hair follicles poorly, and hair infections generally require systemic therapy. No trials or literature support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone. The same limitation applies to the other deep or hair-related predictions: Majocchi granuloma, endothrix infectious disease and tinea profunda.

**Superficial mycosis (rank 5)** overlaps heavily with the approved indications (tinea pedis, corporis, cruris, versicolor). The literature includes a placebo-controlled comparison with griseofulvin and a double-blind comparison with clotrimazole. These studies are old, and tolnaftate's activity is not established for non-dermatophyte or deeper infections.

**Predictions with no plausible mechanistic link** include ophthalmic herpes zoster, infectious mononucleosis (both viral) and orbital cellulitis (typically bacterial, needing systemic antibiotics). These scores probably reflect network proximity to other infectious or skin diseases rather than pharmacology.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (ectothrix infectious disease and the other predictions); Proceed with Guardrails (superficial mycosis only)**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction, ectothrix infectious disease, has no trials or literature. Topical tolnaftate is also unlikely to penetrate hair follicles well, so these hair and deep infections generally need systemic therapy.
- Superficial mycosis has controlled studies and a systematic review, but they are old, and the prediction mostly restates existing labelled use. Predictions such as herpes zoster, infectious mononucleosis and orbital cellulitis have no plausible mechanism.
- The superficial mycosis L1 rating rests on published controlled studies, not registered Phase 3 trials, so it should be treated cautiously.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert, to confirm approved indications, warnings and contraindications (not available in the data compiled for this report)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the older RCTs' methodology and any recent comparative evidence in superficial mycosis
- For ectothrix and endothrix infections, Majocchi granuloma and tinea profunda: evidence of follicular penetration or clinical efficacy before any further consideration
- For cutaneous candidiasis: direct evidence against Candida compared with azoles or polyenes
