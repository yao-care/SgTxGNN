## Risankizumab: Dermatitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Risankizumab is an IL-23 p19 inhibitor antibody, originally used to treat psoriasis (including related conditions such as parapsoriasis).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, and the data compiled for this report include **2 clinical trials** (one completed Phase 2 randomized controlled trial in atopic dermatitis) and **20 publications**.
The evidence is early-stage: the mechanistic fit is weaker than for psoriasis, and efficacy results still need to be verified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Risankizumab is a humanised IgG monoclonal antibody that targets the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 (IL-23). IL-23 supports the Th17 pathway, which drives psoriasis. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, but the published drug profile describes this IL-23 p19 mechanism, and the drug is approved for several psoriasis forms.

Atopic dermatitis and other eczematous diseases are driven mainly by type 2 signalling (IL-4/IL-13), so IL-23 blockade is a weaker mechanistic fit than it is for psoriasis. Partial rationale comes from Th17/Th22 contributions and from the overlap between psoriasis and eczema in some patients. A Phase 2 randomized controlled trial tested risankizumab directly in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. Its published report (PMID 36588137) was available only by title, so no efficacy conclusion is drawn here.

Case-level reports describe combined dupilumab plus risankizumab use in patients with both atopic dermatitis and psoriasis, and risankizumab as a switch option after brodalumab-induced eczematous reactions. These reports only generate hypotheses. Other reports describe eczematous eruptions or paradoxical eczema appearing during risankizumab treatment (incidence estimated at 1–3% of psoriasis patients on biologics), which argues for caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 randomized controlled trial in atopic dermatitis supports an L2 evidence level, but the data compiled here give no efficacy results from it. The mechanistic fit is weaker than for psoriasis, and some reports describe eczema flares during risankizumab treatment. The other nine predictions (for example, neonatal dermatomyositis and amyopathic dermatomyositis) rely on model scores alone (L5) and also warrant Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Efficacy and safety results from NCT03706040 and the full text of PMID 36588137
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore Health Sciences Authority (HSA), along with the approved indication text for the four licences
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A review of the eczematous and paradoxical reactions reported with IL-23 inhibitors
- Comparison with established atopic dermatitis therapies, including type 2-targeted biologics, to define any niche (for example, patients with both psoriasis and eczema)
