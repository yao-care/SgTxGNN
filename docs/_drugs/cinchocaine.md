Cinchocaine (dibucaine) is an amide-type local anesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, and it is currently marketed in Singapore as a topical ointment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general class pharmacology, cinchocaine blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, which dampens nerve signalling at the site of application.

In theory, an anesthetic effect on the airway surface could reduce the cough reflex or airway irritation in bronchitis. This would only relieve symptoms and would not treat the underlying disease. Systemic or inhaled use of a potent local anesthetic also raises toxicity concerns, notably cardiac and central nervous system effects. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only and has no supporting clinical data.
