# Benralizumab: From Severe Eosinophilic Asthma to Thrombocytopenia Due to Immune Destruction

## One-Sentence Summary

Benralizumab is an injectable anti-IL-5Rα antibody that depletes eosinophils. It is marketed in Singapore under the brand name Fasenra.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombocytopenia due to immune destruction** (immune thrombocytopenia), but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction, so the prediction rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Benralizumab binds the IL-5 receptor alpha subunit (IL-5Rα) and depletes eosinophils and basophils through antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC). Its established use is in eosinophil-driven disease.

The mechanistic link to the predicted indication is **weak**. Immune thrombocytopenia is driven mainly by autoantibody-mediated platelet clearance and impaired platelet production (megakaryopoiesis), not by eosinophils. The high score most likely reflects network proximity in the knowledge graph, not a biological rationale. The same weak link applies to related autoimmune cytopenias in the prediction list, such as autoimmune thrombocytopenic and Evans syndrome.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or publications (L5), and the mechanism is at odds with the known biology of immune thrombocytopenia. The drug is already marketed in Singapore, but that does not support this new use.

Among the other predictions, **dermatitis** (rank 2) has the most evidence, at L2. However, the Phase 2 HILLIER trial (PMID 37178404) reported **no effect** on moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, so this route is not encouraging either. Exploratory trials in eosinophil-driven skin reactions such as DRESS (NCT06734884, not yet recruiting) have no results yet.
