## Clobetasol: From Topical Corticosteroid Use to Primary Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clobetasol is a super-potent topical corticosteroid, marketed in Singapore as scalp lotion, cream and ointment.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma** (mainly early-stage mycosis fungoides).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, clobetasol is a super-potent topical glucocorticoid. Glucocorticoid receptor activation can induce apoptosis of malignant T cells and dampen local inflammatory and cytokine signalling in skin lesions.

This is a plausible mechanism for patch- and plaque-stage mycosis fungoides, the most common form of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. Topical corticosteroids are already used in clinical practice for early-stage disease. In one large single-centre experience, topical clobetasol is described as first-line treatment for early-stage disease.

The support applies only to skin-directed control of early-stage disease. It does not extend to advanced, nodal or systemic lymphoma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 records, none with a severity grade (all "Unknown"). Examples include other corticosteroids (prednisone, prednisolone, hydrocortisone, triamcinolone), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole), and calcitriol and vitamin A.
- **Literature safety signals**: Prolonged use of potent topical clobetasol has been linked to skin adverse effects, and case reports describe iatrogenic Cushing syndrome from systemic absorption. Oral candidiasis has been reported in patients using topical corticosteroids. Because mycosis fungoides is a chronic disease needing prolonged treatment, these risks matter.
- **Misdiagnosis risk**: Several case reports show cutaneous lymphoma mistaken for psoriasis or eczema and treated with steroids alone, which can delay diagnosis.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Evidence is limited to observational cohorts and case reports for skin-limited, early-stage mycosis fungoides, with no registered trials. The mechanism is plausible and topical clobetasol is already used in practice, but it supports only skin-directed therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore Health Sciences Authority (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications of the Singapore products
- Biopsy-confirmed diagnosis and specialist oversight, restricting use to early-stage, skin-limited disease
- A monitoring plan for skin atrophy, systemic absorption (HPA-axis suppression, Cushing syndrome) and secondary infection
- Prospective or controlled data comparing clobetasol with established topical options
