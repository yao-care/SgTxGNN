## Bicalutamide: From Androgen Receptor Blockade to Hypertrichosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bicalutamide is a nonsteroidal androgen receptor (AR) antagonist, which the literature describes as commonly used in prostate cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hypertrichosis** (excess hair growth).
Evidence is very thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a comment letter), so this remains a research question.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available from the drug database. Based on known pharmacology, bicalutamide blocks the androgen receptor.

Androgen signalling drives the conversion of fine hair follicles into terminal (thick) hair. Blocking the AR could plausibly reduce androgen-dependent hair growth, which is the basis for the link to hypertrichosis.

The only supporting publication is a 2022 comment on a retrospective review of 35 patients, which reported that bicalutamide improved minoxidil-induced hypertrichosis in female pattern hair loss. This is indirect, secondary evidence. The original study's abstract was not available, so its design and size cannot be confirmed. The prediction also fits only androgen-dependent hair growth. It does not fit congenital forms such as Ambras syndrome, which are genetic rather than androgen-driven.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence for hypertrichosis is one comment letter on a small retrospective study, with no registered trials. The mechanistic link (AR blockade reducing androgen-dependent hair growth) is plausible but unconfirmed. Safety signals, particularly liver-related ones, also need weighing for what is largely a non-life-threatening condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The original retrospective study (35 patients) to assess its design, dose, and outcomes
- Controlled studies in androgen-dependent hypertrichosis or hirsutism
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications for Singapore products, plus the approved indication text
- A liver-safety monitoring plan for non-oncology use
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, **female breast carcinoma** has the strongest support (L2). It has a Phase 2 trial (NCT03650894, bicalutamide with nivolumab and ipilimumab in metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer, n=30, active, not recruiting) and substantial preclinical and review literature on AR-positive triple-negative breast cancer. A single-arm phase II study of bicalutamide plus an aromatase inhibitor (PMID 31434793) showed no synergistic activity. That indication is more suitable for a separate evaluation, with AR-expression-based patient selection as the key guardrail.
