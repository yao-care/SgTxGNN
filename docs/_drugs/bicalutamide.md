# Bicalutamide: From Prostate Cancer to Hypertrichosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Bicalutamide is an oral non-steroidal androgen receptor blocker, best known as an anti-androgen treatment for prostate cancer. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hypertrichosis** (excess hair growth), with a very high model score. The supporting evidence is thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication**, a comment letter rather than a primary study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on known information, bicalutamide is a nonsteroidal androgen receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in hormone-driven cancer is established, and mechanistically an anti-androgenic effect on hair growth is plausible.

The one linked paper is a comment on a retrospective review of 35 patients. That review looked at bicalutamide for **minoxidil-induced hypertrichosis in female pattern hair loss**. This is a narrow, drug-induced, female-specific setting. It is not the same as congenital or generalized hypertrichosis. The high model score is a prediction only. It does not show that the drug works in general hypertrichosis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The hypertrichosis prediction rests on a model score and one comment letter about a narrow, drug-induced, female-specific setting. There are no trials and no direct efficacy data for general hypertrichosis.

Among the ten predicted indications in this pack, **female breast carcinoma** has the strongest support. It has a Phase 2 trial of bicalutamide with nivolumab and ipilimumab (NCT03650894), plus preclinical and case-report evidence in androgen receptor-positive disease. It is capped at L3. It may be a better candidate to prioritise.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the primary retrospective study (35 patients) to confirm its design and outcomes
- Download and parse the HSA package insert for warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of whether the target population is drug-induced, acquired or congenital hypertrichosis
- Route compatibility check (currently only oral forms are registered in Singapore)
