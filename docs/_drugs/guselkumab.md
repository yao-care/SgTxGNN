# Guselkumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Drug-Induced Osteoporosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Guselkumab (an anti-IL-23 antibody, marketed in Singapore as Tremfya) is used to treat immune-mediated inflammatory disease, mainly plaque psoriasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **drug-induced osteoporosis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Guselkumab is a monoclonal antibody that blocks the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 (IL-23). The IL-23/Th17 axis drives psoriasis, which is why the drug works in that disease.

The link to osteoporosis is speculative. The IL-23/IL-17 axis has been implicated in inflammatory bone loss, so blocking it could in theory protect bone. However, no direct clinical or preclinical evidence was retrieved, and the model score alone is not enough to support the prediction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The drug-induced osteoporosis prediction has a high model score but no supporting trials, no literature, and only a speculative mechanism, so the evidence level is L5. The same evidence pack shows much stronger support for two other predictions, psoriasis (L1, many completed Phase 3 trials and RCTs) and ulcerative colitis (L1, including the Phase 3 QUASAR trial). Both are likely already-approved uses, so they validate the model rather than represent new repurposing opportunities.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical or mechanistic studies of IL-23 blockade in inflammatory or drug-induced bone loss
- The HSA package insert, to complete safety information and confirm the approved indications
- Correction of the drug's original-indication record, which is currently empty
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
