## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, guselkumab is a monoclonal antibody that selectively blocks the p19 subunit of IL-23. This cytokine drives the Th17 pathway, which underlies several chronic inflammatory diseases.

**Drug-induced osteoporosis (top-ranked prediction).** IL-23/IL-17 signalling has been linked to inflammatory bone loss. However, there is no direct connection to drug-induced osteoporosis, such as glucocorticoid-induced bone loss. No trials or literature were found, so this prediction rests on the model score alone.

**Psoriasis.** Blocking IL-23 interrupts the Th17 axis that drives keratinocyte overgrowth and skin inflammation. This is guselkumab's established use, not a new repurposing finding. The label should still be checked, because the licence data do not state the approved indications.

**Ulcerative colitis.** IL-23 drives mucosal Th17 inflammation in inflammatory bowel disease, and guselkumab's p19 blockade targets this pathway directly. The Phase 3 QUASAR programme provides direct efficacy and safety evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The only **Major** interaction in the listed results is with **adalimumab**. **Moderate** interactions include simvastatin, apixaban, warfarin, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol, tacrolimus, theophylline, roflumilast, lidocaine, nifedipine, fentanyl, hydrocodone and a tetanus toxoid vaccine. Several zinc salts have **Minor** interactions.
- **Trial-based signals**: Safety findings come from long-term integrated analyses (PMID 37906417) and the QUASAR programme. The ulcerative colitis evidence notes that infection and liver safety should be monitored.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, drug-induced osteoporosis)

**Rationale:**
The top prediction is supported only by a model score of 0.9984. It has no trials, no literature and no clear mechanistic link to IL-23 blockade.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert, to confirm approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data
- For drug-induced osteoporosis: preclinical or mechanistic evidence on IL-23 and bone loss, and then a pilot study if that evidence supports one
- For ulcerative colitis: confirmation of Singapore regulatory status for this indication, and a monitoring plan for infection and liver safety
- A review of co-medications, especially concomitant adalimumab use (Major interaction)
