## Oxymetholone: From Aplastic Anemia to Seborrheic Dermatitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxymetholone is an oral anabolic androgenic steroid, originally used for anemias caused by bone marrow failure, including aplastic anemia and Diamond-Blackfan anemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **seborrheic dermatitis**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction.
This is a computational prediction only, and the direction of effect may even be unfavorable.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, oxymetholone is an anabolic androgenic steroid. Its efficacy in bone marrow failure and anemias is the basis of its original use, but a mechanistic link to seborrheic dermatitis has not been established.

The data compiled for this report suggest that a connection to sebaceous gland or inflammatory modulation is speculative. Androgens typically increase sebum production, so the direction of effect may be unfavorable for seborrheic dermatitis. The high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the TxGNN model's data, not package insert text)**: The following conditions are listed as contraindicated:
  - Liver and biliary: liver failure, liver disease, peliosis hepatis, gallbladder disease
  - Kidney: kidney disease, pyoureter, nephrotic syndrome (including familial form), lipoid nephrosis
  - Cardiovascular: congestive heart failure, heart disease, cardiovascular disease, coronary artery disease, arteriosclerosis disorder, atherosclerosis, hypoalphalipoproteinemia
  - Hormone-sensitive cancers and prostate: prostate cancer, neoplasm and carcinoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia, female breast carcinoma
  - Metabolic: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy
  - Haematological: blood coagulation disease
- **Drug Interactions**: 69 interactions were identified. The ones shown include:
  - **Major**: carfilzomib
  - **Moderate**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide), asparaginase products, brentuximab vedotin, calcipotriol and calcitriol (topical), cannabidiol, chlorpropamide, clofarabine, corticotropin, tetracosactide, cyclosporine, and C1-esterase inhibitors
- **Other concerns noted in the evidence review**: hepatotoxicity is a known concern, and there is a virilization risk with systemic androgen exposure (particularly in vulvar conditions, pregnancy and neonatal settings).

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings, as label-based warnings are not yet available.
