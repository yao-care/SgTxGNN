## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ammonium chloride is traditionally used as an expectorant in cough and cold preparations, and it may help loosen mucus in the upper airway. Mechanistically it could be relevant to nasal cavity disease, but this link is unverified.

The relations recorded in the knowledge graph (pharyngitis, common cold, nasopharyngitis) all involve the upper respiratory tract, which is anatomically and symptomatically adjacent to the nasal cavity. The high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. No trial or publication in the data compiled for this report directly studies ammonium chloride in nasal cavity disease.
