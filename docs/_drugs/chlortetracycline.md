## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Chlortetracycline belongs to the tetracycline class of antibiotics. The provided data do not confirm how it would act in postinfectious vasculitis. Any link would rest on general class knowledge, such as antibacterial or anti-inflammatory effects, and nothing in the evidence pack supports it.

Postinfectious vasculitis is an inflammatory condition that follows an infection. That is loosely connected to an anti-infective drug, but the model score of 99.82% (rank 3,109) is the only support. The only registered product is a topical eye ointment, so it is also unclear how a systemic vascular condition could be reached with the available formulation. Route compatibility has not been assessed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for postinfectious vasculitis has no clinical or literature support, and the mechanism of action and safety data are missing. The only registered product is a topical ophthalmic ointment, which is unlikely to suit a systemic vascular condition. The other predicted indications are also weakly supported. The most supported is otitis externa (L4), backed only by a 1950 paper, so it could be treated as a research question rather than a candidate for development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for chlortetracycline
- The registered indication and route compatibility for the proposed use
- Clinical or observational evidence specific to postinfectious vasculitis
- For infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome (rank 7), a safety review before any further consideration, because antibiotic exposure is generally cautioned against in Shiga toxin-producing E. coli infection. This is general clinical knowledge, not derived from the pack.
