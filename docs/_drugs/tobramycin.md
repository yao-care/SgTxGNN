# Tobramycin: From Topical Ophthalmic Antibacterial Use to Exposure Keratitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic. In Singapore it is registered only in eye products (drops, ointments, and steroid combinations).
The TxGNN model predicts it may help with **exposure keratitis**, but the evidence is thin: **2 loosely related clinical trials** and **7 publications**, none of which tests tobramycin for this condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tobramycin is an aminoglycoside with broad Gram-negative activity, including *Pseudomonas*. It is used topically for bacterial eye infections, and its efficacy there is established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to exposure keratitis.

Exposure keratitis is damage to the corneal surface caused by incomplete eyelid closure, for example in patients who cannot close their eyes voluntarily. The cornea then dries out and is easily infected. A topical antibiotic could act as adjunctive prophylaxis against secondary bacterial infection. That would be a protective role rather than treatment of the underlying disorder.

There is also a safety concern. An in vitro study (PMID 2707046) showed that aminoglycosides can be toxic to corneal epithelial cells. This matters in a cornea that is already damaged.

## Safety Considerations

- **Corneal epithelial toxicity**: an in vitro study found aminoglycosides, including tobramycin, can be toxic to corneal epithelial cells (PMID 2707046). This matters in a cornea already compromised by exposure.
- **Drug interactions**: no interaction records were found.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (99.93%), but there is no human evidence that tobramycin helps exposure keratitis. The two trials and seven papers are indirect, and the one safety paper points to possible corneal toxicity. At best, tobramycin would be adjunctive infection prophylaxis.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Clinical data on topical tobramycin for infection prevention or treatment in exposure keratitis
- An assessment of corneal epithelial toxicity in patients with a damaged ocular surface
- Confirmation of the approved indications of the 5 registered products

**Other predicted indications:** within this pack, **otitis externa** (L3) has more supporting literature, mainly on *Pseudomonas* infections. It is probably already established practice, though, and needs attention to ototoxicity.
