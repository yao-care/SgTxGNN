# Terazosin: From Hypertension and Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia to Hypotrichosis Simplex of the Scalp

## One-Sentence Summary

Terazosin is an alpha-1 adrenergic blocker, originally used to treat hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia.
The TxGNN model ranks **hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp** as its top predicted new indication, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.
Other predictions in the list, notably **migraine** and **Raynaud disease**, have limited older clinical reports and are better supported.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Terazosin is known as a selective alpha-1 adrenergic receptor blocker. It relaxes vascular and prostatic smooth muscle, which explains its use in hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia.

The link to hair loss is speculative. One plausible but unproven idea is that vasodilation could improve scalp perfusion and follicle function, similar to minoxidil, another vasodilating antihypertensive. The high TxGNN score is a graph-based prediction only and does not reflect any observed clinical effect.

The other hair-related predictions are weaker still:
- **Congenital hypotrichosis milia** and **Ambras-type hypertrichosis universalis congenita** are rare genetic conditions with no mechanistic link to alpha-1 blockade. Ambras-type hypertrichosis is also the opposite phenotype (excess hair), which suggests the scores reflect graph artefacts.
- **Diffuse alopecia areata** is autoimmune, and alpha-1 blockade has no known direct immunomodulatory role.
- **Alopecia** has only one indirect review, which discusses extracardiac effects of cardiovascular drugs, mainly minoxidil.

The best-supported signals among the top 10 predictions are:
- **Migraine disorder (rank 5, L3):** alpha-1 blockade may modulate cerebrovascular tone and sympathetic activity. Evidence consists of a small 1994 open clinical study and a 1997 review, and has not been confirmed by modern randomised trials.
- **Raynaud disease (rank 7, L3):** alpha-1 blockade reduces sympathetically mediated vasoconstriction, which fits the pathophysiology. Evidence is a single 1997 study.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** multiple system atrophy, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, orthostatic hypotension, priapism and intraoperative floppy iris syndrome.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The listed ones are mostly Moderate severity, including:
  - Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone
  - Opioids: morphine (including liposomal) and opium
  - SGLT2 inhibitors: canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin
  - Cannabinoids: dronabinol and nabilone
  - Bupropion

  Interactions with calcitriol, pantoprazole and glimepiride have an unknown severity level.
- **Tolerability:** In the migraine report, five of ten patients stopped treatment because of side effects. Hypotension and dizziness are a particular concern in a mania population.

The package insert warnings and contraindications were not available, so please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, rests only on a graph-based score, with no trials or publications. The same is true of most other hair-related predictions. Migraine and Raynaud disease show some older, small-scale clinical signals, but they are not strong enough to move beyond a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, to complete the safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any preclinical or clinical data on terazosin in hair-growth disorders, to test the speculative perfusion hypothesis
- For a more promising direction, a focused evidence review and a well-designed controlled study for migraine prophylaxis or Raynaud's phenomenon, with attention to hypotension and dizziness
- Assessment of route compatibility and formulation, since only oral tablets are registered in Singapore
