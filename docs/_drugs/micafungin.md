## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Micafungin belongs to the echinocandin class, which inhibits 1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase, an enzyme essential for building the fungal cell wall. It has established activity against Candida species.

Candida is a common cause of fungal urinary tract infection and candiduria. The prediction is therefore best read as an extension of micafungin's approved anti-Candida use to the urinary tract, not as a new mechanism. It applies to **Candida UTI only**, not to bacterial UTI.

There is one pharmacokinetic caveat. Only a small fraction of micafungin is excreted in urine, and echinocandins are not routinely recommended for candiduria. However, a pharmacokinetic report (PMID 27424599) found urinary micafungin levels sufficient to treat Candida UTI in six patients, including four with fluconazole-resistant organisms. This partly addresses the concern, but the data are small-scale.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (association, not label text)**: Kidney disease and pyoureter are linked to micafungin. This matters for a urinary indication, so renal function and the urinary tract should be assessed before use.
- **Drug Interactions (5 moderate-level interactions)**: Amphotericin B, nitisinone, everolimus, ribociclib and sirolimus.

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The Candida UTI signal is mechanistically plausible and is supported by several cohort studies, a pharmacokinetic study and multiple case reports. However, there are no registered clinical trials and no RCTs, and the evidence is observational (L3). The low urinary excretion of micafungin remains a concern. The use should be limited to Candida UTI or candiduria, particularly fluconazole-resistant or non-albicans infections, and not extended to bacterial UTI. The other nine predictions are rated Hold because they lack any supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Prospective or comparative clinical data on Candida UTI, ideally including urinary pharmacokinetics and microbiological cure rates
- A patient-selection policy that separates symptomatic Candida UTI from asymptomatic candiduria, which generally should not be treated
- Renal function assessment and a drug-interaction review before use
