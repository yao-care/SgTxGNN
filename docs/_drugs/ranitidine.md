# Ranitidine: From Acid Suppression to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ranitidine is an H2-receptor antagonist that reduces gastric acid secretion, and the Singapore registration records do not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, which is closely tied to its known mechanism and is closer to an established use than a true repurposing.
Support is **1 clinical trial** (only tangentially related) and **19 publications**, including several comparative trials of ulcer healing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ranitidine blocks histamine H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells and so suppresses acid secretion. Acid suppression is the established basis for healing peptic ulcers and preventing relapse.

The Singapore records do not list an original indication for this drug. The prediction fits the drug's known pharmacology so closely that it is better read as confirmation of an established use than as a new repurposing signal. The literature supports this. For example, a study of ranitidine 300 mg daily reported 4-week healing rates of 91% for duodenal ulcer, 81% for gastric corporeal ulcer and 68% for prepyloric ulcer.

One caution applies. Ranitidine products were withdrawn in many markets in 2019–2020 over NDMA contamination, and PPIs or famotidine are usually preferred alternatives. Any recommendation should be limited to approved products that are actually available.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Availability and quality:** Ranitidine was withdrawn or restricted in many markets from 2019–2020 because of NDMA contamination (see PMID 31848148). The current status of each Singapore registration should be confirmed before any use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Ranitidine's acid-suppressing mechanism is well matched to active peptic ulcer disease, and multiple comparative trials and reviews support ulcer healing. However, this is essentially an established use rather than a new one, the single listed trial is only tangential, and the NDMA-related availability issue and the preference for PPIs or famotidine limit the recommendation. The other nine predictions (ranks 2–10) have weaker or no direct evidence, and all are rated Hold or Research Question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to obtain approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Confirmation that each of the five Singapore registrations is currently supplied and NDMA-compliant
- The original indication and detailed mechanism of action recorded in the dataset
- Review of the ranitidine-comparator Phase 3 trials (NCT00633412, NCT00633672, NCT00401752) to confirm ranitidine's role and to support an upgrade to L1 or L2
- A comparison against PPIs and famotidine to justify choosing ranitidine
