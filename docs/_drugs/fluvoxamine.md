## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Fluvoxamine is known as an SSRI, and its use in OCD and social phobia is recorded in the knowledge graph. Mechanistically, a serotonergic drug could plausibly be considered for related psychiatric conditions.

No direct mechanistic link to schizoid personality disorder has been established. The high score (1.0000) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. The same score of 1.0000 is also given to several other personality disorders (histrionic, schizotypal, paranoid), which suggests a general similarity effect between related disorders.

The only linked paper studies personality disorders that occur alongside body dysmorphic disorder. It does not test fluvoxamine as a treatment for schizoid personality disorder.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication relations in the knowledge graph (not label text):** epilepsy, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, inappropriate ADH syndrome, nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, serotonin syndrome.
- **Drug interactions:** The interaction query returned 100 records. The 20 reviewed include these **Major** interactions:
  - Alosetron
  - Diethylpropion
  - Dolasetron
  - Bupropion

  Moderate interactions include rabeprazole, aprepitant, aspirin, clarithromycin, morphine, pioglitazone and several corticosteroids such as hydrocortisone and dexamethasone.
- **Related literature signal:** One linked case report describes seizures when fluvoxamine was combined with levomepromazine, a phenothiazine antipsychotic. This matters because antipsychotics are often used in personality disorders with psychotic-like features.

Warnings and contraindications from the package insert were not available. Please refer to the package insert for label safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only. There are no registered trials, and the single linked paper does not test fluvoxamine for schizoid personality disorder. The evidence level is therefore L5, and there is not enough support to move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence, such as controlled or open-label studies of fluvoxamine in schizoid personality disorder
- A defined mechanism of action and a rationale specific to this condition
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the registered Singapore products
