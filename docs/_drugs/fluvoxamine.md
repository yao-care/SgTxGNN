# Fluvoxamine: From Social Phobia and Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder to Schizotypal Personality Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Fluvoxamine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) originally used for social phobia and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD).
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **schizotypal personality disorder**, but there are **no clinical trials** and only **5 publications** (all case reports or small open-label studies), none of which tests fluvoxamine for this condition.
The best-supported predictions further down the list are anxiety disorder and agoraphobia, which largely overlap with the drug's existing indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, fluvoxamine is an SSRI that increases serotonin signalling. Its efficacy in OCD and social phobia is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to conditions that share anxiety or obsessive features.

For schizotypal personality disorder specifically, the link is weak. The retrieved literature concerns antipsychotic augmentation in fluvoxamine-refractory OCD, in which comorbid schizotypal disorder was associated with response, plus case reports of comorbidity and drug interactions. Any connection comes indirectly through OCD-spectrum overlap and serotonergic modulation. The very high TxGNN score is not supported by clinical data.

Other top-ranked predictions have a very different evidence profile:

- **Schizoid, paranoid, histrionic, dependent and narcissistic personality disorders**: no trials, and the few papers retrieved are comorbidity studies or unrelated reviews. These are prediction-only signals.
- **Benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy**: no trials, no literature, and no plausible mechanism. The score is likely spurious.
- **Anxiety disorder (rank 5)** and **agoraphobia (rank 6)**: supported by Phase 3 trials, placebo-controlled RCTs and meta-analyses. Both are close extensions of the already-approved social phobia and OCD indications, so they are not novel repurposing signals.
- **Endogenous depression (rank 10)**: supported by class-level antidepressant meta-analyses and one Phase 2 trial of unknown status.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from the TxGNN graph, not label text): epilepsy, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, inappropriate ADH syndrome (including the nephrogenic form), and serotonin syndrome.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include alosetron, bupropion, diethylpropion and dolasetron. Moderate-level examples include rabeprazole, aspirin, clarithromycin, morphine, pioglitazone, aprepitant, chlorpropamide and several corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone).
- **Literature signals**: seizures with combined levomepromazine and fluvoxamine (case report); CYP-mediated interactions, including a methadone toxicity case; and pediatric suicidality, sexual dysfunction and gastrointestinal adverse effects, which are class effects of antidepressants.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, schizotypal personality disorder, rests on a very high model score but has no trials and only indirect case-level literature. The same applies to the other personality disorders and to benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy. Anxiety disorder and agoraphobia carry a "Proceed with Guardrails" rating (L1) and are well supported, but they largely overlap with fluvoxamine's existing indications and are not new repurposing opportunities. Endogenous depression remains a research question pending results from the Phase 2 melancholia trial.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (currently unavailable)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Health Sciences Authority (HSA), which are required before any safety screening
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm which indications are already covered
- For schizotypal and other personality disorders: controlled studies, or a clear mechanistic rationale, before any further evaluation
- For anxiety disorder subtypes and agoraphobia: confirmation that current guidelines support use, and a plan to manage CYP-mediated interactions, initial anxiety exacerbation and pediatric suicidality monitoring
- For endogenous depression: results from NCT04160377 and review of its status
