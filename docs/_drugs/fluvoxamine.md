# Fluvoxamine: From Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder to Schizotypal Personality Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Fluvoxamine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) marketed in Singapore. The literature in this pack describes it as used for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and depression.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **schizotypal personality disorder**, with a very high graph score.
Evidence is thin: **0 clinical trials** and **6 publications**, and none of the papers tests fluvoxamine as a treatment for this disorder.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the evidence pack. Fluvoxamine is a known SSRI, so it raises serotonin signalling. Its efficacy in OCD is well established in the literature.

The link to schizotypal personality disorder is indirect. Several of the retrieved papers describe OCD patients who also have schizotypal features. In one case series, patients with OCD who did not respond to fluvoxamine improved when an antipsychotic was added. Response was associated with tic disorders or schizotypal personality disorder, which may point to shared serotonin and dopamine abnormalities. That is a signal about OCD subgroups, not evidence that fluvoxamine treats schizotypal personality disorder itself.

The very high TxGNN score is a graph-based prediction and is not backed by any observed treatment data. It should be read as a hypothesis only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions (literature signal only)**: One case report describes seizures when fluvoxamine was combined with the phenothiazine levomepromazine. This is relevant if an antipsychotic is considered alongside fluvoxamine.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is at model-prediction level (L5). There are no registered trials, and none of the six papers tests fluvoxamine in schizotypal personality disorder. The papers concern OCD populations with schizotypal features. Other candidates in the same evidence pack, such as anxiety disorder, agoraphobia and endogenous depression, have much stronger supporting evidence and are better places to focus effort.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- A treatment study or systematic review of SSRIs in schizotypal personality disorder
- A drug-interaction review, especially with antipsychotics and CYP-metabolised drugs
