## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Ascorbic acid is a well-known water-soluble vitamin that acts as an antioxidant and as a cofactor for collagen synthesis. Its efficacy in correcting vitamin C deficiency and scurvy is established.

For the top-ranked prediction, non-syndromic esophageal malformation, **no mechanistic rationale has been identified**. This is a congenital structural malformation, and ascorbic acid is not expected to correct it. The high score reflects a graph-based prediction only, with no supporting trials or literature.

Other predictions have more plausible links:

- **Injury (L2, Phase 2 RCTs exist):** Ascorbic acid may attenuate oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction and inflammation after tissue injury. However, "injury" is a very broad category, the trials are small, and several were terminated or have unknown status, so condition-specific evaluation is needed.
- **Vitamin deficiency disorder (L3):** This overlaps with the existing indications (deficiency and scurvy), so it is largely on-label rather than true repurposing. Extension to deficiencies of other vitamins is unsupported.
- **Esophageal disease (L4):** Evidence is preclinical, epidemiological or review-level and the direction is mixed. One rat study reported *enhanced* esophageal carcinogenesis in a reflux model treated with ascorbic acid.
- **Perinatal disease (L4):** One small trial in neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy combined ascorbic acid with ibuprofen, which confounds attribution.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links ascorbic acid to a number of conditions as contraindications, including kidney disease, anuria, nephrocalcinosis, primary hyperoxaluria, hereditary hemochromatosis, sickle cell anemia, diabetes mellitus, peptic ulcer disease, liver disease, hypertension, epilepsy, and hypercalcemia. These need verification against the official package insert.
- **Drug Interactions:** 25 interactions were identified. Moderate-level interactions include amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, lisdexamfetamine, metamfetamine, benzphetamine, aluminum hydroxide, bortezomib and deferoxamine. Others, such as paclitaxel, cisplatin, doxorubicin, pseudoephedrine, ephedrine, ethinylestradiol, flecainide, fluphenazine, indinavir and mexiletine, are minor.
- **Esophageal context:** Preclinical data suggest possible harm in reflux settings, which is relevant to the esophageal predictions.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, non-syndromic esophageal malformation, has a high model score but no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism, so it is supported by model prediction only (L5). Of the other candidates, "injury" (L2) is worth pursuing as a research question but is too broad and heterogeneous to act on directly. "Vitamin deficiency disorder" (L3) is essentially the existing indication and could proceed with guardrails, but it is not true repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore-registered products
- Mechanism of action data
- Narrowing "injury" to specific conditions (for example sepsis-induced lung injury, burns or cardiac surgery) and a condition-specific evidence review
- Reconciliation of the knowledge-graph contraindications (e.g. kidney disease, hyperoxaluria, hemochromatosis) with label information
- A decision on whether "vitamin deficiency disorder" should be treated as an on-label extension rather than repurposing
