# Rilpivirine: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Rilpivirine is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) already marketed for HIV-1 infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, an animal model of HIV-1.
Support comes from **0 clinical trials** and **4 preclinical or review publications**, so this is an indirect signal, not a distinct human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rilpivirine inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on known information, rilpivirine is an NNRTI, and its efficacy in HIV-1 has been established. Mechanistically, it may therefore act on closely related retroviruses.

SIV and SHIV (a chimeric SIV carrying the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase) infections in macaques are standard animal models of HIV-1. The predicted "new" indication is therefore largely the existing HIV-1 use seen through a research model. Macaque studies have tested long-acting rilpivirine, combined with cabotegravir, for treatment, remission and post-exposure prophylaxis. This supports the NNRTI mechanism and the long-acting formulation. It does not define a new human indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is an animal model of HIV-1, which rilpivirine already treats, not a distinct human disease. There are no registered clinical trials, and the supporting literature is preclinical or review-level (L4). Other HIV-related predictions in this pack, such as AIDS-related complex and congenital HIV, have trial evidence and may be better candidates for evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert data on warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- The approved indication text for each Singapore registration, to confirm the original indication
- A decision on whether to evaluate the HIV-related human indications (AIDS-related complex, congenital HIV) in place of the SIV model
