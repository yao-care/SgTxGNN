# Montelukast: From Asthma to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist, best known as a once-daily oral treatment for asthma and allergic airway disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, with **23 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved.
Most of that evidence covers related conditions (infant bronchiolitis, post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans, asthma). Only a few studies address bronchitis directly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the input. Based on known pharmacology, montelukast blocks the cysteinyl leukotriene 1 (CysLT1) receptor. This reduces leukotriene-driven airway inflammation, eosinophil recruitment, bronchoconstriction and mucus hypersecretion. These are the same processes that make it effective in asthma, and they overlap with some forms of bronchitis.

The link is strongest for **eosinophilic (non-asthmatic) bronchitis**, where airway eosinophilia drives chronic cough. It is also plausible for **recurrent obstructive bronchitis and post-viral wheezing in young children**. It is much weaker for typical infectious or neutrophilic bronchitis, which is less dependent on leukotrienes.

Many of the trials retrieved actually study *bronchiolitis* (infants) or *bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome* after transplantation. These are different diseases from bronchitis, so they support the mechanism only indirectly. Asthma is a licensed, well-established use of montelukast, so it is not a repurposing finding.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No warnings, contraindications or drug interaction records were available in the input.

Published papers in this evidence set do flag two points to review before any new use:
- **Neuropsychiatric events**: several papers discuss the US FDA boxed warning issued in 2020 on mental health adverse effects of montelukast (for example PMIDs 37758273, 39836401), with mixed findings in observational studies.
- **Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis**: case reports describe this after montelukast use (for example PMID 39959324).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Montelukast has a plausible mechanism in eosinophilic bronchitis and paediatric obstructive bronchitis, and two direct trials exist (NCT01121016, NCT04613180). However, both have unknown status and no results in the evidence pack. The larger trials address bronchiolitis or transplant-related airway disease rather than bronchitis. Package insert safety data is still missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore (HSA) package insert, for warnings, contraindications and interactions
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A defined target population: eosinophilic bronchitis in adults vs recurrent obstructive bronchitis in young children
- Published results or status updates for NCT01121016 and NCT04613180
- A plan to monitor for neuropsychiatric adverse events, especially in children
