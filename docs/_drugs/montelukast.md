# Montelukast: From Allergic Rhinitis and Asthma to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

> Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist, originally used for allergic rhinitis and asthma.
> The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, with **17 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved.
> However, most of this evidence concerns related conditions (bronchiolitis, bronchiolitis obliterans, eosinophilic bronchitis), not typical bronchitis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. The retrieved literature describes montelukast as a selective antagonist of the cysteinyl leukotriene 1 (CysLT1) receptor. By blocking this receptor, it reduces bronchoconstriction, airway swelling and eosinophilic inflammation. This explains its established use in asthma and allergic rhinitis.

Bronchitis and its related airway diseases share parts of this biology. Cysteinyl leukotrienes are released during RSV infection and contribute to airway inflammation. They are also implicated in eosinophilic airway disease that causes chronic cough. This gives a plausible mechanistic reason to test montelukast in bronchitis-type conditions.

The clinical evidence is not specific, though. "Bronchitis" is a broad term, and the studies found cover bronchiolitis in infants, bronchiolitis obliterans after transplant, non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis, and recurrent obstructive bronchitis in children. The data compiled for this report do not show clear benefit in ordinary acute or chronic bronchitis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from graph relationships, not from label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, anxiety, hypereosinophilic syndrome, cutaneous vasculitis.
- **Neuropsychiatric events**: The retrieved literature notes that the US FDA issued a boxed warning in 2020 on mental health adverse effects of montelukast. Several systematic reviews and cohort studies examine this risk, particularly in children, with some conflicting results. This is especially relevant here because many of the studied populations are infants and children.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The moderate-level interactions include dexamethasone, metronidazole, miconazole, nateglinide, nitisinone and troglitazone. Others, such as pantoprazole, omeprazole, metformin and morphine, have unclassified severity.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The one completed Phase 3 RCT is in RSV bronchiolitis, not bronchitis, and the other studies are small, early-phase or of unknown status. Package insert warnings and contraindications are also not yet available, so safety screening cannot proceed. For comparison, the same data set shows much stronger evidence for asthma (an existing labeled use), which is not a new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Define the target condition precisely (acute bronchitis, chronic bronchitis, eosinophilic bronchitis, or pediatric obstructive bronchitis), since the evidence differs for each
- Review the efficacy conclusions of the Cochrane review and the completed trials (NCT00076973, NCT00863317, NCT04613180) in full
- Obtain the Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- A neuropsychiatric safety monitoring plan, especially for children
