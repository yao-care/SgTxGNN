## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for ambrisentan: "Endothelin-1 (ET-1) is a potent autocrine and paracrine peptide. Two receptor subtypes, ET A and ET B, mediate the effects of ET-1 in the vascular smooth muscle and endothelium. […] Ambrisentan is a high-affinity (K i =0.011 nM) ET A receptor antagonist with a high selectivity for the ET A versus ET B receptor (>4000-fold)." ([DailyMed: Letairis (ambrisentan) prescribing information, Gilead Sciences, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=725d4e73-6c83-477a-adc6-0ae4a133a844), label version 2026-06-22; accessed 2026-11-19.) Ambrisentan is known as an endothelin type A receptor antagonist, which reduces vasoconstriction and vascular proliferation in the pulmonary circulation.

Pulmonary arteriovenous malformations (commonly seen in hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia, HHT) involve abnormal vascular remodelling, which is the plausible reason the model links the two. However, the only linked publication describes pulmonary arterial hypertension coexisting with HHT, not treatment of the malformation itself.

Endothelin blockade is not known to close or shrink such malformations, and vasodilation could theoretically worsen right-to-left shunting. The high score most likely reflects the shared pulmonary vascular and HHT-PAH context rather than a demonstrated therapeutic effect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Interaction screening returned 100 records. Moderate-level interactions include canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, eliglustat and naltrexone. Minor-level interactions include clarithromycin, miconazole and omeprazole. Others, such as metformin, simvastatin, prednisone and morphine, are of unknown severity.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity**: Ambrisentan carries an embryo-fetal toxicity warning, which is particularly relevant for any use in developmental or congenital conditions.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no trial directly studies this disease, and the single publication covers a related condition (PAH in HHT) rather than the malformation. Mechanistically, endothelin blockade is not known to benefit the malformation, and vasodilation could theoretically worsen shunting.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies that directly evaluate ambrisentan in pulmonary arteriovenous malformation
- Mechanism of action data and a safety assessment, particularly regarding shunting
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the regulator
- Clarification of the approved indication text for the local licences
