# Ambrisentan: From Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension to Pulmonary Arteriovenous Malformation

## One-Sentence Summary

Ambrisentan is an oral endothelin receptor antagonist, cited in the literature as approved for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary arteriovenous malformation**, but this direction currently rests on **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication**, a case report. The prediction is essentially unsupported by direct evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Ambrisentan is known from the literature to be a selective endothelin (ETA) receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in PAH has been demonstrated, and PAH is the condition it is used for.

The link to pulmonary arteriovenous malformation is indirect. The only retrieved paper describes PAH occurring in a patient with hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT), a genetic disease in which pulmonary arteriovenous malformations are common. That is PAH alongside the vascular disease, not treatment of the malformation itself. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects this PAH association.

Endothelin receptor blockade has no established effect on the structure of arteriovenous malformations or on their bleeding. The mechanistic case is therefore weak.

## Safety Considerations

- **Known ambrisentan concerns noted in the evidence review**: fluid retention, anemia, and hepatic and hematologic effects. Ambrisentan is teratogenic and contraindicated in pregnancy.

No drug-interaction records were found. For full warnings, contraindications and interactions, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no clinical trials and only one case report, which describes PAH co-occurring with HHT rather than a treatment effect on the malformation. There is no established mechanism linking endothelin blockade to arteriovenous malformations, so this is a model prediction only.

Other predicted indications for ambrisentan in the same evidence pack have much stronger support. These are PAH associated with connective tissue disease (Phase 2 and Phase 4 trials, plus meta-analyses) and PAH associated with congenital heart disease. Both are better candidates to prioritise.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- The approved indication as recorded in the Singapore licence
- Any direct evidence that ambrisentan affects pulmonary arteriovenous malformations, beyond PAH in HHT patients
