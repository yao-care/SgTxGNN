# Dextromethorphan: From Cough to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Dextromethorphan is a marketed cough suppressant (antitussive). This is general pharmacology, because the licence records supplied contain no indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but this rests on a model score alone.
The one registered trial appears to study major depressive disorder rather than a nasal condition, and there are **0 publications** for this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the supplied records. From general pharmacology, dextromethorphan is an NMDA receptor antagonist and sigma-1 receptor agonist that suppresses the cough reflex centrally. It is sold in Singapore mainly as cough elixirs, linctus, lozenges and tablets.

A link to nasal cavity disease is plausible only for symptoms such as cough or post-nasal drip. Suppressing cough would relieve a symptom and would not treat the underlying nasal disease. The very high TxGNN score (0.9998) is a model output, not clinical proof. It may reflect the drug's frequent co-formulation with other cough and cold ingredients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no evidence directly supports nasal cavity disease. The only listed trial is a recruiting Phase 3 study in major depressive disorder, and there is no literature. Any benefit would be symptomatic (cough or post-nasal drip) and not disease-modifying.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the target condition for NCT06958692, or identification of trials that actually study nasal cavity disease
- Literature search for dextromethorphan in upper-airway or nasal conditions
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved-indication text for the Singapore registrations
