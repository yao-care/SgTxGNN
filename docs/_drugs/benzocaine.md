## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, benzocaine is a topical ester local anesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, which reduces nerve signalling and produces a numbing effect.

In principle, a numbing effect could relieve the irritation of the ocular surface. However, this is only a conceivable symptomatic benefit, not a treatment of the underlying disease. No clinical or literature evidence was found to support it. The high model score is a computational prediction only.

There are also reasons for caution. Topical ocular use of benzocaine raises concerns about corneal toxicity and sensitization. Papillary conjunctivitis is often allergic or contact-related, so a topical anesthetic could itself act as a contact allergen and worsen the condition.
