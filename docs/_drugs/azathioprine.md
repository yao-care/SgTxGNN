# Azathioprine: From an Unrecorded Original Indication to Inflammatory Bowel Disease

> **Scope note:** The top-scoring prediction (rank 1) is a rare malformation syndrome with no supporting evidence. This report therefore leads with the best-evidenced predictions, **inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)** and **ulcerative colitis (UC)**. The other predictions are summarised at the end of the report.

## One-Sentence Summary

Azathioprine is an oral immunosuppressant that is marketed in Singapore, but its original approved indication is not recorded in the registration data.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **inflammatory bowel disease** (99.52%) and **ulcerative colitis** (99.33%).
**More than 40 clinical trials** and **20 publications** support the IBD prediction, including several completed Phase 3 randomised trials and Cochrane reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. From the pack's rationale, azathioprine is converted to 6-mercaptopurine and then to thioguanine nucleotides. These inhibit purine synthesis and induce T-cell apoptosis through Rac1 inhibition, which dampens the T-cell-driven mucosal inflammation seen in IBD.

IBD and UC are immune-mediated diseases, so the mechanism fits well. Thiopurines are a long-established IBD immunomodulator, and Cochrane reviews and meta-analyses address their use for maintaining remission in UC.

The empty original-indication field is a data gap. This prediction is better read as guideline-supported established use than as novel repurposing.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No warnings, contraindications, or drug-interaction records are available in the Evidence Pack.

The trial and literature evidence points to these risk areas:
- **Pharmacogenetics**: TPMT and NUDT15 variants are linked to thiopurine-induced leukopenia.
- **Myelosuppression and hepatotoxicity**: metabolite levels (6-TGN, 6-MMP) relate to efficacy and toxicity.
- **Infection and malignancy**: the pack recommends counselling on infection and lymphoma risk.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (IBD and UC only)

**Rationale:**
- Several completed Phase 3 randomised trials and Cochrane-level reviews support azathioprine in IBD and UC (evidence level L1).
- The prediction reflects established use more than novel repurposing.
- The other eight predictions are **Hold**, as detailed below.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to obtain the approved indications, warnings and contraindications (blocking gap).
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- A TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping and blood count/liver monitoring plan.
- Confirmation that the Singapore label covers IBD or UC, or an off-label use policy.
