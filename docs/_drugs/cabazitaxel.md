# Cabazitaxel: From Prostate Cancer (Taxane Chemotherapy) to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Cabazitaxel is a taxane chemotherapy drug, known in the literature for treating metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer after docetaxel.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, cabazitaxel is a taxane that stabilises microtubules and disrupts mitotic spindle dynamics, the same class mechanism as docetaxel and paclitaxel, both established in breast cancer.

Cabazitaxel is also a weak P-glycoprotein substrate. It may therefore retain activity in taxane-resistant tumours, and preclinical work shows it is less cross-resistant than paclitaxel and docetaxel in some resistant cell models.

The supplied literature is largely indirect: nanoparticle and lipid formulation studies in breast cancer cell lines and xenografts, resistance-mechanism studies, and pharmacology reviews. Only a few clinical studies are included (see below).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The breast cancer prediction is mechanistically plausible because taxanes are already established there, and a randomised phase II study (GENEVIEVE) exists. However, no registered trials are in the compiled data, the evidence is mostly preclinical or indirect, and the local licence record contains no approved indication or label safety text. Other predicted indications (sickle cell syndromes, HIV, hyperthyroidism, neuroblastoma, rheumatoid arthritis) have no supporting evidence and are not recommended for further consideration at this stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (HSA) warnings, contraindications and approved indication
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- Review of the GENEVIEVE phase II efficacy and safety results versus paclitaxel
- Search for registered phase II/III breast cancer trials of cabazitaxel
- Safety plan addressing myelosuppression, hepatic impairment and CYP3A-related interactions
