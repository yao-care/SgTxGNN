## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, cabazitaxel is a taxane that stabilises microtubules and disrupts mitotic spindle function, the same class mechanism as docetaxel and paclitaxel, which are established treatments in breast cancer.

Cabazitaxel is also a weak P-glycoprotein substrate. Laboratory work suggests it is less cross-resistant than docetaxel or paclitaxel in resistant cell models, including MCF-7 breast cancer cells. This makes it mechanistically plausible in taxane-pretreated disease.

Direct breast-cancer data come from the GENEVIEVE randomised phase II study (neoadjuvant cabazitaxel versus weekly paclitaxel in triple-negative or luminal B/HER2-negative breast cancer) and from a phase I/II study combining cabazitaxel with capecitabine in metastatic breast cancer. Most other items are formulation or cell/animal studies.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Randomised phase II data (GENEVIEVE and its long-term follow-up) directly study cabazitaxel in breast cancer, and the mechanism is plausible given the taxane class. However, no registered trials are in the compiled data, and the published results are early-phase or preclinical, so confirmatory evidence is lacking. The drug is already marketed in Singapore, with significant myelosuppression risk.
