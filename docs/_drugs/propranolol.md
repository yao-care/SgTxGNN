## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, propranolol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker. It lowers heart rate and myocardial oxygen demand, and has anti-angiogenic and vasoconstrictive effects in hemangioma. Its efficacy in cardiovascular conditions and infantile hemangioma is well established.

For distal myopathy, Tateyama type (a rare caveolin-3-related muscle disease), no plausible beta-adrenergic mechanism is evident. The high score of 0.994 appears to reflect graph-based proximity only. The same applies to the second-ranked prediction, congenital myopathy with excess of thin filaments.

Other candidates in the top 10 are more biologically plausible:

- **Intramuscular hemangioma, breast epithelioid hemangioma and breast capillary hemangioma** share the hemangioma mechanism. Propranolol is first-line therapy for infantile hemangioma, but intramuscular hemangioma is a typically non-involuting lesion, and the breast entities are distinct from infantile hemangioma. Evidence therefore does not transfer directly.
- **Cardiomyopathy** is largely an extension of the existing hypertrophic cardiomyopathy indication rather than true repurposing.
- **Cirrhotic cardiomyopathy** has mixed signals. One 2024 report suggests propranolol may correct prolonged QT, while 2020–2022 reports show blunted cardiovascular effects and possible circulatory and renal harm in advanced disease.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (disease-level associations, not package insert text), including:

- Respiratory and metabolic: bronchitis, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, potassium deficiency
- Organ disease: kidney disease, liver disease, hepatic encephalopathy and coma, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, anuria
- Neuromuscular: myasthenia gravis, myopathy, muscular disease, familial periodic paralysis
- Other: systemic lupus erythematosus, anxiety disorder, hyperparathyroidism, hypercalcemia

Of particular note, the knowledge graph lists **myopathy and muscular disease** as contraindications. This runs against the model's top predictions (distal myopathy and congenital myopathy).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

The top-ranked prediction, distal myopathy, Tateyama type, is supported only by a graph-based score, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. The knowledge graph also lists myopathy as a contraindication. Propranolol is widely marketed in Singapore (8 valid licences), but the better-supported candidates (intramuscular hemangioma, cardiomyopathy) rely on indirect evidence or extend existing indications, so they are better treated as research questions than as new-indication opportunities.
