# Carbidopa: From Parkinson Disease to Rasmussen Subacute Encephalitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Carbidopa is a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor, originally used together with levodopa to treat Parkinson disease and related parkinsonian disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Rasmussen subacute encephalitis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The evidence is model prediction only, so the top-ranked prediction should be treated as a hypothesis rather than a lead.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, carbidopa inhibits L-aromatic amino acid decarboxylase (DDC) outside the brain. It has no antiparkinson action by itself, but when combined with levodopa it raises the amount of levodopa that reaches the brain.

For Rasmussen subacute encephalitis, the link is weak. Rasmussen encephalitis is an immune-mediated, chronic focal encephalitis with epilepsy, and there is no established dopaminergic or decarboxylase-inhibition rationale connecting carbidopa to it. The prediction most likely reflects network proximity to parkinsonism and encephalitis nodes in the knowledge graph (for example, postencephalitic parkinsonism). The high score therefore should not be read as evidence of efficacy.

Other predictions in the top 10 have more plausible mechanistic stories, and they are listed below as alternatives:

## Safety Considerations

**Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text, so they need verification against the insert):
- **Neurological and psychiatric:** epilepsy, drug-induced dyskinesia, multiple system atrophy, psychotic disorders (including schizoaffective and schizophreniform disorders), anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, mental disorder
- **Cardiovascular:** orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
- **Hepatic, renal and gastrointestinal:** liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, gastric ulcer, peptic ulcer disease, diarrheal disease, pyoureter
- **Metabolic and other:** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hemoglobinopathy, melanoma, open-angle glaucoma

The epilepsy signal is worth noting for the top-ranked prediction, since Rasmussen encephalitis involves epilepsy.

**Drug interactions:** The only interaction record is pharmacological. Carbidopa inhibits its target, L-aromatic amino acid decarboxylase (DDC). No drug-drug interactions with severity levels were reported.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, Rasmussen subacute encephalitis, rests on a model score alone (L5). It has no trials, no literature, and no plausible mechanistic link to decarboxylase inhibition. The better-supported candidates (Lewy body dementia, MCT8-related syndromes, PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration) are still limited to reviews, case reports and small observational studies, and they are best treated as research questions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for carbidopa from DrugBank
- A reconciliation of the prediction list with the existing parkinsonism indications (for example, rank 7 is a synonym of an existing indication)
- For the most plausible alternatives (Lewy body dementia, MCT8 deficiency, PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration), a targeted review for controlled evidence and a check that the disease labels map correctly
- A route and formulation compatibility check, which has not yet been performed
