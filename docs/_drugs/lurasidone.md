# Lurasidone: From Atypical Antipsychotic Use to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Lurasidone is an atypical antipsychotic marketed in Singapore under the brand name Latuda.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**, with **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved. Most of this evidence covers bipolar I disorder in the depressive and maintenance phases, so **direct evidence for acute mania is still missing**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available from DrugBank. Based on general pharmacology, lurasidone blocks dopamine D2, serotonin 5-HT2A and 5-HT7 receptors and is a partial agonist at 5-HT1A. This receptor profile is typical of antipsychotics with mood-stabilising and antimanic activity, so applying it to a manic episode is mechanistically plausible.

Bipolar disorder and the psychiatric conditions that antipsychotics treat share dopamine and serotonin pathway abnormalities, which is probably why the model ranks this indication so highly. The trial record shows lurasidone has been studied extensively in bipolar I disorder, including adults, children and adolescents, and as an add-on to lithium or divalproex.

**Caveat:** the completed Phase 3 trials mainly address bipolar *depression*, *maintenance/recurrence prevention* and *cognition in euthymic patients*. None of the listed trials is a completed, mania-specific efficacy study. Antimanic efficacy should not be assumed from this evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Lurasidone has multiple completed Phase 3 trials in bipolar I disorder and is already marketed in Singapore, and its receptor pharmacology plausibly supports antimanic activity. However, the evidence mainly covers depressive, maintenance and cognitive endpoints rather than acute mania, so any move toward the manic indication should be tightly scoped.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, to complete the safety screening and confirm the currently approved indications
- Mania-specific efficacy evidence, such as a completed RCT in acute manic or mixed episodes, since the pediatric mania study (NCT01932541) was withdrawn
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank to replace the general-pharmacology reasoning
- Relevance grading of the trials and publications still marked pending

The other nine TxGNN predictions for this drug (ranks 2-10, e.g. inherited retinal and myopia disorders, hydranencephaly) are model-only (L5) with no trials, no supporting literature and no plausible mechanism. They are on **Hold**.
