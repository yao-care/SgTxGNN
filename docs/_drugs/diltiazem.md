## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Diltiazem is a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. Its calcium channel blockade causes vasodilation and lowers blood pressure, which could plausibly reduce vascular risk, including stroke risk.

The link between the original cardiovascular uses and this prediction is indirect. The predicted term is an obsolete ontology entry rather than a defined clinical condition. The high score (99.08%) most likely reflects graph proximity between diltiazem and cardiovascular or cerebrovascular concepts, not demonstrated treatment benefit. No trials or literature were retrieved to confirm a diltiazem-specific effect.

Among the other model predictions, the best-supported is **cerebrovascular disorder** (score 97.63%, evidence level L4, rank 4). Its evidence is limited to:
- Class-level precedent for calcium channel blockers in cerebral vasospasm.
- Animal studies from 1985 in monkeys and cats.
- Diltiazem–anticoagulant bleeding-interaction studies in atrial fibrillation. These are a safety signal, not efficacy evidence.

None of the retrieved clinical trials tested diltiazem for cerebrovascular outcomes.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on the model score. It targets an obsolete disease term, and no trials or literature support it. Diltiazem-specific evidence in cerebrovascular disease is limited to class-level and animal data. The best-documented clinical concern is the interaction with direct oral anticoagulants, which raises bleeding risk.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert (approved indications, warnings and contraindications).
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- A mapping of the obsolete term to a current, clinically defined stroke-related disease.
- Diltiazem-specific clinical or observational evidence for the mapped condition, which would move the candidate beyond L5.
- A review of bleeding risk with anticoagulants and of conduction-disorder cautions, if a cerebrovascular use is pursued.
