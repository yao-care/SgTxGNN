## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for chloroquine is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, chloroquine is an antimalarial that is also used as an antirheumatic agent. Its immunomodulatory effects include raising lysosomal pH, reducing antigen processing and presentation, lowering pro-inflammatory cytokine signalling and inhibiting Toll-like receptors.

Rheumatoid factor-positive polyarticular JIA resembles adult rheumatoid arthritis in its disease process. Chloroquine's proven use in adult RA therefore makes a mechanistic extrapolation plausible.

There is an important caveat. The only paper retrieved for this exact prediction concerns leflunomide, not chloroquine, so it does not test the hypothesis. Modern JIA care has also moved to conventional synthetic DMARDs and biologics.

Among the other predictions, only osteoarthritis has a registered trial involving chloroquine. This is a completed Phase 4 add-on study (NCT00805519, n=230). It is not a chloroquine-alone design, and no results were available.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (from knowledge graph associations, not label text):** epilepsy; porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disorders; liver disease; gallbladder disease; eye, retinal and vision disorders (including retinitis, scotoma and blindness); hearing disorders; psoriasis and parapsoriasis; neutropenia, agranulocytosis and leukopenia; anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia); G6PD deficiency; glycogen storage disease; cystinuria; galactosemia; substance abuse/dependence.

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found in the interaction database. Major-level examples include:
- Agalsidase beta
- Bupropion
- Cisapride
- Clarithromycin
- Dolasetron
- Granisetron

Moderate-level examples include antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate), H2 blockers (famotidine, cimetidine), sulfonylureas and insulin (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, insulin human), metronidazole, loperamide and aprepitant. Many of the major interactions involve QT-prolonging drugs, and antidiabetic drugs may need glucose monitoring.

**Other signals from the literature:** reports of chloroquine-associated cardiac toxicity (atrioventricular block), a QTc interval study, and vitiligo. These matter particularly in a paediatric population.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings, as label warnings were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (99.41%), and the mechanism is plausible given chloroquine's use in adult RA. However, there are no clinical trials, the only supporting paper concerns another drug, and the evidence level is L4. Cardiac, retinal and other safety concerns are significant in children. JIA also already has established, better-evidenced treatments.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for chloroquine from a drug database
- The HSA package insert, including warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Direct clinical evidence of chloroquine in JIA, such as a systematic review of antimalarials in JIA or a controlled study
- A paediatric safety assessment covering retinal and cardiac (QT/AV block) risk and dosing
- A comparison against current standard JIA therapies (methotrexate, biologics) to define any unmet need
