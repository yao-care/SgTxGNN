# Aspartic Acid: From Potassium Deficiency to Renal Tubular Acidosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Aspartic acid is a non-essential amino acid, originally used to treat potassium deficiency and widely present in parenteral nutrition solutions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **renal tubular acidosis**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and the **4 retrieved publications** do not test aspartic acid as a treatment.
This is a computational prediction with essentially no supporting clinical evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, aspartic acid is an amino acid involved in the urea cycle and in renal ammonia and glutamine metabolism, which is loosely related to renal acid-base handling. It has been used in potassium-related supplementation, and mechanistically this could be tenuously linked to electrolyte and acid-base disorders.

However, the link is weak. None of the retrieved papers tests aspartic acid as a therapy for renal tubular acidosis. They cover genetic diagnosis of distal RTA, SLC4A1 mutations, a pyruvate carboxylase deficiency case with RTA, and tubular ammonia production. Standard RTA treatment is alkali replacement (bicarbonate or citrate), and there is no rationale for aspartate replacing it. The high TxGNN score should be read as a model output, not as clinical support.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests solely on a model score. There are no clinical trials, and the literature is either unrelated or a single confounded case report. Standard RTA therapy (alkali replacement) is well established, and there is no mechanistic reason for aspartate to substitute for it. The knowledge-graph contraindication with kidney disease adds further caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the registered Singapore products (currently missing and blocking for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Preclinical or clinical evidence that aspartic acid affects acid-base handling in RTA
- Clarification of the approved indication text for the Singapore licences
