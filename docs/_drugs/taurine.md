# Taurine: From Parenteral Nutrition Component to Alcohol Withdrawal Delirium

## One-Sentence Summary

Taurine is an amino acid. In Singapore it is registered only as an ingredient of intravenous amino acid infusion products, and no approved indication is stated in the registration records.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **alcohol withdrawal delirium**.
The supporting evidence is thin: **1 clinical trial** (not testing taurine) and **7 publications** (none testing taurine for this condition).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Taurine is an inhibitory amino acid. It is reported to act at GABA-A and glycine receptors and to modulate glutamate excitotoxicity. This fits the neuronal over-excitation seen in alcohol withdrawal, when inhibitory GABA signalling drops and glutamate signalling rises.

The retrieved literature does not test taurine itself. It covers standard alcohol-use-disorder drugs and acamprosate, a taurine structural analog (calcium acetylhomotaurinate). The link to taurine is therefore by analogy only. The high TxGNN score is not backed by any taurine-specific clinical result.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a plausible mechanism (inhibitory neuromodulation) and indirect evidence from acamprosate, a taurine analog. No study has tested taurine for alcohol withdrawal delirium, and the only registered trial does not involve taurine. Withdrawal delirium is also a serious, potentially life-threatening condition with established treatments, so a supplement should not be positioned as a substitute.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information from the package inserts (warnings, contraindications), still missing.
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) to strengthen the mechanistic analysis.
- Taurine-specific preclinical or clinical evidence, such as an animal withdrawal model or a controlled trial of taurine alone.
- Route compatibility assessment. Singapore products are intravenous only, and the dosage form needed for this use is unclear.
- Among the other ranked predictions, migraine is the strongest candidate for a focused research question. It has several studies showing altered taurine levels in patients, but no controlled trial of taurine alone.
