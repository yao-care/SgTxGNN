# Doxorubicin: From Lymphoma and Leukaemia to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma and Langerhans cell histiocytosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**, a prediction that is already supported by **28 clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the data compiled for this report, including several randomised Phase 3 trials of regimens that contain doxorubicin.
Doxorubicin is already a backbone drug in standard Ewing sarcoma regimens, so this is better understood as confirmation of current practice than as a brand-new use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the drug record. In general, doxorubicin is a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator. It damages DNA and kills rapidly dividing tumour cells.

Its original indications are haematological cancers, in which doxorubicin is already a standard component of regimens such as CHOP and ABVD. Ewing sarcoma is a fast-growing bone and soft-tissue cancer that mainly affects children, adolescents and young adults. It is treated with multi-agent chemotherapy, and doxorubicin is a core drug in the VDC/IE and VAC/VAI regimens (vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide). Because both diseases respond to DNA-damaging chemotherapy, the model's prediction is mechanistically plausible.

An important caveat: the Phase 3 evidence evaluates whole regimens, not doxorubicin alone. The contribution of doxorubicin cannot be separated from the other agents.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not from label text):** liver failure and liver disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, heart disease (including cardiomyopathy, heart failure, heart conduction disease and sudden cardiac arrest), infectious disease and G6PD deficiency. Other graph entries (for example gallbladder disease, hyperbilirubinaemia and Gilbert syndrome) also appear. These require confirmation against the approved package insert.

**Cardiotoxicity:** the literature highlights anthracycline-related cardiotoxicity as a major limitation, especially in children and young adults (PMID 30231396). Dexrazoxane was used in some Ewing sarcoma trials as cardioprotection.

**Drug Interactions:** the interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. The main high-severity ones are:
- **Major:** dolasetron, cisapride, papaverine
- **Moderate (examples):** ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron, aprepitant, rolapitant, dexamethasone, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, famotidine, loperamide

Several of these are antiemetics and QT-prolonging agents that are commonly co-prescribed with chemotherapy, so concurrent use needs review.

Please also refer to the package insert for the full warnings, as label warning text was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed randomised Phase 3 trials and large literature reports support doxorubicin-containing regimens in Ewing sarcoma, which is why the evidence level is L1. However, those trials test whole regimens rather than doxorubicin alone, and the drug is already used in this setting. The cardiac and bone-marrow toxicity profile requires strict safeguards.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore Health Sciences Authority (HSA), which are currently missing and block the safety screening
- Approved-indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm whether Ewing sarcoma or sarcoma is already covered
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A cardiac monitoring plan with cumulative-dose limits and consideration of cardioprotection
- Confirmation of which regimen and dose would be used in Singapore practice

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** soft-tissue sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma carry L1 evidence (Phase 3 trials NCT00003052 and NCT00003212), with a Proceed with Guardrails recommendation, although those trials mainly involved adult soft-tissue sarcoma and doxorubicin is not part of the current standard regimens for paediatric rhabdomyosarcoma. Pleuropulmonary blastoma and chronic myelogenous leukaemia are research questions with limited or indirect evidence. The remaining predictions (primary pulmonary lymphoma, monocytic leukaemia, rare rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes, ganglioneuroblastoma and fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung) are on Hold because of very limited direct evidence.
