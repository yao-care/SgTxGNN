## Moclobemide: From Anxiety Disorder to Agoraphobia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A), originally used for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **agoraphobia** (the top-ranked prediction), supported by **no registered clinical trials** but **10 publications**, including 2 randomised controlled trials in panic disorder with agoraphobia.
This is a near-label extension of its existing anxiety use rather than a true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled record. Based on known pharmacology, moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of MAO-A (the compiled data also list MAO-B as a target). This raises serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine levels. The same monoaminergic mechanism is shared by antidepressants used in panic disorder with agoraphobia.

Agoraphobia usually occurs together with panic disorder, and anxiety disorders are already among moclobemide's original indications. Randomised trials in panic disorder (with or without agoraphobia) support the link. One placebo-controlled trial tested moclobemide, cognitive-behavioural therapy and their combination, and another compared moclobemide with clomipramine. The exact population of the first trial is not stated in its title, so its fit to agoraphobia is inferred.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned pharmacological target records only (MAO-A and MAO-B inhibition), not specific drug-drug interaction entries. The literature indicates moclobemide may raise exposure to triptans, and the wider MAO-inhibitor class carries tyramine and serotonergic interaction concerns.

Please refer to the package insert for other safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomised trials in panic disorder with or without agoraphobia, together with several reviews, support moclobemide in this area. However, there are no registered trials specific to agoraphobia, and moclobemide is already marketed for anxiety disorders, so this is a near-label extension.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (currently unavailable, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the agoraphobia-specific population and outcomes in the key RCT (PMID 10448444)
- A guardrail plan for serotonergic drug and tyramine interactions
