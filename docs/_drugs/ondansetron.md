## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Ondansetron is a selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist. Its efficacy against chemotherapy-induced and post-operative nausea and vomiting is well established.

5-HT3 receptors modulate dopamine transmission in the mesolimbic and striatal circuits implicated in tic generation. By blocking these receptors, ondansetron may dampen dopaminergic hyperactivity. This is the proposed link between an antiemetic and a tic disorder.

Clinical findings are consistent with this idea. A 1999 open-label pilot study, a 2005 randomized placebo-controlled trial, a 2025 RCT of high-dose ondansetron and a case report all examined it in Tourette syndrome or related conditions. One genetic study (2005) found no involvement of the HTR3A/HTR3B genes in Tourette syndrome. This weakly argues against a direct genetic basis, though it does not rule out a pharmacological effect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: Ondansetron has contraindication links to long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, liver failure, epilepsy, basal ganglia disease, focal hand dystonia and blepharospasm. Cardiac QT risk is especially important at the high doses (24 mg/day) used in the tic trials.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Examples rated **Major**:
  - Serotonergic agents: tramadol, sumatriptan, almotriptan, dextromethorphan
  - Opioids: fentanyl, alfentanil
  - QT-prolonging agents: amiodarone, amisulpride, amitriptyline, anagrelide

  Examples rated **Moderate**: codeine, dihydrocodeine, famotidine, formoterol, salbutamol, adenosine, alfuzosin, celecoxib, abiraterone and abametapir.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Tourette syndrome is the only predicted indication with meaningful evidence. It has a completed Phase 4 trial (n=110), a 2025 RCT and a 2005 placebo-controlled RCT. However, the available RCTs appear small or exploratory, no Phase 3 trial is documented, and the Phase 4 results are not in the compiled data. It is therefore graded L2 and treated as a research question rather than a ready-to-adopt use. The other nine predictions (including the top-ranked nephrogenic SIAD) are model predictions only (L5) and should be on Hold.
