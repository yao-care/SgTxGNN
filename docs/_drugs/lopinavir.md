## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Lopinavir is generally known as an HIV-1 protease inhibitor, a class that blocks the cleavage of viral polyproteins needed to make mature virus particles.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus related to HIV, so there is a theoretical basis for the prediction. However, FIV protease differs from HIV-1 protease in substrate specificity and in how sensitive it is to inhibitors, and HIV protease inhibitors have shown limited activity against FIV. The very high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases such as HIV/AIDS, not biological evidence of benefit in cats.

Two other predictions in the data are weaker. Simian immunodeficiency virus infection has only preclinical macaque studies, and it is an animal model rather than a human indication. A rare neurodevelopmental disorder has no identifiable mechanistic link at all.

For context, three preclinical macaque studies were found for the related prediction of simian immunodeficiency virus infection (not this disease): PMID [16973590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16973590/) (2006, viral decay under quadruple antiretroviral therapy), [12951220](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12951220/) (2003, oral HAART including lopinavir/ritonavir in SHIV-infected monkeys), and [17350308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17350308/) (2007, a SHIV construct for testing protease inhibitors). These do not count as evidence for the feline disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or literature, so the evidence level is L5. The mechanistic link is weak because HIV protease inhibitors have shown limited activity against FIV. It is also a veterinary condition, which adds little to the existing human HIV/AIDS use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct studies of lopinavir in FIV-infected cats, including in vitro protease inhibition data
- Mechanism of action data and package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing
- Confirmation of whether a veterinary indication falls within the repurposing scope
- A review of the cardiac, hepatic and metabolic safety signals and the large drug interaction burden
