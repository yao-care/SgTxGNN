## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Roxithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic. Macrolides inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit. Detailed mechanism of action data is not otherwise available in the data compiled for this report. Its in vitro antibacterial activity is similar to erythromycin, mainly against Gram-positive bacteria and some Gram-negative bacteria such as *Legionella pneumophila*.

Leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, so an antibacterial agent is a biologically sensible candidate. Mouse footpad studies (1991) found roxithromycin and clarithromycin consistently active and bactericidal against *M. leprae*, while erythromycin and azithromycin were inactive. Clarithromycin was superior to roxithromycin, partly because of higher drug levels at the infection site. A Japanese review (1999) also notes anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects of roxithromycin, which could matter for leprosy-related nerve inflammation.

The plausibility is therefore good, but roxithromycin appears less potent than clarithromycin, which is already the macrolide used in leprosy regimens. No roxithromycin-specific human data is available.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Pharmacology data lists the motilin receptor (MLNR) as a target of roxithromycin, consistent with the prokinetic activity of macrolides. A 2023 in vitro study also found that macrolides and ketolides inhibited the cytotoxic effect of trastuzumab emtansine in HER2-positive breast cancer cells, suggesting a potential drug-antibody-drug conjugate interaction that deserves caution.

Please refer to the package insert for other safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The leprosy prediction is biologically plausible and supported by preclinical studies (in vitro and mouse). However, there are no clinical trials or human data for roxithromycin, and clarithromycin was more active and is already used in leprosy regimens. Evidence is insufficient to advance this as a treatment candidate, so it is best treated as a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Roxithromycin-specific clinical or comparative data against clarithromycin in leprosy
- Evaluation of whether roxithromycin adds value over existing leprosy regimens
