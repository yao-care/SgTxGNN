# Turoctocog alfa: From Hemophilia to Primary Release Disorder of Platelets

## One-Sentence Summary

Turoctocog alfa is a recombinant human factor VIII (B-domain truncated) product, originally used to treat **hemophilia**. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary release disorder of platelets**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction. The prediction rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, turoctocog alfa is a recombinant factor VIII that replaces the missing FVIII activity in the intrinsic coagulation pathway. Its efficacy in hemophilia comes from correcting that specific deficiency.

Primary platelet release disorders arise from defects in platelet granule secretion or signalling, not from FVIII deficiency. There is therefore no clear mechanistic rationale for this prediction. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects shared "bleeding disorder" neighbours in the knowledge graph and is not clinical support.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no registered clinical trials and no literature, so it sits at evidence level L5. Primary platelet release disorders are platelet-function defects, not FVIII deficiencies, so there is no supporting mechanistic rationale. The same applies to most other predicted indications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website (currently missing, and a blocker for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
- A focused literature review on acquired coagulation factor deficiency (rank 5), the only candidate with a plausible mechanistic link, with attention to inhibitor-driven cases
- Verification of the "flood factor deficiency" ontology entry
