# Esomeprazole: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Esomeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor originally used for acid-related conditions such as peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **duodenogastric reflux**, but this top-ranked prediction is supported by **0 clinical trials** and only **1 general review**.
The strongest evidence in this candidate set is for the closely related, already-established indication of **duodenal ulcer**, which has **more than 30 registered trials** and **19 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, esomeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor. It blocks the gastric H+/K+ ATPase and strongly suppresses gastric acid. Its efficacy in acid-related disease is well established.

Duodenogastric reflux is the backflow of duodenal contents (bile and pancreatic juice) into the stomach. Acid suppression may reduce the mucosal injury and symptoms that refluxed material causes. However, bile and pancreatic reflux is largely acid-independent, so any benefit is probably symptomatic and partial. The only supporting source is a general PPI review (PMID 18679668), which has no disease-specific data. This prediction is therefore plausible but unproven.

By contrast, the prediction for **duodenal ulcer** is effectively an established use, close to the original label. It is supported directly by completed Phase 3 trials and is covered in the evidence sections below.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, duodenogastric reflux)

**Rationale:**
- Duodenogastric reflux has no trials and only one general PPI review, and the mechanism is only partly plausible because bile reflux is largely acid-independent.
- The closely related **duodenal ulcer** and **active peptic ulcer disease** predictions each have a "Proceed with Guardrails" rating. Their evidence is strong (several completed Phase 3 trials), but they are established uses, not new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Disease-specific studies of esomeprazole in duodenogastric reflux, such as symptom and endoscopic or histological outcomes in a controlled trial
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- For the ulcer indications, guardrails: confirm H. pylori status, review CYP2C19 and clopidogrel interactions, and limit therapy duration
