## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on its drug class, undecylenic acid is a fatty-acid antifungal that is thought to disrupt fungal cell membranes and inhibit hyphal morphogenesis. Tinea cruris is a superficial infection of the groin skin caused by dermatophytes, so this mechanism is plausible against it. Topical access to the infected skin is also reasonable.

The registration data does not state an approved indication. Its use for superficial fungal skin infections is inferred from the drug class and the topical cream product, and **must be confirmed against the HSA label**.

Among the other predicted conditions, superficial ones such as pityriasis versicolor, tinea manuum and cutaneous candidiasis are also mechanistically plausible. Deep or hair-involving infections (Majocchi granuloma, tinea profunda, endothrix/ectothrix, scalp or beard tinea) usually need systemic therapy, and a topical fatty acid is unlikely to reach the infection site. The literature retrieved for scalp/beard dermatophytosis consists of keyword matches on "beard" (hair transplantation, zebrafish neurons and the like), and none addresses undecylenic acid. It should be treated as noise, not evidence.
