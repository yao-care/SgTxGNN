## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, undecylenic acid is a topical antifungal fatty acid used on dermatophyte infections of the skin, such as tinea pedis and tinea corporis. The scores come from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

Ectothrix infections are dermatophyte infections of the hair (for example *Microsporum* and *Trichophyton* species in tinea capitis), in which fungal spores form a sheath outside the hair shaft. The link is plausible because the causative organisms are shared with skin dermatophytosis.

However, the extrapolation is weak. Hair-shaft and follicular infections generally need systemic therapy because topical agents penetrate the follicle poorly. The high score (0.9908) is a computational prediction and has no clinical corroboration in the data compiled for this report.

Two other predictions were also generated:
- **Endothrix infectious disease** (score 0.9903): fungal growth is inside the hair shaft, so topical fatty-acid antifungals are even less likely to reach the organism. Standard care is systemic (e.g., griseofulvin or terbinafine).
- **Pityriasis versicolor** (score 0.9901): a superficial *Malassezia* yeast infection of the skin. It is the most tractable of the three because the topical route fits the infection site, but established topical azoles and selenium sulfide are well-validated comparators.
