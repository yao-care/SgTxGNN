# Undecylenic Acid: From Tinea Pedis and Tinea Corporis to Dermatophytosis of the Groin and Perianal Area

## One-Sentence Summary

Undecylenic acid is a topical fatty-acid antifungal, originally used for tinea pedis, tinea corporis and other tinea infections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatophytosis of the groin and perianal area (tinea cruris)**.
There are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** specific to this site, so the support is model prediction and mechanistic reasoning only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, undecylenic acid is a topical fatty-acid antifungal. Its efficacy in tinea pedis and tinea corporis is the basis of its original use, and mechanistically it may be applicable to other dermatophyte infections of the skin.

Tinea cruris is a dermatophyte infection of the same class as the original indications. The causative organisms and the superficial skin location are very similar, so activity in the groin is plausible by extension. The very high TxGNN score (0.999) most likely reflects this close similarity to the approved indications. It is not independent clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, tinea cruris, is mechanistically reasonable because it is a dermatophyte infection of the same type as the original indications. However, no trials or site-specific publications support it, and the high TxGNN score probably reflects similarity to the approved indications rather than new evidence. The deeper or hair-based infections in the list are unlikely to respond to a topical fatty acid.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert for the Singapore product (VETIC CREAM), to confirm the approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data for undecylenic acid
- Site-specific efficacy data for tinea cruris, ideally from controlled trials
- Confirmation of whether tinea cruris is already covered by the current labelled use ("tinea infection"), which would reduce the added value of a repurposing claim
- Direct evidence for any other candidate indication, such as cutaneous candidiasis or tinea manuum, before considering it further
