## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (DrugBank query pending — see Data Gap DG002). Based on known information, undecylenic acid is a long-chain unsaturated fatty acid with fungistatic activity. It has historically been used in topical antifungal preparations for conditions such as tinea pedis and tinea cruris, where it is thought to inhibit dermatophyte cell membrane synthesis and interfere with fungal morphogenesis.

The predicted new indication — dermatophytosis of groin and perianal area — belongs to the same disease family (superficial dermatophyte infection) as the drug's traditional use. Mechanistically, this is a coherent extension rather than a novel therapeutic hypothesis: both the historical use case and the predicted indication involve superficial skin fungal infection, and the drug's known fatty-acid antifungal action would be expected to apply similarly. However, this rationale is currently based on class-level pharmacological plausibility rather than any direct clinical or literature evidence specific to this indication.

It is worth noting that of the 10 TxGNN-predicted indications reviewed, most (ranks 2–10) scored lower confidence, are staged at S0 ("Hold"), and lack any supporting evidence. A broader literature search across related fungal indications (e.g., "dermatophytosis of scalp or beard," "cutaneous candidiasis") returned mostly false-positive keyword matches (e.g., "beard" matching beard-reconstruction surgery papers) rather than substantive pharmacological evidence, reinforcing that the current evidence base for this drug's repurposing potential remains preclinical/mechanistic only.
