# Botulinum Toxin Type A: From Neuromuscular Indications to Parkinson Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

> Botulinum toxin type A is a neurotoxin that blocks acetylcholine release at nerve endings, and it is registered in Singapore as Botox (an injectable powder).
> The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Parkinson disease** (symptom management), the strongest-supported of its top predictions,
> with **6 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Evidence points to relief of specific symptoms, not disease modification.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. From general pharmacology, botulinum toxin A blocks release of acetylcholine from presynaptic terminals (via the SNARE protein complex) at neuromuscular junctions and autonomic cholinergic synapses. This produces a local, temporary reduction in muscle activity and glandular secretion.

That mechanism plausibly addresses several focal, symptom-level problems in Parkinson disease:

- **Sialorrhea (drooling)**, through cholinergic innervation of the salivary glands
- **Limb tremor** and **dystonia-associated pain**, through reduced overactive muscle activity
- **Overactive bladder** and possibly constipation, through autonomic effects

It does not act on dopaminergic neurodegeneration, so any benefit is symptomatic rather than disease-modifying. For the glaucoma, insomnia and bronchitis predictions, the data contain no supported mechanistic link.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug interactions:** 41 interactions were identified; the 20 shown in the data compiled for this report include:

- **Major:** aminoglycosides and related antibiotics (kanamycin, neomycin, streptomycin) and polymyxin B, which may add to neuromuscular blockade
- **Moderate:** anticholinergic agents (atropine, glycopyrronium, hyoscyamine, scopolamine, dicyclomine, trospium and others) and sedating antihistamines (promethazine, brompheniramine, carbinoxamine and others)

For use in Parkinson disease, the symptom-management rationale also calls for monitoring for dysphagia and spread of toxin effect beyond the injection site. Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available, so please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (for Parkinson disease symptom management only)

**Rationale:**
- One completed Phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trial (sialorrhea, n=184), supported by several randomised and open-label studies and reviews, supports symptom-level use in Parkinson disease. The other trials are small, terminated or of unknown status, and the evidence does not support disease modification.
- The glaucoma, insomnia, bronchitis, juvenile parkinsonism and autosomal recessive Parkinson disease predictions lack supporting evidence and should remain on **Hold**. Restless legs syndrome is a research question only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- The Singapore approved indication text and a confirmed original indication
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the NCT02091739 results and its sialorrhea endpoint
- A guardrail plan covering injection site and dose limits, monitoring for dysphagia and toxin spread, and review of interacting medicines (aminoglycosides, polymyxin B, anticholinergics)
