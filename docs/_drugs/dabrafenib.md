# Dabrafenib: From BRAF-Mutant Melanoma to Choroideremia

## One-Sentence Summary

Dabrafenib is a BRAF kinase inhibitor, marketed in Singapore as Tafinlar and used for BRAF-mutant melanoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **choroideremia**, an inherited retinal degeneration.
**No clinical trials** and **no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, dabrafenib is a BRAF V600 kinase inhibitor. Its efficacy in BRAF-mutant melanoma is established, and it acts by blocking MAPK-pathway signalling in tumour cells.

The link to choroideremia is weak. Choroideremia is an X-linked retinal degeneration caused by loss of CHM/REP1 function. It is not driven by BRAF activation, so a BRAF inhibitor has no plausible mechanistic connection to it. The high score looks like a graph-based artefact rather than a biologically grounded signal. No trial or paper supports it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or literature (L5), and choroideremia has no plausible link to BRAF inhibition. Other predicted indications for dabrafenib have far stronger support. Non-cutaneous melanoma (rank 2) has completed Phase 2 trials in BRAF-mutant melanoma (evidence level L2) and is the more credible research direction.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank and a biological rationale connecting BRAF/MAPK signalling to CHM/REP1 loss
- Preclinical evidence (for example retinal cell or animal models) supporting any benefit
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are required for safety screening
- A safety assessment for a non-oncology, retinal-disease population
