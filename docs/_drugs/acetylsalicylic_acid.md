# Acetylsalicylic Acid (Aspirin): From Established Analgesic and Antiplatelet Use to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Aspirin is a long-established COX inhibitor used as an analgesic and antiplatelet drug. The Singapore registration records do not state its approved indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and **19 publications** on this direction. None of these publications tests aspirin in this specific subtype, so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aspirin irreversibly inhibits COX-1 and COX-2, which reduces prostaglandin and thromboxane A2 signaling and platelet aggregation. This may lessen neurogenic inflammation and platelet-related microembolic or vascular contributions to migraine aura. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in DrugBank, so this reasoning comes from known pharmacology.

Aspirin is already an established acute migraine treatment. The 2015 American Headache Society evidence assessment covers acute migraine pharmacotherapies. A 1999 randomized double-blind trial compared intravenous aspirin lysinate with subcutaneous sumatriptan and placebo in acute migraine.

For aura-related migraine specifically, support comes from a retrospective study of low-dose aspirin for preventing migraine with aura (203 patients, 95 on aspirin) and a 2025 systematic review of antithrombotics for migraine prevention. No evidence specific to the brainstem aura subtype was found. This prediction is therefore an extrapolation from migraine with aura in general.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is indirect (L3): aspirin has supporting data in acute migraine and in migraine-with-aura prevention, but nothing specific to the brainstem aura subtype and no registered trials. The safety data are also missing. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough to move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of approved indications for the Singapore licences
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Evidence specific to brainstem aura, such as subtype-stratified analyses of the aspirin-in-aura studies or a prospective study
- A bleeding-risk assessment, especially with concurrent anticoagulants or dual antiplatelet therapy, and a dose and duration plan
