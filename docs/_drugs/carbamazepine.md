# Carbamazepine: From Epilepsy and Trigeminal Neuralgia to Trigeminal Nerve Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Carbamazepine is a sodium channel–blocking antiseizure drug, originally used for epilepsy, bipolar disorder and trigeminal neuralgia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **trigeminal nerve neoplasm** (score 99.998%), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the 20 publications retrieved are mostly case reports and reviews of tumour-related neuralgia. Evidence is **model prediction only (L5)**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, carbamazepine is an established treatment for trigeminal neuralgia and is understood to act by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels, which suppresses abnormal neuronal firing.

The link to the predicted indication is weak. The retrieved literature concerns trigeminal neuralgia **caused by** tumours or lesions (schwannoma, meningioma, lipoma, dermoid cyst, lymphoma, melanoma, pituitary adenoma). In these cases carbamazepine can at most relieve pain symptomatically. Several reports describe patients whose pain was not controlled by carbamazepine, which in some cases led to the discovery of the tumour. There is no evidence that it treats the neoplasm itself. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the closeness of "trigeminal nerve neoplasm" to "trigeminal neuralgia" in the knowledge graph rather than a true anti-tumour effect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and risk conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** the following conditions are linked to carbamazepine as contraindicated or high-risk:
  - Blood disorders: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, and congenital neutropenia syndromes.
  - Cardiac conditions: atrioventricular block, heart conduction disease, coronary artery disease, and sudden cardiac arrest.
  - Organ disease: liver disease, kidney disease and impaired renal function, and gallbladder disease.
  - Other: glaucoma and ocular hypertension, inappropriate ADH syndrome, absence epilepsy, and anxiety or dysthymic disorders.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major interactions include **clarithromycin** and **eliglustat**. Moderate interactions include doxycycline, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone), vitamin D analogues, pioglitazone, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, metronidazole, cimetidine, canagliflozin and activated charcoal.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on knowledge-graph proximity alone. The literature shows carbamazepine only as symptomatic pain treatment in tumour-associated neuralgia and often as ineffective. There are no trials, and no evidence of an effect on the tumour. The drug also carries notable haematological, cardiac and interaction risks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore licences. This gap blocks safety screening.
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank).
- Evidence of a direct anti-tumour effect, or a clear reframing of the question as symptomatic pain control in tumour-related trigeminal neuralgia. That use is already covered by the existing trigeminal neuralgia indication.
