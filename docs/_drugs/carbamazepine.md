## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for carbamazepine are not available in the data compiled for this report. Carbamazepine is generally known to act by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels, which reduces abnormal nerve firing. This is the basis for its use in nerve-pain conditions such as trigeminal neuralgia.

The retrieved publications mostly describe trigeminal neuralgia that is *caused by* a tumour or other lesion (schwannoma, meningioma, lipoma, dermoid cyst, lymphoma, melanoma, pituitary adenoma). In these reports carbamazepine was used for pain relief, and in several cases it failed or was poorly tolerated, which led to surgery or to the discovery of the lesion. Nothing indicates that carbamazepine acts on the tumour itself.

The high TxGNN score therefore most likely reflects the closeness of "trigeminal nerve neoplasm" to trigeminal neuralgia in the model's training data, not evidence of anti-tumour activity. At best, any benefit would be symptomatic pain control.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no clinical trials exist and no cited study shows that carbamazepine treats trigeminal nerve neoplasm itself. The literature concerns tumour-related neuralgia, where carbamazepine offers only symptomatic relief and was often ineffective. Evidence is therefore at level L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Evidence that carbamazepine has an anti-tumour effect on nerve-sheath or trigeminal tumours (preclinical or clinical), as opposed to symptomatic pain relief
- Mechanism-of-action data linking carbamazepine to the tumour biology
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator, since these are currently missing
- Clarification of whether the intended use is tumour treatment or pain control in patients with tumour-related neuralgia
