# Clofarabine: From Relapsed/Refractory Pediatric Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia to Myeloid Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Clofarabine is a purine nucleoside analog whose established use, per the US FDA labeling quoted in the trial records, is relapsed or refractory acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) in children.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**, with **50 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the evidence pack.
Only one clearly Phase 3 randomized trial is confirmed (AML08, in children), so the evidence is strong but not yet at the top tier.

**Evidence level note:** L1 requires at least 2 completed Phase 3 RCTs. Only NCT00703820 (AML08, Phase 3, n=324) is clearly Phase 3. NCT00454480 (Phase 2/3, n=2000) is a large older-AML program, but the clofarabine randomization is not confirmed from the trial title. I therefore rate this L2, and it moves to L1 if that randomization is confirmed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the DrugBank field of the submitted data. The published literature does describe it. Clofarabine is a second-generation purine nucleoside analog. Its active triphosphate inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and DNA polymerases, which depletes the nucleotide pools needed for DNA synthesis. It also disrupts mitochondrial membranes and triggers apoptosis. It was designed to combine the best features of fludarabine and cladribine, with better resistance to enzymatic breakdown.

Lymphoblastic and myeloid leukemias are both fast-dividing blood cancers that depend on DNA replication. A drug proven in relapsed ALL is therefore plausibly active in myeloid blasts. The clinical program supports this. Single-agent and combination trials cover adult and pediatric AML, and clofarabine has been used in transplant conditioning regimens.

The benefit reported so far is mainly a higher response rate, without a clear survival gain. Use is therefore best confined to relapsed/refractory disease or patients unfit for intensive chemotherapy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is credible. Completed Phase 2 trials, one Phase 3 pediatric RCT (AML08) and a large Phase 2/3 program support activity in myeloid leukemia. The benefit is mainly higher response rates rather than proven survival gain, so use should stay in relapsed/refractory or unfit populations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications. This is a blocking gap, and safety screening cannot pass without it.
- The approved indication text of SIN14026P, to confirm the Singapore label status.
- Confirmation that NCT00454480 randomized clofarabine, which would raise the evidence to L1.
- DrugBank mechanism of action data.
- A monitoring plan for hepatic and renal toxicity, capillary leak and myelosuppression, and a comparison against current standard AML options.
