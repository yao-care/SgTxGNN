# Clofarabine: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia to Myeloid Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Clofarabine is a purine nucleoside analogue chemotherapy, originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, clofarabine is a second-generation purine nucleoside analogue. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and DNA polymerase, depleting the building blocks for DNA synthesis, and it induces apoptosis. These actions target rapidly dividing cells.

Lymphoid and myeloid leukemic blasts are both rapidly proliferating and both depend on DNA synthesis. This is why a drug proven in ALL may also work in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Published reviews describe single-agent activity in AML that compares favorably with standard agents. Clofarabine has also been combined with cytarabine and other drugs in many AML trials and used in transplant conditioning. This mechanistic link rests on class pharmacology rather than recorded MOA data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomised trials (NCT02085408, n=727; NCT00317642, n=326) plus many Phase 2 studies support clofarabine in AML, which meets the L1 level. However, the data gathered here do not show whether these trials met their primary endpoints, and no summary results were retrieved. The drug is highly myelosuppressive and has organ-toxicity signals, so use needs strict safeguards.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review the published results of NCT02085408 and NCT00317642 (efficacy and survival outcomes)
- Obtain and review the HSA package insert, including approved indication, warnings and contraindications
- Confirm the mechanism of action from DrugBank
- Define a toxicity monitoring plan (blood counts, liver and kidney function) and an interaction check for concomitant drugs
- Clarify the Singapore-approved indication versus the proposed use
