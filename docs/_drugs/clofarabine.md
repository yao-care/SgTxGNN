## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not recorded in the data compiled for this report. The published reviews do describe how clofarabine works. It is a second-generation purine nucleoside analog designed to combine the best qualities of fludarabine and cladribine. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and DNA polymerase, which depletes the nucleotides needed for DNA replication. It also is incorporated into DNA and induces apoptosis.

These actions target rapidly dividing blast cells. Lymphoid and myeloid leukemic blasts share this vulnerability, so activity in myeloid leukemia is mechanistically plausible.

The clinical record supports this: clofarabine has been studied alone and in combination with cytarabine, idarubicin, and other agents in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). It has also been studied as part of conditioning regimens before stem cell transplant.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. All 20 shown in the data are rated Moderate. They include amphotericin B (several formulations), clarithromycin, levofloxacin, kanamycin, neomycin, minocycline, mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, pioglitazone, acarbose, bupropion, naltrexone, and nitisinone. Many of these are nephrotoxic or hepatotoxic agents or anti-infectives commonly used in neutropenic patients, so co-medication needs review.
- **Kidney toxicity**: A published case report describes clofarabine-induced kidney toxicity.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (n=727 and n=326) and many Phase 1/2 studies directly address clofarabine in myeloid leukemia, which supports an L1 evidence level. The drug is already marketed in Singapore as a single-ingredient product. However, the published literature is mostly reviews, and the safety profile of this cytotoxic drug demands careful controls.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review the published results of the two Phase 3 trials (NCT02085408, NCT00317642) for efficacy, survival, and toxicity outcomes in myeloid leukemia
- Obtain the HSA package insert to confirm warnings, contraindications, and the local approved indication text

- Define a safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, renal and hepatic function, and interacting co-medications (especially nephrotoxic agents)
