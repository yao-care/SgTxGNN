## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for this drug are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, camphor is a topical counter-irritant that modulates TRP channels (it activates and then desensitises TRPV1, TRPV3 and TRPA1). This is a plausible but indirect link to the trigeminovascular pain pathways involved in migraine.

Some indirect support exists. A 2026 preclinical animal study (PMID 42849823) reported that natural (+)-camphor alleviated reserpine-induced migraine with local cerebral vasospasm, via the p38β MAPK/ATF2 pathway. Camphor is also a constituent of feverfew (*Tanacetum parthenium*), a traditional migraine herb. However, the papers on feverfew only describe chemistry or anticancer activity and do not show clinical benefit.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence. Two case reports, in fact, describe cluster headache **linked to** toothpastes containing camphor and eucalyptus oils, which points in the opposite direction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials for camphor in migraine, and the available literature consists of case reports and one animal study, none showing human efficacy. The only human reports link camphor-containing products with headache. The prediction is best treated as a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert for the Singapore licence (SIN03627P), to confirm the approved indication, warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank), to support the TRP channel hypothesis
- Further preclinical work confirming the 2026 animal findings, and review of the full text of that study
- Assessment of route compatibility, since the marketed product is a topical ointment and migraine would likely need a different delivery approach
- A safety assessment covering neurotoxicity and seizure risk, and the headache-triggering reports
