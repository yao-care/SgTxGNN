# Camphor: From Topical Counterirritant to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Camphor is a topical counterirritant, and the only Singapore-registered product in this pack is an ointment. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **Migraine Disorder**, but **0 clinical trials** and **no camphor efficacy publications** support this. The prediction is model-only, and the retrieved literature actually points to a safety concern.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, camphor is a topical counterirritant that acts on TRP channels: it desensitizes TRPV1 and activates TRPV3 and TRPA1. These pathways are involved in trigeminovascular pain signalling, which is the main reason a link to migraine is plausible.

This link is unproven. No study in the pack tests camphor for migraine. The very high TxGNN score is a graph-based prediction, not clinical evidence.

There is also a safety problem. Camphor is pro-convulsant and neurotoxic when ingested. Any evaluation would have to be limited to topical or aromatic use.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The literature above also raises a concern that the package insert should be checked against. Camphor-containing essential oils are described as pro-convulsant, and they were temporally linked to cluster headache in case reports. No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but it rests on the model alone. No trials exist, no paper tests camphor for migraine, and the available reports suggest camphor may provoke headache rather than relieve it. The neurotoxicity of ingested camphor further limits how it could be studied.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank), to test the TRP-channel hypothesis
- Preclinical or early human data on topical or aromatic camphor in migraine models
- A review of the headache case reports, to judge whether camphor exposure is a trigger

**Other predictions in the pack:** The other nine ranked predictions are also weak.
- **Pulmonary hypertension:** The 36 trials and 20 papers are artifacts. "CAMPHOR" there is the Cambridge Pulmonary Hypertension Outcome Review questionnaire, not the drug, so this count is not clinical evidence.
- **Erectile dysfunction:** The one paper is a rat oral toxicity study, not efficacy evidence.
- **Others** (Raynaud disease, migraine with brainstem aura, Tourette syndrome, and the rest): These have no supporting studies. Some are also unfavorable given camphor's seizure-provoking toxicity.
