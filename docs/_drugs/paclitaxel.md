# Paclitaxel: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Paclitaxel is a microtubule-stabilizing taxane chemotherapy, originally used for ovarian cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,
with **40 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Paclitaxel is already an established standard-of-care taxane in breast cancer, so this is closer to a label-adjacent indication than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, paclitaxel stabilizes microtubules and blocks disassembly of the mitotic spindle. This causes G2/M arrest and apoptosis in rapidly dividing tumour cells.

This mechanism does not depend on hormone-receptor signalling. It therefore applies to ovarian cancer and to breast cancer alike, including ER-negative and triple-negative disease, where chemotherapy is a mainstay. Older reviews (1997–2002) describe paclitaxel and docetaxel as evidence-based components of therapy for advanced breast and ovarian carcinoma, and paclitaxel was licensed in the USA for adjuvant treatment of node-positive early breast cancer.

Other breast-related predictions in the same ranking show a similar pattern. ER-negative disease and ER-positive disease (mainly high-risk or endocrine-resistant settings) have the strongest support. Hormone-resistant disease has moderate support. Bilateral and nipple carcinoma rest on case reports only. Ehrlich tumour is a mouse model, not a human indication.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for these.

- **Disease contraindications from the knowledge graph (not label text)**: These include neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, liver disease, kidney disease, peripheral neuropathy, myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac arrest, asthma, diabetes mellitus and hyperbilirubinaemia. They should be treated as signals needing verification against the label.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interactions in total. The first 20 listed are mostly moderate: aprepitant, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dexamethasone, eliglustat, metronidazole, miconazole, rolapitant, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, tinidazole and troglitazone. Ascorbic acid and levofloxacin are minor. Glimepiride, doxycycline, morphine, metformin and omeprazole are of unknown severity.
- **Reported adverse events in the literature**: These include paclitaxel-induced pneumonitis, uveitis, corneal epithelial lesions, taxane-induced polyneuropathy and chemotherapy-related amenorrhoea.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Paclitaxel is already a standard taxane in breast cancer. Multiple Phase 2 trials and several completed Phase 3 trials use it as a core component, and it is marketed in Singapore in 14 registrations. The evidence does not isolate paclitaxel's own effect in the Phase 3 studies, and the supporting reviews are old. The recommendation is therefore to proceed with guardrails, not a full Go.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications on Singapore labels, which are currently blank in the data
- Verification of the knowledge-graph contraindications against the label, especially for patients with baseline neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, hepatic impairment or neuropathy
- Subtype-specific guidance, since endocrine therapy plus CDK4/6 inhibition is often preferred over chemotherapy in ER-positive disease
- A monitoring plan for myelosuppression, neuropathy and infusion hypersensitivity
