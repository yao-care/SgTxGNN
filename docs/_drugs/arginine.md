# Arginine: From Parenteral Nutrition Amino Acid Supply to Gastroparesis

## One-Sentence Summary

Arginine is an amino acid used in Singapore as a component of intravenous nutrition and amino acid infusion products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **gastroparesis**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **9 publications**, almost all from animal studies, supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, arginine is the substrate for nitric oxide synthase, which produces nitric oxide (NO). It is a standard component of amino acid infusions and parenteral nutrition, and mechanistically it may be applicable to gastroparesis.

The link to gastroparesis runs through NO signalling. Nitrergic nerves use NO to relax the stomach and pylorus, and this relaxation is needed for normal gastric accommodation and emptying. One mouse study found that glucocorticoids induced gastroparesis by depleting L-arginine, which suggests that arginine shortage may impair this pathway. A rat model of Parkinson's disease also showed impaired nitrergic relaxation in the pyloric sphincter.

This reasoning rests on animal data only. No human trial has tested arginine supplementation in gastroparesis, so the prediction remains a hypothesis. Several other retrieved papers concern gastric stimulation, diabetic neuropathy and ghrelin. They relate to gastroparesis but not to arginine.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Knowledge-graph data (not label text) link arginine to contraindication relationships with several conditions. These are not confirmed contraindications, and they should be checked against the product labels:
- **Kidney and metabolic:** chronic kidney disease, end stage renal failure, metabolic acidosis, renal tubular acidosis, nephrolithiasis, hypercalcemia, hyperparathyroidism, phenylketonuria
- **Cardiovascular and other:** heart disease, cardiovascular disease, ventricular fibrillation, digitalis poisoning, sickle cell anemia, sarcoidosis, constipation

No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (99.42%), but the supporting evidence is limited to animal and mechanistic studies (L4). There are no clinical trials, and the key data on approved indications, mechanism of action and label safety are missing. The knowledge-graph contraindications, especially renal and cardiovascular, call for caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, to allow safety screening
- Approved indication text for the Singapore products
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Human pilot data or a well-designed trial of arginine in gastroparesis
- Route and formulation feasibility, since current Singapore products are intravenous nutrition formulations and not designed for gastric motility
- A safety plan for patients with renal impairment, acid-base disturbance or heart disease
