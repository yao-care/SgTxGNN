# Diazepam: From Anxiety to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Diazepam is a long-established benzodiazepine, widely used for anxiety, and marketed in Singapore in tablet, injection and enema forms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Insomnia**, but the supporting evidence is thin.
Although **22 clinical trials** and **20 publications** were retrieved, none of the trials tests diazepam's efficacy for insomnia, and the only direct clinical study is a **1981 comparative trial**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source data. Based on known information, diazepam is a benzodiazepine, a class that acts as positive allosteric modulators of GABA-A receptors. This produces sedative, anxiolytic and hypnotic effects. Its efficacy for anxiety is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to insomnia.

Anxiety and insomnia commonly occur together, and benzodiazepines as a class are widely prescribed for both. A 2024 review of GABA-A modulators lists insomnia among their clinical applications, with diazepam as a prototypical example.

There are important caveats. Most registered trials concern **stopping** hypnotics rather than testing diazepam as a sleep treatment. Guidelines discourage long-term benzodiazepine use because of dependence, cognitive impairment and fall risk. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible, but it is not backed by modern efficacy evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Concerns raised in the retrieved literature and guidelines**: dependence and withdrawal, cognitive impairment (including a 2022 mouse study linking long-term diazepam to cognitive decline), falls risk in older adults, and an association between long-term benzodiazepine or Z-drug use and breast cancer risk.
- **Drug Interactions**: no interaction records were found in the queried source.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The GABA-A mechanism makes a hypnotic effect plausible, but the only direct clinical evidence is a 1981 study in which another benzodiazepine outperformed diazepam. Registered trials focus on stopping hypnotics rather than diazepam efficacy, and guidelines discourage long-term benzodiazepine use. Package insert warnings and contraindications are also missing, which blocks the safety screening step.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website (blocking)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations, to confirm the original indication and any existing insomnia labelling
- Modern head-to-head or placebo-controlled evidence of diazepam in insomnia, or a documented rationale for preferring it over agents already used for insomnia
- A risk-benefit assessment for short-term use only, given dependence and cognitive risks, particularly in older adults

The other nine predicted indications are also on Hold. Rank 3 (sleep initiation and maintenance disorder) largely duplicates the insomnia entry, and the rest have weak or no supporting evidence. This report evaluates only the top-ranked prediction.
