# Cilastatin: From Bacterial Pneumonia to Staphylococcus aureus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cilastatin is a renal dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor. It is used with the carbapenem imipenem to protect it from breakdown in the kidney, and the combination is used for bacterial infections such as pneumonia, peritonitis and endocarditis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Staphylococcus aureus infection**, supported by **1 clinical trial** (Phase 4, small, status unknown) and **20 publications**, none of them RCTs.
Cilastatin has no antibacterial activity of its own, so any benefit would come from the imipenem/cilastatin combination, not from cilastatin alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. From the literature, cilastatin inhibits dehydropeptidase-I in the renal tubules. This prevents imipenem from being degraded and raises its urinary and tissue exposure. Cilastatin itself is not an antibiotic.

The link to *S. aureus* therefore runs through imipenem. Imipenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem with activity against methicillin-susceptible *S. aureus* (MSSA). Its original indications (pneumonia, endocarditis, bone and joint infections, peritonitis) already include infections in which *S. aureus* is a common pathogen. This makes the prediction biologically plausible.

There are important limits. Imipenem is generally unreliable against methicillin-resistant *S. aureus* (MRSA), so the MRSA literature mostly describes combination or salvage regimens, for example with fosfomycin, linezolid, arbekacin or vancomycin. The evidence supports the imipenem/cilastatin combination in selected cases, not cilastatin as an independent agent.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for *S. aureus* infection is limited to one small Phase 4 trial of unknown status, older small clinical series, case reports and animal or in vitro work, with no RCTs. The activity comes from imipenem, not cilastatin, and imipenem is unreliable against MRSA. The better-supported use of the combination is hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia, which has several Phase 3 trials, including RESTORE-IMI 2 with imipenem/cilastatin/relebactam. However, pneumonia is already a labeled original indication, so that evidence confirms an existing use and is not new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled clinical data on imipenem/cilastatin specifically in MSSA infections, with MRSA addressed separately.
- Confirmation of which carbapenem was used in NCT01356472.
- The approved indication text and the package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator, which are currently missing.
- Mechanism of action data for cilastatin.
- Review of the knowledge-graph disease associations (seizure risk, renal impairment, *C. difficile* colitis) against the package insert, plus an antimicrobial stewardship assessment.
