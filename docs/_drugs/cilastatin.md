# Cilastatin: From Imipenem/Cilastatin Combination Antibiotic Therapy to Staphylococcus aureus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cilastatin is a renal enzyme inhibitor used only as a partner in the imipenem/cilastatin injectable antibiotic. It does not kill bacteria itself.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Staphylococcus aureus infection**, but the supporting evidence is **3 loosely related clinical trials** and **20 publications**, most of them old, indirect, or laboratory and animal studies.
The high score most likely reflects the imipenem/cilastatin co-formulation in the knowledge graph, not an independent effect of cilastatin.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. From general pharmacology, cilastatin inhibits renal dehydropeptidase-I. This enzyme would otherwise break down imipenem in the kidney. Cilastatin therefore preserves imipenem exposure and has no antibacterial activity of its own.

Any anti-staphylococcal effect would come from imipenem, and only against methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MSSA). MRSA is intrinsically resistant to imipenem. The MRSA literature therefore mostly describes combination or salvage regimens, such as imipenem/cilastatin with fosfomycin, arbekacin, vancomycin or cefotiam.

The TxGNN score likely comes from the drug pair sharing a graph neighbourhood with S. aureus, not from a mechanism specific to cilastatin. This is a plausible hypothesis for the combination product, not a repurposing signal for cilastatin alone.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For S. aureus infection, cilastatin has no antibacterial activity of its own. The supporting material is indirect trials, small old studies, and laboratory and animal work, and imipenem is not effective against MRSA. Evidence is at L3, and any benefit belongs to imipenem.

Among the other predictions, only **pneumonia** has strong evidence (L1, several Phase 3 RCTs, including the RESTORE-IMI 2 trial, PMID 32785589). That evidence is for the imipenem/cilastatin combination, not for cilastatin alone, and is a better starting point than S. aureus.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Verification of the full trial records (arms and prespecified pathogens) for NCT00707239, NCT01356472 and NCT03583333.
- A decision on whether to evaluate the imipenem/cilastatin combination, with a pneumonia focus, instead of cilastatin alone.
- MSSA/MRSA susceptibility data for the target population.
