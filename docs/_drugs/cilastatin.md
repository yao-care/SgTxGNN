## Cilastatin: dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor (imipenem partner) → staphylococcus aureus infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cilastatin is a renal dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor that is given together with the carbapenem antibiotic imipenem to preserve its exposure in the body; it has no antibacterial activity of its own.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **staphylococcus aureus infection**, with **1 clinical trial** (a small study of a different drug) and **19 publications** in the compiled data, most of which concern the imipenem/cilastatin combination rather than cilastatin alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for cilastatin is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the pharmacology records, cilastatin inhibits dehydropeptidase-I in the kidney. This prevents imipenem from being broken down there and keeps its levels high.

Activity against *S. aureus* comes from imipenem, not cilastatin. Imipenem works reasonably well against methicillin-susceptible strains but is generally unreliable against MRSA. The MRSA literature therefore mostly describes combination or salvage regimens, for example imipenem/cilastatin with fosfomycin, arbekacin or vancomycin. The prediction is thus plausible only for the imipenem/cilastatin combination in selected cases. It does not support cilastatin as an independent agent.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Published experience supports imipenem/cilastatin in selected *S. aureus* infections, mainly as a combination or salvage option. The only trial is small, tests a different drug, and has unknown status. Cilastatin itself has no antibacterial effect, and imipenem is unreliable against MRSA.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
