## Cetrorelix: From Inhibition of Premature LH Surges in Fertility Treatment to hypertrichosis (disease)

## One-Sentence Summary

Cetrorelix is a GnRH receptor antagonist used to prevent premature LH surges in women undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation as part of fertility treatment.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hypertrichosis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction.
The prediction rests on the model score alone (evidence level L5).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Cetrorelix is a peptide antagonist of the GnRH1 receptor (gene GNRHR). By blocking this receptor it suppresses LH and FSH release and, downstream, gonadal sex-steroid production. According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for cetrorelix: "GnRH induces the production and release of luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) from the gonadotrophic cells of the anterior pituitary. Due to a positive estradiol (E2) feedback at midcycle, GnRH liberation is enhanced resulting in an LH-surge." ([DailyMed: Cetrotide (cetrorelix acetate) prescribing information, EMD Serono, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=aca7768e-28a7-4027-b1d8-e66247665f79), label version 2025-09-22; accessed 2026-11-20.)

In theory, lowering androgen signalling could affect androgen-dependent hair growth. However, most forms of hypertrichosis (congenital, drug-induced, syndromic) are not mainly driven by gonadal androgens, so the link is speculative. The high TxGNN score is not backed by any trial or publication.

The other top predictions are also weakly supported:
- **Ambras type hypertrichosis universalis congenita, malformation syndromes, Dandy-Walker–related syndrome, isolated hair shaft abnormality, familial trichomegaly, persistent fetal circulation syndrome:** these are genetic or developmental conditions with no plausible GnRH-pathway mechanism. Their scores come from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.
- **Aromatase excess syndrome:** the primary defect is peripheral aromatisation, so aromatase inhibitors are the logical therapy.
- **Familial male-limited precocious puberty:** this is the most biologically coherent hypothesis, flagged as a research question. The defect (activating LHCGR mutation) lies downstream of GnRH, so monotherapy is not expected to work. No direct evidence exists.
- **Central precocious puberty:** this is mechanistically well aligned, but cetrorelix is short-acting and not formulated for chronic depot use, and GnRH agonists already serve this indication. It is also flagged as a research question.

## Safety Considerations

- **Other considerations:** Use in neonates (relevant to persistent fetal circulation syndrome) would raise additional safety concerns.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for hypertrichosis is supported only by a model score. There are no trials or publications, and the mechanistic link is speculative. Safety data from the package insert has not yet been obtained.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A literature and trial search for cetrorelix in hypertrichosis
- If pursued, a focused review of **central precocious puberty** and **familial male-limited precocious puberty**, the two most mechanistically coherent candidates, considering the short-acting formulation and existing standard therapies
- Assessment of route and formulation compatibility (currently only an injection is available)
