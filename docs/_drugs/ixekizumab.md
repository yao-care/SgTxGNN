# Ixekizumab: From Ankylosing Spondylitis to Inflammatory Spondylopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Ixekizumab is an IL-17A–blocking antibody, originally used for ankylosing spondylitis and related spondyloarthropathy.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but this is supported by no direct evidence. The best-supported prediction is **inflammatory spondylopathy** (rank 6), which has **18 listed clinical trials** and **20 publications**, and which largely overlaps with the drug's existing indications.

> Note: The top-ranked prediction (rheumatoid vasculitis, score 97.5%) has only one incidental trial and no literature. This report therefore presents the rank 1 prediction in the overview, and discusses the best-evidenced prediction, inflammatory spondylopathy, in the evidence sections.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ixekizumab is a monoclonal antibody that neutralises IL-17A, a cytokine that drives inflammation and new bone formation in axial spondyloarthritis. Its efficacy in ankylosing spondylitis is the basis of its original indication.

**Inflammatory spondylopathy (rank 6):** this is the most credible prediction, but it is closer to an on-label or label-adjacent use than to true repurposing. Multiple Phase 3 randomised trials show benefit in radiographic and non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis. The main step needed is to confirm which disease subtype is meant.

**Rheumatoid vasculitis (rank 1):** IL-17A is implicated in autoimmune inflammation, so a theoretical link to vasculitis exists. However, there is no direct evidence that ixekizumab works here, and the high score is a computational prediction only.

**Other predictions (leukaemias, coccyx hypermobility, Kümmell disease, gingival fibromatosis):** no clear IL-17A mechanism links these conditions to the drug. Several likely reflect proximity in the knowledge graph (for example, spinal conditions near spondylitis), and the two CLL/SLL entries share an identical score, suggesting one shared graph signal rather than independent evidence. **Polyarticular juvenile arthritis** is plausible, since IL-17 inhibitors have been studied in related juvenile subtypes, but no supporting trials or literature were compiled.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The only one rated **Major** among those listed is **adalimumab** (combining two biologic immunomodulators). Many listed interactions are rated Moderate, including warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol, simvastatin, tacrolimus, theophylline, aminophylline, roflumilast, nifedipine, lidocaine, fentanyl and hydrocodone, and a tetanus toxoid vaccine. Zinc salts are rated Minor.
- **Class-related risks to monitor:** exacerbation of inflammatory bowel disease and candida infections, as noted in the evidence for IL-17 blockers.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold for rheumatoid vasculitis (rank 1); Proceed with Guardrails for inflammatory spondylopathy (rank 6)**

**Rationale:**
- Rheumatoid vasculitis has a high model score but no direct trials or literature, so it remains a prediction only (L5).
- Inflammatory spondylopathy is supported by multiple completed Phase 3 randomised trials in axial spondyloarthritis (L1), but it largely overlaps with the drug's original indication, so it is not a true new use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, to complete safety screening (currently blocking)
- Confirmation of the specific disease subtype for inflammatory spondylopathy and vertebral disease
- Any mechanistic or exploratory data for rheumatoid vasculitis before further consideration
- A monitoring plan for inflammatory bowel disease flares and candida infections, and for combination use with other biologics
