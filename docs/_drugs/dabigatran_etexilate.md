# Dabigatran etexilate: From Anticoagulation to Sclerosing Cholangitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dabigatran etexilate is an oral anticoagulant that works by directly inhibiting thrombin. It is marketed in Singapore as Pradaxa capsules.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **sclerosing cholangitis**, but only **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** are linked to this prediction. That publication is a drug-interaction study of a different drug, not efficacy evidence, so the prediction rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Dabigatran is generally known as a direct thrombin inhibitor, and its efficacy as an anticoagulant is established.

Nothing in the available evidence links direct thrombin inhibition to cholestatic or fibro-inflammatory bile duct disease. The single linked publication is a cytochrome P450 and transporter drug-drug interaction evaluation of cilofexor, an FXR agonist in development for primary sclerosing cholangitis. It does not test dabigatran as a treatment.

The 99.82% score is therefore a graph-based prediction with no clinical support. It may reflect drug-disease co-mentions in the literature rather than a real therapeutic signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No trials support this prediction, and the only linked publication is an unrelated drug-interaction study. No mechanism connects dabigatran to sclerosing cholangitis, so the high model score is not credible on its own.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (HSA), which are required before any safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Any preclinical or clinical study of dabigatran in cholangitis or bile duct disease. None is currently identified.
- Bleeding-risk assessment for cholestatic liver disease populations

Among the other predicted indications, rheumatoid arthritis is the only one with a plausible rationale. A 2022 rat study reported that dabigatran reduced arthritis through the kallikrein-kinin system. It is flagged as a research question, but it has no human efficacy data.
