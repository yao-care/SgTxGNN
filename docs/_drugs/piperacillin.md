# Piperacillin: From Bacterial Infection Treatment to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Piperacillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic, marketed in Singapore as an injectable, mainly in combination with tazobactam.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid arthritis**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and the 15 retrieved publications are mostly case reports that show co-occurrence, not therapeutic benefit.
This prediction is best viewed as a computational signal and not as supported evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Piperacillin is known to be a beta-lactam antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It has no established anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory action relevant to rheumatoid arthritis.

The original indication (bacterial infection) and the predicted indication (an autoimmune joint disease) do not share a clear pathway. The high TxGNN score is most likely a graph-based artifact. The retrieved literature describes patients with rheumatoid arthritis who were treated for infections, or who had adverse events from other drugs such as methotrexate. This shows co-occurrence, not benefit.

Other predictions (ranks 2–10) were also reviewed, and none has a plausible mechanistic link supported by trials. Sclerosing cholangitis is the only one with a loosely related rationale (antibiotics for bacterial cholangitis complications), but no direct evidence was supplied.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. There are no clinical trials, no plausible anti-inflammatory mechanism, and the literature reflects infections and drug toxicity in RA patients and not treatment benefit. Evidence is at level L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Preclinical or clinical studies that directly test piperacillin in rheumatoid arthritis
- The Singapore package insert (approved indications, warnings and contraindications), needed before any safety screening
