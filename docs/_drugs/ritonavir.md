# Ritonavir: From HIV/AIDS and Hepatitis C to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ritonavir is an HIV protease inhibitor and pharmacokinetic booster, originally used to treat HIV/AIDS and chronic hepatitis C (as part of combination regimens).
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a primate model of HIV.
There are **no clinical trials** and **11 publications** (all preclinical or laboratory-based) supporting this prediction, so it has little direct clinical repurposing value.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ritonavir is an HIV protease inhibitor and a CYP3A4 inhibitor used to boost other protease inhibitors, and its efficacy in HIV infection is established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to SIV.

SIV is a lentivirus closely related to HIV, and its protease is inhibited by HIV protease inhibitors, though susceptibility varies. A laboratory study found ritonavir inhibited SIVmac239 with a 50% effective concentration of about 13 nM, compared with about 25 nM for HIV-1. Macaque studies of combination antiretroviral therapy also support biological plausibility.

However, SIV infection is an animal model of the disease ritonavir already treats. This is best read as a preclinical validation of the existing HIV indication, not a new human indication. The model's other top predictions (feline AIDS, a genetic neurodevelopmental disorder) have no supporting evidence and are likely graph-based artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** long QT syndrome and torsades de pointes, other cardiac conduction disorders (AV block, sudden cardiac arrest), coronary artery disease and cardiomyopathy, pancreatitis, liver disease and cirrhosis, gallbladder disease, diabetes and hyperglycaemia (including diabetic ketoacidosis), hypertriglyceridaemia and familial hyperlipidaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome), gout, potassium deficiency, chronic hepatitis B and haemophilia.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions in the data include loperamide, triamcinolone and budesonide. Moderate-level ones include metformin and other antidiabetics, other corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone), clarithromycin, metronidazole, bupropion, aprepitant, alosetron and lorcaserin. Ritonavir is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, so interaction screening is essential.
- **Population-specific signals from the literature:** adrenal dysfunction in infants exposed to lopinavir/ritonavir, and birth-defect surveillance in pregnancy.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings, as label text was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV prediction rests on laboratory and macaque studies only, with no human trials. SIV is an animal model of HIV, which ritonavir already treats, so it offers no new clinical indication. The other high-scoring predictions are either on-label HIV uses, indirect evidence from ritonavir used as a booster, or unsupported artefacts. The one possible lead is hepatitis E virus infection (L4, in vitro only: ritonavir blocked viral internalization and cleared virus with ribavirin), which would be worth a separate research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data (DrugBank)
- For any human indication of interest (e.g. hepatitis E), controlled clinical data and a DDI plan for immunosuppressed and transplant patients
