# Empagliflozin: From Type 2 Diabetes to Focal Stiff Limb Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Empagliflozin is an SGLT2 inhibitor that lowers blood glucose through the kidneys and is used to treat type 2 diabetes.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **focal stiff limb syndrome**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.
It is a knowledge-graph signal only, and the pack found no plausible mechanistic link.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Empagliflozin is an SGLT2 inhibitor that blocks glucose reabsorption in the kidney. Its efficacy in glucose control is established, but that mechanism does not obviously apply to focal stiff limb syndrome.

Focal stiff limb syndrome is an autoimmune or neuroinflammatory disorder of central nervous system motor control. Empagliflozin acts on renal glucose handling and has no known effect on this pathway.

The high TxGNN score (0.99) is therefore best read as a knowledge-graph association, not a treatment rationale. The closely related prediction, classic stiff person syndrome, has the same score. It is linked to type 1 diabetes only as a comorbidity, which suggests the score may be an artifact of diabetes-related neighbours in the graph.
