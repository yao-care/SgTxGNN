# Vinpocetine: From Cerebrovascular Disorders to Coronary Artery Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Vinpocetine (marketed in Singapore as Cavinton) is a vasodilator that the literature describes as used for cerebrovascular conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **coronary artery disease**.
Support is weak so far: **0 clinical trials** and **3 publications**, all indirect (a cerebrovascular review, a preclinical study and a small 1988 hemodynamic study).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Vinpocetine inhibits PDE1, which raises cAMP and cGMP in vascular tissue. It also has vasodilatory, hemorheologic (blood-flow) and anti-inflammatory (IKK inhibition) effects. Together these could plausibly help vascular and ischemic conditions. The DrugBank mechanism entry was not retrieved, so this summary comes from the literature.

The link to the original use is that cerebrovascular disease and coronary artery disease are both ischemic vascular conditions. Improving blood flow to hypoperfused tissue is the shared therapeutic goal.

The supporting papers are indirect. They include a cerebrovascular review, a preclinical PDE-inhibitor study in pulmonary vasculature, and a 1988 hemodynamic study in patients with atherosclerosis. No coronary outcome data were identified. The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction, not evidence of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no registered trials and no coronary-specific outcome data. The only support is a plausible PDE1/vasodilator mechanism, one preclinical paper, one review and one small, old study of unverified design. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify moving forward.

A related prediction, **myocardial ischemia** (rank 2, evidence level L3), has more support. It rests on a 2019 rat myocardial infarction study and several small 1988–2006 human studies in ischemic or coronary heart disease. It may be a better starting point for a research question than coronary artery disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to confirm the registered indication and obtain warnings and contraindications. This is currently a blocking gap for safety screening.
- DrugBank mechanism-of-action data, to support the mechanistic analysis.
- A drug-interaction and cardiac-conduction safety review before any clinical question is framed.
- A focused literature search for controlled studies in coronary artery disease or myocardial ischemia, to confirm the design and quality of the older studies.
