# Entecavir: From Chronic Hepatitis B to Chronic Hepatitis C Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Entecavir is an oral antiviral originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection**.
The evidence is indirect: **2 clinical trials** and **20 publications** were retrieved, but they address HBV management in patients who also have HCV, not HCV treatment itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Entecavir is known to be a guanosine nucleoside analogue that inhibits the HBV polymerase. Its efficacy in chronic hepatitis B is well established.

The prediction looks like a network association rather than a true pharmacological link. HCV is an RNA virus with no DNA polymerase or reverse transcription step, which are the steps entecavir blocks. The model's signal most likely reflects HBV/HCV co-infection and shared hepatitis-related associations. The trials and publications retrieved are about preventing or managing HBV in patients who also have HCV, usually while they receive direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) for HCV. Any anti-HCV effect in these studies comes from the DAAs, not from entecavir.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged in a drug-disease knowledge graph (not package insert text):** impaired renal function, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and lactic acidosis.
- **Drug Interactions:** 44 interactions were recorded, and those listed in detail are all rated Moderate. They include:
  - Acid suppressants: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine.
  - Metformin.
  - Amphotericin B (including lipid, liposomal and cholesteryl sulfate formulations).
  - Antibiotics: vancomycin, kanamycin, neomycin, levofloxacin, amoxicillin, polymyxin B.
  - Mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine and sulfasalazine.
  - Orlistat.
- **Additional safety notes:** please refer to the package insert for key warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Entecavir has no recognised activity against HCV. The retrieved trials and publications concern HBV management in HCV co-infected patients, not HCV treatment. The high model score therefore appears to reflect network association rather than a genuine anti-HCV effect. The established use of entecavir in HBV/HCV co-infection is for HBV reactivation prophylaxis, which is an HBV indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and in vitro evidence of anti-HCV activity, if any exists.
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator, for a full safety review.
- A controlled study with an HCV efficacy endpoint (for example, sustained virological response) showing an effect from entecavir itself.
