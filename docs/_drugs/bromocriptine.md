Bromocriptine is a dopamine receptor agonist, originally used for Parkinson disease, hyperprolactinaemia and acromegaly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, bromocriptine is a dopamine D2-family receptor agonist. Its efficacy in Parkinson disease and prolactin-related disorders is established.

No mechanistic link to fucosylation pathways has been identified in the data compiled for this report. The high TxGNN score is a computational output only and should not be read as biological or clinical support. The disease is a rare congenital metabolic disorder, and dopamine receptor agonism has no evident connection to the defective fucosylation underlying it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone. There are no clinical trials, no literature and no identifiable mechanistic link between dopamine agonism and defective fucosylation. The evidence is not sufficient to justify further investment at this time.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A biological rationale linking bromocriptine's mechanism to fucosylation pathways, together with detailed mechanism of action data
- Preclinical studies in relevant disease models
- The official package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA
- Review of the other ranked predictions, since some have more supporting data. For example, retinal dystrophy has indirect preclinical and case-level evidence, and schizophrenia has one small Phase 4 trial focused on metabolic effects. Bromocriptine-induced psychosis has also been reported, so that direction carries a safety concern.
