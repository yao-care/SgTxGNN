# Nilotinib: From Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia to Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans

## One-Sentence Summary

Nilotinib is an oral kinase inhibitor marketed in Singapore as Tasigna, and it is generally used for chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP)**, a rare skin sarcoma.
Support is thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 review article** (class-level, not nilotinib-specific).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for nilotinib is not currently available in the record. The rationale below is based on the biology of the disease and on the drug's known targets.

DFSP is driven by a COL1A1-PDGFB gene fusion. This fusion makes tumour cells produce their own PDGFB growth signal, which continuously activates the PDGFRB receptor. Nilotinib inhibits PDGFR, so it can in principle switch off this driver. Imatinib has a similar target profile and is already an established DFSP therapy, which makes the prediction biologically plausible.

However, the only supporting item is a 2018 review of PDGFR inhibitors in neoplastic disorders. There is no nilotinib-specific study in DFSP. The high score reflects plausibility, not clinical confirmation.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The Singapore package insert has not yet been retrieved, and no drug interaction records were found.

One signal from the literature is a case report of pancreatic carcinoma developing after nilotinib therapy for CML (PMID 26376592). This is a single case, not evidence of causation, but it is worth keeping in view during safety review.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is credible, but there is no nilotinib-specific trial or study in DFSP. Safety screening cannot proceed without the Singapore package insert.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications) from the HSA website
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Nilotinib-specific DFSP evidence, such as case series or trials, and comparison with imatinib, the established therapy
- A safety review covering the pancreatic carcinoma signal and drug interactions

Among the other predicted indications, liposarcoma has the most evidence: a Phase I/II trial of nilotinib plus doxorubicin in retroperitoneal sarcomas (NCT02587169, status unknown) and a published Phase I report (PMID 30037815). It may be worth evaluating as a parallel research question.
