# Nilotinib: From Chronic Myeloid Leukemia to Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans

## One-Sentence Summary

Nilotinib is a second-generation BCR-ABL/PDGFR/KIT tyrosine kinase inhibitor, originally developed for Philadelphia chromosome-positive chronic myeloid leukemia (CML). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans (DFSP)**, a rare PDGFR-β-driven soft tissue sarcoma, but this direction is currently supported by only **1 general review article** and **no DFSP-specific clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for nilotinib is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known information, nilotinib is a second-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting BCR-ABL, PDGFR-α/β, and KIT — its efficacy in BCR-ABL+ CML is well established, and mechanistically this target profile may extend to PDGFR-driven tumours outside of CML.

DFSP commonly harbours a COL1A1-PDGFB fusion gene, which drives constitutive activation of PDGFR-β. Because nilotinib directly inhibits PDGFR-β, the mechanistic link to DFSP is direct rather than speculative. This is further supported by precedent: imatinib, a first-generation TKI with an overlapping target profile, is already an approved treatment for DFSP — making nilotinib a mechanistically plausible alternative or salvage option in this same molecular subtype.

That said, the current literature evidence for this specific pairing (PMID 29408302) is a general review of PDGFR inhibitors across neoplastic disease, not a DFSP-focused or nilotinib-specific study — so the mechanistic plausibility is stronger than the direct clinical evidence at this stage.
