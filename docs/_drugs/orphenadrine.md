# Orphenadrine: From Musculoskeletal Pain and Spasm to Retinal Dystrophy with or without Extraocular Anomalies

## One-Sentence Summary

Orphenadrine is an anticholinergic muscle relaxant used for musculoskeletal pain and spasm, and is also used for drug-induced parkinsonism.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **retinal dystrophy with or without extraocular anomalies**, but this rests on a graph-based score alone.
There are **0 clinical trials** and **no publications that evaluate orphenadrine** for this condition (15 general ophthalmology papers were retrieved, none about the drug).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the database. The evidence pack describes orphenadrine as an anticholinergic and H1 antagonist, with weak NMDA-antagonist and sodium-channel-blocking activity. Its efficacy in musculoskeletal pain and spasm is established, but nothing in that pharmacology points to a role in retinal disease.

The review found **no credible mechanistic link** between orphenadrine and inherited retinal degeneration. The high score (0.993, model rank 8305) is a statistical output of the knowledge graph. The retrieved papers cover pediatric ophthalmology, congenital anomalies of the eye and extraocular muscles, and orbital disease, and none of them tests orphenadrine.

This prediction should not be read as a treatment signal. Only the schizophrenia prediction (rank 5) has any drug-specific literature, and that literature concerns managing antipsychotic side effects, not treating the disease itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials, no drug-specific literature, and no plausible mechanistic link (evidence level L5, stage S0). The drug is widely marketed in Singapore, but that says nothing about efficacy in retinal dystrophy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA. This gap currently blocks safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking orphenadrine to retinal degeneration pathways.
- If the team wants a more tractable question, review the schizophrenia prediction (rank 5, L3) separately. It is best framed as adjunct management of antipsychotic-induced extrapyramidal symptoms, not a new disease indication, and the Cochrane reviews do not support benefit for tardive dyskinesia.
