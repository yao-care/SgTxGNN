## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Hesperidin is a polyphenol flavanone glycoside (hesperetin-7-rutinoside) sold as a single-ingredient oral tablet. Its original approved indication is not available in the data compiled for this report, so a direct link between the original use and the new indication cannot be drawn.

The only publication for myeloproliferative neoplasm is an in silico repurposing screen aimed at the BCR kinase domain in chronic myeloid leukemia, a specific MPN subtype. Binding to BCR-ABL is a computational prediction without experimental or clinical validation. The very high TxGNN score reflects model output, not clinical evidence.

Cell-line studies report that hesperidin may induce intrinsic apoptosis in leukemia cells, modulate Mcl-1 expression, and deactivate PI3K/Akt signalling (with salinomycin). It may also enhance cytarabine cytotoxicity (with silibinin) and bind CD33 in docking studies. However, a 2003 study suggests the C7 rutinoside moiety reduces pro-apoptotic activity compared with the aglycone, and hesperidin's low oral bioavailability raises doubts about achievable concentrations. These findings do not directly validate the MPN prediction.
