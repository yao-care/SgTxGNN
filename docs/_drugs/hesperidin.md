# Hesperidin: From Registered Use (Indication Not Recorded) to Myeloproliferative Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Hesperidin is a citrus flavonoid sold in Singapore in oral tablet products (Daflon and others), but the registry record does not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN)**.
There are **0 clinical trials** and **2 publications**, both preclinical, and neither tested hesperidin in MPN models or patients.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Hesperidin is a citrus flavonoid glycoside. Its aglycone, hesperetin, has shown anticancer and antioxidant properties in laboratory studies. The link to myeloproliferative neoplasm rests on indirect signals:

- An in silico repurposing study targeting the BCR kinase domain in chronic myeloid leukemia (CML). The retrieved excerpt does not show hesperidin-specific results.
- A cell-line study in which hesperetin increased membrane progesterone receptor expression and reduced reactive oxygen species in human myeloid leukemia cells.

CML is a subtype of MPN, so these findings are loosely relevant. However, nothing tests hesperidin itself in MPN models or patients. The cell-line work used the metabolite hesperetin, which is not the marketed compound.

The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis. For context, the same evidence pack ranks **myeloid leukemia** (rank 8) with a much larger body of in vitro and in silico work. It includes synergy with cytarabine and azacitidine in leukemia cell lines. It is still preclinical (L4, "Research Question"), and oral bioavailability of hesperidin is low.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no clinical trials exist and the two retrieved papers are preclinical and only indirectly related to MPN. The mechanistic link is weak, and the original indication and safety information are not available. The evidence does not justify advancing this candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert (approved indication, warnings, contraindications), which is blocking for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data for hesperidin
- Direct preclinical data in MPN models (for example JAK2-mutant cell lines) using hesperidin itself rather than hesperetin
- Pharmacokinetic data showing whether achievable exposure matches the effective in vitro concentrations
- Consideration of the myeloid leukemia indication (rank 8) as a separate, better-supported research question
