## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. The published literature gives a consistent picture. Teriflunomide selectively and reversibly inhibits the mitochondrial enzyme dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH). This blocks de novo pyrimidine synthesis and reduces the proliferation of activated T and B lymphocytes.

RRMS is an immune-mediated disease in which activated lymphocytes drive inflammatory demyelination. Slowing lymphocyte proliferation is therefore a plausible way to reduce relapses and disability accumulation.

The clinical evidence is strong. Phase 3 randomized trials with a long-term extension exist, and several recent pivotal trials of newer drugs used teriflunomide as the active comparator. The pack does not say whether RRMS is an already-approved indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
At least two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (NCT00134563 and NCT00883337), a long-term extension, and many comparator-based RCTs and meta-analyses support teriflunomide in RRMS, giving evidence level L1. The registration record is silent on the approved indication, so the label status must be confirmed before any positioning.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the HSA package insert to confirm the approved indication (blocking for safety screening).
- Obtain DrugBank mechanism of action data to formally document the DHODH link.
- Set up monitoring and counselling for hepatotoxicity, teratogenicity and contraception requirements, and drug-interaction screening.
- Track other predicted indications separately. Thrombocytopenia (four Phase 2 trials, none with results) needs a hematologic safety assessment first, because a case report links the parent compound leflunomide to TTP. The other predictions have no trial or literature support and should stay on hold.
