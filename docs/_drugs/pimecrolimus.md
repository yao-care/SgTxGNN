## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. From the published literature, pimecrolimus is a topical calcineurin inhibitor. It blocks T-cell activation, reduces release of inflammatory cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, interferon-gamma, TNF-alpha), and inhibits mast cell degranulation. It is designed for skin application, with low systemic absorption.

Atopic dermatitis and seborrheic dermatitis are both chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin diseases that affect areas such as the face. Seborrheic dermatitis is also linked to an inflammatory response to *Malassezia* yeast on the skin. Because pimecrolimus suppresses T-cell-driven inflammation, it is mechanistically reasonable for this condition. It also avoids the long-term adverse effects of topical corticosteroids, which limit their use on the face. Systematic reviews of randomized trials report that pimecrolimus 1% cream is well tolerated and effective for facial seborrheic dermatitis, with efficacy comparable to the comparators examined.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the drug knowledge graph (not label text):** common wart, epidermodysplasia verruciformis, anogenital human papillomavirus infection, herpes simplex, herpes simplex dermatitis, herpes zoster, chickenpox, infectious mononucleosis, chronic Epstein-Barr virus infection syndrome, lymphatic system disease, Netherton syndrome, and ichthyosis linearis circumflexa. Overall, viral skin infections, lymphatic disease, and skin-barrier disorders are the main concerns.
- **Drug interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 records. The 20 listed (e.g., pantoprazole, omeprazole, doxycycline, metformin, simvastatin, prednisone, triamcinolone, hydrocortisone, budesonide, aspirin) have no severity grading ("Unknown"), so their clinical significance is unconfirmed.
- **Class-level concerns from the literature:** Topical calcineurin inhibitors carry a boxed warning on long-term use and malignancy (including lymphoma) concerns, and use in infants is limited. A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis examined cancer risk with pimecrolimus and tacrolimus, and a 5-year randomized trial in infants provides long-term safety data. Pimecrolimus can also mask tinea (tinea incognito) and cause skin irritation if combined with alcohol intake.

The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications have not yet been obtained. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 randomized trial and two systematic reviews of randomized trials directly support pimecrolimus in seborrheic dermatitis, and the mechanism overlaps with its established atopic dermatitis use. However, the direct clinical evidence is limited to one short Phase 2 study, no Phase 3 trial exists for this indication, and Singapore safety label data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications, boxed-warning language), which is a blocking data gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the Singapore approved indication wording, which is blank in the licence record
- Review of Phase 2 trial results and the primary RCT data underlying the systematic reviews
- A confirmatory, longer-duration controlled study, with a plan for limiting duration of use, avoiding infants, and monitoring for infection and malignancy
- Interaction severity grading for the co-medications flagged
