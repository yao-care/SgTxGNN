## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. From general pharmacology, mivacurium is a short-acting nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker. It antagonizes nicotinic receptors at the skeletal muscle end plate and causes temporary muscle paralysis.

Cauda equina syndrome is a compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots that needs surgical decompression. Muscle paralysis does not relieve the compression and offers no therapeutic benefit, and the patient would need ventilatory support. We found no plausible mechanistic link. The high score most likely reflects a pattern in the knowledge graph rather than a real pharmacological relationship.

The other nine top-ranked predictions (scores 94.5–96.8%) have the same problem. They are obsolete neurogenic bladder, migraine disorder, irritable bowel syndrome, migraine with brainstem aura, primary hereditary glaucoma, open-angle glaucoma, hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, seborrheic dermatitis and congenital hypotrichosis milia. Each has no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism.
