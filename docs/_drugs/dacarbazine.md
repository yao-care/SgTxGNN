# Dacarbazine: From Hodgkin Lymphoma and Metastatic Melanoma to Upper Aerodigestive Tract Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Dacarbazine is a DNA-alkylating chemotherapy drug originally used for Hodgkin lymphoma and metastatic melanoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm**, but **no clinical trials** and only **indirect literature** (about 20 publications on related tumours) currently support this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, dacarbazine is a DNA-alkylating agent. It is a methylating prodrug activated by hepatic metabolism, and its efficacy in melanoma and Hodgkin lymphoma (as part of the ABVD regimen) is established.

The retrieved literature touches the head and neck region only indirectly. It covers mucosal melanoma, Hodgkin lymphoma at head and neck sites, paraganglioma, and medullary thyroid carcinoma. These are dacarbazine-sensitive tumour types that can occur in that region. No study tests dacarbazine in squamous cell carcinoma of the upper aerodigestive tract, which is the typical meaning of this predicted indication. The high TxGNN score is therefore not backed by direct clinical evidence.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for this predicted indication.

(For reference, two early-phase dacarbazine combination trials were linked to the broader "head and neck cancer" prediction, but both enrolled melanoma, sarcoma or medullary thyroid carcinoma patients, not upper aerodigestive tract cancer.)

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, pyoureter, thrombocytopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), leukopenia, hepatic vein thrombosis, and Budd-Chiari syndrome. Liver-related items are consistent with the known risk of hepatic vein thrombosis and hepatocellular necrosis with dacarbazine, and the haematological items with its myelosuppression.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The main ones with a graded severity are naltrexone (Moderate) and levofloxacin (Minor). Many others, including pantoprazole, omeprazole, morphine, metformin, prednisone, simvastatin, ondansetron and aprepitant, have no severity grade assigned.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by model scoring and indirect literature on other tumour types occurring in the head and neck region. No trial tests dacarbazine in upper aerodigestive tract cancer, and the drug's substantial haematological, hepatic and emetic toxicity is hard to justify without efficacy evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of which tumour types the predicted indication covers (squamous carcinoma versus dacarbazine-sensitive histologies such as mucosal melanoma)
- Direct clinical or preclinical efficacy data in the target population
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website, and the approved indication text for both Singapore licences
- A comparison against current standard therapies for the target tumour types
