# Desogestrel: From Oral Contraception to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Desogestrel is a progestin used in hormonal oral contraceptives; in Singapore it is registered as Mercilon tablets.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Amenorrhea**, but the direction of effect is unclear because desogestrel-containing products commonly *cause* amenorrhea.
Only **2 clinical trials** (both indirect) and a small body of mostly indirect literature support this prediction, so the evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on known information, desogestrel is a progestin that suppresses the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and acts on the endometrium. Its efficacy in contraception is well established, and mechanistically it may be relevant to menstrual disorders.

The link to amenorrhea is uncertain and cuts both ways. Progestins are used to induce withdrawal bleeding in secondary amenorrhea. Yet desogestrel-containing products, especially progestin-only pills, often cause amenorrhea as a side effect. The very high TxGNN score may therefore reflect a drug-induced-amenorrhea association rather than a therapeutic one.

None of the retrieved trials confirms that desogestrel was tested as a treatment for amenorrhea. Other predictions are weaker still: acne has the best support (L3, from combined-pill studies), and the remaining predictions are model output only, with no clinical or literature evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Venous thromboembolism**: Desogestrel-containing combined pills carry a higher venous thromboembolism risk than some other progestins (PMID 10652979). This should be weighed against alternative options.
- **Amenorrhea and irregular bleeding**: Progestin-only desogestrel pills commonly cause irregular bleeding, including amenorrhea (PMID 35261299).

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The 99.96% model score is not backed by direct evidence. The two trials are indirect and the literature is mostly on contraception. Desogestrel commonly causes amenorrhea, so a therapeutic benefit is doubtful. Package insert safety data are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the trial arms in NCT00946192 and NCT01588873 to see whether desogestrel was tested
- Evidence that desogestrel treats amenorrhea, rather than causing it
- If a repurposing candidate is wanted sooner, review acne (L3) first, using combined-pill data and the thromboembolism risk
