# Carvedilol: From Hypertension to Malignant Hypertensive Renal Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Carvedilol is a beta-blocker with additional alpha-1 blocking and vasodilating activity, originally used to treat **hypertension**.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant hypertensive renal disease**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The top-ranked prediction is largely an extension of the existing hypertension indication, so the evidence is model-based only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, carvedilol is a non-selective beta-blocker with alpha-1 blockade and vasodilating properties. This lowers blood pressure, which is the basis of its use in hypertension.

Malignant hypertensive renal disease and malignant renovascular hypertension are severe forms of hypertensive disease. A blood pressure-lowering rationale therefore exists. However, the very high TxGNN scores (about 99.5%) most likely reflect closeness to the original hypertension indication in the knowledge graph. They are not independent evidence of benefit in these severe conditions.

Other predictions in the list have more literature but weaker links:
- **Pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease/hypoxia (score 99.54%, L4):** only indirect preclinical data, such as carvedilol reversing hypoxia-induced hypertension in rats. Non-selective beta-blockade also carries a theoretical respiratory risk.
- **Chronic pulmonary heart disease (score 94.55%, L3):** mostly observational data on carvedilol tolerability in heart failure patients with COPD, which is a safety question rather than evidence of efficacy.
- **Cerebrovascular disorder (score 68.08%, L4):** preclinical neuroprotective and antioxidant findings only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from package insert text):
  - Respiratory: asthma (including intrinsic and allergic asthma), bronchitis, pulmonary emphysema, bronchial disease
  - Cardiac conduction: atrioventricular block, sick sinus syndrome (including the familial form)
  - Circulatory: hypotensive disorder, peripheral vascular and arterial disease, intermittent claudication, Prinzmetal angina
  - Metabolic: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis
  - Other: liver failure, anaphylaxis, intraoperative floppy iris syndrome
- **Relevance to the predictions:** non-selective beta-blockade is a particular concern in the pulmonary hypertension and lung disease predictions, because of respiratory risk and possible harm to right ventricular function.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. The one rated Major is **epinephrine**. Examples rated Moderate include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), atropine and hyoscyamine, bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin and acetohexamide, and calcium salts. Aspirin and aluminium hydroxide are rated Minor.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications, as this information could not be retrieved for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions (malignant hypertensive renal disease and malignant renovascular hypertension) have no trials or literature and mainly reflect closeness to the existing hypertension indication. The predictions with some evidence (pulmonary heart disease, cerebrovascular disorder) rest on indirect observational or preclinical data, and some carry respiratory safety concerns. None reaches the level needed to justify a repurposing programme now.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data for carvedilol
- Targeted searches for clinical data on carvedilol in hypertensive renal disease and renovascular hypertension
- Confirmation of whether these predictions represent genuinely new indications or only a restatement of the current hypertension use
- For the cerebrovascular and pulmonary directions: well-designed clinical studies and a respiratory and haemodynamic safety assessment
