## One-Sentence Summary

Emtricitabine is an antiretroviral drug originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (an animal study of combination antiretroviral therapy in cats), so the prediction rests mainly on model output.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, emtricitabine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) that acts against HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus related to HIV and also depends on reverse transcriptase, so activity against it is biologically plausible.

The single publication (PMID 37112803, 2023) tested a combination antiretroviral regimen in FIV-infected cats. According to the abstract excerpt, the regimen included dolutegravir, tenofovir and emtricitabine. The excerpt is truncated, so the outcomes cannot be confirmed here.

The very high TxGNN score mostly reflects similarity to the HIV indication that emtricitabine is already used for, not independent evidence. Because this is a veterinary condition, translation to human clinical use does not apply. Its main value is as a comparative or preclinical model.

For context, the second-ranked prediction, simian immunodeficiency virus infection, has more supporting animal data. Several macaque studies show that emtricitabine combined with tenofovir (TDF or TAF) protects against SHIV challenge. These are surrogate models for HIV, which is already a known use, so they do not represent true repurposing.
