# Emtricitabine: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Emtricitabine is an antiretroviral drug originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, with **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The evidence is almost entirely from macaque studies, so this is a preclinical extension of the existing HIV indication rather than a new human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, emtricitabine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). Its efficacy in HIV infection has been established, and it may act in the same way against closely related lentiviruses.

SIV and SHIV (simian/human immunodeficiency virus) are close relatives of HIV-1 and rely on a similar reverse transcriptase. This explains the high TxGNN score. Macaque studies show that emtricitabine combined with tenofovir (TAF or TDF) protects animals from SIV/SHIV acquisition. Examples include rectal and vaginal challenge models and a study of a tenofovir-resistant virus.

The prediction therefore mostly reflects emtricitabine's established anti-HIV activity, seen through an animal model of the same disease. Its clinical relevance is as a translational model for HIV PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) and treatment. It does not point to a new human disease.

Other predictions in the list were also reviewed:
- **AIDS related complex** and **congenital HIV** overlap with the approved HIV/AIDS indications, so they are not true repurposing.
- **Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV)** has only one veterinary study.
- Predictions such as benign prostate tumours, Brenner tumour and familial combined hyperlipidemia have no mechanistic link and no supporting evidence.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The package insert warnings and contraindications could not be retrieved for this report.

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph links emtricitabine to a range of conditions, including:
  - Liver and kidney disease: liver failure, liver disease, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, kidney disease, Fanconi renotubular syndrome
  - Metabolic and bone conditions: lactic acidosis, hypophosphatemia, osteomalacia, rickets, hypertriglyceridemia, obesity
  - Viral hepatitis: chronic hepatitis B and hepatitis C virus infection
  - Cardiac rhythm and others: long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, and some neuropsychiatric conditions

  These come from knowledge-graph edges and should be verified against the official label.

- **Drug Interactions**: 20 interactions were found (DDInter). The two **Major** interactions are:
  - Leflunomide
  - Teriflunomide

  Most of the others are **Moderate**, including cobicistat, naltrexone, orlistat, methotrexate, interferon beta-1a/1b, cladribine, clofarabine, epirubicin, idelalisib and cabozantinib. Eribulin is **Minor**.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The predicted indication is a nonhuman primate model of the already-approved HIV indication. The 20 publications are macaque and review studies, with no registered human trials. This supports translational research but not a new clinical use.
- The package insert safety data are missing, which is a blocking gap for safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication text) downloaded and parsed
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A decision on whether this should be treated as a research question about preclinical PrEP models rather than a clinical repurposing candidate
- If a human-relevant direction is wanted, a review of other candidates such as prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT). The pregnancy pharmacokinetic and safety data for tenofovir/emtricitabine (PMID 23169329, 29406430) are the most clinically relevant evidence found. This overlaps with the existing HIV indication.
