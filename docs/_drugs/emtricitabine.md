# Emtricitabine: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Emtricitabine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) used against HIV-1, both for treatment and as part of PrEP regimens.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a non-human primate disease.
Support is **2 loosely related clinical trials** and **20 publications**, mostly macaque studies. There are no direct human trials for this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Emtricitabine belongs to the NRTI class. NRTIs block viral reverse transcriptase, an enzyme that lentiviruses need to replicate. SIV and the SIV/HIV hybrid virus (SHIV) are lentiviruses with the same target, so the mechanism is plausible.

The link is largely tautological, though. SIV and SHIV infection in macaques is the standard animal model for HIV research. The macaque studies test emtricitabine-based regimens for PrEP and treatment as a preclinical model of HIV. They do not point to a new human indication, and HIV-1 is already covered by the approved use. The model's high score therefore mostly reflects the close biological relationship between SIV and HIV.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
SIV infection is an animal model of HIV, not a human indication. The supporting evidence is macaque work and two weakly related HIV trials, which places it at L4. The approved HIV-1 use already covers what this evidence shows, so this is not a novel repurposing opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert safety information (warnings and contraindications) from HSA. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank.
- A decision on whether SIV infection should count as a human-relevant indication at all. If not, review the other predicted indications that overlap with HIV, such as AIDS-related complex and congenital HIV, as HIV-spectrum label questions rather than new repurposing.
