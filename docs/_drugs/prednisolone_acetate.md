## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prednisolone acetate is a topical glucocorticoid that suppresses ocular surface inflammation. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to non-infectious, immune-mediated follicular conjunctivitis.

The link has important limits. Follicular conjunctivitis is often infectious (viral or chlamydial) or toxic/drug-induced, and corticosteroids may be unhelpful or harmful in those settings. The retrieved data contain no prednisolone-specific evidence for this condition, so the high score reflects model association rather than clinical proof.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. HSA warnings and contraindications were not obtained.

Class-level points from the retrieved evidence, not specific to this product's label:
- **Intraocular pressure (IOP) elevation and cataract**: Prolonged topical steroid use carries a risk of raised IOP, secondary glaucoma, and cataract. Steroid-induced glaucoma has been reported in children with chronic vernal disease.
- **Infectious causes**: Steroids may be inappropriate when follicular conjunctivitis is viral, chlamydial, or toxic/drug-induced, so the cause must be established first.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Support for this indication is model prediction plus one loosely related trial and one case report, with no prednisolone-specific evidence. The disease is heterogeneous and often infectious, so steroid use is not clearly appropriate.

Among the other predicted indications, **vernal conjunctivitis** and **papillary conjunctivitis** have stronger mechanistic plausibility (allergic or immune-driven disease) and more literature. Both are rated "Research Question", but their evidence also comes mainly from other agents such as cyclosporine and loteprednol. They may be better candidates to pursue first.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of whether prednisolone is an arm in NCT04705584, and retrieval of the full case report (PMID 29801089)
- Definition of the target aetiology (non-infectious, immune-mediated follicular conjunctivitis only)
- A safety-focused review of IOP and cataract risk with chronic use
