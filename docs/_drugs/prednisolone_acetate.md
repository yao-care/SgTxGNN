# Prednisolone Acetate: From Uveitis and Conjunctivitis to Chronic Follicular Conjunctivitis

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prednisolone acetate is a topical ophthalmic corticosteroid. Its efficacy in ocular inflammation, including uveitis and conjunctivitis, is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to chronic follicular conjunctivitis. Corticosteroids generally suppress ocular surface inflammation.

The predicted disease sits in the same family as the conjunctivitis conditions already linked to this drug, which explains the high score. However, the prediction is based on the model alone. Chronic follicular conjunctivitis has a range of causes (infectious, toxic, allergic), and a steroid is not appropriate for all of them.

Other high-scoring predictions follow the same pattern. Several are conjunctival diseases (serous conjunctivitis, conjunctival folliculosis, Angelucci syndrome) with only a plausible anti-inflammatory rationale and no evidence. Others are poor fits: parasitic and acute hemorrhagic (viral) conjunctivitis carry a risk of worsening infection, and postinfectious vasculitis is a systemic disease that a topical eye drop is unlikely to reach.

## Safety Considerations

- **Class concern (from the literature):** Prolonged topical corticosteroid use can raise intraocular pressure and lead to glaucoma, and may cause cataract. This matters for chronic conditions such as vernal conjunctivitis.
- **Conditions flagged as contraindications in the knowledge graph (not label text):** herpes simplex virus keratitis, viral eye infection, fungal infection of eye, ocular tuberculosis, eye infectious disease, open-angle glaucoma, ocular hypertension, and corneal pigmentation. The graph also lists several systemic conditions (e.g. diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, porphyria, G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosemia), which should be verified against the product label.
- **Drug interactions:** No interaction data were found.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, chronic follicular conjunctivitis, rests on a model score alone (L5) with no trials or literature. Because the drug is already registered for conjunctivitis, the mechanism is plausible. Still, the cause of chronic follicular conjunctivitis matters, since steroids can worsen infectious causes. Papillary and vernal conjunctivitis (L4) are better candidates for further research, but the evidence concerns other drugs (loteprednol, cyclosporine) and is indirect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore-registered product
- Mechanism of action data for prednisolone acetate
- Studies of prednisolone acetate specifically in chronic follicular, papillary or vernal conjunctivitis
- Clarification of the aetiology (allergic, toxic, infectious) in which a steroid would be appropriate
- An intraocular pressure and infection-exclusion monitoring plan for any extended use
