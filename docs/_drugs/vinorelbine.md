# Vinorelbine: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Vinorelbine is a vinca alkaloid chemotherapy originally used for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and lung cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**,
with **3 related clinical trials** (two Phase 2 studies in relapsed or refractory paediatric tumours) and **6 publications** currently supporting this direction. The evidence is indirect and comes mostly from mixed tumour populations.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source database. Based on known pharmacology, vinorelbine is a vinca alkaloid that inhibits microtubule polymerisation. This causes mitotic arrest and apoptosis in rapidly dividing cells.

Ewing sarcoma is a rapidly proliferating small round cell sarcoma with high relapse rates, especially in multi-metastatic disease. Vinorelbine's efficacy in lung cancer is established, and its antimitotic action is not specific to one tumour type. When combined with low-dose metronomic cyclophosphamide, it has shown activity in relapsed or refractory Ewing sarcoma and other paediatric solid tumours.

Preclinical work also shows synergy between microtubule-interfering drugs, including vinorelbine, and PLK1 inhibition in Ewing sarcoma cells. A 2026 review rationalises vinorelbine plus low-dose cyclophosphamide as maintenance therapy in the international INTER-EWING-1 trial.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindicated conditions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure, infectious disease, ileus, intestinal obstruction, interstitial lung disease, lung disease, obsolete rare pulmonary disease, leukopenia.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Examples:
  - **Major**: clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate**: aprepitant, dexamethasone, metronidazole, eliglustat, miconazole, rolapitant, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, tinidazole, glycerol phenylbutyrate.
  - **Minor**: levofloxacin.

  Several moderate interactions involve CYP3A4-related drugs (for example azole antifungals, aprepitant and clarithromycin). Review co-medications carefully before use.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Ewing sarcoma has the strongest evidence among the predicted indications, with Phase 2 data for vinorelbine plus cyclophosphamide in relapsed or refractory paediatric solid tumours, a 2026 review and a supporting preclinical study. However, the clinical data come from mixed tumour populations, one key trial has unknown status, and no Ewing-specific randomised evidence is available. Other predicted indications, such as benign lung tumours and gingival fibromatosis, are not supported by evidence and are not recommended.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (not yet available), plus approved indication text for the three licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Ewing-specific efficacy data, for example results from INTER-EWING-1 or subgroup analyses of the Phase 2 trials
- Dosing and route selection for paediatric and young adult patients (injectable versus oral metronomic regimens)
- A safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, CYP3A4 interactions and organ function, delivered within a specialist paediatric oncology setting
