# Streptomycin: From Tuberculosis and Other Bacterial Infections to Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Streptomycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic originally used for pulmonary tuberculosis and several other bacterial infections, such as plague, tularemia and brucellosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **conjunctivitis**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only a handful of relevant publications, mostly old case reports and reviews, so the evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Streptomycin is generally understood to be a bactericidal aminoglycoside that inhibits the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit. Its efficacy in its original infectious indications is established.

The link to conjunctivitis is indirect. Some of streptomycin's original target infections can involve the eye. Tularemia can cause Parinaud oculoglandular syndrome, and brucellosis can cause keratoconjunctivitis. Old reports also describe tuberculous conjunctivitis treated with streptomycin. These cases show a pathogen-specific connection, not evidence for common bacterial conjunctivitis.

There are also practical reasons for caution. Topical ocular use is not supported by the data compiled for this report. Systemic aminoglycoside toxicity (ear and kidney damage) is a concern for a condition that is usually self-limiting. Safer topical aminoglycosides, such as tobramycin, are already established for eye infections.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): kidney disease, pyoureter, hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disorders, central nervous system origin vertigo, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction diseases, Parkinson disease and parkinsonian disorders.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level interactions include:
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Human immunoglobulin G (intravenous)
  - Bacitracin

  Moderate-level interactions include other nephrotoxic or aminoglycoside agents (amphotericin B, amikacin, topical neomycin) and several NSAIDs (ibuprofen, ketorolac, celecoxib, aspirin).
- **Aminoglycoside toxicity**: As a class, aminoglycosides carry risks of ear and kidney toxicity. This is relevant to any non-life-threatening use such as conjunctivitis.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and precautions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no clinical trials, and the supporting literature is limited to reviews, old case reports and microbiology surveys. The risks of systemic aminoglycoside use outweigh the likely benefit in a mostly self-limiting condition, and safer topical alternatives already exist.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA), which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Evidence for a topical ocular formulation, including safety and efficacy, or a narrower focus on pathogen-specific ocular infections (for example tularemic or tuberculous conjunctivitis)
- Comparative data against established topical agents such as tobramycin
