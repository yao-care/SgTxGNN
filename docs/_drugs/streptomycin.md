# Streptomycin: From Antibacterial Use to Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Streptomycin is an injectable aminoglycoside antibiotic registered in Singapore as a 1 g powder for injection. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **conjunctivitis**. There are **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but these are mostly historical, veterinary, laboratory or indirect, and none is a controlled modern study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the record. From general pharmacology, streptomycin binds the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit and is bactericidal against susceptible aerobic gram-negative bacteria and mycobacteria. The Singapore record does not include a registered indication text.

The plausible link is a narrow one: infectious conjunctivitis caused by organisms to which streptomycin's systemic spectrum applies. Examples are tularemia-related Parinaud oculoglandular syndrome, Brucella keratoconjunctivitis and tuberculous conjunctivitis. The literature includes case reports of these, along with mid-20th-century reports of streptomycin eye-washes.

There is no modern evidence for common bacterial conjunctivitis, where topical agents are standard. The high TxGNN score probably reflects these infection-linked associations rather than a validated new use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Class-level toxicity (from the evidence retrieved):** Streptomycin is ototoxic, particularly to the vestibular system, and the literature includes reports of streptomycin-associated labyrinthitis. Aminoglycosides also carry nephrotoxicity risk.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information, including warnings, contraindications and drug interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but the supporting evidence is weak. There are no clinical trials, and the literature consists of historical reports, case reports, veterinary and laboratory studies, and reviews that mostly concern other drugs or diseases. Established topical agents already cover common bacterial conjunctivitis, and the only registered streptomycin product is injectable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to complete warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A defined target population, such as pathogen-confirmed tularemia, brucellosis or tuberculous conjunctivitis, rather than conjunctivitis in general
- Modern controlled or well-documented clinical data in that population
- A route and formulation assessment, since no ophthalmic product is registered and systemic use carries ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity risk
