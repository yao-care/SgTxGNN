## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, streptomycin is a first-generation aminoglycoside antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit. It was the first clinically effective agent against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and has broad activity against many gram-negative and some gram-positive organisms.

Bacterial conjunctivitis can be caused by organisms susceptible to aminoglycosides (e.g., *Haemophilus* species, staphylococci, and other gram-negative bacteria). Historically, streptomycin was used topically as eye drops or instillations for conjunctivitis and trachoma before newer aminoglycosides such as gentamicin and tobramycin became the standard ophthalmic agents — the tobramycin review in this evidence set (PMID 3317953) explicitly traces this lineage back to streptomycin's discovery.

Mechanistically, the prediction is plausible given the aminoglycoside class effect on susceptible ocular pathogens. However, the supporting evidence predates modern antibiotic resistance patterns, controlled trial standards, and current ophthalmic formulation practices, so the historical precedent alone is not sufficient to establish contemporary clinical utility.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for streptomycin in conjunctivitis consists entirely of historical (1940s–1950s) field studies and case reports, with no registered clinical trials and no modern controlled data. Combined with a blocking data gap in safety warnings/contraindications and the drug's current unmarketed status in Singapore, the evidence base is insufficient to proceed at this time.
