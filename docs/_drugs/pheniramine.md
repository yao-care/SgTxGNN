# Pheniramine: From H1 Antihistamine Use to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Pheniramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine. In Singapore its only registered product is a combination eye-drop solution. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria** (score 99.67%), but there are **no pheniramine-specific trials** and the literature is mostly class-effect data from related antihistamines and case reports. The single retrieved trial was judged an irrelevant match.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for pheniramine is not available in the retrieved record. Based on known information, pheniramine is a first-generation H1 receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in histamine-driven allergic symptoms is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to allergic urticaria.

Urticaria is driven largely by histamine released from mast cells, which causes wheals, flare and itching. Blocking the H1 receptor is therefore a coherent mechanism. The high TxGNN score agrees with this.

The link is class-based rather than drug-specific. The supporting studies involve chlorpheniramine, dexchlorpheniramine, bilastine and astemizole, not pheniramine. This may therefore be closer to an on-class or on-label use than true repurposing, and it should be checked against the local label.

The only Singapore product is an ophthalmic solution. That form would not treat a skin condition such as urticaria, so a systemic formulation would be needed. This is my inference, and the pack's route-compatibility check is still pending.

Other predictions are weaker. Cold urticaria (score 98.4%) has a 1977 double-blind study of related antihistamines but nothing on pheniramine. Nasal cavity disease (99.3%) has only animal-model data. Predictions ranked 3 and 5 to 10 have no supporting evidence, or evidence unrelated to pheniramine.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The retrieved literature shows these signals, which are not label data:
- **Hypersensitivity**: A 2025 case report describes an immediate reaction to pheniramine itself. Anaphylaxis to the related chlorpheniramine has also been reported.
- **Serious event with injection**: A 2025 case report describes loss of consciousness after intravenous pheniramine given with hydrocortisone.
- **Sedation**: This is the most frequent side effect of H1 antihistamines.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The H1-blockade mechanism fits allergic urticaria, but no pheniramine-specific efficacy evidence exists. The one trial retrieved is unrelated. The only Singapore product is an eye drop, and the package insert safety review is still outstanding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to complete the safety screening (currently blocking).
- Confirmation of whether pheniramine already has a labelled allergic or urticarial use in Singapore or elsewhere, which would make this on-label use rather than repurposing.
- A systemic formulation, or a route-compatibility assessment, since the registered ophthalmic solution cannot address urticaria.
- Pheniramine-specific clinical data for urticaria, or an explicit justification for relying on class-effect evidence.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
