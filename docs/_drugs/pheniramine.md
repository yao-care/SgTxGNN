## Pheniramine: Nasal cavity disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Pheniramine is a first-generation H1-antihistamine, originally used for allergic rhinitis, the common cold, allergic conjunctivitis, urticaria and angioedema.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, pheniramine is a first-generation H1-antihistamine, and its efficacy in histamine-mediated allergic conditions is well established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to allergic urticaria, which is driven by histamine release from mast cells.

Urticaria and angioedema are already among pheniramine's known original indications. The prediction is therefore not a true repurposing finding but a restatement of existing use, which is consistent with the very high TxGNN score.

Other predicted indications, such as nasal cavity disease, acute laryngopharyngitis and cold urticaria, also overlap with existing uses.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Allergic urticaria is already within pheniramine's existing use, so this is not a genuine repurposing opportunity. No clinical trials were found, and the retrieved literature contains no efficacy data for pheniramine. The only registered Singapore product is an ophthalmic solution, which is not an obvious route for treating urticaria.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indication and available systemic formulations in Singapore
- Pheniramine-specific efficacy evidence for urticaria, compared with the second-generation antihistamines that are the usual standard of care
