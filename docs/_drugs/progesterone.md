# Progesterone: From an Unspecified Registered Indication to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Progesterone is a natural steroid hormone marketed in Singapore as a vaginal pessary and a vaginal gel, but the registration records do not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Amenorrhea**, and this is probably an established use rather than true repurposing.
More than 40 clinical trials and 20 publications were retrieved, but none is a trial of progesterone as a primary treatment for amenorrhea, so the evidence is **L4 (mechanism and background literature)**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Progesterone is the body's own luteal-phase hormone. Reviews in the retrieved literature describe it, together with estradiol, as controlling the cyclic proliferation and decidualization of the endometrium. They also describe it as acting on the hypothalamic neurons that regulate LH and FSH pulsatility.

In estrogen-primed women, stopping progesterone causes the endometrium to shed. A progestin challenge is therefore a standard diagnostic and therapeutic step in secondary amenorrhea. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this known biology.

Because the registration records list no approved indication, the label should be checked first to see whether amenorrhea is already covered. If it is, this is a confirmation of an existing use rather than new repurposing.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically sound, and progestin challenge is standard in secondary amenorrhea. However, there is no direct trial evidence for progesterone in amenorrhea, and the registered indications and safety information are missing. The other nine predictions (breast fibrocystic disease, benign mammary dysplasia, renal hypoplasia, and several forms of endometriosis and breast adenosis) are also on Hold. They rest on receptor-expression studies, case reports, or preclinical work.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to confirm approved indications, warnings, and contraindications. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- A check of whether amenorrhea is already an on-label use of the two registered products.
- Mechanism of action data for progesterone.
- Direct clinical evidence, such as RCTs or guideline recommendations on progestin therapy in amenorrhea.
