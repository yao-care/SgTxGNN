## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for atropine are not available in the data compiled for this report. Atropine is generally known as a non-selective muscarinic cholinergic antagonist.

Preclinical work suggests that cholinergic signalling in meningeal mast cells and trigeminal afferents may contribute to neurogenic inflammation and pain, which are thought to be involved in migraine. A non-selective muscarinic antagonist could therefore plausibly modulate this pathway.

The caveat is substantial: the supporting studies are in animals (rat dura mater, guinea pig basilar artery) or are unrelated to atropine's use in migraine (anisodamine pharmacology, drug adverse-event reports). In addition, one mouse study of the related prediction, migraine aura, found that muscarinic *activation* suppresses cortical spreading depression, which suggests the direction of effect could be opposite to atropine's action. No human efficacy data exist.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening returned 100 records. Most of those listed are of Moderate severity, notably with other anticholinergic or central nervous system-depressant drugs, including:
- Opioids: fentanyl, alfentanil, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol
- Antihistamines and other anticholinergics: chlorpheniramine, acrivastine, phenyltoloxamine, hyoscyamine, aclidinium, amitriptyline
- Others: botulinum toxin type A, acebutolol, acetylcholine, galantamine, amantadine, loperamide, ethanol

Minor interactions include acetaminophen and hydrochlorothiazide.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no clinical trials and no human efficacy data for atropine in migraine, and the available mechanistic evidence is preclinical and partly points in the opposite direction. The evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- Preclinical or early clinical evidence that muscarinic antagonism, rather than agonism, is beneficial in migraine
- A route and formulation assessment, since registered products are injectable, ophthalmic and oral, and none is labelled for migraine
