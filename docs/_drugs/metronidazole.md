# Metronidazole: From Anaerobic Bacterial and Protozoal Infections to Pneumocystosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Metronidazole is a nitroimidazole antimicrobial originally used for anaerobic bacterial infections, giardiasis, bacterial vaginosis and rosacea.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pneumocystosis** (score 99.99%), but **no clinical trials** are registered and none of the **10 retrieved publications** shows metronidazole working against Pneumocystis.
This top-ranked prediction therefore rests on model output alone, and other lower-ranked predictions (such as cap polyposis) have more plausible support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, metronidazole is a nitroimidazole antimicrobial. Its efficacy in anaerobic bacterial and protozoal infections is established, and it is also used topically for rosacea. It is activated by nitroreduction in anaerobic organisms.

For pneumocystosis, this link is weak. Pneumocystis is a fungus that lacks the nitroreduction pathway metronidazole depends on, so no plausible mechanism is evident. The high model score appears to reflect general anti-infective associations. The retrieved literature consists of general antiparasitic reviews and HIV opportunistic infection case reports. In those reports, metronidazole was given for a co-existing infection such as amoebiasis, not for Pneumocystis.

Among the other predictions, **cap polyposis** (rank 9) has the most plausible rationale. It is linked to gut dysbiosis, and case reports describe remission after metronidazole-containing regimens. **Ulcerative proctosigmoiditis** (rank 3) has indirect support from metronidazole's use in other inflammatory bowel conditions. These are noted as follow-up research questions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The pneumocystosis prediction is purely computational (L5). There are no clinical trials, and the retrieved literature does not support metronidazole activity against Pneumocystis. Pneumocystis lacks the pathway metronidazole needs, and standard therapy is trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole-based. Cap polyposis, ulcerative proctosigmoiditis, myiasis (adjunctive use) and vulvar ulceration are better candidates for further exploration, but each is still only a research question at L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- For cap polyposis, a structured review of case reports and, if justified, a small controlled study
- Re-evaluation of the model's predictions after removing associations driven by general anti-infective similarity
