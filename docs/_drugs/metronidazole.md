# Metronidazole: From Anaerobic and Protozoal Infections to Pneumocystosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Metronidazole is a nitroimidazole antimicrobial, used mainly against anaerobic bacterial and protozoal infections such as amoebiasis and trichomoniasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pneumocystosis**, but **no relevant clinical trials** and **no supporting publications** were found.
The high model score is not backed by clinical evidence, so this is a model-only prediction (L5).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Metronidazole is a nitroimidazole antimicrobial. It is active against anaerobic bacteria and protozoa, and its efficacy in those infections is well established.

The link to pneumocystosis is weak. *Pneumocystis* is a fungus and has no nitroimidazole-sensitive pathway. Standard therapy is trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX), and one review in the evidence set (PMID 7355683) says the same. The retrieved literature consists of general antiparasitic or AIDS opportunistic-infection reviews. In two case reports, metronidazole appears only as treatment for a co-existing amoebic or diarrhoeal illness, not for pneumocystis pneumonia.

The score of 99.99% therefore most likely reflects the model's pattern-matching across anti-infective drugs, not a biological rationale.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

The search returned 20 registered studies, but none involves metronidazole or pneumocystosis. They are unrelated primary-care, health-services, behavioural and care-delivery studies, so none is listed here.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported by the model score only. There are no relevant trials, and the literature contains no direct evidence. The known pharmacology also argues against it, since *Pneumocystis* is a fungus without a nitroimidazole target and TMP-SMX is the established therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any direct preclinical or clinical evidence that metronidazole is active against *Pneumocystis*
- Mechanism of action data
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, for the safety screen
- Consideration of higher-ranked alternatives from the same run. Cap polyposis and vulvar ulceration (both L4, advanced to S1) have a more coherent mechanistic rationale, and the vulvar case could reach L3 after full-text review of the vulvar Crohn's cohorts.
