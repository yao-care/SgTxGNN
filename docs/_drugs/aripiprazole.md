# Aripiprazole: From Schizophrenia and Bipolar Disorder to Major Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Aripiprazole is an atypical antipsychotic originally used for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and autism-spectrum-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **major affective disorder** (in practice, major depressive disorder as add-on treatment),
with **35 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several completed Phase 3 randomized trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aripiprazole is a partial agonist at dopamine D2/D3 receptors and at serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, and an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the compiled drug record, so this description comes from the mechanistic rationale prepared for the prediction. Together, these actions may adjust dopamine and serotonin signalling in mood-regulating brain circuits and strengthen the response to serotonergic antidepressants.

Bipolar disorder is itself a major affective disorder, and aripiprazole is already used for it. Depressive episodes and manic episodes share overlapping neurochemical pathways. Adding aripiprazole to an antidepressant is therefore a natural step for patients whose depression has not responded adequately. Reviews also note that several atypical antipsychotics, aripiprazole among them, have been approved in the US as add-on treatment for major depressive disorder.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (taken from drug-disease network links, not from label text, and to be confirmed against the package insert): conditions of particular note include

- **Metabolic:** diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperlipidemia, obesity
- **Cardiovascular:** myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, orthostatic hypotension, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, cerebrovascular disorder
- **Haematological:** neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia
- **Neurological:** epilepsy, neuroleptic malignant syndrome

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The first 20 retrieved include one **Major** interaction, with bupropion, and moderate interactions with, for example:

- antidiabetic agents (metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin)
- anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine)
- CYP-inhibiting drugs (clarithromycin, cimetidine)
- cisapride

Note that bupropion is often used alongside aripiprazole in depression treatment, so this pairing needs particular attention.

**Other predicted indications:** aripiprazole has been linked to impulsive-compulsive behaviors, and to trichotillomania as an adverse event.

Please refer to the package insert for key warnings and formal contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large, completed, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials and recent network meta-analyses support add-on aripiprazole in major depressive disorder. The drug is already marketed in Singapore in 14 registrations. However, the Singapore approved-indication text and package-insert safety data are missing, and the knowledge graph lists many metabolic, cardiovascular and haematological cautions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package-insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications from HSA, to confirm whether depression is already covered
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A safety monitoring plan covering metabolic parameters, blood counts and cardiovascular risk, with an interaction review for bupropion and CYP-inhibiting drugs
- A dosing strategy for adjunctive use, as the trials point to low doses
