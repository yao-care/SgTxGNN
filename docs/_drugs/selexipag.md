# Selexipag: From Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension to Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension Associated with Congenital Heart Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Selexipag is an oral prostacyclin (IP) receptor agonist originally developed and used for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) in general.
The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **PAH associated with congenital heart disease (PAH-CHD)**,
a recognized clinical subtype of PAH, with **2 clinical trials** and **15 publications** currently supporting this direction — including a post-hoc analysis of the pivotal GRIPHON RCT.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed DrugBank mechanism-of-action data is currently unavailable for Selexipag. Based on the supporting literature in this pack, Selexipag is an oral, selective prostacyclin (IP) receptor agonist — pharmacologically distinct from prostanoid analogues — that induces pulmonary vasodilation and has anti-remodeling effects on the pulmonary vasculature. It is approved for treating PAH in adults, and this efficacy has been established through randomized controlled trials in the general PAH population.

PAH-CHD is not a separate disease but a recognized WHO Group 1 subtype of PAH, arising when unrepaired or repaired congenital heart defects (e.g., Eisenmenger syndrome, corrected shunts) drive the same pulmonary vascular remodeling and increased pulmonary vascular resistance seen in idiopathic PAH. Because the underlying pathophysiology largely overlaps with the general PAH population in which Selexipag's efficacy was proven, mechanistic extrapolation to PAH-CHD is biologically plausible.

This is further supported by real-world data: a post-hoc subgroup analysis of the pivotal Phase 3 GRIPHON trial specifically evaluated Selexipag in corrected CHD-PAH patients, and multiple case series (including pediatric cohorts) describe its use as an oral alternative to parenteral prostacyclin therapy in CHD-PAH and Eisenmenger syndrome, where the risks of parenteral administration are a particular concern.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Selexipag is not currently marketed in Singapore, and mechanism-of-action and safety/labeling data (warnings, contraindications) are both missing — the latter being a blocking gap for initial safety screening. While the repurposing evidence itself (GRIPHON RCT subgroup, multiple case series, and an ongoing pediatric Phase 2 trial) is reasonably supportive for PAH-CHD, the absence of local regulatory status and safety documentation means the candidate cannot yet proceed to formal evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/HSA package insert with warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking data gap)
- Mechanism-of-action data via DrugBank API query
- Confirmation of Singapore market entry plans/registration pathway for Selexipag
- Given several supporting studies are pediatric, dosing and safety data specific to pediatric CHD-PAH patients
