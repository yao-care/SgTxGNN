# Ticlopidine: From Antiplatelet Therapy to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ticlopidine is an oral antiplatelet drug (P2Y12 antagonist) that is currently marketed in Singapore.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **rheumatoid arthritis**.
Support is thin: **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 small, decades-old studies (1985, 1989)** that tested ticlopidine directly in this condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Ticlopidine is known to be a P2Y12 antagonist with antiplatelet and hemorheological (blood-flow) effects.

Two older clinical reports tested ticlopidine in rheumatoid arthritis. They looked at serum sulphydryl levels and whole blood filterability, which points to a microvascular or blood-flow rationale. Neither shows true disease-modifying or anti-inflammatory efficacy.

The prediction is therefore best read as a research question. Antiplatelet effects might help with RA-related vascular or rheological changes, but this is unproven. The model score alone is not evidence of efficacy.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

## Literature Evidence

| PMID | Year | Type | Journal | Key Findings |
|------|-----|------|------|---------|
| [2790405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2790405/) | 1989 | Double-blind, placebo-controlled study | Br J Rheumatol | 40 RA patients on an NSAID; ticlopidine improved ESR (-28%) and whole blood filterability versus placebo (p<0.01) |
| [3000794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3000794/) | 1985 | Clinical study (small, uncontrolled) | Eur J Clin Pharmacol | 22 RA patients on 250 mg/day for 18 months; fewer involved joints, lower ESR and Tc-index, higher serum SH levels |
| [16729012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16729012/) | 2006 | Case report | Nat Clin Pract Cardiovasc Med | RA patient with lupus anticoagulant and myocardial microangiopathy; context only, not efficacy evidence |
| [26939212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26939212/) | 2015 | Case report | Rom J Intern Med | RA patient on corticosteroids and NSAID with atherothrombotic disease and severe bleeding; shows the bleeding-risk setting |
| [27188755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27188755/) | 2016 | Open-label study | BMC Musculoskelet Disord | Studied clopidogrel (not ticlopidine) in systemic sclerosis, with RA patients as a comparator; clopidogrel was associated with worsening endothelial function |

Only the first two studies tested ticlopidine in RA. Both are small and rely on laboratory or biomarker endpoints, with no modern clinical outcomes.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Concerns**: Hematologic toxicity (neutropenia, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, aplastic anemia) is a known concern with ticlopidine. This matters in RA, where patients often take myelosuppressive DMARDs. Thienopyridine-associated TTP has also been described in the literature.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings, contraindications and drug interaction information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is two small studies from the 1980s with laboratory endpoints, and there are no registered trials. The known hematologic toxicity is a real concern in RA patients on other myelosuppressive drugs. The safety documentation (package insert) is also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A modern controlled study with clinical RA outcomes (for example, disease activity score)
- A risk assessment of ticlopidine against other antiplatelet options in RA patients on DMARDs
