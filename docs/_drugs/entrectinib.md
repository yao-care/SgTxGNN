# Entrectinib: From Oncology Use (NTRK/ROS1-Positive Tumours) to Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia

## One-Sentence Summary

Entrectinib is an oral TRK/ROS1/ALK kinase inhibitor, marketed in Singapore as Rozlytrek capsules; the approved indication text is not available in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model ranks **multiple endocrine neoplasia** as its top prediction, but only **1 clinical trial** (terminated, 2 patients) and **1 publication** (indirect, preclinical/mechanistic) are linked to it.
Of the top 10 predictions, **female breast carcinoma** has the strongest supporting evidence (3 clinical trials and 7 publications), so it is discussed alongside the top-ranked prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, entrectinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets TRKA/B/C, ROS1 and ALK, and it has some activity against RET in certain contexts.

Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2) is driven by activating germline RET mutations. Inhibiting RET signalling is therefore mechanistically plausible. However, the only publication linked to this prediction concerns resistance to *selective* RET inhibitors (selpercatinib, pralsetinib) in a RET-driven medullary thyroid carcinoma. This is indirect and does not show that entrectinib works in MEN. The one linked trial is a precision-oncology platform study, not specific to MEN.

For the second-tier candidate, **female breast carcinoma**, the rationale is stronger. NTRK fusions (e.g., ETV6-NTRK3) occur in rare breast secretory carcinoma, and entrectinib is a potent TRK inhibitor. Evidence is strongest for NTRK-fusion-positive tumours, not for unselected breast cancer.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs; the 20 reviewed here include several rated **Major**:
  - Aprepitant
  - Dexamethasone
  - Clarithromycin
  - Dolasetron

  Many **Moderate** interactions were also noted, for example famotidine, loperamide, bisacodyl, budesonide, cimetidine, levofloxacin, naltrexone, saxagliptin, empagliflozin and several bowel-preparation laxatives. Minor interactions include naloxegol and metronidazole. The pattern of Major interactions with aprepitant and clarithromycin suggests a CYP3A-related liability, but the data do not state the mechanism.
- **Safety signal from the literature**: A 2025 case report describes pulmonary tumour thrombotic microangiopathy (PTTM) progressing despite tumour response during entrectinib therapy in NTRK fusion-positive colon cancer.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (multiple endocrine neoplasia, the top-ranked prediction)

**Rationale:**
- The high TxGNN score (98.58%) is not backed by interpretable evidence. The only trial was terminated with 2 patients and is not MEN-specific, and the only publication is indirect and about other RET inhibitors.
- By contrast, **female breast carcinoma** could be considered **Proceed with Guardrails**, restricted to biomarker-selected (NTRK/ROS1/ALK fusion-positive) patients, because of the large STARTRK-2 basket study. Since entrectinib already holds tumour-agnostic approval for NTRK fusion-positive tumours elsewhere, this is largely an extension of established use rather than a new repurposing finding.
- The other predictions (amenorrhoea, thrombocytopenia, cytomegalovirus infection, pulmonary hypertension and others) have no therapeutic evidence and should stay on Hold. Thrombocytopenia and pulmonary hypertension are more plausibly adverse-event contexts than treatment targets.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert (approved indications, warnings, contraindications), which is currently missing and blocks safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank), including RET activity relevant to MEN2
- For MEN2: preclinical or early clinical data on entrectinib against RET-mutant disease, compared with approved selective RET inhibitors
- For breast carcinoma: breast-specific results from STARTRK-2 and an NTRK-fusion testing and patient selection plan
- A drug-interaction management plan, especially for strong CYP3A-related interacting drugs
