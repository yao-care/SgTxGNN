# Entrectinib: From NTRK Fusion-Positive Solid Tumours to Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia

## One-Sentence Summary

Entrectinib is an oral multi-kinase inhibitor (TRK/ROS1/ALK) used in fusion-driven solid tumours.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN)**.
Only **2 linked clinical trials** and **1 publication** are on file, and none of them studies entrectinib in MEN patients, so this remains a research question.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the record. Based on known information, entrectinib inhibits TRK A/B/C, ROS1 and ALK kinases. It is used in tumours driven by abnormal kinase signalling.

MEN type 2 is driven by activating germline mutations in the RET kinase gene. A link to entrectinib is therefore plausible, because RET is also a receptor tyrosine kinase. But the link is indirect. Entrectinib is not a selective RET inhibitor, and the evidence pack does not show it acting on RET.

The only publication linked to this prediction concerns resistance to selective RET inhibitors (selpercatinib) in a RET-driven medullary thyroid carcinoma. It says nothing about entrectinib activity in MEN. Both linked trials involve other tumour types. The high model score is therefore a computational signal, not clinical support.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score and an indirect RET-related mechanism. No trial or publication tests entrectinib in MEN, and the one linked paper concerns other RET inhibitors. The evidence supports keeping this as a research question, not moving to clinical evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert with warnings and contraindications, which is a blocking gap for any safety screening
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank, including any evidence of entrectinib activity against RET
- Preclinical or clinical data on entrectinib in RET-driven MEN2 tumours
- Separate review of the rank 8 prediction, **female breast carcinoma**. It has a stronger evidence base (L2, with a Phase 2 entrectinib study in ROS1-positive invasive lobular carcinoma) and was rated Proceed with Guardrails, limited to NTRK- or ROS1-positive patients confirmed by molecular testing.
