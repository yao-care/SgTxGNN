# Calfactant: From Neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome to Adult Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Calfactant is a natural calf lung surfactant, originally used for neonatal respiratory distress syndrome and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **adult acute respiratory distress syndrome**, with **1 clinical trial** (a terminated Phase 3 study) and **5 publications** currently bearing on this question.
Because ARDS is already a listed original indication, this is not a true repurposing candidate. The open question is whether adults benefit.

**Evidence level caveat:** The data compiled for this report assign L1. However, the only adult Phase 3 trial was terminated, not completed, so it does not meet the strict L1 definition (two or more completed Phase 3 RCTs). The evidence is better read as direct but non-confirmatory.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. The surfactant replacement mechanism is well established, however. Calfactant is a calf lung surfactant extract containing phospholipids and surfactant proteins B and C. In ARDS, endogenous surfactant is inactivated and depleted. This causes alveolar collapse, reduced lung compliance and impaired oxygenation. Replacing surfactant is therefore biologically plausible.

The original indications already include neonatal respiratory distress syndrome and ARDS. The real question is whether the benefit seen in neonates and children extends to adults. Earlier trials of several surfactant preparations in adults with acute lung injury were unsuccessful. Calfactant contains a high level of surfactant protein B, which was the rationale for testing it again in adults.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The surfactant replacement mechanism is plausible and the drug is already marketed in Singapore. However, the only adult Phase 3 trial was terminated, and the other supporting literature is mainly paediatric or review material. There is no confirmed adult efficacy. The other nine predicted indications, such as neurolymphomatosis, plasmacytoma and myasthenia gravis, have no plausible mechanism and no trials or literature, and are likely knowledge-graph artifacts. Pulmonary edema has only a partial rationale that overlaps with ARDS.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The reason for termination of NCT00682500 and its published results, including the mortality and ventilation outcomes in adults
- The Singapore package insert, covering approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A subgroup analysis of adults versus children in the Adult Calfactant in ARDS trial
- Confirmation that the Singapore product's intratracheal route suits the intended adult ventilated population
