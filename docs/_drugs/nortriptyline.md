## Nortriptyline: Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nortriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant, originally associated with anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **15 publications** (including one controlled study in children and adolescents and a Cochrane-type review of tricyclics for ADHD) currently supporting this direction.

By the strict trial-based rules this would be closer to L3, so the rating should be read as conservative-to-moderate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, nortriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant that mainly inhibits norepinephrine reuptake, with weaker effects on serotonin. Noradrenergic enhancement in prefrontal circuits is also the mechanism of non-stimulant ADHD drugs such as atomoxetine, which gives a plausible basis for efficacy.

The original indications (anxiety, neurotic and dysthymic disorders) and ADHD are all neuropsychiatric conditions that respond to modulation of monoamine signalling. A PET study (PMID 24345533) showed nortriptyline occupies the norepinephrine transporter in patients, supporting target engagement in the relevant system. Reviews describe the more noradrenergic secondary-amine tricyclics (desipramine and nortriptyline) as established alternative treatments for ADHD, although their narrow therapeutic index and cardiovascular toxicity have limited use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, myocardial infarction and coronary thrombosis, stroke and other cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, epilepsy, manic bipolar affective disorder, open-angle glaucoma and glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia, substance abuse/dependence, liver failure, kidney failure and uremia, hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis), and hemoglobinopathy.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include epinephrine, bupropion, lorcaserin, potassium citrate and dolasetron. Moderate-level examples include famotidine, cimetidine, clarithromycin, morphine, loperamide, and several anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine).
- **ADHD-specific considerations (from the literature)**: cardiac conduction effects and a narrow therapeutic index, anticholinergic effects, and overdose toxicity. ECG monitoring is advisable, particularly in children and adolescents. Tricyclics can occasionally worsen tics.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Nortriptyline has a plausible noradrenergic mechanism for ADHD, a published controlled study in children and adolescents, a Cochrane-type review and several supporting clinical reports. However, there are no registered trials, the key studies are small and dated, and the cardiac and anticholinergic safety profile is a significant limitation compared with current first-line options.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore Health Sciences Authority (HSA), which are currently missing and are required for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A cardiac safety plan, including baseline and follow-up ECG, especially for patients with the conduction or QT-related conditions listed above
- Review of the interaction list against typical ADHD co-medications
- Ideally, a modern controlled trial in the target population, positioned against current standard ADHD treatments
