# Cisplatin: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Cisplatin is a platinum-based chemotherapy drug, originally used to treat ovarian cancer and related ovarian tumours.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **20 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction. Most trials are indirect, and only a few compare cisplatin directly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known pharmacology, cisplatin forms DNA crosslinks that cause replication stress and cell death (apoptosis). This effect is especially relevant in tumours with defective homologous recombination DNA repair, such as those with BRCA1/2 or PALB2 mutations, and in triple-negative breast cancer.

Ovarian cancer and breast cancer share a biological link through BRCA-related DNA repair deficiency. The original indication list even includes hereditary breast ovarian cancer syndrome. Tumours that cannot repair DNA crosslinks should be more sensitive to platinum drugs, which supports the model's prediction. The cited literature on PALB2 and on cisplatin response in breast tumours is consistent with this reasoning.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening returned 100 interacting drugs in total, and the first 20 are listed in the data compiled for this report. Almost all are rated Moderate, with one Minor (ascorbic acid). The main groups are:
  - Proton pump inhibitors (rabeprazole, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole)
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, triamcinolone, betamethasone, beclomethasone)
  - Amphotericin B products (conventional, lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate, liposomal)
  - Mesalazine and balsalazide
  - Metronidazole, exenatide and kanamycin

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Mechanistically, cisplatin is plausible for breast cancer, especially BRCA/PALB2-related and triple-negative disease. However, the direct evidence consists of one small-to-moderate completed Phase 2 trial (NCT01670500, no results summarised) and one ongoing Phase 3 trial without results. There are no breast-specific RCT publications. In addition, the Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications are not available, so safety screening cannot be completed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT01670500 and, when available, NCT03201861
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (download and review the HSA documents)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the two Singapore registrations
- Definition of the target population (e.g., BRCA-mutated or triple-negative breast cancer) and a plan for monitoring toxicity

**Other predicted indications in the compiled data:**
- **Choriocarcinoma of ovary, gonadal germ cell tumour, adult germ cell tumour** (L1, Proceed with Guardrails): these have many Phase 2/3 trials of cisplatin-based regimens such as BEP, and they are close to the existing ovarian indications. They may be stronger repurposing candidates than breast carcinoma.
- **Ovarian primitive germ cell tumour** (L3, Proceed with Guardrails): the evidence is mostly extrapolated from the wider germ cell tumour trials.
- **Five testicular yolk sac tumour histologic patterns** (L5, Hold): model prediction only, with no trials or literature.
