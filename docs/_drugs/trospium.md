## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, trospium is a peripheral antimuscarinic. It blocks muscarinic receptors on smooth muscle, which is the basis of its use in overactive bladder.

Antimuscarinic antispasmodics are also used to reduce gut smooth-muscle spasm. So the drug class is plausible for IBS, especially when abdominal pain and diarrhea predominate. This is the main reason for the high model score.

There are two caveats:
- The only linked paper concerns antimuscarinic use in older adults with dementia and overactive bladder. It does not study IBS.
- Anticholinergic effects such as constipation and dry mouth are a concern, particularly in constipation-predominant IBS.
