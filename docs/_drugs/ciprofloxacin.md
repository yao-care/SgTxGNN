# Ciprofloxacin: From Antibacterial Therapy to Diffuse Scleroderma

## One-Sentence Summary

Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic that is widely marketed in Singapore.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **diffuse scleroderma**, with a very high score but **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications**, so support is limited and mostly indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Ciprofloxacin is known as an antibacterial that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. That mechanism does not directly explain an effect on scleroderma.

Two indirect signals support the prediction:

1. **Antifibrotic effect.** A 2010 study examined oral ciprofloxacin for skin fibrosis in scleroderma patients. Scleroderma is characterised by excessive skin fibrosis and has no established effective drug therapy.
2. **Gut bacterial overgrowth.** Small bowel bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) is common in systemic sclerosis, so antibiotic treatment of gut dysbiosis is plausible. This would treat a complication of the disease, not the disease itself.

The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction only. It is not clinical proof.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output plus one small, indirect literature signal. No clinical trials are registered and the 2010 study results could not be confirmed. Current status is a research question, not a candidate for clinical use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full text and outcome data of the 2010 ciprofloxacin scleroderma trial, and any replication
- Clarification of whether any benefit is antifibrotic or only from treating gut bacterial overgrowth
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, since safety data is currently missing
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank

**Note:** In the same prediction list, **septicemic plague** (rank 10) has much stronger support: a completed Phase 2 randomised trial (NCT01243437, n=200) and a 2025 NEJM randomised trial in bubonic plague. It reaches L2 with a "Proceed with Guardrails" recommendation, and would be worth evaluating as a separate report. Whether plague is already a labelled use needs to be checked first.
