## Acarbose: From Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus to Focal Stiff Limb Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Acarbose is an oral intestinal alpha-glucosidase inhibitor, originally used to control blood sugar in type 2 diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **focal stiff limb syndrome**, but this is a TxGNN model (deep learning) prediction only, with **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Acarbose inhibits alpha-glucosidases in the small intestine, which slows carbohydrate digestion and reduces the rise in blood glucose after meals. Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the compiled data, but this is its well-known pharmacology.

Focal stiff limb syndrome is a neurological, likely autoimmune, disorder involving impaired GABAergic and glycine signalling in the central nervous system. Acarbose acts only in the gut and has no known effect on this pathway. The high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases (for example, the association of stiff-person-spectrum disorders with diabetes), not biological evidence. There is no plausible mechanistic reason for this prediction at present.

The same pattern holds for the other top-ranked predictions, including classic stiff person syndrome, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, opsismodysplasia and several localized lipodystrophies. None has any supporting trial or publication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. There are no registered trials or publications for focal stiff limb syndrome, and acarbose has no plausible effect on its underlying neurological pathology.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any clinical or preclinical evidence linking acarbose to stiff limb or stiff-person-spectrum disorders. Without it, this candidate should not advance.
- The Singapore package insert, to confirm label warnings, contraindications and approved indications.
- Confirmation of the mechanism of action from DrugBank.

**Side note on a more credible prediction:** Among the lower-ranked predictions, **type 1 diabetes mellitus** has a plausible mechanism, as adjunct therapy to insulin that blunts post-meal glucose spikes. It also has controlled-study literature, including a double-blind placebo-controlled study ([PMID 10227568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10227568/)), a multicentre study ([PMID 9051366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9051366/)) and a network meta-analysis ([PMID 33300282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33300282/)). Reported benefits appear modest and gastrointestinal side effects are a concern. If a diabetes-related repurposing direction is of interest, this indication should be evaluated as a separate candidate.
