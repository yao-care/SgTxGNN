# Acarbose: From Diabetes Management to Focal Stiff Limb Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Acarbose is an oral alpha-glucosidase inhibitor that slows carbohydrate digestion and is marketed in Singapore as an antidiabetic. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **focal stiff limb syndrome**, but this rests on the model score alone, with **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting it. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Acarbose is known to inhibit intestinal alpha-glucosidase. This delays carbohydrate digestion and lowers the rise in blood glucose after meals.

No plausible pathway links this mechanism to focal stiff limb syndrome, a rare, stiff-person-spectrum neurological disorder that is usually autoimmune. The pack identifies no mechanistic link. The prediction is best read as a statistical association in the knowledge graph, possibly driven by shared diabetes-related neighbours. It is not evidence of a biological effect.

A very high score (0.996) does not make this a strong lead. The closely related "classic stiff person syndrome" received an identical score, and it also has no trials or literature.
