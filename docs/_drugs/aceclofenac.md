## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, aceclofenac is a COX-2-preferential NSAID that inhibits prostaglandin synthesis and relieves inflammatory pain. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and osteoarthritis is well documented.

For the top-ranked prediction, **no mechanistic link could be identified**. Brachyolmia-amelogenesis imperfecta syndrome is a heritable skeletal and dental disorder. A symptomatic anti-inflammatory drug would not be expected to modify its underlying genetic pathology. The high score is a model output only.

The same holds for the other highly ranked skeletal dysplasias: acromesomelic dysplasia (Hunter-Thompson type), brachyolmia and pseudoachondroplasia. At most, NSAIDs could offer symptomatic pain relief in these conditions.

A few lower-ranked predictions have some indirect plausibility:
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy (rank 8):** Aceclofenac is already used in ankylosing spondylitis, so this is mostly a re-labeling of an existing indication and not true repurposing.
- **Myosclerosis and hypermobility of coccyx (ranks 3 and 6):** NSAIDs are commonly used for musculoskeletal pain, but there is no aceclofenac-specific evidence.
- **Rheumatoid vasculitis and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis (ranks 7 and 9):** These are adjacent to the approved rheumatoid arthritis use. Rheumatoid vasculitis needs immunosuppression, and no paediatric data or dosing are available.
- **Kummell disease (rank 10):** Symptomatic pain relief is the most that could be expected.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One published case report describes generalized pustular psoriasis precipitated by aceclofenac. No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked predictions are rare genetic skeletal or dental disorders with no clinical trials, no literature and no plausible disease-modifying mechanism for a symptomatic NSAID. The only prediction with literature (inflammatory spondylopathy) overlaps with an existing indication and does not represent new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for aceclofenac (DrugBank)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, which are required before any safety screening
- Singapore approved-indication text for the registered product
- Full review of the literature for inflammatory spondylopathy, if that indication is pursued
- Paediatric safety and dosing data before considering polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis
- A clear rationale for symptomatic-only use (for example, pain management) if any skeletal dysplasia is considered
