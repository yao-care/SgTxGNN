# Laronidase: From Mucopolysaccharidosis I to Lysosomal Storage Disease with Skeletal Involvement

## One-Sentence Summary

Laronidase is a recombinant enzyme replacement therapy (alpha-L-iduronidase) used for mucopolysaccharidosis type I (MPS I).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lysosomal storage disease with skeletal involvement**, but this is mostly a broader label for the already-approved MPS I use rather than a true repurposing.

**Note on evidence level:** The Evidence Pack labels this prediction L1. Under the level rules, L1 needs at least 2 completed Phase 3 RCTs. This prediction has no listed trials, and its literature is reviews, a case report and an in vitro study. The pivotal Phase 3 RCT (PMID 15126990) concerns MPS I and appears only under the Sanfilippo entry. I therefore graded it L4 (mechanism and preclinical evidence).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Laronidase is recombinant human alpha-L-iduronidase (IDUA). It replaces the enzyme that is deficient in MPS I (Hurler, Hurler-Scheie and Scheie syndromes). Without this enzyme, glycosaminoglycans (dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate) build up in tissues and cause skeletal dysostosis multiplex, among other problems.

The predicted label, "lysosomal storage disease with skeletal involvement", largely overlaps with MPS I. In vitro work shows laronidase is taken up by MPS I fibroblasts and osteoblasts, mainly through mannose 6-phosphate receptors. It then travels to lysosomes, which supports a bone-relevant mechanism (PMID 18758061).

The prediction should not be read as covering other lysosomal storage diseases. The mechanism only applies to biochemically confirmed IDUA deficiency. Other lysosomal disorders have different enzyme or transporter defects that laronidase cannot correct.
