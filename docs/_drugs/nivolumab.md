# Nivolumab: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer and Metastatic Melanoma to Non-Cutaneous Melanoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Nivolumab is a PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor originally used for non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell lung carcinoma, and metastatic melanoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **non-cutaneous melanoma** (for example mucosal, uveal and other non-skin subtypes).
Currently, **40 clinical trials** and **9 publications** are linked to this prediction, but most trials are early-phase combination studies and the literature is mainly cohort studies and case reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the pharmacology records, nivolumab is an antibody that targets programmed cell death 1 (PD-1, gene PDCD1). Blocking PD-1 is understood to restore T-cell antitumour activity. This is the same mechanism that underlies its approved use in melanoma.

Non-cutaneous melanomas (mucosal, uveal and other subtypes) are melanocyte-derived cancers that share PD-1/PD-L1 axis biology with cutaneous melanoma. They usually respond less well, often because of a lower mutational burden, so benefit should not be assumed to match that seen in skin melanoma.

Other melanoma subtypes also appear in the ranked predictions. Mucosal melanoma and acral lentiginous melanoma each reach L2 and the same "Proceed with Guardrails" decision. Several other subtypes have only case reports or no data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions** (14 records): 
  - **Major**: lenalidomide, pomalidomide and thalidomide.
  - **Moderate**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone, methylprednisolone, deflazacort) and idelalisib.
  - The corticosteroid interactions matter because steroids are the usual treatment for immune-related adverse events.
- **Signals from the literature** (case reports, not label data): fatal myocarditis with rhabdomyolysis after ipilimumab plus nivolumab, late-onset fulminant myocarditis, sarcoid-like pulmonary granulomatosis, capillary leak syndrome, bullous pemphigoid and colitis.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Nivolumab is already marketed in Singapore, and the PD-1 mechanism is shared with its approved melanoma use. Phase 2 and observational evidence supports activity in non-cutaneous melanoma, particularly mucosal and acral subtypes. However, no completed Phase 3 trial for this indication exists, response rates are lower than in cutaneous melanoma, and the evidence comes largely from combination, early-phase or small terminated studies.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which is not yet available in the compiled data
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Subtype-specific efficacy data (mucosal, uveal and acral), ideally from randomised or larger prospective studies, including Asian populations
- An immune-related adverse event monitoring plan, with attention to myocarditis and to steroid use
- Review of results from the ongoing Phase 2 trials in mucosal and acral melanoma
