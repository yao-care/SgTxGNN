# Trastuzumab: From HER2-Positive Breast Cancer to Progesterone-Receptor Positive Breast Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Trastuzumab is a HER2-targeted monoclonal antibody already used in HER2-positive breast cancer. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progesterone-receptor positive breast cancer**. The prediction is supported by **36 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, though most of this evidence concerns HER2-positive disease in general, not progesterone-receptor status. This is better read as confirmation of a subgroup within an existing use than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Trastuzumab binds the extracellular domain of HER2. This triggers antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and inhibits HER2 signalling. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the drug record, but this mechanism is well established for the drug class.

Its effect depends on HER2 overexpression or amplification, not on progesterone-receptor (PR) status. Progesterone-receptor positive tumours that are also HER2-positive (often hormone-receptor positive/HER2-positive, or "triple-positive" disease) are a receptor-defined subgroup of HER2-positive breast cancer. In this subgroup, crosstalk between hormone and HER2 pathways is the rationale for combining trastuzumab with endocrine therapy or CDK4/6 inhibitors.

The prediction is therefore reasonable only for confirmed HER2-positive disease. It does not suggest benefit in PR-positive, HER2-negative tumours.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials and a body of HR-positive/HER2-positive literature support trastuzumab-based therapy. However, efficacy is driven by HER2 status, not progesterone-receptor status. This is a subgroup of an existing use, so any recommendation should be restricted to confirmed HER2-positive disease.

Other predicted indications are weaker. Evidence is limited for luminal A/B (L2) and the normal-like subtype (L3). The PR-negative breast cancer prediction mirrors this one (L1, same HER2 caveat). The remaining predictions have no supporting evidence and should be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Approved-indication wording from the Singapore licences, to confirm the existing label scope.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Confirmation of HER2 status as an eligibility requirement in any use.
- Subgroup results by PR status from the key Phase 3 trials, including confirmation of HER2 eligibility in NCT01275677.
- A cardiac safety monitoring plan.
