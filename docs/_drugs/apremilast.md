# Apremilast: From Psoriatic Arthritis to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Apremilast is an oral PDE4 inhibitor, first approved for active psoriatic arthritis according to the literature. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder** (score 98.66%), but **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** support this specific prediction. The best-supported prediction for this drug is **rheumatoid arthritis** (rank 3), which has Phase 2 trial data and a published Phase II RCT.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the dataset. Based on the literature, apremilast is an oral small-molecule PDE4 inhibitor. Blocking PDE4 raises intracellular cAMP and lowers pro-inflammatory mediators such as TNF-alpha, IL-17 and IL-23. Its efficacy in psoriatic arthritis is established.

For migraine, the link is speculative. It rests on possible PDE4/cAMP signalling and neuroinflammation effects, and the only support is the TxGNN score. No trial or paper in the dataset tests apremilast in migraine, and the same applies to the second-ranked "migraine with brainstem aura".

The rheumatoid arthritis prediction is far better grounded. Apremilast suppressed spontaneous TNF-alpha production in RA synovial cells and reduced Th1/Th17 activity in a mouse arthritis model. Clinical benefit in the Phase II RCT was modest, and no Phase 3 programme exists.
