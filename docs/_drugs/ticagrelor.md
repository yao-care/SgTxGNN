## One-Sentence Summary

Ticagrelor is an oral antiplatelet drug (P2Y12 inhibitor) whose relations recorded in the knowledge graph include acute coronary syndrome and peripheral arterial disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **intracranial arteriosclerosis**, with **2 clinical trials** and **2 publications** in the data compiled for this report. None of them reports efficacy results for ticagrelor in this disease yet.

**Note on evidence level:** The data compiled for this report grades this indication as L1. I have downgraded it to L5. The Phase 3 CAPTIVA trial (NCT05047172) studies the right disease, but it is still active and has no results. Neither the trial registry data nor the publications confirm that ticagrelor is one of its arms. The other trial (NCT06714526) is small, still recruiting, and has no results. Neither study provides outcome data showing that ticagrelor works for this disease, so the evidence supports only a model prediction for now.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Ticagrelor is generally known as a reversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist that blocks platelet aggregation. Its established role is in preventing clots in coronary artery disease.

Symptomatic intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis involves platelet-driven clotting and small clots travelling downstream from the narrowed artery. Blocking platelets is therefore a plausible approach. Ticagrelor is also being studied in related cerebrovascular settings, such as minor stroke and TIA. A large randomized trial (PMID 34708996) compared it with clopidogrel in stroke/TIA patients carrying CYP2C19 loss-of-function genes. That trial concerns stroke prevention in general, not intracranial arteriosclerosis specifically, so it supports the mechanism rather than this exact indication.

A small cohort report (PMID 39658130) describes ticagrelor 60 mg twice daily plus aspirin for intracranial stenting. It is experience from a single centre, not a comparative efficacy result for the disease.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, and intracranial hemorrhage. The last is especially important for a disease in the brain's arteries.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found in total. Those listed include:
  - **Major:** clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate:** acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), morphine, simvastatin, dexamethasone, budesonide, aprepitant, and miconazole, among others.
  - **Minor:** naloxegol and prucalopride.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and label contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence for intracranial arteriosclerosis is two studies with no results, and ticagrelor's role in the larger one is unconfirmed. The high TxGNN score is therefore a model prediction only. Bleeding risk, especially intracranial hemorrhage, adds a safety concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation that ticagrelor is a treatment arm in CAPTIVA, and its published results (expected after the study completes in 2027)
- Results from the genotype-guided pilot study (NCT06714526)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A bleeding-risk and patient-selection plan, including CYP2C19 status and intracranial hemorrhage history
