# Linagliptin: From Type 2 Diabetes to Opsismodysplasia

## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not recorded in the evidence pack. Linagliptin is known to inhibit dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4). This raises incretin hormone levels (GLP-1 and GIP) and enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, which is how it lowers blood glucose in type 2 diabetes.

Opsismodysplasia is caused by loss of function of the INPPL1 gene, which disrupts skeletal development. No link between DPP-4 or incretin signalling and this disease is known. The two conditions share no obvious disease biology. The high score most likely reflects patterns in the knowledge graph rather than a biological rationale.

The same run also produced other high-scoring predictions, none with clinical evidence. The most notable are thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, stiff person syndrome and its focal variant, several localized lipodystrophies, pancreatic agenesis and homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. Most have weak, speculative or implausible mechanistic links. The two stiff person syndrome variants share an identical score (0.9423), which suggests they come from the same graph neighborhood rather than independent evidence.
