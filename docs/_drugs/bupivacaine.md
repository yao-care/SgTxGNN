## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Bupivacaine is a local anaesthetic of the amide class and is generally understood to block sodium channels in nerves. It is used for nerve blocks, infiltration and spinal anaesthesia, not as a disease-modifying treatment for skin conditions.

Acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans is a late-stage skin infection caused by *Borrelia*. A local anaesthetic has no plausible way to treat the infection itself. At most it could relieve local pain or neuropathic symptoms. The high score probably reflects closeness to dermatitis-related nodes in the knowledge graph rather than a true biological link.

The other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. Dermatomyositis variants, connective tissue disease-associated lung disease, acne keloid, familial hydroa vacciniforme and middle ear polyp have no clinical or literature evidence and no plausible mechanism.

Predictions with some evidence (uterine polyp, epulis, vocal cord polyp) concern bupivacaine as procedural anaesthesia or a co-injected agent. They do not show that it treats the disease itself.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are graph associations, not package insert text):
- Cardiovascular: hypertension, hypotension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, atrial fibrillation, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest
- Hepatic and renal: liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease
- Respiratory: chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, respiratory failure
- Metabolic and endocrine: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypothyroidism, potassium deficiency
- Other: epilepsy, angle-closure glaucoma, inflammatory bowel disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hemoglobinopathy, substance abuse/dependence

**Drug Interactions:** 56 interactions were recorded. Those with a graded level are moderate, with aprepitant and liposomal morphine. The rest have no graded level, including proton pump inhibitors (pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole), corticosteroids (dexamethasone, prednisone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, triamcinolone), ondansetron, metoclopramide, famotidine, ranitidine, simvastatin, vancomycin, metronidazole, atropine, naloxone and potassium chloride.

Please also refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications. Bupivacaine is myotoxic and has been associated with transient neurological syndrome after spinal use, as shown in the literature above.
