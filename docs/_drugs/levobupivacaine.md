# Levobupivacaine: From Local Anaesthesia to Gastroduodenitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Levobupivacaine is a long-acting amide local anaesthetic used for regional anaesthesia and pain control.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gastroduodenitis** with a very high score (99.09%), but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank is not currently available. Levobupivacaine is known to be a long-acting amide local anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels. Its established use is anaesthesia and analgesia, not gastrointestinal disease.

**We could not identify a plausible mechanistic link** between sodium channel blockade and gastroduodenal inflammation. The high TxGNN score is therefore best read as a knowledge-graph association, not as evidence of therapeutic benefit. The other top-ranked predictions (peptic ulcer disease, gout, rhinitis, bronchitis, ankylosing spondylitis) show the same pattern of high scores without supporting evidence. Only migraine has even a theoretical link, through modulation of trigeminovascular nociceptive signalling, and it has no supporting data either.
