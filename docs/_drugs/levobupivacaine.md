## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, levobupivacaine is a long-acting amide local anaesthetic, and it is understood to act by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve fibres. Its anaesthetic and analgesic efficacy is established, but this does not extend to inflammatory gastrointestinal disease.

For gastroduodenitis, no mechanistic link could be identified. Sodium channel blockade has no established role in gastroduodenal inflammation, and the high TxGNN score reflects graph-based association only. The same applies to the other top-ranked predictions (peptic ulcer disease, gout, rhinitis, bronchitis, ankylosing spondylitis), none of which has any trial or literature support.

**Other predictions with some evidence**

- **Migraine disorder** (score 97.59%, L4): Local anaesthetics are used in greater occipital nerve blocks, which plausibly modulate headache pain signalling. The single 2026 publication is a prospective cohort on nerve blocks across headache types. It does not isolate levobupivacaine. This is indirect procedural evidence, not evidence for systemic repurposing.
- **Hypertensive disorder** (score 96.19%, L4): The studies use levobupivacaine as an anaesthetic in hypertensive or haemodynamically sensitive patients. They do not show that it treats hypertension.
- **Exostosis** (score 96.76%, L4): One case report describes routine perioperative nerve block analgesia, not treatment of the disease.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph** (not label text): kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease, myasthenia gravis (including adult-onset), neuromuscular junction disease, butyrylcholinesterase deficiency, cardiovascular disease, and heart disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 62 interactions were recorded. Examples at Moderate level include aprepitant, cimetidine, clarithromycin, obeticholic acid, miconazole, clotrimazole, cobicistat, deferasirox, ticlopidine, fostamatinib, thiabendazole, primaquine, and other topical or ophthalmic local anaesthetics (lidocaine, benzocaine, cocaine). Minor interactions include dexamethasone, omeprazole, hyaluronidase, and albendazole.
- **Class note**: Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics (central nervous system and cardiovascular) is a recognised risk, and it matters for any use beyond routine anaesthesia.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, gastroduodenitis, rests on a model score alone. There are no trials or publications, and no plausible mechanism links sodium channel blockade to gastroduodenal inflammation. Migraine (nerve-block procedures) is the only prediction with a plausible link, and that evidence is indirect and does not isolate levobupivacaine.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA record (safety screening cannot proceed without them)
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registration
- Mechanism of action data
- For migraine as a research question: studies that specifically use levobupivacaine in nerve blocks for headache
- Route compatibility assessment: the only registered form is an injectable, and the predicted indications have not been checked against it
