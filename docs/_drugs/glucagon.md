## Glucagon: From Hypoglycaemia Rescue to Irritable Bowel Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glucagon is a pancreatic peptide hormone marketed in Singapore as an injectable product, though the approved indication text was not available in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)**.
The support is **indirect**: **2 clinical trials** (both on GLP-1 analogues, not glucagon) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews and studies of GLP-1 receptor agonists).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Glucagon is a proglucagon-derived peptide. In clinical practice it is also known to relax gastrointestinal smooth muscle, and it is used as a spasmolytic during GI imaging.

Reducing gut motility could plausibly relate to IBS symptoms such as pain, cramping and altered transit. However, most of the supporting data concern GLP-1 receptor agonists such as ROSE-010, which are related peptides but act on a different receptor. The link to glucagon itself is therefore indirect and class-based. It should be treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

They are warfarin and a broad group of beta-blockers (including acebutolol, atenolol, bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol, propranolol, labetalol, nadolol, sotalol and timolol, plus ophthalmic beta-blocker forms).

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The only disease-specific clinical evidence for IBS involves a GLP-1 analogue (ROSE-010), not glucagon. The mechanistic link is indirect.
- No trial has tested glucagon in IBS, and package-insert safety data are not yet available for review.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications for the Singapore-registered product
- Mechanism of action data for glucagon, particularly its effects on gut motility
- Direct evidence for glucagon in IBS (for example, a pilot or exploratory trial)
- Assessment of whether the existing injectable formulation and dosing are suitable for a chronic functional GI condition
