## Glucagon: Irritable bowel syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glucagon is a peptide hormone given by injection and currently marketed in Singapore.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **irritable bowel syndrome**, but no study has tested glucagon itself in this condition.
The two linked trials and the supporting literature concern GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) and its analogue ROSE-010, which are different drugs, so the evidence level is **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for glucagon is not available in the data compiled for this report. Glucagon and GLP-1 are both derived from the proglucagon gene and act on related G-protein-coupled receptors in the gut.

Both peptides inhibit gastrointestinal motility. Glucagon is used clinically as a spasmolytic during GI imaging. This is plausibly relevant to IBS symptoms such as pain, cramping and altered transit.

The link is indirect and based on drug class. Nearly all supporting clinical data involve GLP-1 receptor agonists such as ROSE-010, not glucagon. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No study tests glucagon in irritable bowel syndrome. The supporting trials and papers concern GLP-1 and its analogues, so the evidence is L5 and the case rests on a class-level mechanistic hypothesis. The high TxGNN score (0.9924) is not enough on its own to justify clinical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct preclinical or early-phase studies of glucagon in IBS, such as motility or visceral pain endpoints.
- Mechanism of action data for glucagon, to test whether its spasmolytic effect matches the IBS symptom profile.
- Safety information from the package insert, including warnings and contraindications.
- An assessment of whether injectable dosing and short duration of action suit a chronic condition like IBS.
