# D-Glucose: From Intravenous Glucose Infusion to Non-Syndromic Esophageal Malformation

## One-Sentence Summary

D-glucose is registered in Singapore as an injectable glucose (dextrose) infusion. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **non-syndromic esophageal malformation**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support this prediction. It is a graph-based prediction only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. D-glucose is a ubiquitous nutrient and energy substrate, and it is marketed as an intravenous infusion for fluid and carbohydrate supply. The registration records do not state a formal indication.

We could not identify a plausible link between glucose and esophageal malformation. Glucose has no known role in esophageal embryogenesis. The score of 0.84 reflects patterns in the knowledge graph rather than a mechanistic or clinical rationale. Because glucose is involved in so many biological processes, it is connected to a large number of diseases in the graph, which likely inflates such predictions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or publications (L5) and no identifiable mechanism. For a nutrient like glucose, a high graph score alone is not a reason to invest further. Among the other predictions for this drug, open-angle glaucoma has the most published material (L4). That material is indirect, consisting of epidemiology and one small study of topical glucose. It also includes signals that high glucose raises glaucoma risk, so it does not change this decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore-registered products (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for D-glucose, for example from the DrugBank API
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence linking glucose to esophageal development or malformation; none was found
