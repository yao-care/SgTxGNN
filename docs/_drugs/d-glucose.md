## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, D-glucose is a basic carbohydrate used in intravenous fluids, either alone or together with sodium chloride. Its established role is to supply fluid and energy.

Esophageal malformations are structural developmental defects. Supplying glucose cannot correct an anatomical abnormality, and the data compiled for this report identify no mechanistic link. The high model score (0.84, rank 39,795) is not backed by trials or literature, so it should be treated as a computational signal only.

The other top-ranked predictions look similar. Several were checked:
- **Open-angle glaucoma**: the literature describes disrupted glucose metabolism in the disease. These are associative findings, not evidence that giving glucose helps.
- **Esophageal disease**: the apparent signal is a diagnostic artifact. Most papers concern FDG-PET imaging, which uses a glucose analogue as a radiotracer, not D-glucose as a treatment.
- **Vitamin deficiency disorder**: the papers describe how vitamin D status affects glucose metabolism, the reverse of the proposed direction.
- **Dermatitis**: the relevant paper concerns 2-deoxy-D-glucose, a glycolysis inhibitor, which hints that blocking glucose metabolism may help rather than supplying it.
- **Other predictions** (primary hereditary glaucoma, biotin metabolic disease, neonatal dermatomyositis, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, familial hydroa vacciniforme): no trials, no relevant literature, and no plausible mechanism.
