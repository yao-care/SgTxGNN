## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, elacestrant is an oral SERD that binds and degrades the estrogen receptor (ER). Its efficacy is established in ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer, where ER signalling drives tumour growth.

The prediction likely reflects network proximity between elacestrant and estrogen-signalling and hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis genes. Blocking and degrading ER could disrupt HPG feedback and cause amenorrhea. That would be a pharmacological side effect rather than a therapeutic benefit, so the prediction may reflect an adverse effect rather than a treatment opportunity.

Other predicted indications (ranks 2-10) are also supported only by model output. They include bone Paget disease, multiple endocrine neoplasia, primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma and seborrheic dermatitis. The mechanistic links for these are weak or speculative, and none has clinical trial or literature support.
