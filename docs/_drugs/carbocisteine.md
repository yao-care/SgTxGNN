## Carbocisteine: From Mucolytic Use to Gout
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Carbocisteine is a mucolytic, used for respiratory disorders marked by thick, viscous mucus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gout**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction.
The prediction comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) alone and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Carbocisteine is a mucolytic that alters mucus viscoelasticity. Laboratory work also suggests antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.

No data connect these properties to urate metabolism or to the inflammation seen in gout. The very high TxGNN score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. The link between a respiratory mucolytic and a disorder of uric acid metabolism is therefore not mechanistically supported at present.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The gout prediction has a very high model score, but there are no trials or publications, and no identifiable mechanistic link to urate metabolism or gouty inflammation. The evidence is at the lowest level (L5), so there is no basis to advance it.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanistic data showing a plausible effect on uric acid handling or gouty inflammation, for example in preclinical models
- Detailed mechanism of action data for carbocisteine
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator, to complete safety screening
- Confirmation of approved indications for the registered Singapore products

**Note:** Among the other predictions, **bronchitis** has more supporting evidence, at L3. It includes a long-term double-blind placebo-controlled trial (1985) and a 2024 systematic review of mucolytics in stable COPD. It is closer to an on-label use than a true repurposing, and it is the more practical direction to evaluate.
