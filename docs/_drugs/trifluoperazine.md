## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on general class knowledge, trifluoperazine is a phenothiazine antipsychotic with potent dopamine D2 receptor antagonism. Its efficacy in schizophrenia is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to mania.

D2 blockade is the mechanism shared by antipsychotics used for acute mania, so the link is biologically plausible. Schizophrenia and manic episodes of bipolar disorder are both psychotic-spectrum conditions, and several antipsychotics are used in both.

The high TxGNN score is probably driven by trifluoperazine's similarity to other antipsychotics already used in mania, rather than by direct evidence for trifluoperazine itself. The supporting literature is mostly class-level or indirect (antipsychotic safety, adverse effects, prescribing patterns, anxiety efficacy), so it should not be read as proof of efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

  - Neurological: epilepsy, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, lingual-facial-buccal dyskinesia, Alzheimer disease, multiple system atrophy
  - Cardiovascular and autonomic: orthostatic hypotension, hypotensive disorder, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome
  - Haematological: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia)

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible because D2 antagonists are used in acute mania, but no trial or direct study supports trifluoperazine in this setting. Established alternatives exist, and the knowledge-graph contraindication profile is extensive (including neutropenia, agranulocytosis, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome). The other nine predicted indications (for example myopia variants, retinal dystrophy and rare congenital disorders) have no supporting evidence and are not recommended for further work.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data (DrugBank)
- A targeted literature search for trifluoperazine in mania, including older comparative trials
- A comparison against existing antipsychotics approved for mania, including efficacy and safety
- A safety plan for the contraindicated populations listed above
