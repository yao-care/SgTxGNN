# Flunarizine: From Calcium Channel Blocker to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Flunarizine is a non-selective calcium channel blocker. It is already used in several countries for migraine prevention and for vertigo.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, with a score of 99.12%.
The prediction is supported by **19 retrieved clinical trials** (about 7 directly involving flunarizine) and **20 publications**, including meta-analyses and guidelines.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed DrugBank mechanism-of-action data is not available. The following comes from the Evidence Pack's repurposing rationale. Flunarizine blocks calcium channels, and it also has sodium channel and antihistamine activity. It is thought to reduce cortical spreading depression and neuronal hyperexcitability. Both are central to how migraine attacks start, so migraine prophylaxis is a natural fit for this mechanism.

Guidelines in several jurisdictions already reference flunarizine as a migraine preventive. A 2023 European Headache Federation meta-analysis calls it a "repurposed, first- or second-line treatment" for migraine prophylaxis. In practice this prediction largely confirms existing clinical use.

The direct evidence is mostly head-to-head Phase 4 trials against topiramate, propranolol and amitriptyline, not placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials. The evidence is strong but not equivalent to a classic Phase 3 package.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data are not available in the dataset. The HSA package insert should be obtained and reviewed.

Points to check, based on the Evidence Pack's rationale and published literature:
- **Depression:** screen before and during treatment.
- **Extrapyramidal symptoms and parkinsonism:** particularly in older patients.
- **Sedation and weight gain:** reported in adult and adolescent cohorts (for example PMID 40163005).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed and ongoing head-to-head trials, three flunarizine-focused or related meta-analyses, and multiple guidelines support flunarizine for migraine prevention. The direct trials are Phase 4 comparative studies rather than Phase 3 placebo-controlled ones, and the safety data needed for local review are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to confirm the local approved indication, warnings and contraindications.
- A formal evidence-level decision, given the Phase 4 comparative design.
- Drug interaction review, since none is available in the dataset.
- A monitoring plan for depression and extrapyramidal symptoms, especially in older and pediatric patients.

Other predicted indications in the pack have weaker support:
- **Migraine with brainstem aura, migraine susceptibility, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia:** research questions only.
- **Headache disorder:** L2 evidence, but derived almost entirely from migraine.
- **Nephrogenic SIAD, atrophoderma vermiculata, ulerythema ophryogenes, common cold, insomnia:** model prediction only, so Hold.
