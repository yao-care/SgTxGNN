# Regorafenib: From Metastatic Colorectal Cancer, Hepatocellular Carcinoma and GIST to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Regorafenib is an oral multi-kinase inhibitor originally used for metastatic colorectal cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma and gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**, but only **1 clinical trial** and several publications address this directly, and the randomised data are largely **negative for liposarcoma**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Regorafenib is a multi-kinase inhibitor that targets angiogenic kinases (VEGFR1-3, TIE2), stromal kinases (PDGFR, FGFR) and oncogenic kinases (KIT, RET, RAF). Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available from the drug database, so this description comes from the published literature.

Its original indications are solid tumours that depend on angiogenesis and kinase signalling. Soft tissue sarcomas, including liposarcoma, share some of this biology, which is the basis for the model's prediction. Related multi-kinase inhibitors (sorafenib, sunitinib, pazopanib) have shown activity in some soft tissue sarcomas.

However, the efficacy signal in liposarcoma appears **weaker** than in other sarcoma subtypes. The REGOSARC trial showed benefit in non-adipocytic sarcomas but not in liposarcoma. The liposarcoma cohort of SARC024 likewise did not support routine use. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible but not clinically confirmed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified; the top 20 were reviewed. Major interactions include:
  - Acetylsalicylic acid
  - Deferasirox
  - Ibritumomab tiuxetan
  - Tositumomab (including I-131)
  - Abciximab
  - Alteplase
  - Antithrombin III human
  - Apixaban

  Moderate interactions include dexamethasone, clarithromycin, naltrexone, rosuvastatin, cobicistat, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine and sibutramine. Several of the major interactions involve anticoagulants, antiplatelets or thrombolytics, so bleeding risk should be reviewed in any co-prescription.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Regorafenib is marketed in Singapore and has randomised Phase 2 data in sarcoma. However, the liposarcoma-specific results (SARC024 and REGOSARC) did not show benefit and do not support routine use. The recommendation therefore applies only to a cautious, research-oriented approach. Given the negative randomised signal, a **Hold** for routine clinical use would also be defensible.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Singapore package insert, including warnings, contraindications and approved indications, since the available record has none
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Subtype-specific analysis (well-differentiated, dedifferentiated, myxoid and other liposarcoma) to identify any responsive subgroup
- Evaluation of combination approaches, as the SARC024 authors suggest
- A bleeding and hepatic safety monitoring plan, with a review of the major anticoagulant and antiplatelet interactions
