# Bromhexine: From Respiratory Mucolytic Use to Vulvitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Bromhexine is a mucolytic (a drug that thins airway mucus) that is marketed in Singapore in tablet, elixir, syrup and cough-liquid products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **vulvitis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.
The prediction rests only on a knowledge-graph score and should be treated as a hypothesis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Bromhexine is generally known as a mucolytic that thins respiratory mucus, and it has also been described as a TMPRSS2 inhibitor.

Neither activity has a recognized link to inflammation or infection of the vulvar mucosa. The prediction is better read as a knowledge-graph artifact than as a mechanism-based hypothesis. Several of the top predictions (vulvitis, vaginitis, vulvovaginitis, ulceration of vulva) cluster around the same genital-tract area. This suggests one shared graph-neighbourhood signal rather than independent pieces of evidence.

The remaining top-10 predictions, such as bacterial vaginosis, vulvar neoplasm and a hair condition (catatrichy), also lack any identified mechanistic rationale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for vulvitis has no clinical trials, no publications and no plausible mechanistic link. It relies only on a knowledge-graph score, which corresponds to evidence level L5. The drug is already widely marketed in Singapore, but only for non-genital uses.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for bromhexine, and any rationale for activity on vulvar mucosa
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Approved indication text for the licensed products
- Preclinical or early clinical data for vulvitis, or a review of whether a topical route would be required (route compatibility has not yet been assessed)
- A check of whether the clustered genital-tract predictions represent an independent signal or a single artifact
