## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Glipizide is generally known as a sulfonylurea that closes K-ATP channels in pancreatic beta cells and stimulates insulin secretion. Its efficacy in type 2 diabetes is well established.

Opsismodysplasia is a rare skeletal dysplasia caused by INPPL1 mutations (SHIP2 deficiency), which affect PI3K/insulin-signalling-related pathways. The only conceivable connection to glipizide is that both touch insulin-related biology. No plausible mechanistic link has been identified. Glipizide stimulates insulin release and does not correct a SHIP2 defect.

The high score therefore looks like a graph-based association, not a biologically grounded signal. Other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern: stiff person syndrome and focal stiff limb syndrome (shared autoimmune diabetes comorbidity), several localized lipodystrophies (shared diabetes context), and pancreatic agenesis (where the drug is mechanistically implausible because there are no beta cells to stimulate).
