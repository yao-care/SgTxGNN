# Ledipasvir: From Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, marketed in Singapore as part of the fixed-dose combination Harvoni with sofosbuvir for hepatitis C. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, with a score of 99.91%. The retrieved evidence includes **20 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but only **1 Phase 2 trial** (NCT03312023) and 1 paper (PMID 36045503) test the drug in HBV, and the paper's excerpt does not report the outcome. Most of the rest concerns HCV/HBV co-infection, where the main finding is a HBV reactivation risk.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Ledipasvir is known to be an NS5A inhibitor used with the NS5B nucleotide polymerase inhibitor sofosbuvir. Its efficacy in hepatitis C is established.

The link to HBV is weak on mechanistic grounds. HBV replicates through a reverse-transcribing polymerase, and neither NS5A nor NS5B has a known HBV counterpart. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects graph proximity to other antivirals rather than a validated mechanism.

The clinical rationale is an observation from co-infected patients. Retrospective data suggested a modest reduction in HBsAg when ledipasvir/sofosbuvir was given to HBV/HCV co-infected patients. This led to a Phase 2 pilot in HBV mono-infected patients (NCT03312023 / PMID 36045503). Its results are not included in the supplied data. The larger clinical signal is that DAA treatment of co-infected patients can reactivate HBV, which is a safety concern rather than evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings**: Please refer to the package insert. The supplied record has no HSA warning text.
- **Drug Interactions**: The DDI query returned no records. Literature on ledipasvir/sofosbuvir in HIV patients describes a tenofovir interaction, and this is worth checking against the package insert.
- **HBV reactivation (from the literature)**: Multiple reports describe HBV reactivation during DAA therapy in HCV/HBV co-infected patients, including a fulminant hepatitis case (PMID 28366615). Other cohorts, such as the Taiwan/Korea series (PMID 27486112), found no reactivation. Any HBV-related investigation needs HBV DNA and HBsAg monitoring.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The mechanistic support is weak, since neither NS5A nor NS5B has a known HBV target.
- The only HBV-specific trial is a 21-patient open-label Phase 2 pilot whose results are not in the supplied data. The larger body of evidence concerns HBV reactivation risk in co-infected patients.
- Package insert warnings and contraindications are missing, which blocks the safety screening step.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The results of NCT03312023 / PMID 36045503 (HBsAg and HBV DNA change at week 12), and whether any decline was sustained.
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, including any HBV reactivation language.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank, to assess whether any plausible HBV link exists.
- A randomized or controlled HBV mono-infection study, if the pilot results justify further investigation.
- An HBV monitoring or prophylaxis plan for any study population with HBV exposure.
