# Ledipasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, marketed in the fixed-dose combination with sofosbuvir (Harvoni) for chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hepatitis B virus infection**,
but the supporting evidence is thin: **5 related clinical trials** (only 1 directly in HBV) and **20 publications**. Most of these concern HCV treatment in HCV/HBV co-infected patients and HBV reactivation risk, not direct HBV treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ledipasvir is an inhibitor of the HCV NS5A protein and is used together with sofosbuvir, an NS5B polymerase inhibitor, for chronic hepatitis C.

The mechanistic case for HBV is weak. HBV has no NS5A homologue, so direct anti-HBV activity is not expected. The high TxGNN score probably reflects shared viral hepatitis network features (the two viruses infect the same organ and often co-occur) rather than a true antiviral mechanism.

There is one signal worth noting. In retrospective data from HBV/HCV co-infected patients treated with ledipasvir/sofosbuvir, a modest reduction in hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) was observed. This led to a small Phase 2 pilot in HBV mono-infected subjects (NCT03312023, published as PMID 36045503). It is hypothesis-generating only.

The other predicted indications (hepatitis E, hepatitis A, flavivirus diseases, HIV, and animal viral diseases) have even less support: either no trials at all, or HCV-focused studies that were matched by keyword or network overlap. They are not evaluated further here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 65 interactions were identified. Those listed here are mostly moderate, with a few minor.
  - Antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide): moderate
  - Acid-suppressing drugs, which can reduce ledipasvir absorption: H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine) and proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, dexlansoprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole), all moderate
  - Others: eliglustat, linagliptin, loperamide and naldemedine (moderate); fidaxomicin and naloxegol (minor)
- **HBV reactivation**: Published cohorts and case reports show that HBV can reactivate during direct-acting antiviral therapy in HCV/HBV co-infected patients. This is a key safety consideration for the proposed indication and the target population.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Ledipasvir has no expected direct anti-HBV mechanism. The only directly relevant study is a small single-arm Phase 2 pilot (n=21) in which the outcome was a modest HBsAg change. The co-infection trials mainly show that HCV is cured and that HBV reactivation is a safety concern. The high TxGNN score is better explained by network proximity than by antiviral biology. The evidence level of L2 reflects the existence of a completed Phase 2 study, not proof of efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of NCT03312023, including HBsAg and HBV DNA change and durability, ideally confirmed in a larger controlled study
- Preclinical or in vitro data showing whether ledipasvir, as opposed to sofosbuvir, has any anti-HBV activity
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, since these are currently missing from the data compiled for this report
- An HBV reactivation monitoring plan for any co-infected population
- Clarification of the approved indication text in the Singapore registration record
