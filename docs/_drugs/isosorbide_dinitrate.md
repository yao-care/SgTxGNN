## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the supplied record. Based on known pharmacology, isosorbide dinitrate is a nitric-oxide-donating nitrate that relaxes vascular smooth muscle. Its efficacy in its classical cardiovascular use is well established, and mechanistically it might be applicable to alopecia through increased scalp perfusion.

That link is speculative. Nothing in the supplied data supports it, and no trials or publications were retrieved for alopecia. The high score (0.9999) most likely reflects proximity in the knowledge graph to other hair-related indications rather than a documented biological mechanism. Several other hair-related predictions (congenital hypotrichosis milia, hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, diffuse alopecia areata, hypertrichosis) show the same pattern of score without evidence. Alopecia areata is autoimmune and the hypotrichoses are hereditary, so a perfusion-based rationale is even less plausible for them.
