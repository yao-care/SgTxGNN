# Dolutegravir: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Dolutegravir is an HIV integrase inhibitor. The supplied data do not list an original indication, so HIV-1 infection is assumed from general knowledge.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective against **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a nonhuman primate disease model.
Support is thin: **1 clinical trial** (only indirectly related) and **15 publications**, mostly preclinical macaque and in vitro work.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the supplied record. Dolutegravir is known to block the strand-transfer step of lentiviral integrase. SIV integrase is structurally close to HIV integrase, so the drug would be expected to act on SIV as well.

The literature supports this. Macaque studies show that dolutegravir is active against SIV. Resistance mutations selected in SIV mirror those seen in HIV, which suggests the same binding site is involved.

The prediction mostly confirms the known HIV mechanism in an animal model. SIV is not a human disease, so this is not a human repurposing opportunity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV signal comes from animal and in vitro studies. It mainly confirms the known HIV integrase mechanism and does not point to a human repurposing opportunity. The single listed trial is a human HIV study with no SIV endpoint. The more meaningful signals in this pack are the HIV-related entries (AIDS-related complex and congenital HIV). Both are rated L1 with a "Proceed with Guardrails" recommendation, but they are effectively the marketed HIV-1 use rather than new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text (blocking gap for safety screening)
