# Dolutegravir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Dolutegravir is an HIV integrase strand transfer inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, with **no registered clinical trials** and **14 publications** (mostly animal and in vitro studies) currently supporting this direction.
This is essentially a preclinical or veterinary model of the already-approved HIV indication, not a new human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, dolutegravir is an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) used as part of HIV combination therapy. Its efficacy in HIV infection is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to SIV infection.

SIV is a lentivirus closely related to HIV and carries a homologous integrase enzyme. Studies in macaques and cell culture show that dolutegravir inhibits SIV integrase and has antiviral activity. They also show that SIV develops resistance mutations similar to those seen in HIV, including during dolutegravir monotherapy in macaques.

SIV infection of macaques is mainly used as a research model for HIV. The prediction is therefore biologically sensible, but it restates the approved HIV indication in an animal setting. It does not point to a new treatment for human disease.

Other predictions in the list are weaker or not relevant to human repurposing:
- **Feline AIDS (FIV):** a single veterinary combination-therapy study.
- **AIDS-related complex and congenital HIV:** population or terminology extensions of the existing HIV indication.
- **Hepatitis C, hepatitis A, hepatitis E:** no antiviral mechanism. The evidence concerns drug interactions with HIV co-infection or is unrelated.
- **Familial combined hyperlipidaemia and a rare neurodevelopmental disorder:** no mechanistic link and no evidence. These are likely network artifacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 62 interactions are recorded. The main ones are:
  - **Major (polyvalent cation products):** aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, kaolin, attapulgite, calcium salts (acetate, carbonate, citrate, gluconate, lactate, phosphate, glubionate), magnesium salts (carbonate, chloride, citrate) and iron. These products can reduce dolutegravir absorption, so dosing needs to be separated in time.
  - **Minor:** aprepitant, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dexamethasone.
- **Pregnancy signal (from the literature on congenital HIV):** early surveillance in Botswana raised a possible neural tube defect signal with dolutegravir exposure at conception. Later meta-analyses and pharmacovigilance analyses did not definitively confirm it. This remains relevant for women of child-bearing potential.
- **Metabolic effects:** studies report weight gain and adipose tissue changes with integrase inhibitors, including dolutegravir.

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV prediction is mechanistically plausible and well supported by animal and in vitro work. However, it is an animal model of the already-approved HIV indication, with no clinical trials, and it offers no new therapeutic opportunity for human patients. The other top-ranked predictions are either population extensions of the HIV indication or lack mechanism and evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A defined human use case, since SIV and FIV are not human diseases
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, and approved indication text for the Singapore licences
- If congenital or perinatal HIV is pursued, a pregnancy safety review covering the neural tube defect signal and dosing in children
