## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the source record. Amikacin is an aminoglycoside that inhibits the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit and is active against many gram-negative bacilli.
*Salmonella* Paratyphi is a gram-negative pathogen, so activity in vitro is plausible.

The link is weaker in patients. Aminoglycosides generally perform poorly against intracellular *Salmonella*, because they penetrate cells poorly. The literature found is mostly epidemiology, antibiogram surveys and case reports, and few if any studies evaluate amikacin therapy. The prediction is a hypothesis for laboratory and pharmacology work, not a treatment recommendation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Paratyphoid fever has no registered trials, and none of the 12 publications shows amikacin benefit. Aminoglycosides also penetrate poorly into the intracellular niche of *Salmonella*.
The closest related candidates are typhoid fever (in-vitro susceptibility data, PMID 19554971) and salmonellosis (an animal-model study, PMID 8741602). Both are research questions only, and neither supports a clinical recommendation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
