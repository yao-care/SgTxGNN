## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Based on known information, tenecteplase is a fibrin-specific plasminogen activator. It dissolves clots, including coronary thrombi, so it is mechanistically plausible for an occlusive coronary event.

Posterolateral myocardial infarction is an anatomical subtype of acute myocardial infarction. Thrombolysis is a recognised approach for ST-elevation MI as a class. If the drug works for MI generally, it could in principle work for this subtype, which is the likely reason the model scores it so high.

However, the supplied data contain no trials or publications for this specific subtype. The same score (99.87%) was given to the closely related posteroinferior MI. This suggests the model is predicting a broad MI association rather than something specific to the posterolateral wall.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but it is the only evidence. There are no trials or publications for posterolateral MI, and the original approved indication and the safety information are both missing. Safety screening cannot start until the package insert has been obtained.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, covering approved indication, warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Subtype-specific clinical or observational evidence for posterolateral MI. Evidence for MI in general could be assessed for how well it carries over to this subtype.
- Route and dosing compatibility with the intended use, which is still pending

**Other predictions in this pack:** Coronary stenosis is the most developed of the other ten (L2). It has a completed Phase 2 randomised trial of low-dose intracoronary tenecteplase during primary PCI (NCT00604695, n=40) and its safety publication (PMID 31870492). The trial population is STEMI patients undergoing PCI, not coronary stenosis as such, and efficacy is unproven. It could be a better candidate for follow-up as a research question. Septal MI (L4) has only indirect pulmonary embolism literature. The remaining predictions (congenital coronary anomaly, chromosomal deletion, and red cell disorders) have no plausible mechanistic link and should stay on hold.
