## Urea: From Dermatitis-Type Skin Conditions to Dermatitis (Radiation- and Drug-Induced Settings)
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Urea is a topical humectant and keratolytic agent, originally used for skin conditions such as atopic eczema, contact dermatitis, seborrheic dermatitis and seborrheic keratosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, with **10 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Much of this prediction overlaps with existing uses, and the newer signals are prevention and treatment of radiodermatitis and capecitabine-associated hand-foot skin reactions.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, urea is a well-established topical humectant, keratolytic and skin-barrier-enhancing agent. It is a natural component of the skin's moisturising factor and increases stratum corneum hydration. The reviews compiled for this report also describe regulation of keratinocyte differentiation and antimicrobial peptide gene expression.

Dermatitis is already covered by the original indications (atopic, contact, seborrheic and occupational dermatitis), so this prediction is largely on-label. The newer, more specific signals are in treatment-related skin injury: radiodermatitis in breast and head-and-neck cancer patients, and hand-foot skin reaction from capecitabine. In these settings, barrier support and hydration are plausible ways to reduce skin damage.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph signal, not label text)**: Purulent otitis media, ear infection and otitis media.
- **Drug Interactions**: 54 interactions are recorded, all of those listed being Moderate level. Main ones include:
  - Laxatives and bowel-preparation agents (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol, sodium sulfate, lactulose, lactitol, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium citrate, glycerin, castor oil, mineral oil, phenolphthalein)
  - SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin)
  - Exenatide, sibutramine and iothalamic acid

  These are largely relevant to systemic exposure, and the practical significance for topical use is likely low but should be confirmed.

For warnings and contraindications from the label, please refer to the package insert, which was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Urea is already marketed in Singapore as topical creams, and dermatitis overlaps with its established uses. Supporting trials exist, notably in radiodermatitis and capecitabine-related hand-foot reactions. However, several of the key trials are of unknown status, small, or have urea only as a comparator, and the one earlier capecitabine study was negative. The evidence is supportive but not definitive.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (currently missing; this blocks formal safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmed results of NCT05348278, NCT05939726 and the radiodermatitis trials
- Review of the otitis-related contraindication signal and guidance on use in neonates, infants and damaged skin
- Note that the other nine predictions (for example acne keloid, dermatomyositis, interstitial lung disease) have no supporting trials and are best held as model predictions only. Acne (disease) has indirect evidence only, from one benzoyl peroxide in 10% urea vehicle study.
