## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, urea is used topically as a keratolytic and humectant agent in dermatological emollients — its efficacy in restoring skin barrier hydration and reducing hyperkeratosis has been demonstrated in multiple published studies, and mechanistically this may extend to inflammatory and dry-skin dermatitis conditions.

Because Urea has no recorded original indication in this evidence pack (it is not currently marketed in Singapore), the "old indication → new indication" logic cannot be evaluated in the usual sense. Instead, the supporting evidence comes from real-world clinical use of urea-containing topical products (10% urea creams, urea + lactic acid solutions, urea-based moisturizers) that have been tested against various forms of dermatitis, including atopic dermatitis, radiation dermatitis, seborrheic dermatitis, and chemotherapy-induced hand-foot skin reactions.

The mechanistic rationale is plausible but modest: urea's humectant/keratolytic action helps repair the skin barrier and reduce dryness/scaling common to most dermatitis subtypes, which explains why it repeatedly appears as an active or comparator treatment across several independent RCTs — but it is not a targeted anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory agent, so its role is largely adjunctive/supportive rather than disease-modifying.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed RCTs and a systematic review/meta-analysis support urea-based topical formulations as adjunctive or comparator treatments across several dermatitis subtypes (radiodermatitis, atopic dermatitis, seborrheic dermatitis, chemotherapy-induced hand-foot skin reactions), which is reasonable supporting evidence (L2). However, Urea has no marketing license in Singapore, and a **Blocking** data gap exists — local safety-label information (warnings/contraindications) is unavailable, which prevents entry into the S1 safety pre-assessment stage.
