# Tocilizumab: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Ankylosing Spondylitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tocilizumab is an IL-6 receptor antibody originally used for rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, giant cell (temporal) arteritis and Castleman disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ankylosing spondylitis**,
but the two Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials in this disease were **both terminated**, and the **18 publications** retrieved are mostly reviews, with no confirmed efficacy result in the data compiled for this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Tocilizumab is known as a humanised monoclonal antibody against the interleukin-6 receptor (IL-6R). It is established in rheumatoid arthritis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis, and the literature describes use in giant cell arteritis.

IL-6 signalling contributes to inflammation and bone remodelling in axial spondyloarthritis, so blocking the IL-6 receptor is biologically plausible. Ankylosing spondylitis is also closely related to the spondyloarthropathy indication already in the knowledge graph.

However, the two placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials (BUILDER-1 and BUILDER-2 programme) were terminated, and the published literature points to limited efficacy compared with TNF and IL-17 inhibitors. Tocilizumab is therefore best seen as an off-label option only after other biologics have failed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Among those listed, the one rated **Major** is deferiprone. Several **Moderate** interactions are listed, including statins (rosuvastatin, simvastatin), anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol), antimalarials (chloroquine, hydroxychloroquine), nitroimidazoles (metronidazole, tinidazole, secnidazole, benznidazole), naltrexone, disulfiram and lidocaine. Zinc salts are rated Minor.
- **Infection risk**: The literature raises tuberculosis and serious infection risk with IL-6 blockade, so screening is relevant before use.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as label text was not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The biological rationale for IL-6 blockade in AS is plausible, and the TxGNN score is very high. However, both Phase 3 placebo-controlled AS trials were terminated, published literature suggests limited efficacy relative to TNF and IL-17 inhibitors, and package insert safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results and reasons for termination of NCT01209689 and NCT01209702, plus the BUILDER-1/2 publication data
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A defined place in therapy (for example, after failure of TNF and IL-17 inhibitors) with an infection and laboratory monitoring plan

### Other Predicted Indications (Brief Notes)

- **Polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis** (L1, Proceed with Guardrails): Supported by a completed Phase 3 randomised withdrawal trial (NCT00988221, n=188) and several extension studies. This is effectively covered by the existing juvenile idiopathic arthritis indication, so it is a label-concordant rediscovery rather than true repurposing. Monitor infections, neutropenia, transaminases and lipids, and use weight-appropriate dosing.
- **Rheumatoid factor-positive polyarticular JIA** (L2, Proceed with Guardrails): A subtype within the pJIA programme; evidence is indirect, with subtype-specific data limited to a single-centre case series.
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy** (L2, Hold): The same disease space and caveats as ankylosing spondylitis.
- **Rheumatoid vasculitis** (L4, research question): Only case reports exist, and paradoxical vasculitis-like reactions to tocilizumab have been described.
- **Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial diseases (IL12B deficiency)** (L5, Hold): No clinical evidence, and IL-6 blockade raises a safety concern because of mycobacterial infection susceptibility.
- **Spondyloarthropathy susceptibility, vertebral disease, hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease** (L5, Hold): Predictions are computational only, with no supportive clinical evidence.
