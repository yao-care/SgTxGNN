## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the source record. Insulin degludec is a basal insulin analogue. The literature describes it as forming soluble multihexamers after subcutaneous injection, which slowly release monomers into the blood. This gives a flat profile with a duration of action of more than 42 hours (PMID 23620200; PMID 23890782). T1DM is defined by absolute insulin deficiency, so replacing insulin is a direct and physiologically sound approach.

The high TxGNN score matches this established mechanism. However, the input lists no original indications, and T1DM is very likely already on the product label. Verify the label status before presenting this as a repurposing finding.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No warnings, contraindications, or drug interactions were available in the source record.

The pack's rationale for other predicted indications notes that injected insulin is a known cause of injection-site lipohypertrophy and lipoatrophy. This is a general safety signal to bear in mind, not a finding from this record.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

There are at least five completed Phase 3 trials in T1DM, several with degludec as the studied or reference basal insulin. Randomised trials, including EXPECT, and meta-analyses also support its use in T1DM. Safety data and the original indication text are missing, and T1DM is probably already labelled, so this is likely not a true repurposing case.

  - The stiff person syndrome variants, autoimmune oophoritis and opsismodysplasia are probably graph artefacts.
