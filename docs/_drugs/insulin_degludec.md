## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin degludec is a long-acting basal insulin analogue, originally used for diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, insulin degludec is an ultra-long-acting basal insulin analogue. It binds the insulin receptor and replaces deficient endogenous insulin, giving a flat, stable glucose-lowering profile.

Type 1 diabetes is defined by absolute insulin deficiency, so basal insulin replacement is the standard of care. The predicted indication falls directly within the original indication of diabetes mellitus. The high TxGNN score is therefore consistent with known pharmacology rather than being a novel discovery.

Other diseases in the prediction list (for example autoimmune oophoritis, stiff person syndrome and localized lipodystrophy) have no supporting trials or literature.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified, and 20 are shown in the data compiled for this report. Most are rated Moderate, and a few are Minor. Notable moderate interactions include:
- Other glucose-lowering drugs: metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide, alpelisib
- Sympathomimetics and beta-agonists: epinephrine, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine, salbutamol, formoterol
- Beta-blocker: acebutolol
- Hormones and others: hydrocortisone, estradiol, levonorgestrel, norethisterone, hydrochlorothiazide, doxycycline, ethanol

Minor interactions include topical corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, alclometasone).

Localized lipodystrophy at repeated injection sites is a recognized effect of injected insulin. Rotating injection sites is the standard prevention.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several Phase 3 randomized trials, including SWITCH 1 and BOOST T1, together with randomized trials and meta-analyses in type 1 diabetes, support degludec's efficacy and safety. The indication essentially overlaps with the existing diabetes indication, and the product is already registered in Singapore. The guardrails reflect gaps in regulatory and safety data, not doubts about efficacy.
