# Lovastatin: From Hypercholesterolemia to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Lovastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) used to lower cholesterol, and it is marketed in Singapore under 5 registrations.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)**.
The 3 listed clinical trials test other drugs (ezetimibe, alirocumab) on a statin background, not lovastatin. Of the 19 publications, only a few small, older reports involve lovastatin directly, and their results are mixed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lovastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which lowers LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) by upregulating LDL receptors in the liver. Detailed mechanism data were not supplied in the drug record, but this is the standard statin mechanism.

Familial hypercholesterolemia is a disorder of LDL receptor function. In homozygous FH, the response to a statin depends on how much residual LDL receptor activity the patient has. The lovastatin reports reflect this:

- Three children with receptor-negative HoFH showed no fall in LDL-C on lovastatin (PMID 3397806).
- A child who had a liver transplant reached normal cholesterol levels on lovastatin (PMID 3534334).
- Two patients with receptor-defective disease were treated with lovastatin plus probucol and cholestyramine (PMID 1785747).

The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible only for patients with some residual receptor function. It is weak for receptor-negative patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, but no lovastatin trial in HoFH exists in the supplied data. The Phase 3 trials test ezetimibe or alirocumab. The lovastatin reports are small, from the 1980s and 1990s, and mixed, with no effect in receptor-negative patients. Established HoFH management already relies on other agents. By comparison, lovastatin has stronger direct support in heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (placebo-controlled RCTs in adolescents, PMID 9917116 and 16140708) and in hyperlipoproteinemia. This candidate should be treated as a research question rather than a development target.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Evidence that lovastatin adds benefit over current HoFH therapy, stratified by LDL receptor status
- A drug-interaction and safety monitoring plan (CYP3A4 substrate, myopathy and liver monitoring, pregnancy avoidance), particularly for pediatric patients
