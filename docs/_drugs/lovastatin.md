# Lovastatin: From Hyperlipidaemia to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Lovastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used to treat hyperlipidaemia and related lipid disorders, including atherosclerosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia**, but the supporting evidence is weak: **1 clinical trial** (which tests a different drug) and **19 publications**, mostly small case-level reports and reviews.
Evidence is stronger for closely related lipid indications such as hyperlipoproteinemia and familial hypercholesterolemia, which are largely extensions of the original lipid-lowering use rather than new mechanisms.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lovastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis. This lowers intracellular cholesterol and upregulates LDL receptors in the liver, which increases LDL clearance from the blood.

This is why the prediction is only partly convincing. In homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, the benefit of a statin depends on how much LDL receptor activity the patient still has. A study of three children with receptor-negative disease found no fall in LDL-cholesterol on lovastatin (PMID 3397806). In contrast, small reports describe benefit in patients with some residual receptor function, usually in combination with other agents (PMID 1785747, PMID 3534334). The prediction is therefore biologically plausible, but it is a close extension of the original lipid-lowering use, not a new-mechanism repurposing. Response is expected to vary by patient genotype.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from a knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, hyperglycaemia, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, peptic ulcer disease, myopathy and other muscle disease, rhabdomyolysis, gout, hypotensive disorder, blood coagulation disease, acute coronary syndrome, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, hemoglobinopathy and hypophosphatemia. These should be confirmed against the official package insert, because graph edges are not label wording.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major: **clarithromycin**. Moderate: aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, metronidazole, tinidazole, cimetidine, clotrimazole, miconazole, naltrexone, eluxadoline, pectin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and troglitazone. Other lipid-lowering agents (fibrates, niacin) raise myopathy risk.
- **HIV protease inhibitors**: lovastatin is a CYP3A4 substrate, and combination with HIV protease inhibitors markedly increases exposure and myopathy risk (PMID 23703578).
- **Special populations**: use in children and adolescents requires growth and maturation monitoring, and females of reproductive age need contraception counselling.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report; please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, the top-ranked prediction)

**Rationale:**
- The prediction score is very high (99.89%), but the supporting evidence is limited to small, mostly decades-old case-level reports, and the one registered trial tests a different drug. Evidence from a small paediatric study shows no LDL-C response in receptor-negative disease, so benefit is likely restricted to patients with residual LDL receptor function.
- By contrast, **hyperlipoproteinemia** and **familial hypercholesterolemia / autosomal dominant hypercholesterolemia** have stronger support (a Phase 2/3 placebo-controlled lovastatin trial and adolescent FH studies) and could be considered **Proceed with Guardrails**. These are extensions of the existing lipid-lowering use.
- Other predictions (CYP7A1 deficiency, CETP deficiency, hepatic lipase deficiency, the neurodevelopmental disorder, and SIV infection) have no meaningful evidence and should be held. The HIV indication has a Phase 2 trial but needs its clinical endpoints confirmed and carries a significant protease-inhibitor interaction concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Genotype-stratified (receptor-negative vs receptor-defective) efficacy data for lovastatin in homozygous FH
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications, and approved indication text for the five licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A monitoring plan covering myopathy, liver enzymes, CYP3A4 and fibrate interactions, and paediatric growth
- Confirmation of the HIV trial endpoints (PMID 23085245) before considering that indication further
