# Ceftazidime: From Bacterial Infections to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ceftazidime is a third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as pneumonia, meningitis, peritonitis and staphylococcal or streptococcal infections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection** (the highest-ranked prediction with real supporting evidence), with **4 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that this is an antibacterial use largely consistent with its existing infectious disease role, so it is not a true repurposing case.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ceftazidime is a third-generation cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It is active against Gram-negative uropathogens, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

Urinary tract infection is a bacterial disease, so it fits the antibacterial class of ceftazidime. Its approved-type indications (pneumonia, peritonitis, meningitis) are also bacterial infections, which makes the model's prediction mechanistically coherent.

One caution: most of the available evidence concerns **ceftazidime-avibactam** (a combination with a β-lactamase inhibitor) rather than ceftazidime alone, so applying the findings to the single agent requires care.

Other model predictions were reviewed too. Several are not biologically plausible (hyperamylasemia, polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, congenital analbuminemia, blood group incompatibility) and appear to be graph-based artefacts. *Ureaplasma* urethritis conflicts with the known mechanism, as *Ureaplasma* has no cell wall. Chronic suppurative otitis media (listed under "infectious otitis media") has some clinical literature and is a reasonable research question.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, pyoureter, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis and congenital prothrombin deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 records found; main moderate-level ones):
  - Amphotericin B (including cholesteryl sulfate, lipid complex and liposomal forms)
  - Aminoglycosides: kanamycin, neomycin, streptomycin
  - Picosulfuric acid
  - Several further interactions of unknown severity (e.g. vancomycin, pantoprazole, omeprazole, metformin, morphine)

For full warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Urinary tract infection is the only prediction with a completed randomised Phase 2 trial, and it fits the drug's antibacterial class. However, the strongest evidence uses ceftazidime as a comparator or in combination with avibactam, and this indication largely overlaps with existing infectious disease use, so it is not a genuine new indication. The other predictions are either implausible (L5) or backed only by weak, indirect evidence, and are held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm whether UTI is already covered
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Ceftazidime-alone efficacy data in UTI (the current evidence mostly involves ceftazidime-avibactam)
- Local susceptibility and resistance data for uropathogens in Singapore
- Renal dose adjustment guidance, given the kidney disease caution
- Optional follow-up: chronic suppurative otitis media as a separate research question, based on paediatric studies of ceftazidime versus aztreonam and outpatient parenteral therapy
