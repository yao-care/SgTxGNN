## Alanine: From Nutritional Amino Acid Supplementation to gastroparesis (disease)
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Alanine is an amino acid used as a component of parenteral nutrition and amino acid infusion products. The TxGNN model (deep learning) predicts it may be effective for **gastroparesis**, but this prediction comes from the model and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence: **no clinical trials** are registered and none of the **3 retrieved publications** studies alanine as a treatment for the condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, alanine is a component of multi-amino-acid infusion and parenteral nutrition products, where it serves as a nutritional substrate. No specific pharmacological action on gastric motility is documented in the data compiled for this report.

The relationship between the original use (nutritional support) and gastroparesis (delayed gastric emptying) is not supported by the data. The three retrieved papers concern hepatic and gastrointestinal complications of end-stage renal disease and diabetes. They do not study alanine or gastric motility. The high TxGNN score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence, and no credible mechanistic link was identified.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. There are no clinical trials, the retrieved literature does not evaluate alanine for gastroparesis, and no mechanistic link was identified. Alanine is also an amino acid nutrient with no established prokinetic action, and the knowledge graph flags several renal and metabolic contraindications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a plausible biological link between alanine and gastric motility
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore-registered products
- Preclinical or early clinical data on alanine in gastroparesis
- Assessment of route and formulation suitability, since current products are intravenous nutrition solutions
