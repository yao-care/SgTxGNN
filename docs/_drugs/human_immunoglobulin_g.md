## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug, and the original indication could not be confirmed from the registration record. The prediction therefore cannot be tied to a known mechanism.

The only link found is indirect. A 2025 mass-spectrometry study found that changes in the sugar patterns (Fc N-glycosylation) of a patient's own IgG differ between non-diabetic, nonproliferative and proliferative diabetic retinopathy. This makes IgG a possible **disease marker**. It does not show that giving pooled human IgG would treat retinopathy.

The 0.998 score reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, not demonstrated therapeutic benefit. Standard treatment for this disease relies on other approaches, such as anti-VEGF antibodies, and no evidence suggests pooled IgG plays such a role.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy.

For the broader term "diabetic retinopathy", three retrieved trials (adalimumab in uveitis, a type 1 diabetes beta-cell study, and aflibercept in diabetic macular edema) were graded C. None tests human IgG, so they were not counted as supporting evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a very high model score but no clinical trials and only one biomarker study, with no evidence that giving IgG helps in this disease. Safety data and the approved indication are also missing, so the candidate cannot yet pass initial safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication), which is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data for the product, for example from DrugBank
- Preclinical or clinical evidence that IgG administration, not IgG as a marker, affects diabetic retinopathy outcomes
- A route-of-administration assessment, since only an injectable form is registered and no ocular route has been evaluated
