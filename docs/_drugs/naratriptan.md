# Naratriptan: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Naratriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist originally used for the acute treatment of migraine.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials**, and the **19 publications** found concern migraine in general rather than this subtype specifically.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, naratriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D agonist (a triptan). Its efficacy in acute migraine is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to migraine with brainstem aura.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype that shares trigeminovascular pathophysiology with common migraine, so efficacy is biologically plausible. The literature found covers acute treatment guidelines, naratriptan versus sumatriptan, prodrome prevention, menstrual migraine and paediatric use. None of it studies the brainstem aura subtype specifically.

There is also a safety question. Triptans have historically been used cautiously in aura subtypes with brainstem or hemiplegic features because of theoretical vasoconstriction concerns. This needs explicit review before any further development.

The other nine predictions (for example atrophoderma vermiculata, amenorrhea, sciatic neuropathy, dysthymic disorder and several hair disorders) have no trials or literature and no established mechanistic link. They are graph-based predictions only, so this report focuses on the top-ranked indication.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals (knowledge-graph data, not label text):** The drug is linked to contraindications in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular conditions, including hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischaemia, Prinzmetal angina, stroke, transient ischaemic attack, cerebral infarction, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, and peripheral vascular disease. It is also linked to liver failure, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, obesity, hemoglobinopathy, and familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine.
- **Relevance to the new indication:** Vertebrobasilar insufficiency and hemiplegic migraine are closely related to brainstem aura. This reinforces the vasoconstriction concern noted above.
- **Drug Interactions:** 39 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include:
  - serotonergic and anorectic agents: lorcaserin, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, sibutramine
  - 5-HT3 antagonists: ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, palonosetron
  - methylene blue

  Moderate-level interactions include morphine (including liposomal morphine). Several others are of unknown severity, including omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, ranitidine, clarithromycin, bupropion, prednisone, budesonide and promethazine. Many of the major interactions relate to a possible additive serotonergic effect.
- Package insert warnings and contraindications could not be obtained. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but it is model-based only. There are no clinical trials and no literature specific to brainstem aura. The evidence is general migraine data, and the subtype carries a theoretical vasoconstriction safety concern that is reflected in the knowledge-graph contraindications (vertebrobasilar insufficiency, hemiplegic migraine). The indication is best treated as a research question until these gaps are resolved.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- A focused review of triptan safety and efficacy in brainstem aura and hemiplegic migraine
- Literature or trials specific to migraine with brainstem aura
- A confirmed approved indication text for the Singapore licence SIN14787P
