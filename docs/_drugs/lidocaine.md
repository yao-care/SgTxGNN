# Lidocaine: From Dermatitis and Neuralgia to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Lidocaine is a local anaesthetic that has been used for conditions such as dermatitis, neuralgia, postherpetic neuralgia, haemorrhoids and scabies.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis** (score 99.99%), but **no clinical trials or publications** currently support this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Lidocaine is generally known as a sodium channel blocker, which interrupts nerve signalling and produces local anaesthesia. Its established uses are pain control and surface or infiltration anaesthesia, including in the eye.

Mechanistically, the link to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is weak. Easing ocular surface discomfort is conceivable, but this is a symptom-level effect, not a disease-modifying one. Topical anaesthetics are also known to be toxic to the corneal epithelium and can cause keratitis when misused, which argues against therapeutic use in this condition.

For context, other conditions among the top ten predictions show somewhat more activity. **Conjunctival disorder** has two completed trials and several publications, though they concern anaesthesia for eye surgery, not treatment of the disease. **Atopic conjunctivitis** has indirect preclinical evidence (sensory nerve involvement in allergen passage), which remains a hypothesis. These are noted only as secondary research directions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on the model score alone, with no trials or publications for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. The known corneal toxicity of topical anaesthetics is a safety signal pointing the other way.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a clear disease-specific hypothesis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which block safety screening
- Preclinical or clinical evidence in ocular surface disease, including corneal epithelial safety
- Assessment of route and formulation compatibility (no ophthalmic product is evident in the Singapore registrations)
- Optionally, a separate evaluation of conjunctival disorder or atopic conjunctivitis as alternative research questions
