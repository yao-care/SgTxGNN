## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Lidocaine is generally known as a sodium channel blocker that stops nerve conduction. It is used as a surface and injectable anaesthetic.

For punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, the only plausible role is symptomatic: numbing ocular surface pain. There is no support for a disease-modifying benefit. Topical anaesthetics can also impair epithelial healing, and misuse of them is a recognised cause of toxic corneal damage. The very high model score therefore should not be read as evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, liver failure and liver disease, cirrhosis, epilepsy, kidney disease, adrenocortical insufficiency, hypothyroidism, open-angle glaucoma, herpes simplex keratitis, varicella zoster infection, psychotic disorders, osteoporosis and others. These need confirmation against the package insert.
- **Drug interactions:** the screening found 100 interactions. One is rated Major: **bupropion**. Moderate ones include aprepitant, cimetidine, liposomal morphine, obeticholic acid and several bowel-preparation products (polyethylene glycol with electrolytes, picosulfuric acid, sodium sulfate). Minor ones include dexamethasone, omeprazole and the aminoglycosides kanamycin, neomycin and streptomycin.
- **Published safety signals:** neuropsychiatric and cardiac adverse effects with intravenous lidocaine (PMID 19250287), methaemoglobinaemia with combined topical agents (PMID 41995267), and seizures at low doses in an animal report (PMID 19858165).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications, as label data was not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no trial or publication studies lidocaine in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, so the evidence level is L5. The plausible effect is symptomatic only, and corneal toxicity from topical anaesthetics is a safety concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical or preclinical studies in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis
- A review of corneal toxicity and epithelial healing risks with ocular lidocaine
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications
