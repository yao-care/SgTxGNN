# Hydroxychloroquine: From Malaria, Lupus and Rheumatoid Arthritis to Folliculitis Decalvans

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) is an antimalarial and anti-inflammatory drug, approved by the US FDA for malaria, lupus erythematosus and rheumatoid arthritis. The TxGNN model predicts it may help **Folliculitis Decalvans** (Quinquaud's folliculitis decalvans), a chronic scarring hair-loss disease. Support is currently **0 clinical trials** and **4 publications**, including one 49-patient retrospective study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not currently available. HCQ is known to have immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects, including inhibition of Toll-like receptor signalling and reduced cytokine production. These effects are plausibly relevant to the neutrophilic and lymphocytic inflammation seen in folliculitis decalvans.

Folliculitis decalvans is a chronic, relapsing neutrophilic scarring alopecia. The goal of treatment is to control inflammation and prevent further hair loss. HCQ is already used in other inflammatory and autoimmune conditions, and reviews list it among the therapies used for folliculitis decalvans.

Direct evidence is limited. The main support is a retrospective study of HCQ as **adjuvant** therapy. Its effect cannot be separated from that of the co-medications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, and the mechanism is plausible. However, the evidence is limited to reviews, one retrospective adjuvant-therapy cohort and a case report, with no registered trials. The Singapore package-insert safety data has also not been obtained. This is best treated as a research question rather than a candidate for adoption.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
