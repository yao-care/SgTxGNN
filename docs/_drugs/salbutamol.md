# Salbutamol: From Bronchodilator Use to Papillary Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Salbutamol is a beta2-adrenergic agonist bronchodilator that is already marketed in Singapore. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **papillary conjunctivitis**, but **no clinical trials or publications** currently support this prediction. It rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Salbutamol is a beta2-adrenoceptor agonist, and its efficacy in airway disease is well established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to papillary conjunctivitis.

One plausible but unverified route is beta2-adrenoceptor-mediated suppression of mast cell mediator release in the conjunctiva, which would reduce allergic inflammation. No trial or publication has tested this for papillary conjunctivitis. The very high TxGNN score (0.99996) should therefore be read as an unvalidated hypothesis, not as evidence of efficacy.
