# Salbutamol: From Asthma to Papillary Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Salbutamol is a short-acting beta-2 agonist bronchodilator, originally used for asthma and bronchial disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **papillary conjunctivitis** (rank 1), but **no clinical trials and no publications** were found for this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.
Among the other top-ranked predictions, **obstructive lung disease** has the strongest support (Phase 3 evidence), though it is close to the existing asthma/bronchial indication and is not a true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known pharmacology, salbutamol is a selective beta-2 adrenergic receptor agonist. It relaxes airway smooth muscle, which explains its efficacy in asthma and bronchial disease. Beta-2 agonism may also reduce mast cell mediator release and vascular permeability.

For papillary conjunctivitis, this offers only a speculative link, through possible suppression of allergic mediator release in the eye. Papillary conjunctivitis is often mechanically or contact-lens induced, so the benefit of a bronchodilator is uncertain. Preclinical work in other conjunctival models (guinea-pig allergic conjunctivitis and topical conjunctival anti-inflammatory activity of beta-2 agonists) is listed under the related prediction "atopic conjunctivitis", not papillary conjunctivitis itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / cautions (from knowledge-graph drug-disease links, not label text)**: hypertension, long QT syndrome, epilepsy, potassium deficiency, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, metabolic acidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia and bladder neck obstruction, ocular hypertension, angle-closure glaucoma, hyperthyroidism/Graves disease/thyrotoxicosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, myocardial ischemia and coronary artery disease. The glaucoma and ocular hypertension links deserve particular attention for any ocular use.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples of moderate-level ones include epinephrine, clarithromycin, cisapride, famotidine, and several antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, chlorpropamide). Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, budesonide, dexamethasone, and others) are minor.
- **Other signals**: Anaphylaxis to salbutamol has been reported (case report, PMID 18447148). A meta-analysis of maternal drug use and anorectal malformations (PMID 29747656) raises a possible teratogenicity question, not a therapeutic signal.

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, papillary conjunctivitis, has a very high model score but no trials or literature, and its mechanistic link is speculative. The only strongly supported prediction (obstructive lung disease) is essentially the existing asthma/bronchial indication, not a new use. Anaphylaxis and bronchiolitis are the more genuine repurposing candidates, but the anaphylaxis trial was terminated at n=24 and the bronchiolitis literature is largely negative.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, plus approved indication text for the Singapore licences
- Mechanism of action data
- For papillary conjunctivitis: preclinical or early clinical studies, and a feasibility assessment of an ophthalmic formulation, as none is registered
- For anaphylaxis: results or a re-powered trial of inhaled salbutamol as an adjunct to epinephrine
- For bronchiolitis: a review of whether new evidence would overturn guideline recommendations against routine bronchodilator use
- Safety review of the cardiovascular, metabolic and glaucoma-related cautions for any new population or route
