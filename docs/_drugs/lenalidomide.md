# Lenalidomide: From Multiple Myeloma and Del(5q) MDS to Myeloid Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenalidomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug derived from thalidomide. Published literature describes its approval for del(5q) myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and multiple myeloma. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**, and **50 clinical trials** and **20 publications** are linked to this direction. Most of the trials are small, early-phase or terminated studies, and the literature on AML efficacy is mixed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed original mechanism-of-action data is not available in the record. From the mechanistic rationale, lenalidomide binds cereblon (CRBN) in the CRL4-CRBN E3 ligase complex. This directs degradation of CK1α and of the transcription factors IKZF1 and IKZF3. It also activates T and NK cells.

Del(5q) MDS and AML are both myeloid diseases, and MDS can progress to AML. Del(5q) disease is haploinsufficient for CK1α, which makes it especially sensitive to lenalidomide. This is the best-established link between the drug and myeloid disease.

The mechanism is coherent but its direct AML activity is only plausible, not proven. The literature on AML remains contested: a 2018 meta-analysis (PMID 30271212) states that whether lenalidomide significantly improves response and survival in AML "remains controversial". The strongest supporting evidence comes from MDS, which is adjacent to AML but not the same disease.

## Safety Considerations

- **Secondary malignancy signal**: A 416-patient analysis of therapy-related myeloid neoplasms found lenalidomide exposure promotes TP53-mutated disease (PMID 35512188). Reports of secondary B-lymphoblastic leukemia after lenalidomide, mainly in myeloma and CLL, also exist, though causality is not proven.
- **Teratogenicity**: Lenalidomide is a thalidomide analogue, so pregnancy-prevention requirements should be confirmed in the package insert.

No drug-interaction records were found. For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Lenalidomide has a plausible CRBN-based mechanism and many AML trials, but these are mostly small Phase 1/2 studies, several terminated early. There is no completed Phase 3 in AML, and a meta-analysis calls its benefit in AML controversial. Published safety signals for therapy-related myeloid neoplasms and the missing local package insert data add further reasons to wait.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which block the safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Efficacy results from the completed AML Phase 2 trials, stratified by karyotype (especially del(5q) and TP53 status)
- A risk assessment for therapy-related myeloid neoplasms in this population

Two other predictions for this drug carry stronger evidence: partial deletion of chromosome 5q, and lymph node cancer (follicular lymphoma with rituximab). Both are rated L1, and they may be better candidates to pursue first.
