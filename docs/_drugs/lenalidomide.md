## Lenalidomide: Myeloid leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenalidomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug originally used for plasma cell myeloma, mantle cell lymphoma and myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**,
with **44 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently linked to this direction. Most trials are small, early-phase, or single-arm studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available from the drug database for this report. Based on the mechanistic analysis compiled for this prediction, lenalidomide binds cereblon (CRBN) and redirects the CRL4-CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase to degrade IKZF1, IKZF3 and CK1alpha. The CK1alpha degradation drives the response in del(5q) MDS. Lenalidomide also activates NK and T cells.

Myeloid leukemia is closely related to MDS, which is already an established use. MDS often progresses to acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and the two share biology such as del(5q) and immune dysregulation. Preclinical work also shows that CRBN levels influence how AML cells respond to lenalidomide (PMID 39881283).

This rationale is plausible, but the evidence in AML is weaker than in MDS. It is limited to small single-arm studies of combinations and maintenance therapy.

## Cytotoxicity

Lenalidomide is an antineoplastic drug, but it is an immunomodulatory agent rather than a conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy.

| Item | Content |
|------|------|
| Cytotoxicity Classification | Immunomodulatory / targeted protein-degradation agent (thalidomide analogue), not a conventional cytotoxic |
| Myelosuppression Risk | High. Neutropenia and thrombocytopenia are listed as contraindicated conditions in the knowledge graph, and haematological toxicity is a well-known dose-limiting effect |
| Emetogenicity Classification | Please refer to the package insert warnings and precautions |
| Monitoring Items | Complete blood count with differential, renal function (impaired renal function is flagged), thromboembolism signs |
| Handling Protection | Please refer to the package insert warnings and precautions. As a thalidomide analogue with embryo-related concerns, strict pregnancy-prevention precautions are expected |

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph** (not taken from label text): thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pulmonary embolism, impaired renal function, cancer, embryoma.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The two flagged as Major are **rosuvastatin** and **simvastatin**. Eliglustat and naltrexone are Moderate. The rest, including prednisone, metformin and omeprazole, are of unknown severity.
- **Safety signal in the literature**: several case reports and series describe secondary B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia after prolonged lenalidomide maintenance in myeloma patients. This is a long-term safety concern.

The package insert warnings and contraindications could not be retrieved for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Many phase 1 and 2 studies, including the larger randomised azacitidine ± lenalidomide trials, and the approved use in del(5q) MDS support biological plausibility in myeloid malignancies. However, AML-specific evidence comes from small, mostly single-arm studies, and no completed phase 3 trial in myeloid leukemia was found. Haematological toxicity and secondary-malignancy signals call for careful patient selection.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from the drug database
- Published results and outcomes from the randomised trials (e.g. NCT01358734, NCT01522976) to confirm the benefit in AML
- A haematological monitoring and risk-management plan, including pregnancy-prevention measures
- Review of the Major statin interactions and long-term secondary-malignancy surveillance
