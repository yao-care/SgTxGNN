# Acetazolamide: From Glaucoma and Heart Failure to Exercise-Induced Malignant Hyperthermia

## One-Sentence Summary

Acetazolamide is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, originally used for glaucoma, absence epilepsy, congestive heart failure and altitude sickness.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia**,
but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, acetazolamide is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. Its efficacy in glaucoma, epilepsy, heart failure and altitude sickness is established, and it may mechanistically influence muscle pH and ion handling.

The link to exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia is speculative. It is a graph-based inference with no supporting trial or literature data. The very high score (99.95%) reflects network proximity, not demonstrated clinical benefit.

Other predictions in the same list (ranked 2 to 10) have somewhat more indirect evidence, but none reaches Phase 2/3 trial-level support. Examples are hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and cardiomyopathy, supported only by heart failure reviews, case reports and one animal study. For intestinal obstruction, the literature points to harm (acetazolamide-induced ileus).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, renal tubular acidosis, hematologic disease, gallbladder disease, gout, respiratory failure, type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, and adrenal insufficiency conditions (Addison disease, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, adrenocortical insufficiency and related disorders).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Those shown include:
  - **Major**: acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), cisapride.
  - **Moderate**: antidiabetic agents (acarbose, acetohexamide, alogliptin, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, dapagliflozin, albiglutide, dulaglutide), corticosteroids (beclomethasone, betamethasone, dexamethasone), amphotericin B formulations, and laxatives (bisacodyl, castor oil).
  - **Minor**: doxycycline.
- **Case-report safety signals**: non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema after intravenous acetazolamide, and acetazolamide-induced adynamic ileus. Acetazolamide may also worsen metabolic acidosis and raise ammonia in cirrhosis.

Package insert warnings were not available. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia has no trials or publications (L5), and the mechanistic link is unconfirmed. The drug is already marketed in Singapore, but only for its existing indications, and the safety profile includes notable contraindications and interactions.
