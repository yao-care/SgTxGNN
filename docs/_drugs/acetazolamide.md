## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for acetazolamide: "Acetazolamide is a potent carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, effective in the control of fluid secretion (e.g., some types of glaucoma), in the treatment of certain convulsive disorders (e.g., epilepsy), and in the promotion of diuresis in instances of abnormal fluid retention (e.g., cardiac edema). Acetazolamide is not a mercurial diuretic." ([DailyMed: acetazolamide prescribing information, Chartwell RX, LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=e8e48a25-5f6c-4b5a-a5f2-ee87aabea33b), label version 2026-11-10; accessed 2026-11-19.) Acetazolamide is known as a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, a class that alters acid–base balance and ion handling in tissues.

One plausible, but speculative, link is that changes in muscle pH and ion handling could influence the heat and metabolic stress seen in exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia. No study retrieved for this report tests or supports that idea. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis generated by the model, not as evidence of benefit.
