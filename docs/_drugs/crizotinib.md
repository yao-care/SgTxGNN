# Crizotinib: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Gingival Fibromatosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Crizotinib is an oral kinase inhibitor originally used to treat non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gingival fibromatosis**, but this is a computational prediction only, with **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting it.
Among the other top-ranked predictions, only the lung-related ones have any supporting studies, and these mostly describe the drug's existing NSCLC use rather than a new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature retrieved, crizotinib is an ATP-competitive inhibitor of the receptor tyrosine kinases ALK, ROS1 and c-MET. Its efficacy is well established in ALK-rearranged and ROS1-rearranged NSCLC.

For the top-ranked prediction, gingival fibromatosis, no plausible link to ALK, ROS1 or MET inhibition is supported by the available data. The very high score (0.998) is a model output and should not be read as evidence of benefit. The same applies to most of the other top-ten predictions (fibroma of lung, pulmonary sulcus neoplasm, VCP-related myopathy, junctional epidermolysis bullosa and a rare pigmentation syndrome).

A few lower-ranked predictions are lung-related and have some supporting material, but it is indirect:
- **Lung hilum carcinoma:** Two case reports describe crizotinib in ROS1-positive and ALK-positive lung adenocarcinoma. This is the already approved NSCLC use. The hilar location is incidental, so it is not a new indication.
- **Lung benign neoplasm:** The literature consists of Phase 3 trials in malignant ALK-positive NSCLC. These do not show benefit in benign lung tumours.
- **Lung germ cell tumour:** Two broad trials exist, but neither is shown to enrol this tumour type.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. The first 20 reviewed include these **Major** interactions:
- Clarithromycin
- Cisapride
- Dolasetron
- Eliglustat
- Granisetron

Several Moderate interactions were also noted, including famotidine, metformin, loperamide, bupropion, aprepitant, dexamethasone and budesonide.

**Safety signals from the literature (case reports):** The retrieved publications describe fatal fulminant liver failure, multiple cardiac toxicities (bradycardia, QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmia, pericarditis), sick sinus syndrome, erythema multiforme, erosive oesophagitis and dysgeusia during crizotinib treatment. These are individual reports and do not establish incidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, gingival fibromatosis, rests on a model score alone (Evidence Level L5). No trials or publications support it, and no plausible mechanism has been identified. The lung-related predictions with some evidence either duplicate the existing NSCLC indication (lung hilum carcinoma, where the evidence is limited to case reports) or are only indirectly supported (lung benign neoplasm, lung germ cell tumour). The safety signals, including hepatotoxicity and cardiac effects, also argue against pursuing weakly supported indications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, to assess whether ALK, ROS1 or MET signalling is relevant to gingival fibromatosis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, to complete safety screening
- Preclinical or case-level evidence showing an ALK, ROS1 or MET driver in gingival fibromatosis
- A review of the lung hilum carcinoma case reports to confirm whether they represent a distinct indication or the existing molecularly defined NSCLC use
- A benefit-risk assessment covering hepatic and cardiac monitoring and the major drug interactions, if any indication advances
