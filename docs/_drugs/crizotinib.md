# Crizotinib: From ALK-Positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Gingival Fibromatosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Crizotinib is an oral ALK/ROS1/MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor, described in the literature as approved for ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gingival fibromatosis** with a very high score (99.81%).
However, **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Crizotinib is an ATP-competitive inhibitor of the receptor tyrosine kinases ALK, ROS1 and c-MET. Its established benefit is in cancers driven by ALK or ROS1 rearrangements. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in this evidence pack.

Gingival fibromatosis is a benign overgrowth of gum tissue. No ALK, ROS1 or MET involvement in this condition is documented. The link between the approved cancer use and this new indication is therefore not established by any data provided. The high score reflects a pattern in the knowledge graph, not clinical or preclinical evidence. Without a shown kinase driver in the tissue, the prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Safety Considerations

Published case reports and reviews in the evidence pack point to these signals:
- **Hepatotoxicity**: a case of fatal fulminant liver failure after 24 days of treatment (PMID 26898609).
- **Cardiac effects**: bradycardia, QT prolongation and other cardiac toxicities (PMID 29717400).
- **Interstitial lung disease**: reported in a case with successful rechallenge (PMID 24872405).

No drug-interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The gingival fibromatosis prediction is L5, with no trials, no literature and no documented kinase-driven mechanism. Crizotinib's known hepatic and cardiac toxicity is hard to justify for a benign gum condition without any supporting evidence.

Among the other predicted indications, lung hilum carcinoma has the most direct evidence (case reports of ALK/ROS1-positive lung cancer). It is a restatement of the approved lung cancer use, not a new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indication), which is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Evidence of ALK, ROS1 or MET activity in gingival fibromatosis tissue
- Preclinical or case-level data showing an effect in this condition
- A risk-benefit assessment given the known toxicity profile
