## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid. It binds tubulin and disrupts microtubule assembly, which causes cells to arrest in mitosis. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects the known pharmacology of the drug class.

Rhabdomyosarcoma is a rapidly proliferating soft tissue sarcoma, so an antimitotic agent is biologically plausible. A related vinca alkaloid, vincristine, is already a standard part of rhabdomyosarcoma regimens (such as VAC). This gives class-level support, but it is not direct evidence for vinblastine itself.

The evidence specific to vinblastine is thin. It consists of case-level reports in which vinblastine was given within multi-drug regimens, so its individual contribution cannot be separated out. There is also preclinical work showing that microtubule-destabilising drugs act synergistically with PLK1 inhibitors in rhabdomyosarcoma models.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, and the antimitotic mechanism is plausible. However, there are no registered trials. The only vinblastine-specific clinical evidence is a few case reports, with vinblastine given within multi-drug regimens. Vincristine, not vinblastine, is the established vinca alkaloid in rhabdomyosarcoma.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A systematic review of vinblastine-containing regimens in rhabdomyosarcoma, compared with the vincristine-based standard
- Evidence for the indication text of the Singapore licences
- Other predicted indications are weaker or more indirect: monocytic leukaemia has the most clinical data, but it is old, small and no longer reflects standard care, and the remaining predictions rest on the model score alone.
