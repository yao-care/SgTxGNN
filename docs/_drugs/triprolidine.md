# Triprolidine: From Oral Antihistamine to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Triprolidine is a first-generation H1-antihistamine, and its Singapore registration records do not state an approved indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Triprolidine is a first-generation H1-receptor antagonist, and histamine-mediated wheal and flare is the core mechanism of allergic urticaria. H1 antihistamines are the standard therapy class for urticaria, so the biological rationale is strong.

However, this is largely a class-effect inference. Most of the retrieved papers concern acrivastine, a triprolidine-derived analogue, not triprolidine itself. The only triprolidine-specific paper (1965) studies skin response to UV radiation, not urticaria.

The drug is already marketed in Singapore, so its general safety profile is established. No triprolidine-specific urticaria trial is available.

Among the other nine predictions, only autoimmune urticaria has any literature, and that is also indirect (acrivastine trials in chronic idiopathic urticaria). The rest have no retrieved evidence. Some are broad categories or traits rather than treatable indications, such as "nasal cavity disease" and "IgE responsiveness, atopic".

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The class rationale for H1 antihistamines in urticaria is sound. However, the evidence comes from acrivastine and other antihistamines, with no triprolidine-specific urticaria trial. The package insert warnings and contraindications are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications), downloaded and parsed
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Triprolidine-specific clinical evidence in urticaria, or a justified class-effect argument with acrivastine and other H1 antagonist data
- Review of the Singapore product labels against urticaria use
- Narrowing of the broader predicted terms (for example, nasal cavity disease) to specific conditions before any evaluation
