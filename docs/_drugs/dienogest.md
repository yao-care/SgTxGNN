# Dienogest: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Dienogest is an oral progestin, and published literature describes it as approved for endometriosis in many countries. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea**, but the **4 linked clinical trials** and **6 publications** all concern endometriosis. No study directly tests dienogest as a treatment for amenorrhea, and the predicted link probably reflects dienogest *causing* amenorrhea rather than treating it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, dienogest is a progestin with highly selective progesterone activity and minimal androgenic activity. It suppresses ovulation and the endometrium, and its efficacy in endometriosis is well documented.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects that dienogest commonly induces amenorrhea. Inducing amenorrhea is even a stated goal of hormonal endometriosis therapy (PMID 41329046). That is a treatment effect or adverse effect, not evidence that dienogest treats amenorrhea, and the direction of the association is unresolved.

The mechanistic case is therefore weak. A drug that suppresses the menstrual cycle is not an obvious therapy for a condition defined by absent periods. Its use would depend on the cause of the amenorrhea, which the evidence does not address.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is indirect only. All trials and literature address endometriosis rather than amenorrhea, and the high model score most likely reflects dienogest inducing amenorrhea. The other nine predicted indications (for example primary ovarian failure, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and several chromosomal or genetic syndromes) also lack plausible mechanistic links, and several lack any evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA website, which block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- A clinical rationale for which type of amenorrhea (if any) dienogest could treat, and confirmation that the direction of effect is therapeutic
- Protocol review of NCT07164183 to confirm whether any amenorrhea population or endpoint is included
- Approved indication text for the four Singapore registrations
