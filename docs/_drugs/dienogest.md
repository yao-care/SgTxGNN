## Dienogest: From Endometriosis-Type Progestin Therapy to amenorrhea (disease)
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dienogest is an oral progestin that is marketed in Singapore as 2 mg tablets (its approved indication text is not available in the data compiled for this report). The TxGNN model predicts it may be associated with **amenorrhea**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **7 publications** retrieved, most of which are indirect. Amenorrhea is better understood as a known effect of dienogest than as a treatment target.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the literature, dienogest is a fourth-generation progestin with selective progesterone-receptor activity and minimal androgenic activity. It has an antiproliferative effect on the endometrium and partly suppresses ovarian oestradiol production.

Amenorrhea is a frequent, well-documented outcome of dienogest treatment in endometriosis, where inducing amenorrhoea and a hypoestrogenic environment is a treatment aim. This is the likely source of the model's prediction. However, the link is mainly **a pharmacodynamic effect or adverse effect**, not evidence that dienogest *treats* amenorrhea. Progestins as a class are used in secondary amenorrhea (withdrawal bleeding, endometrial protection), which gives plausible but indirect support. No direct data for dienogest in treating amenorrhea were found.

Other predicted indications (ranks 2–10) are weaker. Primary ovarian failure and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism are mechanistically opposed to dienogest's ovary-suppressing action. Breast fibrocystic disease has one small, low-level study. The remainder (such as growth hormone deficiency and chromosomal disorders) appear to be model artefacts with no supporting evidence.

- **Drug Interactions**: 37 interactions were identified in total. Major-level interactions among those listed include sugammadex, griseofulvin, bexarotene, brigatinib, dabrafenib and encorafenib. Moderate-level interactions include linaclotide, metreleptin, prucalopride, miltefosine, adalimumab, anakinra, cladribine and others. Ethanol is a minor interaction.
- **Literature signal**: a 2026 case report describes primary hyperparathyroidism unmasked after starting dienogest, which is consistent with the calcium-related knowledge-graph flags.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and no literature testing dienogest as a treatment for amenorrhea. Amenorrhea is mainly an expected effect of dienogest, so the prediction is mechanistically circular rather than a true new therapeutic use. Evidence is limited to L4, and the safety label data needed for screening were not available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (HSA) warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Direct clinical evidence of dienogest treating (not causing) amenorrhea, or clarification of the intended use
- A safety review of the knowledge-graph contraindications, especially cardiovascular, hepatic and calcium-metabolism concerns
- Reassessment of other predicted indications, of which breast fibrocystic disease has the only (weak) supporting signal
