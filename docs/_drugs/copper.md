# Copper: From Parenteral Trace-Element Supplementation to Esotropia

## One-Sentence Summary

Copper is a trace element supplied in parenteral nutrition products. No approved indication text is recorded for it in Singapore.

The best-supported alternative among the predictions is **dermatitis**, with **2 clinical trials** (one withdrawn, one very small) and **20 publications**, mostly reviews and indirect studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, copper is an essential trace element given in parenteral nutrition, mainly to prevent or correct copper deficiency. Its mechanistic relevance to a disorder of ocular alignment is not apparent.

For esotropia, no mechanistic link between copper and the condition could be identified. The score of 0.96 comes from graph-based prediction only, with no supporting trials or literature. The prediction should be treated as a weak, unverified signal.

Of the other predictions, dermatitis is the only one with a plausible biological rationale. Copper and zinc balance is linked to skin inflammation, and preclinical work suggests that cuproptosis (copper-dependent cell death) contributes to chronic skin inflammation. However, much of the literature concerns zinc rather than copper. Copper can also act as a trigger of contact dermatitis rather than a treatment. No clinical evidence supports copper as a therapy for dermatitis. Dry eye syndrome has some preclinical support (a copper-selenide nanoparticle hydrogel), but that is a nanomaterial formulation, not copper as a marketed drug.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The literature compiled for this report notes that copper can cause contact hypersensitivity reactions, and that zinc supplementation can induce copper deficiency. No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, esotropia, has no trials, no literature and no identifiable mechanism, so it rests on the model score alone (evidence level L5). Dermatitis is the only prediction with some indirect support (L4), but the evidence is weak: one withdrawn trial, one tiny pilot, and mostly zinc-focused or hypersensitivity-related literature. It is best treated as a research question rather than a candidate for development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences
- For dermatitis: controlled clinical studies showing a therapeutic benefit of copper, with clarification of whether copper is therapeutic or a trigger
- Route compatibility assessment, since the marketed products are injectables while skin or eye uses would likely need topical or ophthalmic routes
