# Betaxolol: From Hypertension and Glaucoma to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Betaxolol is a beta-1 selective blocker originally used for hypertension, open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **primary hereditary glaucoma** (score 99.7%), but **no clinical trials or publications** support this specific term.
The best-supported related prediction, **open angle glaucoma** (2 Phase 3 trials, 18 publications), overlaps with betaxolol's existing use rather than being a true new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, betaxolol is a beta-1 selective adrenergic antagonist. In the eye, it lowers intraocular pressure (IOP) by reducing aqueous humour production. Systemically, it lowers blood pressure and heart rate.

The top prediction, primary hereditary glaucoma, is probably driven by betaxolol's established IOP-lowering effect in other glaucoma types. No evidence was found in a hereditary or congenital glaucoma population, so this remains a model extrapolation only.

The strongly supported predictions (glaucoma 1, open angle; open angle glaucoma) largely match betaxolol's existing use, so they confirm current practice and are not new repurposing opportunities. Other predictions, such as malignant renovascular hypertension, pulmonary hypertension and closed-angle glaucoma, appear to arise from shared "hypertension" or "glaucoma" terms in the knowledge graph. Their mechanistic fit is weak, and some carry clinical concerns (for example, beta-blockers are generally used cautiously in pulmonary arterial hypertension).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease relationships, not from the package insert): asthma, pulmonary emphysema and other bronchial disease; atrioventricular block; diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis and hypoglycaemia; myasthenia gravis; phaeochromocytoma and related tumours; kidney, liver and gallbladder disease; psoriasis; and anxiety or dysthymic disorders.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found in the interaction database, and 20 are listed in the data compiled for this report, mostly Moderate and some Minor. Moderate examples include epinephrine, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), atropine, hyoscyamine, bupropion, morphine, acetohexamide and several calcium salts. Minor examples include acetylsalicylic acid, aluminium hydroxide and attapulgite.
- **Class-related cautions for the glaucoma use:** systemic beta-blocker effects (bradycardia, AV block, decompensated heart failure, severe asthma or COPD). Ocular betaxolol also lowers IOP somewhat less than non-selective beta-blockers and prostaglandin analogues.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, primary hereditary glaucoma)

**Rationale:**
- The high TxGNN score (99.7%) is not backed by any trial or publication, and the prediction is likely an extrapolation from betaxolol's known IOP-lowering action in other glaucomas. Evidence level is L5.
- The open angle glaucoma prediction has L1-level support and merits **Proceed with Guardrails**, but it largely duplicates betaxolol's existing indication and is not new repurposing.
- The other predictions (malignant hypertensive conditions, pulmonary hypertension, closed-angle glaucoma, obsolete or very rare terms) lack evidence or are clinically implausible, so they stay on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the registered Singapore product
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Direct clinical or genetic-cohort evidence for betaxolol in hereditary or congenital glaucoma
- Assessment of route compatibility, since the registered product is oral and glaucoma treatment typically needs an ophthalmic formulation
- A systemic beta-blocker safety screen (cardiac, respiratory and diabetic risks) before any new use
