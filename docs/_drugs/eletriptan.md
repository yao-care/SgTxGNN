## One-Sentence Summary

Eletriptan is a triptan (5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist) originally used for the acute treatment of migraine.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Eletriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist. The literature compiled for this report describes it as acting by constricting cranial blood vessels and inhibiting release of trigeminal neuropeptides. Detailed mechanism data were not available in the structured drug record, so this description comes from the published reviews.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype that shares the core biology of migraine: trigeminovascular activation and cortical spreading depression. A mechanistic link is therefore plausible.

However, **the link does not by itself support efficacy**. Triptans have historically been cautioned against, or contraindicated, in this subtype because of the theoretical risk of vasoconstriction. The retrieved literature is general migraine evidence (guidelines, reviews, pharmacology) and is not specific to brainstem aura. One randomised study (PMID 15469451) found no benefit when eletriptan was taken during the aura phase. One case report (PMID 25155004) describes a myocardial infarction after eletriptan.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for migraine with brainstem aura.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and warnings from the knowledge graph (not label text):** The following conditions are linked to eletriptan.
  - Cardiovascular: hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischaemia, Prinzmetal angina, peripheral vascular disease, Raynaud disease.
  - Cerebrovascular: stroke, transient ischaemic attack, cerebral infarction, brain ischaemia, vertebrobasilar insufficiency.
  - Migraine subtype: familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine.
  - Other: serotonin syndrome, ischaemic bowel disease, liver disease or failure, diabetes, obesity.
- **Drug interactions:** 94 interactions are recorded.
  - Major: clarithromycin (a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor), the serotonergic or appetite-suppressant agents lorcaserin, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine and sibutramine, and the 5-HT3 antagonists ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron and palonosetron.
  - Moderate: aprepitant, morphine, cimetidine, clotrimazole and miconazole.

Please refer to the package insert for the full official warnings, because the label text was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is not backed by any trial or subtype-specific publication. The available evidence is general migraine data, one study showing no benefit during the aura phase, and a cardiovascular safety signal. Triptans are also traditionally cautioned in this subtype.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific clinical or observational data on triptan use and safety in migraine with brainstem aura
- A review of the Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication), which is a blocking gap
- A cardiovascular and cerebrovascular risk assessment for this population
- A CYP3A4 and serotonergic drug-interaction management plan
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
