# Eletriptan: From Acute Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Eletriptan is a triptan (5-HT1B/1D agonist) marketed for the acute treatment of migraine.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but **no clinical trials** are registered for this subtype. The 18 retrieved publications cover acute migraine in general, and one aura-phase RCT was negative.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. From the literature, eletriptan is a 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist. It constricts cranial blood vessels and inhibits trigeminal neuropeptide release, which is the standard mechanism for acute migraine relief.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine, so a link is plausible. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the drug's general association with migraine rather than any subtype-specific signal.

There are three caveats:
- None of the retrieved literature addresses the brainstem-aura subtype.
- One RCT (PMID 15469451) found no benefit when eletriptan 80 mg was taken during the aura phase of migraine with aura.
- Triptan labelling generally lists basilar-type migraine (the older name for this subtype) and hemiplegic migraine as contraindications. This is a direct safety concern for the proposed use.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for migraine with brainstem aura.

## Safety Considerations

- **Cardiovascular events**: A published case report describes myocardial infarction shortly after eletriptan use (PMID 25155004).
- **Subtype-specific concern**: Triptan labelling generally lists basilar-type and hemiplegic migraine as contraindications, so use in brainstem aura needs specialist review before any further work.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings, contraindications and drug interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on the drug's general link to migraine, with no subtype-specific trial or study. The one aura-phase RCT was negative, and triptan labelling raises a possible contraindication for this subtype.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A clinical and safety review of eletriptan in brainstem aura, including the contraindication question
- Subtype-specific efficacy and safety evidence, for example a trial or a dedicated systematic review
