## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Venlafaxine is known to be an SNRI. It blocks serotonin reuptake and, at higher doses, norepinephrine reuptake. Its efficacy in depression and anxiety disorders is well established.

The link to the predicted indication is weak. Ohdo syndrome is a rare genetic disorder (KAT6B-related) with intellectual disability and blepharophimosis (narrow eyelid openings). It is not a monoaminergic disorder, and no known pathway connects SNRI pharmacology to its underlying biology. The same applies to the closely related entries "blepharophimosis - intellectual disability syndrome, Ohdo type". These are likely neighbouring-node artefacts in the knowledge graph, not independent signals.

The high score reflects graph-based pattern matching, not pharmacological or clinical reasoning. On the available information, this prediction should be treated as unsupported.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a graph-based score, with no trials, no publications, and no plausible mechanistic link between SNRI pharmacology and KAT6B-related pathology. Paediatric safety is an additional concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible mechanistic hypothesis, ideally supported by preclinical or cell-based data.
- Detailed mechanism of action data and the Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications.
- A paediatric safety assessment.

**Note:** Other predictions from the same analysis have far more support and may be better candidates to pursue first:
- Dysthymic disorder (L2): a meta-analysis and several venlafaxine-specific studies.
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder (L1): double-blind RCTs against paroxetine and a large pragmatic trial in progress.
