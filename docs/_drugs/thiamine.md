## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Thiamine is generally known as a cofactor for enzymes in carbohydrate and energy metabolism, such as pyruvate dehydrogenase and transketolase.

Resistance to thyroid hormone is a monogenic disorder caused by mutations in the thyroid hormone receptor beta gene (THRB) that impair receptor function. No known pathway links thiamine to correcting a defective receptor. The high TxGNN score (0.9943) is a computational prediction only, and the data compiled for this report do not support a mechanistic link.

### Other predictions

Two further predictions were returned. Both are also model-driven and neither is supported by a study directly testing thiamine as a treatment:

- **Primary hereditary glaucoma** (score 0.9940, L5): no trials or literature. A plausible but unvalidated idea is that thiamine's role in mitochondrial energy metabolism could matter for retinal ganglion cell vulnerability. This does not address the genetic causes of the disease.
- **Open-angle glaucoma** (score 0.9936): the literature includes nutritional epidemiology and a 1979 blood-level study (see below). Because none of these studies tests thiamine as a treatment for the disease, this is also treated as L5 under the evidence-level rules, although it is the best-supported of the three predictions. The data pack labelled it L4, but the studies are associational and do not show that thiamine changes outcomes.
