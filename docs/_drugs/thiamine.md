# Thiamine: From Nutritional Supplementation to Hyperthyroidism

## One-Sentence Summary

Thiamine (vitamin B1) is marketed in Singapore as a single-ingredient vitamin product, mainly in B-vitamin tablets and injections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hyperthyroidism**, specifically as supportive repletion of the relative thiamine deficiency that a hypermetabolic state can cause, not as an antithyroid treatment.
Support is limited to **1 small completed trial (12 patients)** and **case reports and observational literature**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, thiamine is an essential vitamin that acts as a coenzyme in carbohydrate and oxidative energy metabolism. Its efficacy in correcting thiamine deficiency is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to hyperthyroidism.

Hyperthyroidism raises metabolic rate and thiamine turnover, which can cause relative thiamine deficiency. The literature describes the consequences: high-output heart failure resembling wet beriberi and Wernicke's encephalopathy, especially when severe vomiting accompanies hyperthyroidism in pregnancy. In this setting, thiamine would support patients as repletion of a secondary deficiency. It would not treat the thyroid disease itself.

Graves disease, the main cause of hyperthyroidism, ranks 8th among the predictions (score 98.15%). It shares the same rationale and the same limited evidence. Other predicted indications were considered, but the evidence for each is weaker or indirect:
- **Pulmonary hypertension** (score 98.97%) has the most clinical literature. Multiple case series and a prospective observational study describe thiamine-responsive pulmonary hypertension in thiamine-deficient infants. There is no RCT, and the finding applies only to deficient or at-risk populations. This is the only prediction at the "Proceed with Guardrails" stage.
- **Open-angle glaucoma** has only associative observational and mechanistic studies.
- **Resistance to thyroid hormone, primary hereditary glaucoma, hyperthyroxinemia, kyphoscoliotic heart disease, peripheral vascular disease and neonatal thyrotoxicosis** have no supporting evidence, or only indirect evidence, beyond the model prediction.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The data compiled for this report did not include warnings or contraindications from the Singapore package inserts, and no drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The hyperthyroidism prediction rests on one small, exploratory trial (12 patients, no results available) and case reports. Thiamine would support deficiency-related complications and would not treat the thyroid disease. Safety label data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from the completed pilot trial (NCT02767245), or a larger controlled study in thyrotoxic patients with confirmed thiamine deficiency
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- Mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the local registrations
- A separate evaluation of pulmonary hypertension in thiamine-deficient populations, where the observational evidence is stronger, as a possible alternative research direction
