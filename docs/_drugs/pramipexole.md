# Pramipexole: From Parkinson's Disease and Restless Legs Syndrome to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Pramipexole is a dopamine agonist, described in the retrieved literature as a treatment for Parkinson's disease and restless legs syndrome. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**. Direct evidence is very thin: **1 registered trial** (not ADHD-specific) and **9 retrieved publications**, only one of which is an ADHD-specific case report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for pramipexole is not available in the input record. Based on the pharmacology described in the retrieved literature, pramipexole is a non-ergot D2/D3 dopamine agonist with D3 preference. Its efficacy in Parkinson's disease and restless legs syndrome is well documented, and mechanistically it may be applicable to ADHD.

Dopaminergic dysregulation is implicated in ADHD, so a dopamine agonist is a plausible candidate. The supporting signals are indirect:
- A randomized, placebo-controlled trial in Tourette's syndrome.
- Restless legs and periodic limb movement literature. These conditions often co-occur with ADHD.
- A preclinical study linking the dopamine D4 receptor, which is genetically associated with ADHD, to impulsive-control disorders.

The only ADHD-specific clinical item is a single case report, in which pramipexole was combined with atomoxetine. The link is plausible but unproven.

## Safety Considerations

Safety fields in the Singapore registry data are empty, and no drug interactions were found. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The retrieved literature raises these signals:
- **Impulse-control problems**: A Swedish nationwide register study reported an increased risk of gambling disorder with pramipexole. The NCT00558766 summary notes that Parkinson's medications can cause compulsive behaviour. This is relevant to ADHD, where impulsivity is a core feature.
- **Psychiatric effects**: A case report describes schizophrenia decompensation when pramipexole was added to clozapine.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The ADHD prediction rests mainly on the model score and indirect dopaminergic reasoning. No ADHD-specific trial exists, the single registered trial is unrelated, and only one case report addresses ADHD. In addition, pramipexole's documented impulse-control risk is a concern for an impulsivity-related disorder. Of the other predictions in the pack, only schizophrenia has a Phase 3 add-on trial (NCT01320982, status unknown) and a 2022 RCT (PMID 35921506), and it is rated L2. Schizophrenia may be a better candidate for prioritisation than ADHD.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- ADHD-specific controlled evidence, or a decision to re-prioritise toward the schizophrenia indication
- An impulse-control and psychiatric safety monitoring plan
