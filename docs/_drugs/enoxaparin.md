# Enoxaparin: From Pulmonary Embolism to Thrombophilia due to Protein C Deficiency

## One-Sentence Summary

Enoxaparin is a low-molecular-weight heparin originally used for thromboembolic conditions such as pulmonary embolism, disseminated intravascular coagulation and purpura fulminans.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombophilia due to protein C deficiency (autosomal recessive)**,
but currently there are **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** directly supporting this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, enoxaparin is a low-molecular-weight heparin that inhibits factor Xa and thrombin through antithrombin. Its efficacy in thrombotic conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to protein C deficiency.

Protein C deficiency is a prothrombotic state in which the body's natural anticoagulant pathway is impaired. Anticoagulation is therefore plausible for preventing and treating thrombosis in this setting. This also resembles purpura fulminans, an existing enoxaparin-related indication that is a known complication of severe protein C deficiency.

However, no trials or literature were retrieved for this prediction. The high TxGNN score (0.996) is a model prediction only and has not been verified by clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure, thrombocytopenia (including heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), haematologic and blood coagulation diseases, haemophilia, antithrombin III deficiency, Glanzmann thrombasthenia and other qualitative platelet defects, intracranial/intracerebral haemorrhage, placental abruption, active peptic ulcer disease, infective endocarditis, inflammatory bowel disease/ulcerative colitis, biliary tract disease/obstructive jaundice, osteoporosis, and several retinal diseases (diabetic, hypertensive retinopathy, retinitis).
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; the sample reviewed includes these **Major** interactions:
  - Antiplatelet and NSAID agents: acetylsalicylic acid, ketorolac, ibuprofen, abciximab
  - Thrombolytic: alteplase
  - Other agents: deferasirox, acalabrutinib, trastuzumab emtansine, ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab (including I-131)
  - Moderate interactions include fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, sibutramine, sugammadex, aliskiren, radioactive iodide (I-123, I-131) and topical diclofenac.

  Most of these combinations increase bleeding risk.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible, because anticoagulation is used in protein C deficiency. However, there are no trials or literature for it, so the evidence level is L5 (model prediction only). The label safety data were also unavailable.

Other predicted indications were reviewed and also remain at Hold. Several are mechanistically doubtful or potentially harmful, such as primary platelet release disorder, pseudo-von Willebrand disease and the retinal conditions, where bleeding risk is a concern. Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura and vertebral artery occlusion have only indirect case-level literature (L4).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, to complete safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clinical studies or guideline evidence on enoxaparin in protein C deficiency, including dosing and monitoring in this population
- A bleeding-risk assessment, given the major interactions and the knowledge-graph contraindications above
