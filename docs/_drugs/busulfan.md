# Busulfan: From Hematologic Conditioning (Original Indication Not Recorded) to Myelodysplastic Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Busulfan is a myeloablative alkylating agent registered in Singapore as an injection (Busulfex) and an oral tablet (Myran). It is predicted by the TxGNN model to be useful for **myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)**, where it is already used as a conditioning agent before stem cell transplantation. The data compiled for this report include **40 clinical trials** (including a completed Phase 3 RCT) and **20 publications** for this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, busulfan is a bifunctional alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and is myeloablative. In MDS it forms the backbone of conditioning regimens given before allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). It eradicates the malignant clone and creates marrow space for donor engraftment.

This is established transplant practice rather than a new mechanism. Busulfan is a conditioning component, not a standalone disease-modifying therapy for MDS. The "new indication" is therefore best understood as a transplant-conditioning use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications (label)**: Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug–disease relationships, not label text): epilepsy, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anemia (including macrocytic anemia and Fanconi anemia), pulmonary fibrosis, hyperglycemia, infectious disease (including fungal infections and dermatophytosis), potassium deficiency, and hyperuricemia.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The main ones are:
  - **Major**: metronidazole
  - **Moderate**: aprepitant, miconazole
  - **Minor**: levofloxacin
  - Others with unknown severity include amphotericin B, vancomycin, corticosteroids (prednisone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone), proton pump inhibitors, H2 blockers, antiemetics (ondansetron, granisetron, metoclopramide), morphine, and mannitol.
- **Long-term risk**: Literature notes a risk of secondary malignancy and long-term complications after busulfan-based conditioning, particularly in children.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple Phase 2 trials, completed Phase 3 randomized trials and published RCTs support busulfan-based conditioning in MDS. However, this reflects established transplant practice, not a new standalone therapy, and the label-level safety data are missing. The lower-ranked predictions (L5 candidates such as seborrheic keratosis and feline AIDS) have no supporting evidence and should be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), as the current licence records have no indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation that the use is limited to the injectable formulation within specialist transplant centres, with a defined dosing and exposure-monitoring plan
- A safety monitoring plan for high-risk groups (older or comorbid patients, children, and patients with pre-existing cytopenias, lung disease or seizure history)
- Review of the major interaction with metronidazole and the antifungal and antiemetic co-medications commonly used in transplant
