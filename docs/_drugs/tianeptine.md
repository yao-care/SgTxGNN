## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tianeptine is an atypical antidepressant with a modified tricyclic structure. The supplied literature describes it as increasing serotonin uptake in the brain (unlike most antidepressants), reducing the stress-related hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal response, and reducing stress-induced dendritic atrophy.

For migraine, a plausible link is tianeptine's modulation of glutamatergic neurotransmission and its mu-opioid receptor agonism. This remains speculative, since no trials or publications were supplied for migraine. The opioid activity also raises concerns about medication-overuse headache and dependence in a headache population.

The other migraine-related predictions (brainstem aura, susceptibility phenotype, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia) most likely score highly because of their closeness to "migraine disorder" in the knowledge graph rather than independent evidence. The dermatological predictions (atrophoderma vermiculata, ulerythema ophryogenesis) and the common cold have no plausible mechanistic link and likely reflect graph artifacts.

Dysthymic disorder is the most biologically coherent prediction, as it sits close to tianeptine's known use in depression. However, the evidence is indirect (see below).
