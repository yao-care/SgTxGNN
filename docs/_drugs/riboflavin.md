# Riboflavin: From Vitamin Supplementation to Biotin Metabolic Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Riboflavin (vitamin B2) is marketed in Singapore as a component of a multivitamin injection used for vitamin supplementation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **biotin metabolic disease**, but **8 clinical trials** and **20 publications** were retrieved and none tests riboflavin in this condition. The evidence is indirect, so this remains a model-driven hypothesis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, riboflavin is a B-vitamin that serves as a precursor of the FAD and FMN cofactors in mitochondrial energy metabolism. Its use as a vitamin supplement is established, and mechanistically it may be relevant to biotin-related metabolic conditions.

Biotin and riboflavin-derived cofactors both support mitochondrial energy pathways, and combined deficiency can cause lactic acidosis. A 2023 case report describes twin premature infants with riboflavin and biotin deficiency during prolonged parenteral nutrition. Reviews of vitamin-transporter disorders and treatable inborn errors of metabolism also place these vitamins in the same clinical space.

This link is nutritional co-deficiency rather than a repurposing mechanism. No retrieved evidence shows that riboflavin treats a primary biotin metabolic disease such as biotinidase or holocarboxylase synthetase deficiency. The high model score likely reflects the two vitamins' close association in the knowledge graph.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (94.88%), but no study tests riboflavin in biotin metabolic disease. The retrieved evidence shows only a nutritional co-deficiency association, and the single Singapore registration is a multivitamin injection.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert (warnings and contraindications), which is currently blocking safety screening
- Mechanism of action data for riboflavin, to test whether a link to biotin pathways exists beyond co-deficiency
- Direct clinical evidence of riboflavin benefit in a defined biotin-related condition, such as biotinidase or holocarboxylase synthetase deficiency
- The approved indication text for SIN11975P, to confirm the original indication

The other predicted indications are weaker. Most are model-only (L5), and corneal pigmentation (L4) rests on riboflavin's existing use in UVA corneal cross-linking for keratoconus, which is a different disease.
