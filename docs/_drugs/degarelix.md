# Degarelix: From Prostate Cancer (Hormone Therapy) to Hypertrichosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Degarelix is a GnRH receptor antagonist, marketed in Singapore as FIRMAGON injection. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hypertrichosis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction, so the prediction rests on knowledge-graph proximity alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Degarelix blocks the GnRH receptor, which lowers LH and FSH and, in turn, gonadal androgens. Detailed mechanism data from DrugBank are not currently available, so this description relies on the drug's known class action.

A link to hypertrichosis would only make sense if the excess hair growth were driven by androgens. Hypertrichosis is generally not androgen-dependent, so the mechanistic link is weak. The very high TxGNN score therefore reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, not clinical or biological support.

The other top predictions (Ambras-type congenital hypertrichosis, hair shaft abnormalities, familial trichomegaly, and others) are also genetic or structural conditions with no plausible GnRH mechanism. The one exception is **central precocious puberty**. It is the most biologically plausible prediction, because the disease is driven by early GnRH pulsatility. However, GnRH agonists are the standard of care, no degarelix studies were found, and any paediatric use would need dedicated safety data.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Two points from the prediction review are worth noting:
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including urticaria, are reported as adverse events of degarelix.
- Injection-site reactions are common with degarelix, and long-term sex-hormone suppression would need evaluation in any new population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication has no clinical trials or publications, the mechanistic link is weak, and the evidence level is L5. The high score alone does not justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore HSA package insert (warnings and contraindications), to allow safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Evidence of an androgen-driven hypertrichosis subtype, if this indication is to be pursued at all
- A separate review of central precocious puberty as a research question, including paediatric safety data
