## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled records. Based on known information, tremelimumab is an antibody that binds CTLA-4 (CD152), a T-cell checkpoint. Blocking it enhances T-cell activation, which is the basis for its use in cancer.

A plausible mechanistic link to diabetic cataract is **not supported** by the data compiled for this report. Diabetic cataract is driven by hyperglycaemia-related lens damage (polyol pathway, oxidative stress, glycation), and tremelimumab does not target these pathways. The high TxGNN score appears to reflect similarity within the knowledge graph (many cataract subtypes receive near-identical scores), not independent evidence.

There are also reasons for caution. Immune checkpoint blockade is associated with immune-related ocular adverse events (such as uveitis) and immune-related endocrinopathies, including diabetes. This argues for caution rather than benefit.

Other top-ranked predictions (all L5, all Hold, no trials or literature) include immature, mature, tetanic, craniostenosis, type 2 diabetes-associated, cortical, nuclear senile and senile cataract, and diabetic retinopathy. None has a supported mechanistic rationale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only (L5), with no clinical trials or publications and no plausible mechanistic link between CTLA-4 blockade and lens opacification. The known risk of immune-related ocular and endocrine adverse events makes this a poor repurposing candidate for a non-malignant eye condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking CTLA-4 signalling to lens pathology
- A risk-benefit analysis addressing immune-related ocular toxicity, and consideration of whether a systemic biologic is appropriate for a condition that is treated surgically
