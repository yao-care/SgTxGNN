# Thyrotropin alfa: From Thyroid Cancer Follow-Up to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Thyrotropin alfa (recombinant human TSH) is used diagnostically to support thyroid cancer surveillance and as an adjunct to radioiodine therapy for non-toxic goiter. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Migraine Disorder**, but this prediction is currently supported by **no clinical trials** and **no published literature**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (flagged as a High-severity data gap in this evidence pack). Based on the information available, Thyrotropin alfa is a recombinant form of human thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) that acts on the thyroid TSH receptor to stimulate thyroid hormone release; it is used clinically for diagnostic and pre-treatment purposes rather than as a chronic therapeutic agent.

The evidence pack's own mechanistic assessment concludes there is **no known pathophysiological link** between TSH receptor activation and migraine — migraine is primarily driven by the trigeminovascular system and the CGRP pathway, neither of which has an established connection to thyroid hormone signaling. No animal, in vitro, or clinical data connecting Thyrotropin alfa to migraine were found.

Given the absence of a plausible mechanism and the complete lack of supporting clinical or literature evidence, this prediction should be treated as a pure model output (L5) rather than a biologically grounded hypothesis at this time.
