## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available. Based on the pharmacology data compiled for this report, dydrogesterone is a synthetic progestogen that targets the progesterone receptor (PGR), and it is used for menstrual-cycle disorders and for supporting pregnancy in women at risk of miscarriage.

**Seborrheic keratosis (rank 1).** No plausible mechanistic link between progesterone receptor agonism and seborrheic keratosis is established in the available data. The high score (0.92) is a computational output and not evidence of benefit.

**Other top-ranked predictions.** Ranks 2 to 8 are largely haematological conditions: myelodysplastic syndrome and related entities (including refractory cytopenia of childhood, unclassified MDS, and the 5q deletion), aregenerative anaemia, and a congenital sideroblastic anaemia. They form one cluster of related predictions, so they are not independent evidence. Rank 3 is vulvar inverted follicular keratosis. None has any supporting trial or publication, and a hormonal effect on blood-cell production is speculative here.

**Amenorrhea (rank 9).** This is the only prediction with a biologically plausible link. Progestogen or estroprogestagen therapy is used to induce withdrawal bleeding and support endometrial development in amenorrhea, premature ovarian insufficiency, and Turner syndrome. It is also close to dydrogesterone's existing use in menstrual disorders, so it may be an extension of the current indication rather than a true repurposing.

**Cholecystolithiasis (rank 10).** Progestogens are generally associated with increased gallstone risk, so a therapeutic benefit is not supported and a safety concern is possible.
