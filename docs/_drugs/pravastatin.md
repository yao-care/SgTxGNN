# Pravastatin: From Hyperlipidaemia and Atherosclerosis to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pravastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used for hyperlipidaemia, atherosclerosis and stroke-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trial testing pravastatin** in this condition and only **general reviews and guideline literature**, so the supporting evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, pravastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, reduces cholesterol synthesis in the liver and upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, which lowers LDL cholesterol.

In homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, LDL receptor function is absent or severely reduced. Statin efficacy therefore depends on any residual receptor activity and is expected to be modest compared with PCSK9 inhibitors or other add-on agents. One of the reviews notes that statins are "not very effective" in this form of the disease. Pravastatin is already used in children with the heterozygous form, which is related but a distinct population. The prediction is biologically plausible but is unlikely to represent a strong benefit.

A further observation: other predictions for this drug are better supported. **Familial hypercholesterolaemia** and **autosomal dominant hypercholesterolaemia** (both essentially the heterozygous form) reached evidence level L2 with a "Proceed with Guardrails" recommendation. They are close to an established, guideline-supported use rather than true repurposing. **HIV-associated dyslipidaemia** (evidence level L3) also has a considerable body of trials, although it concerns managing lipids and cardiovascular risk, not treating HIV itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (not label text, so they should be verified against the package insert): liver disease, kidney disease, myopathy and other muscle diseases, rhabdomyolysis, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia and thrombocytopenic purpura, peptic ulcer disease, gout, anaemia, hypercalcaemia, hyperparathyroidism, haemophilia, sarcoidosis, substance abuse/dependence, and others.
- **Drug interactions** (100 recorded; moderate-level ones listed): metronidazole, clarithromycin, eluxadoline, naltrexone, pectin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, tinidazole. Combining statins (e.g. with rosuvastatin or simvastatin) is generally not advised. In HIV patients, interactions with protease inhibitors (e.g. darunavir/ritonavir) can raise pravastatin exposure.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there is no pravastatin-specific trial in homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia. Because LDL receptor function is severely impaired, benefit is expected to be limited, and other agents (PCSK9 inhibitors and similar) are better supported. By contrast, pravastatin in heterozygous FH (L2) is already a standard use and could be prioritised instead.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Pravastatin-specific clinical data (even small studies) in homozygous FH, including response by residual LDL receptor activity
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, with official approved indications for the three Singapore registrations
- Comparison against, or add-on assessment with, PCSK9 inhibitors and other agents used in homozygous FH
