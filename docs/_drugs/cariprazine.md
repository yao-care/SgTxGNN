# Cariprazine: From Psychiatric Use to Retinal Dystrophy

## One-Sentence Summary

Cariprazine is an oral psychiatric drug that acts on dopamine and serotonin receptors and is marketed in Singapore as SYMVENU capsules.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **retinal dystrophy with or without extraocular anomalies**, but this prediction has **0 clinical trials** and **no relevant publications** behind it.
It is a model-only signal with no pharmacological support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Based on general pharmacology, cariprazine is a D3-preferring D2/D3 receptor and 5-HT1A partial agonist used in psychiatry.

Retinal dystrophies are genetic disorders of photoreceptors or the retinal pigment epithelium. Nothing in the available data shows that dopaminergic or serotonergic modulation affects these conditions. The high score of 98.70% comes from a knowledge-graph prediction and is not backed by any drug-specific evidence. The mechanistic link is therefore judged **not credible** at this stage.

The other nine predictions in the set are also L5. They cover structural brain malformations, inherited metabolic and neuropathic conditions, myopia variants and a chromosomal deletion syndrome. None has trials or literature. The X-linked and syndromic myopia entries have only a very weak rationale, based on the general hypothesis that retinal dopamine signaling is linked to myopia.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only (L5). There are no trials, no relevant literature and no plausible mechanism linking cariprazine's receptor pharmacology to retinal dystrophy. Because it is a genetic disease, systemic antipsychotic exposure is also unlikely to offer a benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, including warnings, contraindications and the approved indication. This is a blocking gap for any safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Preclinical evidence, such as retinal-cell or animal-model data, showing that D2/D3 or 5-HT1A modulation affects retinal degeneration.
- A manual relevance review of the retrieved literature, or a targeted search on cariprazine and retinal disease.
