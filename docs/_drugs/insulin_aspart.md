## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, insulin aspart is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue. It is absorbed faster than regular human insulin after subcutaneous injection, and its efficacy in diabetes mellitus is well established.

Type 1 diabetes is characterised by absolute insulin deficiency, so replacing insulin is mechanistically direct. Insulin aspart is widely used as the mealtime (bolus) insulin in basal-bolus regimens, in insulin pumps (CSII), and in closed-loop systems. The link between the original indication and the predicted indication is therefore very close. The high TxGNN score is consistent with the extensive clinical evidence, and this should be viewed as confirmation of an existing use, not a new therapeutic discovery.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. Interaction and contraindication data were not available for this report.

Other predicted indications also raise a safety point. The model's prediction of drug-induced localised lipodystrophy is more likely an adverse effect of insulin injections (lipohypertrophy/lipoatrophy) than a therapeutic use. It should be treated as a safety signal, such as injection-site rotation, not as a repurposing opportunity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and several systematic reviews support insulin aspart in type 1 diabetes, and it is already marketed in Singapore under 8 registrations. Evidence is strong (L1), but this is an established use rather than a new one, and the safety information has gaps.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording on each Singapore registration
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A review of the other predicted indications. Several are computational predictions only (for example autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, stiff person syndrome, centrifugal lipodystrophy) and should be held. Permanent neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis are mechanistically plausible, but the evidence is indirect or limited to a single review.
