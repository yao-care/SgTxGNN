# Diphenhydramine: From Allergic Rhinitis and Insomnia to Rosacea Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Diphenhydramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine, originally used for allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, allergic conjunctivitis (atopic, vernal and papillary) and insomnia.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **rosacea conjunctivitis**, but **no clinical trials or publications** support it, so it rests on model prediction alone.
The better-supported candidates in the list are rhinitis (an on-label or near-label use, backed by **5 trials** and **18 publications**) and allergic urticaria.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, diphenhydramine blocks the histamine H1 receptor and has anticholinergic and sedative effects. Its efficacy in allergic conjunctivitis and allergic rhinitis is established.

The model's high score for rosacea conjunctivitis probably reflects its similarity to the approved allergic eye indications and to H1 antagonism. However, rosacea-associated eye disease is driven mainly by meibomian gland dysfunction and inflammation rather than histamine, so the mechanistic rationale is **weak**. No trials or literature were found.

For the other predictions:
- **Rhinitis:** H1 blockade reduces sneezing, rhinorrhoea and itching. This is effectively an existing use rather than true repurposing.
- **Allergic urticaria and cold urticaria:** Histamine release from mast cells drives wheals and itch, so H1 blockade is plausible. Newer-generation antihistamines are preferred first-line for safety reasons.
- **Cauda equina syndrome, viral conjunctivitis, glossodynia:** No plausible mechanistic link, or only speculative; these look like graph artefacts.
- **Neuralgia, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia, nasopharyngitis:** Indirect or historical evidence only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions are recorded. Two **Major** ones are shown in the sample reviewed: potassium citrate and eliglustat. Many **Moderate** ones involve anticholinergics (hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine) and CNS depressants or opioids (morphine, nabilone, dronabinol). Caution is needed with CNS depressants and anticholinergics.
- **Class Effects (from the literature):** Sedation, cognitive impairment, dry mouth, dizziness and orthostatic hypotension, especially in children and older adults. Rapid IV administration or large doses may cause toxicity.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, rosacea conjunctivitis)

**Rationale:**
The model score is high (99.20%), but there are no trials or publications, and the mechanism (meibomian gland dysfunction and inflammation) is not histamine-driven. The evidence level is L5.

**Other candidates:**
- **Rhinitis:** Level L1, Proceed with Guardrails. It is effectively an existing indication, so it is not a true repurposing opportunity. Guardrails are sedation, anticholinergic effects and cognitive impairment, plus DDI caution.
- **Allergic urticaria:** Research question (L4). The only Phase 3 trial tests IV cetirizine, not diphenhydramine.
- **Cold urticaria, nasopharyngitis, neuralgia, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia:** Hold. Evidence is indirect, historical or case-level.
- **Cauda equina syndrome, viral conjunctivitis, glossodynia:** Hold. No plausible link or no evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For rosacea conjunctivitis: any clinical or mechanistic evidence of benefit, plus a review of whether the prediction is an artefact of the allergic conjunctivitis indications
- For urticaria: head-to-head data against second-generation antihistamines
- Route and formulation compatibility assessment for the target indication
