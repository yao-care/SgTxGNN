## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on known information, norgestrel is a synthetic progestin (the racemate of levonorgestrel). Its established use is hormonal contraception, and mechanistically it may be applicable to amenorrhea.

Progestins are used clinically to induce withdrawal bleeding and to manage hypothalamic or hormonal amenorrhea. Combined norgestrel/ethinyl estradiol can also provide estrogen-progestin replacement. This gives the TxGNN prediction a plausible biological basis.

However, the link is indirect. The retrieved literature is largely general contraception reviews, and no trial tests norgestrel for treating amenorrhea. The prediction should therefore be treated as a research question rather than as confirmed efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 records. The 20 shown in the data compiled for this report are all rated Moderate, and mostly involve glucose-lowering drugs:
- **Antidiabetic agents**: acarbose, alogliptin, metformin, canagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, nateglinide, albiglutide, liraglutide, and several insulin products
- **Other drugs**: aprepitant and clarithromycin

Hormonal contraceptives can alter glucose tolerance, so blood glucose monitoring is advisable when norgestrel is combined with antidiabetic therapy.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for these.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (98.81%), and progestins have a plausible role in some forms of amenorrhea. However, there are no clinical trials, and the literature consists mainly of general contraception reviews and indirect studies. The evidence remains at the L4 level, and package insert safety information is missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication) from the HSA website
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A focused literature search on progestin treatment of primary and secondary amenorrhea, including guidelines and comparative studies
- Clarification of which amenorrhea types (for example hypothalamic or post-pill) are the target population
- A safety review for this population, especially estrogen status, thromboembolic risk and pregnancy exclusion
