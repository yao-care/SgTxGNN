## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Ampicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic, a class that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis, so it can act against susceptible bacteria.

Bacterial infections of the larynx and the tissue just above it, such as epiglottitis caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* and deep neck abscess, are plausibly susceptible to this kind of antibiotic. This is the likely basis of the link.

There is an important caveat. Most laryngitis is viral, and an antibiotic would not be expected to help. The retrieved papers mostly concern epiglottitis, laryngeal abscess and neck infections, not laryngitis itself. The very high TxGNN score therefore should not be read as clinical support.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but no cited study tests ampicillin in laryngitis itself, so the evidence level is L5. Laryngitis is usually viral, and the literature covers bacterial epiglottitis and neck infections, where ampicillin resistance in *H. influenzae* is a documented concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data on whether ampicillin helps in bacterial laryngitis, separate from epiglottitis and deep neck infection
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- Mechanism-of-action data
- Approved-indication text for the local licences
- Local resistance data for the likely pathogens
