# Danazol: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea (Menstrual Suppression)

## One-Sentence Summary

Danazol is a synthetic steroid with androgenic and antigonadotropic effects. The published literature describes its use in endometriosis, benign fibrocystic breast disease and hereditary angioedema. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **amenorrhea**, with **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 retrieved publications** (mostly reviews and indirect studies). The prediction is better understood as **menstrual suppression** than as treatment of pathological amenorrhea.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Structured mechanism-of-action data is not currently available for this drug. Based on general pharmacology, danazol suppresses the pituitary-ovarian axis. It lowers FSH and LH, inhibits ovarian steroidogenesis and reduces estrogen, and it causes endometrial atrophy. A 1990 review (PMID 2404115) also describes direct effects on steroidogenic enzymes and immunoregulatory actions.

Amenorrhea is therefore a pharmacologic effect of danazol. It is not a treatment for women whose amenorrhea is itself a disease, and the graph label may have the direction of the relationship reversed. Amenorrhea induced by danazol is the mechanism behind its use in endometriosis, because lesions regress when ovarian activity is down-regulated (PMID 16280355). It is also the basis for recent off-label use in menstrual suppression for transgender and nonbinary individuals (PMID 39051650).

A reasonable reading of the prediction is "danazol can suppress menstruation", not "danazol treats amenorrhea". The evidence below should be judged against that narrower claim.

## Safety Considerations

The registry record has no package-insert warnings or contraindications, and no drug-interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The retrieved literature does raise the following signals:

- **Androgenic effects**: Vellus hair pigmentation and voice change (reversible per PMID 39051650), and body weight gain in danazol-treated women (PMID 2720755).
- **Drug interaction**: A case report describes rhabdomyolysis and pancreatitis when danazol 600 mg/day was combined with lovastatin 40 mg/day (PMID 18691993).
- **Pregnancy**: Danazol is androgenic and generally avoided in pregnancy, which matters for any menstrual-suppression use in women who could conceive.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Danazol's ability to induce amenorrhea is pharmacologically plausible and supported by controlled endometriosis studies, but there are no registered trials. The label "amenorrhea" also does not match how the drug would actually be used. In addition, the Singapore package-insert safety data is missing and blocks any safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain the HSA package insert and extract the approved indication, warnings and contraindications.
- Reframe the candidate as "menstrual suppression" (endometriosis, abnormal uterine bleeding, gender-affirming care) and rerun the evidence search on that basis.
- Check the primary papers for design (RCT vs open-label), especially PMIDs 2523321 and 39051650.
- Obtain DrugBank mechanism-of-action data.
- Benign mammary dysplasia (L3) and breast adenosis (L3) are the other candidates with danazol-specific clinical papers. They overlap heavily, so consider reviewing them together, and note that the lovastatin interaction signal appears in the breast adenosis literature.
