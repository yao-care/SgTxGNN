# Apixaban: From Thromboembolic Disease (Anticoagulation) to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Apixaban is an oral direct factor Xa inhibitor anticoagulant, marketed in Singapore as Eliquis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but the support is thin: **1 clinical trial** (not migraine-specific) and **4 publications**, all case reports or small studies. One of the case reports describes migraine worsening on apixaban.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the source record, so this is based on its known drug class. The plausible link is a possible thromboembolic or hypercoagulable contribution to migraine with aura, for example through a patent foramen ovale (PFO) or antiphospholipid antibodies. This is a hypothesis only.

Some case reports describe migraine with aura improving on warfarin or other anticoagulants. The one case involving apixaban points the other way. Symptoms returned when a patient was switched from warfarin to apixaban and resolved again on resuming warfarin. Another report describes migraine with aura worsening after apixaban was started. The high TxGNN score is a model output, not clinical proof, and the current human evidence does not support benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no migraine-specific trial exists, and the only apixaban-specific reports suggest worsening or no benefit. The evidence is limited to model prediction, case reports and a mechanistic hypothesis.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to complete the safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data for apixaban
- Controlled or prospective data on factor Xa inhibitors in migraine, ideally in patients with PFO or antiphospholipid antibodies
- Confirmation of which agents were used in the antiphospholipid antibody study (PMID 33402037)

**Other candidates in this pack:** Rheumatoid arthritis and pulmonary hypertension are flagged as research questions rather than holds. Rheumatoid arthritis has one preclinical study in rats (PMID 32141012) describing anti-arthritic effects via FXa-mediated JAK2/STAT3 and MAPK signalling. Pulmonary hypertension is plausible only for thromboembolic subtypes such as CTEPH, where anticoagulation is already standard care. Neither has human efficacy data for apixaban.
