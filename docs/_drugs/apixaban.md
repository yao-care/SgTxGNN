## Apixaban: Migraine disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Apixaban is a direct oral anticoagulant that blocks factor Xa. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **migraine disorder**, but the evidence level is **L5**: no study supports apixaban for this condition. The published case reports describe migraine with aura that worsened, or did not resolve, on apixaban.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor, which reduces thrombin generation and clot formation.

The link to migraine is indirect and speculative. Migraine with aura has been associated with patent foramen ovale and paradoxical microemboli, and with prothrombotic states such as antiphospholipid antibodies. Anticoagulation could therefore plausibly help in selected thrombotic or PFO-related subsets.

However, the available case reports point the other way: migraine with aura worsened after starting apixaban, and in another case resolved on warfarin but not on apixaban. The very high TxGNN score is not supported by clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L5. No study supports apixaban for migraine, and the available case reports describe worsening or non-resolution of migraine with aura on apixaban. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough to justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA records
- Mechanism of action data
- Controlled clinical data in a defined subgroup (for example, PFO-related or antiphospholipid-antibody-positive migraine) before any further consideration
