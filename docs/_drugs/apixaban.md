# Apixaban: From Thromboembolic Conditions to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Apixaban is an oral direct factor Xa inhibitor (anticoagulant) marketed in Singapore as Eliquis. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but the supporting evidence is weak: **1 clinical trial** (not actually about migraine) and **3 publications** (two case reports and one small retrospective study), with the case reports suggesting no benefit or even worsening on apixaban.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor that blocks thrombin generation and clot formation.

The proposed link to migraine is indirect and speculative. Migraine with aura has been associated with patent foramen ovale (PFO), paradoxical microemboli and prothrombotic states such as antiphospholipid antibodies. Anticoagulation could therefore plausibly help in a selected thrombotic or PFO-related subset of patients.

However, the available case reports point the other way. One describes migraine with aura worsening after starting apixaban. Another describes a patient whose migraine with aura remained in remission for 12 years on warfarin, returned within 3 weeks of switching to apixaban, and resolved again after warfarin was resumed. The very high TxGNN score is therefore not supported by clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a very high model score only. The sole registered trial studies stroke prevention rather than migraine, and the case reports suggest no benefit or possible worsening on apixaban. Bleeding risk is also a concern for a non-life-threatening condition such as migraine.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, which are currently missing
- Mechanism of action data
- A clearly defined migraine subgroup (for example PFO-related or antiphospholipid-antibody-positive) with a plausible rationale
- Controlled clinical data in that subgroup, including a bleeding risk-benefit assessment
