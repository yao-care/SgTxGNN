## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for zinc chloride is not available. Based on known pharmacology, zinc chloride serves as a source of the trace element zinc, which functions as a cofactor for Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase (SOD) and contributes to antioxidant defense and immune regulation. This general mechanistic profile underlies most of the repurposing hypotheses generated for this compound.

For severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy specifically, the prediction rationale relies on the same mechanism proposed for diabetic retinopathy in general — oxidative stress is a well-documented driver of retinal damage in diabetes, and zinc's antioxidant/SOD cofactor role is theoretically protective. However, this is a more advanced disease subtype, and no clinical trial or publication has directly studied zinc chloride in this specific severity stage. The supporting evidence instead comes from the broader "diabetic retinopathy" candidate (a related but separate entry in this evidence pack), where one Phase 2 RCT (NCT02443155, n=308) and one animal study (PMID 15541754) provide indirect, extrapolated support.

Because the link to this specific indication is inferred rather than directly tested, the mechanistic connection is plausible but indirect, and should be treated as a research hypothesis rather than an actionable clinical signal at this stage.
