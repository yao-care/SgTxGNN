# Gimeracil: From Gastric Cancer to Colonic Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Gimeracil is a component of the S-1 combination (tegafur/gimeracil/oteracil), originally used to treat gastric cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **colonic neoplasm**, with **6 clinical trials** and **12 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that this evidence applies to the S-1 combination, not gimeracil alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology knowledge, gimeracil is part of S-1 (tegafur + gimeracil + oteracil). It inhibits the enzyme dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD), which slows the breakdown of 5-FU and raises and prolongs systemic 5-FU exposure from tegafur. Gimeracil has no standalone antitumour activity.

Gastric cancer and colorectal cancer are both gastrointestinal tumours, and colorectal cancer is sensitive to 5-FU. The mechanism therefore links directly to the original gastric cancer indication. This supports the reasonableness of the TxGNN prediction, but any benefit would come from the S-1 combination as a whole.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No drug interactions were found in the queried database, and no knowledge-graph contraindications were recorded.

Adverse events reported in the literature for S-1-containing regimens include hand-foot syndrome, erythroderma with mucosal involvement, hypertriglyceridaemia and haematological toxicity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 trial (NCT00660894, n=1535) tests S-1 directly in stage III colon cancer, and a further Phase 3 trial and several Phase 2 trials are registered. The strongest evidence level (L1) is nominal: only one of the Phase 3 trials is completed, and the other has an unknown status with no results. The evidence also concerns the S-1 combination, not gimeracil alone, so any decision must be made at the combination level.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (currently missing, and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Published results of the completed Phase 3 trial (NCT00660894) and the SOX trial (NCT03448549)
- The approved indication text for the Singapore licences, to confirm the original indication
- A safety monitoring plan covering haematological toxicity, hand-foot syndrome and DPD-related risks
