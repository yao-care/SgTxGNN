# Burosumab: From X-linked Hypophosphatemia to Renal Osteodystrophy

## One-Sentence Summary

Burosumab is an anti-FGF23 antibody used for X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **renal osteodystrophy**. So far this rests on **0 clinical trials** and **1 review article**, with no direct data in chronic kidney disease (CKD).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Burosumab is a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23). This restores renal phosphate reabsorption and raises active vitamin D levels. Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is not available for this report, so this description comes from the pack's mechanistic rationale.

FGF23 is markedly elevated in CKD-mineral bone disorder (CKD-MBD), which includes renal osteodystrophy. Blocking it is therefore biologically conceivable.

The mechanism is only a partial fit, however. In CKD, high FGF23 is largely a compensatory response to phosphate retention. Blocking it risks hyperphosphatemia and ectopic calcification. The one linked paper is a pediatric review of XLH and other metabolic bone disorders, and it contains no direct CKD-MBD data. The prediction is best seen as a hypothesis, not a supported new use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: No interaction records were found.
- **Renal function** (from the pack's mechanistic analysis, not a verified HSA insert): The pack states that burosumab labeling contraindicates use in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease. Blocking FGF23 in CKD risks hyperphosphatemia and ectopic calcification.

Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (96.93%), but there are no registered trials and only one indirect review. The mechanism is also questionable in CKD, because FGF23 elevation there is compensatory and blocking it may cause harm.

Among the other predicted indications, "bone remodeling disease" (rank 4) has the strongest support, including a completed randomized phase 3 trial (NCT02526160). That evidence is for XLH-related osteomalacia, which is an existing burosumab indication. It should not be generalized to renal osteodystrophy or other bone disorders.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently a blocking gap
- DrugBank mechanism-of-action data
- Direct evidence in CKD-MBD or renal osteodystrophy, such as safety and efficacy studies in patients with reduced GFR
- A defined safety plan covering serum phosphate, PTH and renal function monitoring, and avoidance in severe renal impairment
