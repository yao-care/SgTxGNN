# Pyridoxine: From Pyridoxine Deficiency Anemia to Gonococcal Urethritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) was originally used to treat pyridoxine deficiency anemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gonococcal urethritis**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Pyridoxine is a vitamin B6 supplement, and its efficacy in correcting pyridoxine deficiency is well established.

However, no mechanistic link to gonococcal urethritis has been established. Pyridoxine has no known antibacterial activity against *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, and standard treatment for this infection is antibiotic-based. Another predicted indication, Ureaplasma urethritis, has the identical score (93.87%), which suggests the prediction comes from a shared neighbourhood in the knowledge graph rather than a real pharmacological relationship. This prediction is most likely a model artifact.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: The knowledge graph links pyridoxine to a number of conditions, including liver disease and liver failure, kidney disease and impaired renal function, peptic ulcer disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, systemic lupus erythematosus, peripheral neuropathy, and Wilson disease. These should be verified against the official package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 13 interactions were identified.
  - **Moderate**: altretamine, phenobarbital, levodopa, phenytoin.
  - **Minor**: mainly barbiturates (amobarbital, butabarbital, butalbital, methohexital, pentobarbital, secobarbital, thiopental, methylphenobarbital) and primidone.

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score but no clinical trials or literature, and pyridoxine has no known activity against *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*. Standard antibiotic therapy remains the appropriate treatment, so this prediction is unlikely to be actionable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any plausible mechanistic or in vitro evidence of pyridoxine activity against the pathogen, plus detailed MOA data
- Clinical or observational data in urethritis patients
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA) to support safety screening
- Consideration of better-supported predictions for pyridoxine instead, such as vitamin deficiency disorder. That prediction has a completed Phase 2/3 trial (NCT01128244) and sits close to the approved indication. It would need dose monitoring, since chronic high-dose pyridoxine can cause sensory neuropathy, and attention to the levodopa interaction.
