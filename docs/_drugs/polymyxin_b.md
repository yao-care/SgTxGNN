## Polymyxin B: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is a polypeptide antibiotic active against Gram-negative bacteria. In Singapore it is registered only in single-ingredient licences for ear, eye, skin and vaginal products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**, but this prediction rests on **no registered clinical trials** and a small, dated body of literature, much of it experimental or about safety concerns.
The more credible related directions are **respiratory tract infection** (5 clinical trials, one completed Phase 2) and **conjunctivitis** (several randomized comparisons of polymyxin B/trimethoprim), although conjunctivitis is already a marketed use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general knowledge, polymyxin B binds lipopolysaccharide on the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria and disrupts it, which is bactericidal. It is often a last-line option for multidrug-resistant organisms such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Acinetobacter baumannii* and carbapenem-resistant *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.

This makes bacterial infections of the airways a plausible target. One cohort study reported inhaled polymyxin B for salvage treatment of pneumonia and initial treatment of tracheobronchitis caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacilli, and a comparison study looked at ventilator-associated pneumonia and tracheobronchitis.

There is an important caution. Much of the bronchitis-related literature is not about treatment. It describes polymyxin B as a **bronchial provocation agent** that triggers bronchoconstriction and basophil degranulation in asthma and chronic bronchitis, and as a tool for creating eosinophilic bronchitis in animal models. One 1971 report warns about the danger of inhalation. So the high model score reflects a biological association, but the real-world evidence does not clearly support use for bronchitis in general.

## Safety Considerations

- **Inhalation risk**: The literature repeatedly reports bronchoconstriction and histamine release after inhaled polymyxin B, particularly in atopic asthma and chronic obstructive bronchitis. This is directly relevant to a bronchitis indication.
- **Nephrotoxicity**: A multicentre cohort study identifies renal failure as the main adverse effect of polymyxins when given systemically.
- **Hypersensitivity**: A case of anaphylaxis to polymyxin B-trimethoprim eye drops has been reported.
- **Drug Interactions**: 73 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include:
  - Amikacin
  - Cidofovir
  - Cisatracurium
  - Deferasirox
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Iodinated contrast agents (iothalamic acid, diatrizoate)
  - Intravenous immunoglobulin and related immune globulins
  - Live cholera vaccine

  Moderate-level interactions include capreomycin, estrogens (estradiol, ethinylestradiol, mestranol), mesalazine, balsalazide and ibandronate.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for bronchitis)

**Rationale:**
The bronchitis prediction has a very high model score but no registered trials. The literature is mostly dated, experimental, or about polymyxin B as an airway irritant, and it carries a real inhalation safety signal. Respiratory tract infection (aerosolized or combination therapy in resistant Gram-negative pneumonia) and conjunctivitis are better supported, rated "Research Question". The conjunctivitis use, however, is already marketed as a fixed combination with trimethoprim, so it is not true repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the registered products
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A defined target population, such as bacterial tracheobronchitis from multidrug-resistant Gram-negative organisms rather than bronchitis in general
- Phase 2/3 efficacy and safety data for aerosolized polymyxin B, including bronchospasm monitoring and a plan for patients with asthma or COPD
- An assessment of route compatibility, since no inhaled or injectable product is currently registered in Singapore
