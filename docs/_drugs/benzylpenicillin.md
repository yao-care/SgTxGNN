# Benzylpenicillin: From Systemic Antibacterial Use to Pericoronitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Benzylpenicillin (penicillin G) is a penicillin-class injectable antibacterial. The Singapore licence record does not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pericoronitis** (inflammation around a partially erupted wisdom tooth).
Currently there are **0 clinical trials** and **20 publications** on this indication. None of the publications is a benzylpenicillin-specific controlled trial, so the direction is supported only indirectly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, benzylpenicillin belongs to the penicillin class of beta-lactam antibacterials. It is active against many streptococci and anaerobes, and its approved indication in Singapore is not stated in the licence data.

Pericoronitis is a polymicrobial odontogenic infection, dominated by streptococci and anaerobes such as peptostreptococci, bacteroides and fusobacteria. Penicillin-class agents therefore have a plausible biological fit. Literature from 1991 describes penicillins such as amoxicillin as effective in acute pericoronitis.

There are two limits on this rationale:
- A microbiology study (2003) found beta-lactamase-producing bacteria in third-molar pericoronitis. These organisms can inactivate benzylpenicillin.
- Penicillin-class agents are already standard for odontogenic infections. The prediction therefore reflects existing practice more than a true repurposing signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The biological fit is plausible, but there are no clinical trials and no benzylpenicillin-specific controlled study for pericoronitis. Beta-lactamase-producing bacteria limit its usefulness. Penicillin-class agents are already standard for odontogenic infections, so this is not a true repurposing opportunity. The safety data needed for screening are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert (approved indication, warnings, contraindications)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Benzylpenicillin-specific clinical evidence in pericoronitis, including comparison with oral penicillin-class agents
- A review of route suitability, since the only Singapore product is an injectable powder

Among the other predicted indications, recurrent aphthous ulcer (canker sore) has the most direct penicillin G literature, with three controlled studies of topical penicillin G. It may be worth a separate review.
