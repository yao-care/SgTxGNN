# Benzylpenicillin: From Bacterial Infections to Pericoronitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Benzylpenicillin (penicillin G) is an injectable antibiotic originally used for streptococcal, pneumococcal, meningococcal and staphylococcal infections, as well as endocarditis and anthrax.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pericoronitis** (score 99.4%), but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction. It is a model prediction only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, benzylpenicillin is a penicillin-class beta-lactam antibiotic. Its efficacy against streptococcal and other susceptible gram-positive infections is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to pericoronitis.

Pericoronitis is a mixed infection (streptococci and anaerobes) of the tissue around a partially erupted tooth, usually a wisdom tooth. Many of these organisms are susceptible to penicillins, so the prediction is biologically plausible. The high score appears to reflect this similarity to bacterial infections already on the drug's profile.

There are practical concerns. Benzylpenicillin is given by injection only and has poor oral bioavailability, which makes it an awkward fit for a mainly outpatient dental condition. The data compiled for this report contain no trial or literature evidence, so the 99.4% score should be read as a model output, not as proof of efficacy.

Other top-ranked predictions are weaker still. Most are not primarily bacterial conditions: gingival recession, canker sore, ulcerative stomatitis, chemotherapy-induced oral mucositis, denture stomatitis (Candida-driven) and gingival leukoplakia. Cat-scratch disease (an intracellular organism not typically covered by beta-lactams) and chronic ethmoidal sinusitis are also poorly matched. Conjunctivitis is plausible for susceptible bacteria but has only indirect evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The pericoronitis prediction is biologically plausible because penicillin-susceptible streptococci and anaerobes are involved. However, it rests on a model score alone (L5), with no trials or literature. The injection-only product is also poorly suited to this condition. The other top predictions are mechanistically weak.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical and literature evidence for benzylpenicillin (or other penicillins) in pericoronitis
- Comparison with established oral antibiotic options for dental infections
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA record, and the approved indication text
- Mechanism of action data
- Assessment of route suitability (injection-only product) and of antibiotic stewardship and allergy risk
