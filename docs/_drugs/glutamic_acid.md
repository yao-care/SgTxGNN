## Glutamic Acid: From Parenteral Nutrition Component to Postmenopausal Osteoporosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glutamic acid is an amino acid that is a component of amino acid infusion products marketed in Singapore, and the data compiled for this report do not state a specific approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **postmenopausal osteoporosis**, but there are **no clinical trials** and the **10 publications** retrieved are only indirectly related (mostly about vitamin K or vitamin D, or about different compounds).
The high model score is a prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, glutamic acid is a naturally occurring amino acid delivered in multi-amino-acid infusion solutions, and these products are used for nutritional support. Its role in osteoporosis is not established.

The plausible link is indirect. Vitamin K enables gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues in bone proteins such as osteocalcin, and vitamin K2 is used in osteoporosis treatment in Japan. However, this shows that glutamate residues within proteins matter in bone biology. It does not show that supplying free glutamic acid benefits bone.

The direct support is limited to one animal study (PMID 26144993), in which glutamic acid reduced estrogen-deficiency-induced menopausal-like symptoms in ovariectomized mice. A small human study examined poly-gamma-glutamic acid, a different polymer, on calcium absorption. The high TxGNN score therefore remains a hypothesis to be tested.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** kidney disease (including chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure), nephrolithiasis, hypercalcemia and related calcium or phosphorus disorders, hyperparathyroidism, sarcoidosis, heart and cardiovascular disease, digitalis poisoning, ventricular fibrillation, phenylketonuria and other disorders of phenylalanine metabolism. These need clinical verification and are not confirmed label contraindications.
- **Drug Interactions:** Five moderate-level interactions were identified, all with amphetamine-type drugs: amphetamine, benzphetamine, dextroamphetamine, lisdexamfetamine and metamfetamine.

For the package insert warnings and contraindications, please refer to the product labelling.

Theoretical concerns also apply to the predicted indications. Glutamate excitotoxicity is implicated in retinal ganglion cell damage, which argues against use in glaucoma. The pregnancy setting adds safety concerns.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials, and the literature is indirect: it concerns vitamin K, vitamin D and different glutamate compounds, with only one mouse study on glutamic acid itself. The evidence is at the mechanism or preclinical level (L4), and the knowledge-graph flags for renal, calcium and cardiac disease add safety questions for a bone indication.

The other predicted indications (pregnancy-associated osteoporosis, glaucoma, gastroparesis and others) are supported by prediction only (L5) and are also on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore HSA, to complete safety screening
- Mechanism of action data
- Preclinical confirmation of a bone benefit from glutamic acid itself, in bone density and bone turnover models
- Assessment of whether an injectable nutrition product is a suitable route for chronic osteoporosis treatment
- A clinical safety review of the renal, calcium-metabolism and cardiac flags before any human study
