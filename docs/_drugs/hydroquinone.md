# Hydroquinone: From Skin Depigmentation (Melasma) to Seborrheic Keratosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydroquinone is a topical skin-lightening agent. In the evidence provided, it is used for pigmentary disorders such as melasma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Seborrheic Keratosis**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and only **2 loosely related publications** (one observational study, one review) supporting this direction. The prediction is essentially model-based.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank is currently not available. Hydroquinone is generally understood to inhibit tyrosinase and reduce melanin synthesis, which is why it lightens pigmented skin lesions.

Seborrheic keratosis, and especially its variant dermatosis papulosa nigra (DPN), often causes visible dark lesions. Its histology is described as not significantly different from that of seborrheic keratosis. A depigmenting agent could therefore plausibly lighten these lesions.

There are important limits. Hydroquinone would not be expected to remove the keratotic (thickened skin) component. No hydroquinone-specific efficacy data for seborrheic keratosis were found. The high score of 0.997 is a model prediction only.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One literature report on facial pigmentation discusses exogenous ochronosis, a known adverse effect of hydroquinone-containing skin-lightening creams. This is relevant to any prolonged or repeated use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no registered clinical trials and no hydroquinone-specific efficacy data for seborrheic keratosis. The available literature is general and not specific to hydroquinone. Hydroquinone would also not address the keratotic component of the lesions. Package insert safety data are still missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Hydroquinone-specific efficacy data for seborrheic keratosis or DPN, such as a pilot or controlled study
- A comparison against established treatments for these lesions
