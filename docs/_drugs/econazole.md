# Econazole: From Topical Antifungal to Majocchi Granuloma

## One-Sentence Summary

Econazole is an imidazole antifungal, marketed in Singapore as a topical cream.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Majocchi granuloma**, a deep follicular dermatophyte infection.
This prediction has **no clinical trials and no publications** behind it, so it rests on model output alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism data are not available in the Evidence Pack. Econazole is an azole antifungal, and azoles act by inhibiting fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, which blocks ergosterol synthesis. This mechanism is plausible against dermatophytes, the fungi that cause Majocchi granuloma.

Majocchi granuloma is a deep infection of the hair follicle and dermis, not just the skin surface. Topical econazole may not penetrate far enough into the follicle and dermis to reach effective concentrations. The model score reflects proximity to other dermatophyte diseases in the knowledge graph. It does not reflect any observed clinical benefit.

Registered indications for the Singapore products are not recorded in the data, so the relationship to the original use cannot be assessed formally.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only (L5), with no trials or publications. Topical delivery to a deep follicular infection is a further biological doubt. The Singapore package insert data are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Registered indications for the two Singapore products
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA) from DrugBank
- Any case series or clinical reports of topical azole use in Majocchi granuloma, with evidence that topical delivery reaches the follicle and dermis
- Route compatibility assessment, since Majocchi granuloma is often treated systemically

**Other predictions:** Other predictions for econazole have stronger support. Vulvovaginal candidiasis and vulvovaginitis are graded L2, and superficial mycosis is graded L4. These are probably rediscoveries of existing antifungal uses rather than true repurposing. Only cream forms are registered in Singapore, so vaginal candidiasis would also need a route and formulation check.
