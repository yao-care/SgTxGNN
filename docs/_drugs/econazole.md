# Econazole: From Superficial Fungal Skin Infections (Tinea) to Majocchi Granuloma

## One-Sentence Summary

Econazole is a topical imidazole antifungal, originally used for tinea (pedis, corporis, groin and perianal), cutaneous candidiasis and pityriasis versicolor.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Majocchi granuloma** (score 99.97%), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 case report** for this top-ranked indication.
The better-supported predicted indications are **vulvovaginal candidiasis** and **vulvovaginitis (candidal)**, which have **comparative clinical studies** dating from 1976 to 1994.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, econazole is an azole antifungal. Azoles inhibit fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, which depletes ergosterol and damages the fungal cell membrane. This activity is established against dermatophytes, *Candida* and *Malassezia*.

Majocchi granuloma is a deep dermatophyte infection of hair follicles and dermis, typically caused by *Trichophyton rubrum*. It is closely related to the approved tinea indications, so the mechanistic link is plausible. However, deep follicular involvement generally requires systemic therapy, so topical econazole alone is unlikely to be adequate.

Other predictions follow the same pattern:
- **Ectothrix and endothrix infections, dermatophytosis of the scalp or beard, and tinea profunda** involve the same organisms. Hair-shaft and follicle penetration by topical agents is limited, and these usually need systemic therapy.
- **Superficial mycosis** is essentially an on-label match for econazole's existing indications rather than true repurposing.
- **Vulvovaginal candidiasis and candidal vulvovaginitis** are the most credible new uses. Econazole's anti-*Candida* activity is well established, and vaginal econazole is used in many markets.
- **Acne** likely reflects misclassified *Malassezia* (Pityrosporum) folliculitis rather than true acne vulgaris.
- **Postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis** is driven by estrogen deficiency, not infection. The prediction is probably a network-proximity artefact and has no mechanistic support.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 41 potential interactions were recorded, but none has a severity level assigned. Examples include clotrimazole, fluconazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, metformin, glipizide, rosiglitazone, clopidogrel, heparin, dexamethasone, fluticasone, diazepam, lorazepam, levothyroxine and cytarabine. These are mainly relevant when absorption is increased, for example with vaginal use or large treated areas.

Warnings and contraindications from the Singapore package insert were not available. Please refer to the package insert for these.

For the vaginal-use predictions, the following should be considered:
- Local resistance monitoring is needed, given reports of azole resistance and non-albicans *Candida*.
- Use should be restricted to confirmed candidal infection.
- Irritation risk should be assessed in atrophic tissue.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold for Majocchi granuloma and the other dermatophyte/hair-follicle predictions; Proceed with Guardrails for vulvovaginal candidiasis and candidal vulvovaginitis.**

**Rationale:**
- Majocchi granuloma, ectothrix and endothrix infection, scalp/beard dermatophytosis and tinea profunda have high TxGNN scores but little or no direct evidence. Their evidence is a single case report or prediction only, and deep or follicular infection generally requires systemic therapy.
- Vulvovaginal candidiasis is supported by several comparative clinical studies, but these are old and have no trial registration, so the evidence is capped at L3.
- Acne and postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis are not supported mechanistically and should not be pursued.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications. This is currently a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Confirmation that a vaginal econazole formulation is available or can be registered in Singapore. Current licences are for creams only, and route compatibility has not yet been assessed.
- Local *Candida* susceptibility and resistance data for Singapore.
- For Majocchi granuloma: clinical data on topical econazole as an adjunct to systemic antifungals.
- Review of the interaction data to assign severity levels.
