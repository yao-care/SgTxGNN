# Niraparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Epiglottis Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Niraparib is a PARP inhibitor. The literature in the Evidence Pack describes it as approved for platinum-sensitive recurrent ovarian cancer, although the Singapore (HSA) licence records contain no indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **epiglottis neoplasm** with a very high graph score, but **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** currently support this specific prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Niraparib is a PARP inhibitor. PARP inhibitors work by synthetic lethality: tumours that cannot repair DNA damage through homologous recombination become vulnerable when PARP is blocked. This is the basis of its use in ovarian cancer.

For epiglottic tumours, no homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) data were provided, so the mechanistic link is theoretical only. The high graph score may partly reflect a general association between the drug and neoplasm-type diseases rather than a specific biological signal. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis to test, not as supported evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for epiglottis neoplasm rests on the model score alone, with no trials, no literature and no HRD rationale for this site. Two other candidates in the pack (cystic neoplasm, pre-malignant neoplasm) have indirect evidence, but it concerns ovarian and endometrial serous cancers and established cancers rather than the predicted term. It should not be counted as repurposing evidence until it is re-mapped to specific tumour types.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- HRD or DNA-repair biomarker data for epiglottic and head-and-neck tumours
- Preclinical or early clinical evidence of niraparib activity in this tumour type
- Confirmed original indication text from HSA, so the repurposing baseline can be defined
