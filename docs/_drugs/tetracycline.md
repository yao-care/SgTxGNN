## One-Sentence Summary

Tetracycline is a broad-spectrum antibiotic originally used for infections such as urinary tract infection, bronchitis, acne, gonorrhoea, pharyngitis, conjunctivitis and pneumonia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tetracycline is a member of the tetracycline antibiotic class. Its efficacy against many bacterial infections, including chlamydial conjunctivitis and trachoma, is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis.

The original indications already include conjunctivitis, so an ocular surface link is plausible. Tetracyclines are also known for anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibiting effects, which could matter for corneal epithelial disease.

However, the only supporting paper is a 1992 report of two patients who developed punctate epithelial keratitis after chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis that had been treated with oral tetracycline or doxycycline. It is an indirect observation and does not show that tetracycline treats the condition. No trials are registered.
