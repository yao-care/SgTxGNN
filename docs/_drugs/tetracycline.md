# Tetracycline: From Antibacterial Therapy to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tetracycline is a broad-spectrum antibiotic marketed in Singapore as oral capsules and a topical ointment. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**. Support is very thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a 1992 case series that does not test tetracycline as a treatment for this condition).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the input. Tetracycline is a tetracycline-class antibiotic. It is generally understood to inhibit bacterial protein synthesis at the 30S ribosomal subunit, which gives it activity against *Chlamydia trachomatis*.

*C. trachomatis* is a recognised cause of follicular conjunctivitis, which can be followed by superficial punctate keratitis. That is the plausible link between the drug and this predicted condition.

The link is weak. The only retrieved paper describes keratitis that appeared or persisted after the infection had been treated and had resolved. It therefore does not show that tetracycline treats the keratitis itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the only supporting paper is a case series that does not test tetracycline as a treatment for this condition. There are no registered trials, and no mechanism-of-action or safety data were supplied.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block any safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Controlled evidence of tetracycline treating punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, or a clearly defined route of use (oral versus ocular) compatible with the registered forms
- The approved indication text for the three Singapore registrations

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, **chronic rhinosinusitis** (rank 3, score 99.15%) has the strongest evidence (L2). It has a completed Phase 3 trial (NCT05157412), several systematic reviews and a randomised controlled trial. That evidence is almost entirely for **doxycycline**, not tetracycline, so it is class-level support only. It would be the better candidate for a dedicated evaluation, with tetracycline-specific tolerability and dosing assessed separately.
