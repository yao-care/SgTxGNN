## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (data gap, high severity). Based on known information, norethisterone is a synthetic progestin, its efficacy in menstrual cycle regulation and contraception has been proven, and mechanistically it may be applicable to amenorrhea: progestogens act on endometrial progesterone receptors and are classically used in the "progestogen challenge test" to induce withdrawal bleeding and to manage secondary amenorrhea and dysfunctional uterine bleeding.

The relationship between the drug's established use and the predicted indication is physiologically direct — both involve modulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis and endometrial response to progestogens. However, the supporting evidence base has an important limitation: the majority of the 8 identified clinical trials (e.g., LIBERTY 1, LIBERTY 2, LIBERTY EXTENSION) studied **relugolix** (a GnRH antagonist) for heavy menstrual bleeding associated with uterine fibroids, with norethisterone acetate present only as a low-dose "add-back" component alongside estradiol — not as the primary investigational drug for amenorrhea itself.

The literature evidence is similarly indirect: most publications are general reviews of hormonal/oral contraceptives from the 1970s–1990s, rather than studies specifically evaluating norethisterone for the treatment of amenorrhea. The mechanistic plausibility is reasonable, but direct causal evidence for norethisterone monotherapy in amenorrhea remains limited.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- Safety data (warnings, contraindications) is a **Blocking** data gap, which by itself prevents any initial safety assessment (S1).
- The evidence, while reaching L3 (observational/no direct RCT), is largely indirect: norethisterone appears mainly as an add-back component in relugolix regimens for a different condition (HMB in fibroids), and most literature consists of decades-old general contraception reviews rather than studies targeting amenorrhea treatment directly.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA-equivalent package insert data: warnings, contraindications, and drug interactions (Blocking gap, DG001)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank (High-priority gap, DG002)
- Clinical evidence directly evaluating norethisterone (monotherapy or as primary agent) for amenorrhea, rather than as an add-back component in fibroid/HMB trials
- Confirmation of Singapore market/licensing pathway, since the drug is not currently registered
