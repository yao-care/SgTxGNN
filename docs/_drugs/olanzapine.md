# Olanzapine: From Schizophrenia and Bipolar Disorder to Benign Paroxysmal Torticollis of Infancy

## One-Sentence Summary

Olanzapine is an atypical antipsychotic originally used for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and depressive and anxiety disorders.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support it. The better-supported candidates are mood and anxiety disorders: **neurotic depression** and **melancholia** (systematic reviews and meta-analyses), and **agoraphobia** (open-label and case-level data).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Olanzapine is known as an atypical antipsychotic that blocks dopamine and serotonin (5-HT2A/5-HT2C) receptors. Its efficacy in psychotic and mood disorders is established.

**Top-ranked prediction (benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy).** This is a self-limiting childhood disorder related to the migraine spectrum and often linked to CACNA1A. Any link to olanzapine's receptor pharmacology is speculative. Giving an antipsychotic to infants for a condition that resolves on its own would also raise substantial safety concerns. The high score is a computational prediction only.

**Mood and anxiety predictions.** Neurotic depression, melancholia and dysthymic disorder overlap with major depression, where olanzapine (especially with fluoxetine) is used as augmentation in treatment-resistant cases. The evidence concerns major depression in general, not these specific labels, and much of the value is already covered by existing indications. For agoraphobia, olanzapine's serotonergic and dopaminergic antagonism may plausibly reduce panic and phobic anxiety, but the data come from panic disorder studies and are indirect.

**Genetic and developmental predictions.** For distal 17p13.3 microdeletion syndrome, Ohdo syndrome, and childhood apraxia of speech, no credible mechanistic rationale was found. For retinal dystrophy, the only retrieved item is a case report on managing mania in a patient with Usher syndrome. It does not show any effect on the retinal disease.

## Safety Considerations

Label warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: Conditions flagged include orthostatic hypotension, liver failure and liver disease, epilepsy, diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, hyperlipidemia and hypertriglyceridemia, obesity, benign prostatic hyperplasia, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, and myocardial infarction. These signal metabolic, haematological, cardiovascular and hepatic cautions.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level interactions include **bupropion**, **morphine** and **potassium citrate**. Moderate-level interactions include antidiabetic drugs (metformin, acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), clarithromycin, famotidine, epinephrine, loperamide and lorcaserin.
- **Population concerns**: Metabolic and sedative adverse effects are a particular concern in anxiety and depression populations. Use in infants or children for self-limiting or developmental conditions would need strong justification.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy)

**Rationale:**
The top prediction has only a computational score, with no trials or literature and a poor risk-benefit profile for infants. Neurotic depression and melancholia (L2) and agoraphobia (L3) are better candidates and are classified as Research Questions. Their evidence overlaps with indications olanzapine already covers (major depression) or comes from panic disorder rather than agoraphobia.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA) from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
- For agoraphobia: controlled trials in agoraphobia or panic disorder, with a metabolic and sedation monitoring plan
- For depression-related predictions: confirmation that the use falls outside existing major depressive disorder indications, and registered trial evidence
- For the genetic and developmental predictions (rank 1, 4, 8, 9, 10): no further work unless a credible mechanistic rationale emerges
