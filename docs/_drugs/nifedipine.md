# Nifedipine: From Hypertension to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Nifedipine is a calcium channel blocker, originally used to treat hypertension and Prinzmetal angina.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but the evidence is thin: **no clinical trials** and **2 publications** are linked to this exact subtype. The broader migraine indication has **1 registered trial** (not efficacy-focused) and about **20 publications**, and the available evidence is mixed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, nifedipine is a dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker. Its efficacy in hypertension and angina is established, and it acts by relaxing vascular smooth muscle.

Calcium channel blockade is plausibly relevant to migraine, through cortical spreading depression and cerebrovascular tone. Migraine prophylaxis with calcium channel blockers has been studied for decades. However, nifedipine has limited CNS selectivity, and the literature gathered for this report is not specific to the brainstem aura subtype. Any support is extrapolated from migraine in general.

The evidence for nifedipine in migraine is not encouraging:
- A double-blind study found nifedipine increased headache intensity when used to treat acute attacks of migraine with aura.
- A crossover placebo-controlled study in classic migraine prophylaxis (24 patients) found no difference in headache frequency, with significantly more side effects on nifedipine (54% vs 8%).
- A small crossover study in India reported a satisfactory response in 71.4% of migraine patients (28 patients), so results are mixed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / risk conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: porphyria (including inherited porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disease), kidney disease, liver disease (including cirrhosis), gallbladder disease, hypotensive disorder, acute coronary syndrome, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, hemoglobinopathy, pyloric stenosis, heart failure, intestinal obstruction, pyoureter.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Notable ones include:
  - **Major**: cisapride
  - **Moderate**: clarithromycin, cimetidine, aprepitant, bupropion, acetylsalicylic acid, canagliflozin, corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide), and calcium salts (acetate, carbonate, chloride, citrate, gluconate, lactate and others)
  - **Minor**: ranitidine
- **Migraine-specific tolerability**: nifedipine causes vasodilatory headache, and the literature shows a higher rate of side effects than placebo in migraine prophylaxis.
- Grapefruit juice may increase oral bioavailability through CYP3A4 inhibition.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings. The label warnings and contraindications have not yet been retrieved.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, but no trials target migraine with brainstem aura, and the available migraine studies are old, small and inconsistent. Two studies showed no benefit or worsening headache, and nifedipine is not a standard migraine agent. Brainstem aura evidence is extrapolated from general migraine.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieval of the HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the 2026 AAN/AHS migraine prevention guideline for its conclusions on nifedipine
- Evidence specific to the brainstem aura subtype, or a well-designed prospective trial
- Comparison against established migraine preventives (beta-blockers, flunarizine, and others)

**Other predictions noted:** The "common cold" prediction appears to be driven by the term "cold". The supporting literature actually concerns Raynaud's phenomenon, for which calcium channel blockers have Cochrane-level support. Raynaud's phenomenon could be assessed separately as a distinct indication.
