# Daptomycin: From Gram-Positive Bacterial Infections to Osteoarthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Daptomycin is a cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic registered in Singapore as an injectable product, and it is known to be used against Gram-positive bacterial infections. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **osteoarthritis**, but the data compiled for this report show **0 clinical trials** and **10 publications**, and none of those publications supports treating non-infectious osteoarthritis. The high score most likely reflects the drug's use in bone and joint infections, not a real therapeutic link.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Daptomycin is a cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic with activity against Gram-positive bacteria. Published work cited here describes its use in osteoarticular and prosthetic joint infections caused by organisms such as *Staphylococcus*.

For osteoarthritis, **no mechanistic link has been established**. The publications retrieved concern infection of bones and joints, which is a different disease from degenerative osteoarthritis. The high TxGNN score probably reflects closeness in the knowledge graph between the drug's bone/joint infection use and the joint-disease term, not evidence of benefit in osteoarthritis.

Of the other predictions, **rheumatoid arthritis** has the only plausible mechanistic signal. A 2025 animal study (PMID 39571268) reported that daptomycin reduced collagen-induced arthritis by suppressing inflammatory cytokines and the NF-κB pathway. This is preclinical only and is a research question, not a basis for clinical use. The predictions ranked 5 to 10 are rare genetic skeletal or developmental syndromes with no data and no plausible mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text)**: myopathy and other muscle diseases, familial periodic paralysis, diarrhoeal disease including *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotising enterocolitis, eosinophilic pneumonia and pulmonary eosinophilia, and peripheral neuropathy. These are signals from a knowledge graph and need to be checked against the label.
- **Muscle toxicity**: a published case (PMID 36693494) describes daptomycin-induced rhabdomyolysis followed by acute gouty arthritis. This points to potential harm, not benefit, in gout.
- **Drug interactions (12 found)**:
  - Moderate: rosuvastatin, simvastatin, ibuprofen, diclofenac, celecoxib, ethinylestradiol, cyclosporine, mycophenolic acid, picosulfuric acid, iobenguane (I-131)
  - Minor: warfarin, dicoumarol

  The statin interactions are worth attention given the muscle toxicity signal. NSAID co-use is also relevant, since osteoarthritis patients commonly take NSAIDs.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The osteoarthritis prediction has no clinical trials, and all retrieved literature concerns bone and joint infections, not degenerative osteoarthritis. The high TxGNN score appears to be a knowledge-graph artefact. Daptomycin also carries a known risk of muscle toxicity, and long-term use for a chronic, non-infectious joint disease would be hard to justify. Rheumatoid arthritis is the only prediction with a preclinical mechanistic signal, and it should be treated as a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are required for any safety screening
- Approved-indication text for the Singapore licences
- For rheumatoid arthritis: replication of the animal findings, dose and exposure assessment, and a human-relevant safety evaluation, particularly of muscle toxicity
- Confirmation that route compatibility (injectable only) is acceptable for any chronic joint disease use
