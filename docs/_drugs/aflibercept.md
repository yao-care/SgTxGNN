## Aflibercept: VEGF trap (anti-angiogenic fusion protein) → esotropia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Aflibercept is a VEGF-A/VEGF-B/PlGF trap given by injection, used mainly for retinal vascular diseases.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **esotropia**, but there are currently **no clinical trials** and **no publications** supporting this direction, so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aflibercept binds VEGF-A, VEGF-B and placental growth factor (PlGF), blocking signals that drive abnormal blood vessel growth and leakage. According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for aflibercept: "Vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) and placental growth factor (PlGF) are members of the VEGF family of angiogenic factors that can act as mitogenic, chemotactic, and vascular permeability factors for endothelial cells. VEGF acts via two receptor tyrosine kinases, VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2, present on the surface of endothelial cells. […] Aflibercept products act as a soluble decoy receptor that binds VEGF - A and PlGF, and thereby can inhibit the binding and activation of these cognate VEGF receptors." ([DailyMed: Pavblu (aflibercept-ayyh) prescribing information, Amgen, Inc](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=c61062f3-80ec-4a20-8c28-46e468592a06), label version 2026-10-29; accessed 2026-11-19.) This class of action is well established in retinal vascular disease.

Esotropia is an inward deviation of the eye caused mainly by neuromuscular or refractive factors, not by abnormal blood vessel growth. No plausible mechanistic link between VEGF blockade and esotropia has been identified. The high score (0.9938) is a model output only; it is not backed by any trial or publication, and it should be treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of benefit.
