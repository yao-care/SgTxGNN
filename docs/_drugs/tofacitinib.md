## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on the supplied literature, tofacitinib is a JAK inhibitor (JAK1/JAK3, described as pan-JAK in one paper). Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis comes from blocking JAK/STAT cytokine signalling.

For this predicted indication, no mechanistic link is documented. The disease is an ultra-rare developmental syndrome, and no JAK/STAT pathway involvement is evident. Inhibiting JAK signalling is not expected to correct a structural developmental defect. The high score (0.990) reflects knowledge-graph proximity only. Treat it as a hypothesis-generating signal, not as evidence of benefit.

**Better-supported predictions for this drug:** Two lower-ranked predictions have some biological and literature support, though both remain at the preclinical level (L4, "Research Question"):
- **Plasma cell myeloma (score 96.1%):** A preclinical repurposing study (PMID 29622655) found that tofacitinib reversed the growth-promoting effects of the bone marrow microenvironment on myeloma cells. JAK/STAT signalling supports plasma cell survival.
- **Myeloid leukemia (score 95.4%):** Cell-line studies suggest JAK inhibition has activity in AML and CML models. The only human report is a case of CML arising in a patient on tofacitinib, which is a safety signal, not evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No drug interaction records were found in the Evidence Pack.

Literature supplied for the myeloid and myeloma predictions raises malignancy-related concerns. These include a case report of CML in a tofacitinib-treated patient and pharmacovigilance and cohort studies on myeloma and other cancer risk with immunosuppressants and DMARDs. Tofacitinib is immunosuppressive, so this signal should be weighed carefully for any new indication.
