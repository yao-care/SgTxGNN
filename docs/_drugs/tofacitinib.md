## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, tofacitinib is a JAK inhibitor (mainly JAK1/JAK3) that blocks cytokine signalling through the JAK/STAT pathway. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis comes from dampening inflammatory cytokine signalling.

**Plasma cell myeloma:** The link is mechanistically plausible. IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signalling supports myeloma cell growth and survival, especially within the bone marrow microenvironment. A 2018 preclinical study (PMID 29622655) reported that tofacitinib reversed the growth-promoting effects of bone marrow stromal cells on myeloma cells. This is laboratory evidence, not proof of clinical benefit.

**Indolent plasma cell myeloma** (rank 3) is plausible by analogy, but no specific studies were found.

**Top-ranked prediction and rare syndromes:** For colobomatous microphthalmia-rhizomelic dysplasia syndrome and brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome (ranks 1 and 2), no mechanistic link is supported by the data. These are congenital developmental disorders, and the high scores are graph-based predictions only. A high TxGNN score does not by itself indicate clinical plausibility.

**Myeloid leukaemia** (rank 5) has JAK/STAT biology behind it, but the evidence is preclinical, indirect, or anecdotal, and the direction of effect is mixed (see below).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs in total. Those listed include:
  - **Major:** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone including ophthalmic, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisone, prednisolone) and clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate:** aprepitant, morphine (including liposomal), opium, acetylsalicylic acid, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole and miconazole.
  - **Minor:** zinc sulfate and zinc acetate.
- **Malignancy signals in the literature:** A case report describes CML arising during tofacitinib therapy (PMID 31263618), and a pharmacovigilance study links immunosuppressive medications with sebaceous carcinoma (PMID 38071595). These are signals and not established causal findings, but they deserve attention in any oncology repurposing.
- **Immunosuppression:** Because tofacitinib is immunosuppressive, it could worsen existing T-cell deficiency (relevant to the rank 9 prediction, vertebral anomalies and variable endocrine and T-cell dysfunction).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked predictions (ranks 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 10) rest on model scores alone, with no trials, literature, or mechanistic link. Plasma cell myeloma has plausible biology and one preclinical study, but no clinical trials, and the available epidemiological papers raise malignancy-risk questions rather than showing benefit. The evidence is not yet enough to support clinical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Further preclinical validation of tofacitinib in myeloma models, including in vivo work and combination studies
- Assessment of malignancy risk in patients with plasma cell disorders
- Clarification of the approved indication wording on the Singapore licence
- Route compatibility assessment (oral tablet only is currently available)
