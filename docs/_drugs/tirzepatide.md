# Tirzepatide: From Type 2 Diabetes and Weight Management to Gout

## One-Sentence Summary

Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, marketed in Singapore as Mounjaro and used for type 2 diabetes and weight management.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gout**.
Evidence is thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a post hoc analysis of a uric acid biomarker, not gout outcomes).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the input. Based on known information, tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist. Its efficacy in weight reduction and glycaemic control is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to gout through indirect metabolic effects.

The proposed link is indirect. Tirzepatide-induced weight loss and improved insulin sensitivity may lower serum uric acid (SUA), a key risk factor for gout. The only supporting study is a post hoc analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 randomised trial, which tests whether tirzepatide lowers SUA through weight reduction. SUA is a biomarker. The study does not show fewer gout flares or less incident gout.

The TxGNN score is high, but the evidence behind it is limited. This is best treated as a research question, not a treatment candidate.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The gout prediction rests on a single post hoc biomarker analysis, with no registered gout trials and no gout outcome data. Evidence is at the research-question stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Gout-specific outcome evidence (flares, incident gout), for example from a dedicated trial or a gout-focused analysis of existing tirzepatide trials
- Reported results from the SURMOUNT-1 analysis, including the size of the SUA change
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (needed for any safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- Consideration of other predicted indications: **osteoarthritis** currently has stronger support (evidence level L3, with an ongoing Phase 4 trial, NCT06191848, in obesity with knee osteoarthritis), though it has no reported results yet
