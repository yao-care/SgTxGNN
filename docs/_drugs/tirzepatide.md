# Tirzepatide: From Type 2 Diabetes and Weight Management to Gout

## One-Sentence Summary

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly GIP/GLP-1 receptor dual agonist given by subcutaneous injection, used for type 2 diabetes and weight management.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gout**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one narrative review and one post hoc analysis of an RCT) supporting this direction.
The better-supported lead among the top predictions is **osteoarthritis**, with **1 clinical trial** (recruiting) and **19 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology records show that tirzepatide is a peptide that acts on both the GIP receptor (GIPR) and the GLP-1 receptor (GLP1R). Its established clinical effects are improved glycaemic control and substantial weight loss (about 15–20% body weight reduction in the SURMOUNT-1 trial, depending on dose).

The link to gout is mostly indirect. Obesity is a major driver of hyperuricaemia, and hyperuricaemia is the pathogenic driver of gout. Weight loss and better insulin sensitivity may lower serum uric acid, partly by reducing renal urate reabsorption. A post hoc analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 randomised placebo-controlled trial examined tirzepatide-related changes in serum uric acid and their association with weight reduction. Weight-independent effects are possible but unproven. No prospective data on gout flares are available, so this remains a hypothesis about a biomarker rather than demonstrated clinical benefit.

For osteoarthritis, the rationale is similar. Weight loss reduces mechanical joint loading and systemic inflammation, both relevant to knee osteoarthritis pain and function. This applies to obesity-associated osteoarthritis; benefit in non-obese patients is not established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (gout). Proceed with Guardrails for osteoarthritis in patients with obesity.**

**Rationale:**
- For gout, the evidence is a prediction plus one post hoc analysis of serum uric acid and one narrative review. There are no trials of gout outcomes, so it stays a research question.
- Osteoarthritis has a direct, well-matched Phase 4 trial (NCT06191848) and a plausible weight-mediated mechanism, but the trial has no results yet. The supporting literature is mostly reviews and economic analyses.
- Rheumatoid arthritis evidence is indirect and not specific to tirzepatide. The remaining predictions (rare skeletal dysplasias, osteoarthritis susceptibility, benign prostatic hyperplasia, myosclerosis) have no trials or literature and should be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective data on gout flares, or a trial of tirzepatide in gout with hyperuricaemia and obesity
- Results from NCT06191848 for the osteoarthritis indication
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, needed before safety screening
- Approved indication text for the Singapore licences
