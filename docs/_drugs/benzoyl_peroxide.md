# Benzoyl Peroxide: From Acne and Dermatitis to Vulvar Inverted Follicular Keratosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Benzoyl peroxide is a topical agent originally used for acne and related inflammatory skin conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **vulvar inverted follicular keratosis**,
but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction, so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, benzoyl peroxide is a topical keratolytic and antimicrobial agent. It is established in acne and is linked in the knowledge graph to seborrheic keratosis, and mechanistically it may be applicable to related benign follicular lesions.

Inverted follicular keratosis is a benign lesion related to seborrheic keratosis, which is already among benzoyl peroxide's original indications. That relationship is the main reason the prediction looks plausible. However, the link is unverified. No studies have tested benzoyl peroxide at the vulvar site, where mucosal and skin irritation is a particular concern.

These should be verified against the official label.
- **Site-specific concern**: The predicted vulvar site involves mucosal and thin skin, where irritation risk is higher.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score, with no trials or publications. The mechanistic link through seborrheic keratosis is plausible but untested, and irritation risk at the vulvar site is a concern. Other top predictions are weaker still: several have no plausible mechanism, and for phototoxic dermatitis the only literature describes the drug class as a cause of dermatitis. Acne keloid is the only other prediction with even indirect support, from an acne review that does not test benzoyl peroxide in this condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clinical or case-series evidence for benzoyl peroxide in inverted follicular keratosis, including tolerability at the vulvar site
- Assessment of formulation and route suitability, since the registered products are acne combination gels (benzoyl peroxide with a second active ingredient) rather than single-agent products
- Specialist dermatology or gynaecology review to confirm the diagnosis and the need for drug therapy, since the lesion is benign and other management options exist
