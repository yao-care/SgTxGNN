# Gilteritinib: From FLT3-Mutated Acute Myeloid Leukemia to Bulbar Polio

## One-Sentence Summary

Gilteritinib is an oral FLT3/AXL kinase inhibitor marketed for relapsed or refractory FLT3-mutated acute myeloid leukemia (AML).
The TxGNN model ranks **bulbar polio** as its top prediction, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support it, and there is no plausible biological link. The high score looks like a knowledge-graph artifact.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Gilteritinib inhibits the FLT3 and AXL receptor tyrosine kinases. These kinases drive growth in certain leukemias, which is why the drug is used in FLT3-mutated AML.

Bulbar polio is a viral infection of motor neurons caused by poliovirus. No known biology connects FLT3 or AXL signalling to poliovirus infection or to motor neuron injury from it. The prediction is therefore not credible, and the high score (0.991, rank 9,763 in the model output) most likely reflects a quirk of the knowledge graph. It should not be treated as a repurposing lead.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The bulbar polio prediction rests on model output alone. It has no trials, no literature and no mechanistic rationale, so it is best treated as a knowledge-graph artifact.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any independent biological or clinical evidence linking FLT3/AXL inhibition to bulbar polio. Without it, the prediction should not be pursued.
- The HSA package insert (warnings and contraindications), which has not been retrieved.

**Other candidates in this Evidence Pack:**
Predictions with a real mechanistic link to the approved FLT3-mutated AML setting are better research starting points.
- **Myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative disease** (rank 10, L3, Research Question) is the strongest. The Phase 1/2 trial [NCT04140487](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04140487) tests gilteritinib with azacitidine and venetoclax (n=97, recruiting). It is early-phase and non-randomized, and MDS/MPN eligibility still needs confirming.
- **Therapy-related AML/MDS** (alkylating agent- or radiation-related, ranks 2 and 3, L4, Research Question) is biologically related to the approved setting. It has no gilteritinib-specific studies, and any benefit would depend on the patient's FLT3 mutation status.
