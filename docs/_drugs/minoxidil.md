## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled records. Based on the literature reviewed, minoxidil is a prodrug whose active metabolite, minoxidil sulfate, opens potassium channels. This relaxes vascular smooth muscle and lowers blood pressure. In hair follicles it is thought to prolong the anagen (growth) phase, shorten the telogen (resting) phase, and promote follicular vasodilation and proliferation.

Hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp is a rare, monogenic hair disorder (reported with CDSN variants) marked by miniaturized or poorly sustained follicles. A drug that supports follicle growth could plausibly help. This also fits minoxidil's recorded links to alopecia.

The three reports retrieved all combine minoxidil with other treatments (growth factors, botanical extracts, or platelet-rich plasma). Minoxidil's own contribution therefore cannot be separated out. Because these are case reports and none is a controlled study, the evidence is suggestive only.

**Evidence level note:** Case reports directly address this disease, so L4 is assigned. This is the weakest level a disease-specific study can support. No controlled trial exists.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the supporting evidence is limited to three case reports of combination therapy, with no registered trials. Minoxidil's own effect in this disease cannot be isolated. Singapore licence records contain no indication text, so the local labelled use is unconfirmed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies, or at least minoxidil-monotherapy case series, in hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp
- Mechanism of action data and package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA
- Confirmation of approved indications on Singapore labels
- A safety review for oral use, given the cardiovascular contraindication relations above (topical products carry lower systemic exposure)
