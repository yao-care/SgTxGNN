## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the pack. Irbesartan is an angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor blocker. Activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) drives malignant hypertension and its renal injury, so blocking AT1 is biologically plausible.

The pack cannot confirm whether this indication overlaps with irbesartan's approved hypertension label, because the original indications are not recorded. The high TxGNN score is a prediction based on graph proximity, not clinical evidence.

Other predictions in the pack are weaker:
- **Malignant renovascular hypertension** (rank 2) is also RAAS-dependent. ARBs carry a known risk of acute renal function decline in bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **Chronic pulmonary heart disease** (rank 9) is the only prediction with related evidence (L4). This is a preclinical rat study of chronic mountain sickness, plus heart failure and hypertension studies that do not test this indication.
- The literature attached to **brain small vessel disease 1** (rank 8) is about congenital ocular anomalies and does not mention irbesartan. It is a keyword match and provides no support.
- **Obsolete susceptibility to ischemic stroke** (rank 10) is an obsolete ontology entry and is not a valid target as it stands.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high and the RAAS link is plausible, but there are no trials or literature for this indication and evidence is at L5. Package insert safety data and the original indications are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank

- A safety review for renal impairment and renovascular disease, including renal artery stenosis
