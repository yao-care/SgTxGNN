# Oxycodone: From Opioid Analgesia to Restless Legs Syndrome

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for oxycodone is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, oxycodone is a mu-opioid receptor agonist. Opioid receptor activation modulates pain and sensory pathways and interacts with dopaminergic signalling, both of which are implicated in RLS.

RLS is a sensorimotor disorder with an urge to move the legs, worse at rest and in the evening. First-line treatment is usually an alpha-2-delta ligand (gabapentin, pregabalin) or a dopamine agonist. Long-term dopaminergic therapy can lead to augmentation (symptoms worsening with treatment). Opioids are an established option for severe or refractory cases. Prolonged-release oxycodone/naloxone is approved in Europe for second-line treatment of severe to very severe idiopathic RLS after dopaminergic therapy has failed. The Movement Disorder Society evidence review (2017 revision) considers oxycodone/naloxone efficacious.

**Other predictions are much weaker:**
- Methemoglobinemia (including the alpha type), nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, myositis fibrosa and idiopathic granulomatous myositis have no clinical or literature support and no plausible mechanism. These look like knowledge-graph artefacts. For nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, opioids may even worsen antidiuresis.
- Myofascial pain syndrome, headache disorder, tendinitis and common cold: oxycodone offers at most symptomatic analgesia. Long-term opioid use is generally discouraged for these conditions. The evidence is indirect or consists of postoperative pain studies. All are rated Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (derived from TxGNN drug–disease edges, not from label text): liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, respiratory failure, epilepsy, hypothyroidism/myxedema, inflammatory bowel disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hypotensive disorder, hypertensive disorder, substance abuse/dependence, myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, blood coagulation disorders, thrombocytopenia, anaemia and gallbladder disease, among others. These should be checked against the official package insert.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major interactions include alvimopan, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, dexamethasone, cimetidine, clarithromycin and clotrimazole. Moderate interactions include anticholinergics (hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, clidinium), loperamide, eluxadoline, dolasetron and dronabinol. Concomitant use with other opioids, CNS depressants and CYP3A4/CYP2D6 modulators needs particular care.
- **Use-specific risks** (from the evidence review): misuse, dependence, respiratory depression and sleep-disordered breathing. The last is especially relevant in RLS, where patients have disrupted sleep.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (restless legs syndrome only; Hold for all other predicted indications)

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial of prolonged-release oxycodone/naloxone, together with a Cochrane review, MDS and AASM guidance and European approval for second-line use, supports RLS as a credible new indication. The risks of dependence, misuse and respiratory depression call for strict limits on use. The other nine predictions lack evidence or a plausible mechanism and should not be pursued.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications. These are currently missing and are a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data (for example from DrugBank).
- Confirmation of the Phase 3 results and of whether a second adequate trial exists. Only one is listed, so L1 under the strict two-trial rule is not met.
- Clinical guardrails: restrict use to severe, intractable or augmented RLS after failure of alpha-2-delta ligands and dopamine agonists, with monitoring for misuse, dependence, respiratory depression and sleep-disordered breathing.
- Confirmation that the oxycodone/naloxone formulation (Targin, already registered in Singapore) is the appropriate product, and a regulatory assessment of an RLS indication.
- Route compatibility and similarity-to-original-indication analyses, which are still pending.
