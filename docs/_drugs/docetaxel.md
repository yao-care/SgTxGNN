## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on general pharmacology, docetaxel stabilises microtubules and blocks their depolymerisation. This causes mitotic arrest and apoptosis in rapidly dividing tumour cells, a mechanism that applies broadly to solid tumours, including breast cancer.

Docetaxel is already established in breast cancer practice. The retrieved trials test it in neoadjuvant, adjuvant and metastatic settings, in combination with anthracyclines, cyclophosphamide, carboplatin, trastuzumab and bevacizumab.

The high TxGNN score therefore mostly reflects a known use rather than a new discovery. The local label should be checked before this is treated as a repurposing candidate.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. Warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data are not available in the source record.

The retrieved literature and trials point to several toxicities that need attention when docetaxel is used in breast cancer:
- Lymphedema and fluid retention (PMID 27997437)
- Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (NCT04001829, NCT02125019)
- Cardiotoxicity when combined with anthracyclines or trastuzumab (NCT01641562, NCT00464646)
- Radiation recall dermatitis (PMID 12412364)

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 randomised trials involve docetaxel-containing regimens in breast cancer, which gives Level 1 evidence. Breast cancer is an established use rather than a novel repurposing target. The package insert safety data is missing and is a blocking gap for safety screening, so progress should be conditional.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (the blocking safety gap)
- Confirmation of the approved breast cancer indication on the Singapore labels, since approved indication text is currently empty
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A haematological, neuropathy and fluid-retention monitoring plan, plus cytotoxic handling procedures

Other predicted indications are weaker. Ewing sarcoma is a research question at L2, and the remaining candidates are on Hold at L4–L5.
