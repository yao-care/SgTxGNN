# Acebutolol: From Beta-Blocker Therapy to Malignant Renovascular Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Acebutolol is a cardioselective beta-1 blocker that is marketed in Singapore as oral tablets, though its approved indication is not listed in the local records.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant renovascular hypertension**.
There are **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a 1975 uncontrolled study in general hypertension), so the evidence is weak and indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Acebutolol is a cardioselective beta-1 blocker with intrinsic sympathomimetic activity. Beta-1 blockade lowers renin release, which is plausible in renin-driven renovascular hypertension. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not currently available in the source data, so this reasoning relies on the drug's known class properties.

The only supporting literature is a 1975 open clinical study in general arterial hypertension. This is indirect evidence and does not test malignant or renovascular disease specifically. Malignant hypertension is a hypertensive emergency, and oral beta-blockers are not a standard first-line treatment for it. The high model score therefore likely reflects the general antihypertensive class effect rather than a disease-specific signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score and a single old, uncontrolled study in general hypertension. There are no trials, and no evidence in malignant or renovascular disease. Among the other predicted indications in this pack, atrial flutter has the strongest signal (L3, peri-operative prophylaxis studies from 1985–1986), and it may be a better candidate for follow-up.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications (required for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- Disease-specific clinical evidence in renovascular or malignant hypertension
- A clinical judgement on whether an oral beta-blocker has a role in this hypertensive emergency setting
