# Pregabalin: From Neuropathic Pain and Epilepsy to Tendinitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Pregabalin is a gabapentinoid used for neuropathic pain and partial epilepsy, according to the literature in the Evidence Pack. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **tendinitis**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and **6 publications**, none of which tests pregabalin for tendinitis. The support is therefore indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. From general pharmacology, pregabalin binds the alpha2-delta subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels. This may dampen central sensitization and the neuropathic component of pain.

The link to tendinitis is weak. Any benefit would most plausibly be symptomatic, for pain with a neuropathic or centrally sensitized component. There is no evidence that pregabalin treats tendon inflammation or tendon pathology itself. The high model score is not backed by studies in this condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials and no publication testing pregabalin for tendinitis. The only support is a high model score, and the literature concerns postoperative pain or unrelated topics. A pain-symptom benefit is plausible but unproven.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct studies of pregabalin in tendinopathy or tendinitis pain, such as a pilot RCT
- HSA package insert data (warnings, contraindications) and the approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank

**Other predicted indications with more support:**
- **Migraine disorder** (score 99.47%, L2): two pediatric RCTs (pregabalin vs valproate; propranolol vs pregabalin), a network meta-analysis, and preclinical work on cortical spreading depression. The only Phase 3 migraine-prevention trial (NCT00447369) was withdrawn.
- **Osteoarthritis** (score 98.58%, L1 per pack): multiple pregabalin trials, but most are perioperative knee arthroplasty pain studies. Results of the Phase 3 trial NCT00442546 (n=307) must be retrieved before any upgrade.
