## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in this evidence pack (Data Gap DG002). Based on well-established pharmacology, pregabalin binds the α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels, reducing presynaptic release of glutamate, substance P, and norepinephrine. This mechanism underlies its approved use in neuropathic pain, adjunctive epilepsy treatment, fibromyalgia, and generalized anxiety disorder — conditions that share a common pathway of neuronal hyperexcitability and central sensitization.

Osteoarthritis (OA) was traditionally viewed as a purely peripheral, structural joint disease, but a well-documented subset of OA patients experience pain driven substantially by central sensitization rather than local nociception — the same pathophysiological process pregabalin already targets in its approved indications. This mechanistic overlap is why OA is a biologically plausible repurposing candidate, distinct from a coincidental knowledge-graph association.

Clinically, this is no longer purely theoretical: a completed, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled **Phase 3 trial (NCT00442546, n=307)** evaluated pregabalin for post-total-knee-arthroplasty pain, and a second completed Phase 3 trial (NCT00581685) evaluated pregabalin plus celecoxib for post-total-hip-arthroplasty pain and function. Multiple completed Phase 4 trials further compared pregabalin against duloxetine — another centrally-acting analgesic — specifically in knee and hand OA populations, reinforcing that this is an active, real-world area of off-label use rather than a purely computational signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 trials plus multiple completed Phase 4 comparator trials establish pregabalin's use for the centrally-sensitized pain subtype of osteoarthritis as an active, evidence-supported off-label practice, not merely an algorithmic association — but the drug is currently unregistered in Singapore and formal OA-specific safety labeling does not exist.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/HSA-equivalent safety labeling (warnings, contraindications) for pregabalin — currently a **Blocking** data gap (DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action documentation from DrugBank (DG002)
- A defined patient-selection protocol restricting use to OA patients with a central-sensitization phenotype (e.g., via validated screening tools such as the Central Sensitization Inventory), since evidence does not support use in purely structural/peripheral OA pain
- Regulatory pathway assessment given pregabalin's current non-marketed status in Singapore
- Re-evaluation of the "tendinitis" TxGNN top hit once/if tendinitis-specific trial or mechanistic data becomes available — at present it does not meet the bar for a supported candidate
