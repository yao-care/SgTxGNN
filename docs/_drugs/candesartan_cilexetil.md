## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Candesartan is an AT1 receptor blocker, and renovascular hypertension is driven by the renin-angiotensin system. Blocking AT1 is therefore mechanistically plausible.

There are important caveats:
- ARBs carry a risk of acute kidney injury in bilateral renal artery stenosis or a solitary kidney, which are typical settings for renovascular hypertension.
- Malignant hypertension is normally managed with urgent parenteral agents, not an oral tablet.
- The high score (0.997) has no trial or literature support in this dataset.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is plausible mechanistically, but there is no trial or literature evidence (L5). The renal risk in renovascular disease and the usual need for urgent parenteral treatment in malignant hypertension weigh against oral use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Clinical or observational evidence specific to renovascular or malignant hypertension
- A renal-safety and potassium-monitoring plan for patients with renal artery stenosis

**Related note:** Among the other predictions for this drug, **cerebrovascular disorder** has the strongest support. It is rated L1, with the Phase 3 trials CASE-J and the Scandinavian Candesartan Acute Stroke Trial, plus the SCOPE and ACCESS literature. Its benefit is confounded by blood-pressure lowering and results in acute stroke were mixed, so it is best treated as a research question rather than an established indication.
