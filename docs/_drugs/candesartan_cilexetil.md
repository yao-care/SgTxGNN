## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Candesartan is known to be an angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor blocker. Its efficacy in hypertension is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to renovascular hypertension.

Renovascular hypertension is driven largely by activation of the renin-angiotensin system. Blocking the AT1 receptor is therefore biologically plausible. The new indication is also a severe form of the drug's original indication, hypertension, so it is not a large conceptual leap.

There are two important caveats. First, this is a model prediction only, with no supporting trials or literature. Second, this class of drug carries a known risk in patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis, which is a common cause of renovascular hypertension. A mechanistically sensible link does not guarantee a favourable benefit-risk balance here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the drug-disease knowledge graph, not from package insert text)**: The knowledge graph links candesartan to the following conditions.
  - **Renal:** renal artery obstruction, impaired renal function, anuria.
  - **Blood pressure and electrolytes:** hypotensive disorder, potassium deficiency disease, hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease.
  - **Liver and biliary:** liver disease, hepatic coma and encephalopathy, gallbladder disease, neonatal jaundice.
  - **Metabolic and other:** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, hyperuricemia, hyperparathyroidism, osteitis fibrosa, pancreatitis.
  - **Autoimmune:** systemic lupus erythematosus, including the paediatric form.

  Some of these edges probably reflect combination products such as those containing a thiazide diuretic. They should be verified against the actual label. **Renal artery obstruction** is directly relevant to the predicted indication.

For other safety information, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no trials or publications for malignant renovascular hypertension. The evidence level is L5. The renal artery obstruction link in the safety data adds a specific concern for this indication.

**Other predicted indication with more evidence:**
Among the other predicted indications, **cerebrovascular disorder** (score 84.9%) has the most supporting evidence, at L1. It is backed by a completed Phase 3 trial of candesartan in acute stroke (SCAST, NCT00120003, 2,500 patients) and several clinical publications, including the ACCESS and CASE-J trials. However, the published SCAST results did not show a benefit of early candesartan in acute stroke and suggested possible harm. The relevance is therefore mainly to long-term secondary prevention. This indication is currently rated "Research Question" and should be reviewed separately.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A targeted literature search on ARBs in renovascular hypertension, including the renal artery stenosis risk
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
- A focused review of SCAST outcomes and long-term stroke prevention data if the cerebrovascular direction is pursued
