# Ustekinumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab (brand name Stelara) is an IL-12/IL-23 antagonist originally used for plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, mainly atopic dermatitis.
Currently **7 clinical trials** (2 directly relevant Phase 2 RCTs) and **20 publications** relate to this direction, but the efficacy signal is not confirmed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ustekinumab blocks the p40 subunit shared by IL-12 and IL-23. This suppresses Th1 and Th17 signalling (and Th22 activation, according to the published Phase 2 study). Its efficacy in psoriasis, a Th17-driven skin disease, is well established.

Atopic dermatitis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease like psoriasis. However, it is mainly Th2-driven, with Th17/Th22 contributions in some subtypes (e.g. Asian, paediatric and intrinsic disease). The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible but only partial. It is most credible in the Th17-skewed subset of patients, and the available Phase 2 data suggest efficacy was inconsistent. Other dermatitis subtypes are not well covered.

The other nine predictions (neonatal dermatomyositis, acne keloid, pityriasis lichenoides and others) have no retrieved trials or literature. Their evidence level is L5 and they are on Hold.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 2 RCTs in atopic dermatitis give the prediction a real evidence base (L2). However, the trial results are not in the pack, efficacy appears inconsistent, and atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven. The HSA safety documents are also missing, which blocks the safety screen.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications), as a prerequisite for safety screening
- Primary results of NCT01945086 and NCT01806662, to confirm the efficacy signal
- Review of the Phase 2 publications (PMID 28338223, 27304428) and the systematic reviews (PMID 29164954, 29098604) for pooled efficacy
- Assessment of whether a Th17-skewed atopic dermatitis subgroup could be targeted
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
