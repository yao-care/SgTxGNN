## Ustekinumab: From Psoriasis to Dermatitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is an IL-12/IL-23 antagonist originally used to treat psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis** (mainly atopic dermatitis), with **6 clinical trials** (2 directly relevant Phase 2 trials) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The direct evidence is limited to small Phase 2 studies that showed only modest and inconsistent benefit.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, ustekinumab is a human monoclonal antibody that blocks the shared p40 subunit of IL-12 and IL-23. This suppresses Th1 and Th17 signalling, which is the basis of its efficacy in psoriasis.

Atopic dermatitis is driven mainly by Th2 pathways (IL-4/IL-13), although Th1, Th17 and Th22 activity contributes variably. The mechanistic fit is therefore partial. Any benefit is likely limited to subsets, such as Asian or intrinsic phenotypes or chronic lesions. Phase 2 data show only modest, inconsistent benefit.

The other top-ranked predictions (neonatal dermatomyositis, acne keloid, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, and others) have no trials or literature and are computational predictions only. The two pityriasis lichenoides predictions (ranks 9 and 10) are biologically plausible through their link to parapsoriasis, but also lack clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2, based on small Phase 2 randomised trials in atopic dermatitis (n=79 and n=32–33) that showed only modest, inconsistent benefit. The mechanistic fit is partial, because atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven. The package insert safety data are also missing. Newer targeted agents for atopic dermatitis may make this a lower priority.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Adequately powered Phase 3 evidence, or identification of a responsive atopic dermatitis subgroup (for example, Asian or intrinsic phenotypes)
- A review of how ustekinumab compares with currently approved atopic dermatitis therapies
- A safety assessment covering the interactions listed above, particularly with corticosteroids and anticoagulants
