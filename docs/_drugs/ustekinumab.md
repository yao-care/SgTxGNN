## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in structured form (DG002, High severity). However, literature evidence within this pack (PMID 27304428, PMID 36208443) consistently describes ustekinumab as a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting the shared p40 subunit of interleukin-12 (IL-12) and interleukin-23 (IL-23), thereby suppressing Th1, Th17, and Th22-mediated immune activation. This mechanism underlies its established efficacy in plaque psoriasis and other IL-12/23-driven inflammatory conditions.

Atopic dermatitis, like psoriasis, is an immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease. While AD has classically been characterized as Th2-driven, more recent immunophenotyping — particularly in Asian and pediatric AD populations — shows substantial Th17/Th22 activation overlapping with the IL-12/23 axis ustekinumab targets. This provides a plausible mechanistic bridge, and is directly reflected in the two completed Phase 2 RCTs (one specifically in Japanese patients with severe AD) included in this evidence pack.

The evidence base is nonetheless still exploratory: results across the two RCTs and the real-world effectiveness study have been described in the literature as "conflicting," and no confirmatory Phase 3 trial for AD currently exists.

Proceeding further is not advisable until these gaps are closed.
