# Doxycycline: From Antibacterial Use to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxycycline is a tetracycline-class antibiotic. It is marketed in Singapore as oral capsules and tablets, and it is an established treatment for chlamydial infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**.
Support is very thin: **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a 1992 case report).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available for this report. Based on known information, doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic. Its efficacy against chlamydial infection is established, and it may be mechanistically relevant to some eye-surface inflammation caused by infection.

The only supporting paper describes two patients with chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis. Both were treated with oral tetracycline or doxycycline, and the follicles resolved. Both then developed recurrent, bilateral punctate epithelial keratitis. The link is therefore indirect: doxycycline treats the infectious trigger, not the epithelial keratopathy itself. In these cases the keratitis appeared after antibiotic treatment, so the paper does not show that doxycycline treats the keratitis.

The high TxGNN score is a model prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence is one 1992 case report, and in it the keratitis appeared after doxycycline treatment. There are no registered trials, so the prediction rests almost entirely on the model score.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The HSA package insert, to obtain warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Any controlled clinical evidence of doxycycline for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, including a check of whether a topical or ocular route would be needed
- If other candidates are prioritised: chronic gingivitis and post-infectious syndrome have more supporting evidence (both L3, Research Question). Chronic gingivitis is close to an existing periodontitis use, and post-infectious syndrome has the Q-fever fatigue trial NCT01318356, whose results still need to be checked.
