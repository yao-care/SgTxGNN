# Testosterone: From Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism and Pituitary Hormone Deficiency to Polysomy of X Chromosome

## One-Sentence Summary

Testosterone is an androgen replacement hormone, originally used for hypogonadism (including hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and Kallmann syndrome) and pituitary hormone deficiencies.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **polysomy of X chromosome**, but this top-ranked prediction has **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** behind it.
The data compiled for this report show stronger support for other predicted indications, notably **primary ovarian failure** (2 trials, 20 publications) and **testicular regression syndrome** (20 publications, no trials). These are discussed below.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Testosterone is the main male sex hormone, and it is established practice to use it to replace missing androgen in hypogonadism. Its efficacy in the original conditions rests on this replacement role.

The link to X polysomy is indirect. Testosterone replacement is plausible in sex chromosome aneuploidies that come with hypogonadism (for example 47,XXY). The entity named here, X polysomy, is not directly supported by any data compiled for this report. The link is inferred from the knowledge graph and has not been demonstrated.

Other top-ranked predictions vary widely in plausibility:

- **Testicular regression syndrome, penile/testicular agenesis and Leydig cell hypoplasia:** Androgen is absent or poorly produced, so replacement is biologically reasonable.
- **Primary ovarian failure:** Ovarian androgen output falls along with estrogen, so adding testosterone to estradiol has been proposed. This is the only prediction with registered trials.
- **Tetragametic chimerism, freemartinism, urethral obstruction sequence and arthrogryposis with epileptic seizures:** No convincing therapeutic rationale. The freemartinism literature is entirely animal research, and androgen insensitivity would make testosterone ineffective.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, congestive heart failure, hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hypercalcaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, gynaecomastia, female breast carcinoma, thrombophlebitis and uterine leiomyoma.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. All 20 listed in the data are rated Moderate. They fall mainly into three groups:
  - corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone)
  - antidiabetic agents (sulfonylureas such as glimepiride, glipizide and glyburide, and multiple insulins)
  - others, including aprepitant and topical calcitriol

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications from the label.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, polysomy of X chromosome, rests on a model score alone (L5), with no trials or literature, and the link to testosterone is inferred rather than demonstrated. The best-supported alternative, primary ovarian failure, has a recruiting Phase 3 trial and a Phase 1/2 randomized trial. Neither has results, so the evidence level is not yet L1 or L2.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator (HSA), which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Results from NCT06343870 and NCT01662466 before considering primary ovarian failure further
- Clinical evidence specific to X polysomy, or a decision to prioritize testicular regression syndrome, where testosterone replacement is already standard reasoning
- A review of the topical gel formulations against the dosing and route needs of any chosen indication
- Careful review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (especially cardiovascular, hepatic, renal and breast or prostate conditions) for the target population
