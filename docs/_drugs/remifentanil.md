## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Remifentanil is known as an ultra-short-acting mu-opioid agonist used for anesthesia and analgesia. It has no antiviral action and no role in relieving symptoms of upper respiratory infection.

The two retrieved papers do not support the prediction. One is a study of remifentanil and cold propofol for reducing propofol injection pain. "Cold" there refers to the temperature of the drug, not the illness. The other is a review of awake craniotomy for glioma surgery. Neither studies the common cold.

The mechanistic review therefore concludes that the prediction is most likely an artifact of the knowledge graph, and that there is no sound repurposing rationale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by a model score. There are no clinical trials, and the two retrieved papers do not study the common cold. Remifentanil has no antiviral or symptom-relieving mechanism for upper respiratory infection. The opioid's respiratory depression and hypotension risks also make it a poor fit for this condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a credible pharmacological rationale linking remifentanil to the common cold
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Health Sciences Authority (HSA)
- Any direct clinical or preclinical evidence in the common cold. None currently exists.
