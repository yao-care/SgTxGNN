## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Trametinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor. It blocks MAP2K1 and MAP2K2, which act downstream of BRAF in the MAPK signalling pathway. Detailed mechanism-of-action text is not available beyond this target information. It is used with dabrafenib for BRAF V600-mutant melanoma and other BRAF V600E tumours.

For **choroideremia**, a mechanistic link is hard to establish. It is an X-linked retinal degeneration caused by loss of the CHM (REP1) gene. No connection to MEK/MAPK inhibition is evident in the data compiled for this report. The very high score (99.31%) is therefore best read as a model artefact rather than a biologically supported hypothesis.

By contrast, the lower-ranked melanoma-related predictions are mechanistically coherent. Non-cutaneous melanomas (uveal, mucosal) can carry MAPK-activating alterations. Mucosal melanoma also has preclinical evidence of sensitivity to MEK inhibitor combinations. BRAF frequency differs from cutaneous melanoma, though.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** 15 drug-drug interactions were identified, all graded Moderate. They include:
- Dabrafenib and encorafenib (other BRAF inhibitors)
- Enzalutamide and apalutamide
- Brigatinib, erdafitinib, gilteritinib, midostaurin and tucatinib
- Idelalisib, isavuconazonium, fostamatinib, elagolix, lasmiditan and sarecycline

Dabrafenib is the usual combination partner, so that interaction should be reviewed in context.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert for safety information.

In the literature retrieved for nodular melanoma, panniculitis was reported with BRAF/MEK inhibitor therapy (including dabrafenib + trametinib). This is a case-level safety signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The choroideremia prediction rests on a computational score alone (evidence level L5). No trials or publications support it, and no plausible link between MEK/MAPK inhibition and CHM-related retinal degeneration is evident. Trametinib is already marketed in Singapore, but that does not support this new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A mechanistic rationale connecting MEK inhibition to choroideremia, plus preclinical data in relevant retinal models
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (not available in the data compiled, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Consideration of redirecting effort to **non-cutaneous melanoma** (rank 2, L2, Proceed with Guardrails). Its trial outcomes, the cutaneous/non-cutaneous split of enrolled patients, and BRAF/NRAS mutation status would first need to be clarified.
