# Pioglitazone: From Type 2 Diabetes to Opsismodysplasia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pioglitazone is an oral insulin-sensitizing drug (a PPAR-gamma agonist) used for type 2 diabetes. The Singapore registration data do not state an approved indication, so type 2 diabetes comes from the drug's known use and the retrieved literature.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **opsismodysplasia**, a rare skeletal dysplasia, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Pioglitazone is a thiazolidinedione that activates PPAR-gamma. This improves insulin sensitivity in type 2 diabetes.

The review found no credible link between this mechanism and opsismodysplasia. The disease is a skeletal dysplasia related to the INPPL1 gene, and PPAR-gamma agonism has no known relevance to its pathway. The high TxGNN score (0.996) is a graph-based prediction with no clinical or literature support. Because the drug's mechanism data are missing, the link could not be cross-checked either.

This prediction should be treated as a model output only, not as a lead worth pursuing.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score. There are no trials or publications, no plausible mechanistic link, and the drug's mechanism and safety data are incomplete. Nothing in the evidence justifies further investment for opsismodysplasia.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text and package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA (this blocks safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence linking PPAR-gamma agonism to the INPPL1 pathway or to opsismodysplasia

**Other predicted candidates worth noting:**
Four localized lipodystrophy predictions are marked "Research Question" (drug-induced localized lipodystrophy, centrifugal lipodystrophy, pressure-induced localized lipoatrophy and idiopathic localized lipodystrophy). Pioglitazone's effect on adipogenesis gives them a plausible rationale, but none has trials or literature. The pancreatic agenesis prediction has only general type 2 diabetes literature, which appears to be a keyword match rather than real support.
