## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, pioglitazone is a thiazolidinedione that acts on PPAR-gamma, and its efficacy in type 2 diabetes has been established through its insulin-sensitising effects.

Opsismodysplasia is a rare skeletal dysplasia caused by mutations in INPPL1 (SHIP2), a negative regulator of PI3K/insulin signalling. This gives a theoretical link to insulin signalling and PPAR-gamma biology. However, no trials or literature support it, and the mechanism has not been verified. At this stage the prediction cannot be considered supported.

Other top-ranked predictions are also supported only by model scores. They include focal stiff limb syndrome, classic stiff person syndrome, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, several localised lipodystrophies, pancreatic agenesis and autoimmune oophoritis. The lipodystrophies have a plausible PPAR-gamma adipogenesis rationale. The seven publications linked to pancreatic agenesis all concern thiazolidinediones in type 2 diabetes. They do not provide direct evidence for that disease, which lacks the beta-cell mass that pioglitazone would act on.
