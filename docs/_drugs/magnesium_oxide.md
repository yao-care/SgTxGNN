# Magnesium Oxide: From Antacid Use to Calcium-Alkali Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Magnesium oxide is an oral magnesium and alkali source, used as an antacid and mineral supplement, and is registered in Singapore in a tablet product.
The TxGNN model gives its top-ranked prediction, **calcium-alkali syndrome**, a high score, but the only linked publication describes this syndrome as a **harm caused by** magnesium oxide (with vitamin D), not a treatment target.
Support is minimal: **1 clinical trial** (not relevant) and **1 publication** (a case report of an adverse outcome).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, magnesium oxide is an alkaline magnesium salt that neutralizes acid and supplies magnesium.

That same property explains the prediction, but in the opposite direction. Calcium-alkali syndrome (hypercalcaemia, metabolic alkalosis and renal impairment) results from excess intake of absorbable alkali together with calcium and/or vitamin D. The one linked report describes exactly this: syndrome onset after vitamin D and magnesium oxide administration. The high score most likely reflects a drug-causes-disease link in the knowledge graph rather than a treatment signal, so this prediction should not be read as a repurposing opportunity.

## Safety Considerations

- **Literature signal**: Magnesium oxide combined with vitamin D has been reported to cause calcium-alkali syndrome (PMID 19185403). A separate 2019 case report describes iron deficiency anaemia from magnesium overuse, related to inhibited iron absorption.

No warnings, contraindications or interaction data were retrieved. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The predicted indication is most likely an adverse-effect association, and the single trial and single publication do not support a therapeutic use. Repurposing magnesium oxide for this condition is not supported.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- Redirecting attention to the more plausible, antacid-related candidates in the same prediction list (duodenal ulcer, stomach disease and gastroduodenitis, all rated Research Question). Evidence for these is mostly older, preclinical or class-level antacid work, not magnesium oxide alone.
