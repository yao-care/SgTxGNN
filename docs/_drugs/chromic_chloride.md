# Chromic Chloride: From a Parenteral Trace-Element Supplement to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Chromic chloride is a trivalent chromium salt used as a trace-element supplement in parenteral nutrition and is marketed in Singapore as an infusion concentrate.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but this is a computational prediction only.
Currently there are **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, chromic chloride is a trivalent chromium (chromium III) salt given as a trace element in parenteral nutrition. Chromium is thought to play a role in glucose and lipid metabolism, but that role is not established by the data compiled for this report.

No mechanistic link has been established between chromium(III) and glaucoma. No known pathway connects it to trabecular meshwork function, aqueous humour dynamics, intraocular pressure regulation or retinal ganglion cell protection. The high TxGNN score reflects a knowledge-graph pattern and does not constitute biological or clinical validation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 44 interactions were identified in total. All of the 20 shown in the data compiled for this report are rated **Moderate**. They are mainly with antidiabetic agents, which is consistent with chromium's possible effects on glucose metabolism. Examples:
  - Insulin products (insulin human, insulin aspart)
  - Biguanides and thiazolidinediones (metformin, pioglitazone)
  - Sulfonylureas (chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, acetohexamide)
  - SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin)
  - DPP-4 inhibitors (alogliptin, linagliptin, saxagliptin)
  - GLP-1 receptor agonists and alpha-glucosidase inhibitors (albiglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide, acarbose)

  Concurrent use may require glucose monitoring.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a knowledge-graph score, with no clinical trials, no publications and no plausible mechanism linking chromium(III) to glaucoma. The product is also an intravenous trace-element supplement, which is not a suitable route for an eye disease. The evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore-registered product (this is a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking chromium(III) to intraocular pressure regulation or optic nerve protection
- A route and formulation feasibility assessment, since only an intravenous product is currently registered
- A systematic literature search to confirm that no supporting studies exist for any of the top predictions
