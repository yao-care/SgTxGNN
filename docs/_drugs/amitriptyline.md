## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the drug record. The literature retrieved for other predictions describes amitriptyline as inhibiting serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake, with additional activity at histamine and muscarinic receptors. This is the basis of its use in depression, and it also underlies its use in migraine prevention in adults.

Benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy is regarded as a childhood migraine equivalent. Because amitriptyline is used for migraine prophylaxis in adults, a link between the two is plausible. However, this link is speculative. No trials or publications were found testing it, and there is no pediatric safety or efficacy data for this use. The high graph score should therefore be treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
This prediction has no supporting trials or literature (L5) and no pediatric data. The only support is a speculative migraine-equivalent link, so it does not justify further investment at this stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A targeted literature search for amitriptyline or tricyclic antidepressants in infantile migraine equivalents and paroxysmal torticollis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (from the HSA website), including pediatric use restrictions
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the four Singapore registrations

**Related predictions in the same analysis:**
Two other predictions have much stronger evidence: major depressive disorder and unipolar depression (both L1, "Proceed with Guardrails"). These are essentially established uses, not repurposing. Any review should confirm the approved Singapore label and account for anticholinergic effects, cardiac (QT/arrhythmia) risk, overdose toxicity, seizure threshold, use in elderly patients, and drug interactions.
