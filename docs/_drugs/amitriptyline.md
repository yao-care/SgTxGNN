## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, amitriptyline inhibits serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake and also acts on histamine, muscarinic and other receptors. This is the established basis of its antidepressant effect.

Benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy is regarded as a paediatric migraine equivalent. Amitriptyline is used in migraine prophylaxis, so a link is plausible, but it has not been verified. No trials or publications were found for this condition. Paediatric safety (anticholinergic effects, cardiac effects, QT prolongation) would also need separate evaluation.

The other predictions fall into three groups:
- **Overlap with existing use:** Major depressive disorder, unipolar depression, endogenous depression, melancholia and neurotic depression are depressive conditions, close to amitriptyline's original indications. They reflect terminology overlap, not new repurposing.
- **Indirect evidence:** Agoraphobia and phobic disorder have a plausible monoaminergic rationale, but the retrieved literature is mostly about other drugs.
- **Likely model artifacts:** Ohdo syndrome and its variants are rare genetic syndromes with no pharmacological rationale for amitriptyline.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph (not label text):**
  - Cardiac and blood pressure: long QT syndrome (including the familial form), torsades de pointes, orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, multiple system atrophy.
  - Neurological and psychiatric: epilepsy, basal ganglia disease, manic bipolar affective disorder, psychotic and schizophrenia-spectrum disorders, substance abuse/dependence.
  - Organ and metabolic: liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, porphyria, hyperglycaemia.
  - Haematological: thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis.
  - Other: open-angle glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia, female breast carcinoma.
- **Drug interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 records. The first 20 shown include these **Major** interactions: epinephrine, bupropion, cisapride, dexfenfluramine, dolasetron and ephedrine. The Moderate ones include famotidine, cimetidine, atropine, clarithromycin and several anticholinergic agents (clidinium, dicyclomine).
- **Specific cautions noted in the literature:** cardiotoxicity, anticholinergic burden, overdose lethality, caution in older adults, and suicidality monitoring in young patients. For the top prediction in infants, the same cardiac and anticholinergic effects would need separate paediatric evaluation.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, has only a model score (97.72%). There are no trials or publications, and the safety of amitriptyline in infants is unaddressed. The better-supported depressive-disorder predictions (L1 for major depressive disorder and unipolar depression) are essentially confirmations of existing use, not new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Literature and clinical evidence for amitriptyline in paediatric migraine equivalents, including benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy
- A paediatric safety assessment covering QT prolongation, anticholinergic effects and overdose risk
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA, plus the approved indication text for the four Singapore licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- If depressive subtypes are pursued, confirmation that they add nothing beyond the existing indications, with guardrails on cardiac risk, anticholinergic burden, overdose lethality, use in older adults, and suicidality monitoring
