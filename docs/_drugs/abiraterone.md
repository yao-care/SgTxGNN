# Abiraterone: From Prostate Cancer to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Abiraterone is a CYP17A1 inhibitor that suppresses androgen synthesis and is used in prostate cancer treatment.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but this is a knowledge-graph prediction only, with **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in DrugBank for this record. Based on known information, abiraterone inhibits CYP17A1 and so reduces androgen production. Its efficacy in prostate cancer is well established.

No mechanistic link to migraine has been identified. The high TxGNN score comes from knowledge-graph patterns alone. No trials or papers connect abiraterone to migraine, so the prediction cannot currently be called reasonable on biological grounds.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One point comes from the evidence pack's assessment of other predictions: abiraterone can cause mineralocorticoid excess (hypertension, hypokalemia, fluid retention). This matters for any new use in a non-cancer population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score, with no clinical trials, no relevant literature and no mechanistic link to migraine. The other nine predicted indications in the pack are also L5 with no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (DrugBank) and a plausible biological link between androgen synthesis inhibition and migraine
- Drug-specific preclinical or clinical evidence, since the retrieved papers are epilepsy genetics reviews and do not study abiraterone
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications, which currently block safety screening
- Safety assessment for non-cancer use, given the mineralocorticoid-excess risk and the need for co-administered prednisone
