## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the compiled sources. Based on general knowledge, abiraterone inhibits CYP17A1 and thereby suppresses androgen synthesis. It is used together with prednisone, mainly in advanced prostate cancer.

Sex-steroid fluctuation has been implicated in migraine, which is the only plausible bridge to this prediction. However, there is no evidence that CYP17A1 inhibition is therapeutic in migraine. The high TxGNN score (0.988) reflects network proximity in a knowledge graph, not clinical or experimental support.

The other top-ranked predictions are similarly unsupported. They include migraine subtypes (a genetic susceptibility phenotype and migraine with brainstem aura), leprosy, pulmonary hypertension, nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, rheumatoid arthritis, kyphoscoliotic heart disease, atrophoderma vermiculata and ulerythema ophryogenesis. None has supporting trials. The five publications retrieved under pulmonary hypertension all concern prostate cancer and appear to be retrieval mismatches rather than evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: A screen returned 100 interacting drugs. Major-level interactions in the listed results include cisapride, dolasetron, eliglustat and loperamide. Moderate interactions include clarithromycin, levofloxacin, granisetron, metoclopramide, famotidine and several laxatives (bisacodyl, lactulose, magnesium citrate). One minor interaction is metronidazole. Several of these are QT-prolongation or CYP-related concerns and need review before any new use.
- **Other safety considerations**: The package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for them. Abiraterone is known to carry risks of hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention, and it is given with a corticosteroid.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone (L5). There are no trials, no relevant literature and no established mechanistic link between CYP17A1 inhibition and migraine. The drug's known cardiovascular and mineralocorticoid-excess adverse effects, plus long-term corticosteroid co-administration, make a non-oncology use hard to justify without evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the Singapore registrations
- Preclinical or mechanistic studies linking androgen or CYP17A1 pathways to migraine
- A risk-benefit assessment of long-term use in a non-cancer, likely younger and female population
