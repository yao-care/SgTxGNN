## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not currently available in the compiled data. Clozapine is known pharmacologically for its broad receptor profile, with antagonism at dopamine D2/D4, serotonin 5-HT2A, muscarinic, adrenergic and histaminergic receptors. This broad activity plausibly explains antimanic and mood-stabilising effects, especially in patients whose mania has not responded to conventional treatment.

The prediction is consistent with the clinical literature. A systematic review and meta-analysis of clozapine in bipolar disorder (PMID 32182485) pooled three small comparative studies. It found clozapine similar to other antipsychotics in manic episodes and better in treatment-resistant bipolar disorder. A completed double-blind Phase 2 trial in treatment-resistant mania (NCT00029458, n=42) points the same way, though its results are not summarised in the available data.

The evidence is rated L2 because no Phase 3 trials in this indication are available. The supporting studies are small, and the meta-analysis rests on only three comparative studies.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key warnings and contraindications from the package insert:** not available in the compiled data. Please refer to the package insert.
- **Safety issues reported in the supporting literature:**
  - Cardiotoxicity, including myocarditis and cardiomyopathy (PMID 27222142).
  - Seizures (PMID 2375443).
  - Metabolic and glucose dysregulation (PMID 26407790, PMID 32930957).
  - Somnolence (PMID 27372312).
  - Agranulocytosis, which requires blood monitoring (PMID 39788917).
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The listed **Major** interactions include bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate. Moderate interactions include metformin, pioglitazone, other antidiabetic agents, anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), cimetidine, famotidine, omeprazole and epinephrine.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Clozapine has a plausible mechanism, a completed Phase 2 trial and a systematic review and meta-analysis supporting use in treatment-resistant mania. The evidence is L2: there are no Phase 3 trials, and the supporting studies are small. Clozapine also carries serious known risks and a heavy interaction burden, so any use in this indication needs strict safeguards.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications, which are a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data.
- Published results of NCT00029458, or a larger confirmatory randomised trial in mania.
- A monitoring plan covering absolute neutrophil count, cardiac, metabolic and seizure risk, plus an interaction review for co-prescribed medicines.
