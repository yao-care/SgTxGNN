## Clozapine: From Treatment-Resistant Psychosis to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clozapine is an atypical antipsychotic marketed in Singapore as tablets, and it is established for treatment-resistant psychotic illness. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**. The data compiled for this report show **1 completed Phase 2 trial** specific to treatment-resistant mania, a **completed Phase 3 trial** in the broader bipolar disorder group, and **20 publications** (including a systematic review and meta-analysis) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the known pharmacology, clozapine blocks several receptors: dopamine D2/D4, serotonin 5-HT2A, muscarinic, adrenergic and histamine receptors. This broad profile is what makes it effective in treatment-resistant psychosis.

Mania and psychosis share dopaminergic and serotonergic pathways, and many patients with bipolar disorder do not respond to first-line mood stabilisers or cannot tolerate them. Clozapine's multi-receptor activity plausibly explains antimanic and mood-stabilising effects in refractory cases. The high TxGNN score is consistent with the clinical literature, which supports use in treatment-refractory bipolar illness.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, epilepsy, liver and gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus, hyperglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperlipidaemia, obesity, coronary artery disease, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, pulmonary embolism, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, orthostatic and other hypotensive disorders, respiratory failure, angle-closure glaucoma, paralytic ileus and benign prostatic hyperplasia.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level examples include bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate. Moderate-level examples include antidiabetic drugs (metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), famotidine, cimetidine, omeprazole, epinephrine, loperamide, bisacodyl and lorcaserin.
- **Literature-reported risks**: myocarditis and cardiomyopathy, seizures, metabolic syndrome and weight gain, and somnolence. Blood monitoring for agranulocytosis is a central requirement.

Please also refer to the package insert, as label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed double-blind Phase 2 trial in treatment-resistant mania, a completed Phase 3 trial in treatment-refractory bipolar disorder (paediatric), and a systematic review with meta-analysis support clozapine in refractory bipolar illness. The mania-specific trial is small and its results are not summarised in the data compiled, and clozapine carries serious known risks, so use should be restricted to treatment-refractory patients under strict monitoring.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications for the Singapore-registered products (not available in the data compiled; this is a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Published results of NCT00029458 and the adult bipolar trials (for example NCT02562287)
- A monitoring plan covering absolute neutrophil count, cardiac (myocarditis), metabolic and seizure risk
- A drug-interaction management plan for common co-medications
