## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Escitalopram is an SSRI whose efficacy in depression is established, and it acts by inhibiting the serotonin transporter (SERT).

Ohdo syndrome is a rare congenital (KAT6B-related) developmental disorder. No mechanistic link between serotonin reuptake inhibition and its pathophysiology has been identified. A related entry, blepharophimosis - intellectual disability syndrome, Ohdo type, also scores high (97.87%). This suggests the model is picking up on closeness between disease terms in the knowledge graph rather than on pharmacology. The high score alone should not be read as evidence of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for Ohdo syndrome rests on the model score alone. There are no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism, and the score likely reflects disease-ontology proximity.

Other predictions for this drug are much better supported:
- **Dysthymic disorder** (L2, Proceed with Guardrails): a placebo-controlled escitalopram trial (n=36) and a meta-analysis of antidepressants for dysthymia.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder** (L2, Proceed with Guardrails): a relapse-prevention RCT, an open-label Phase 3 high-dose study and several small trials.
- **Agoraphobia** (L2, Research Question): evidence comes from panic disorder trials, not agoraphobia alone.

If the team wants to advance this drug, those indications are the better place to start.

**To proceed with the Ohdo syndrome prediction, the following is needed:**
- A mechanistic rationale linking SERT inhibition to KAT6B-related disease biology
- Any supporting preclinical, case or trial data
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Evidence on escitalopram use in the relevant (likely paediatric) population
