## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Amiodarone is known to block several ion channels and also has beta-blocking and calcium-channel-blocking activity.

CPVT is a rare inherited arrhythmia disorder. It is usually caused by faulty calcium handling (RYR2 or CASQ2 mutations) and triggered by exercise or adrenergic stress. Amiodarone's beta-blocking and calcium-channel-blocking effects give a plausible but indirect rationale.

This rationale is weak. Beta-blockers (such as nadolol) and flecainide are the standard of care for CPVT, and amiodarone is generally considered ineffective or only an add-on. The retrieved literature describes the disease and its management, not amiodarone's efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no trial tests amiodarone in CPVT and the available literature only describes the disease. Established treatments (beta-blockers, flecainide) are the standard of care, and amiodarone is generally regarded as ineffective or adjunctive. It is a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies that directly assess amiodarone's efficacy in CPVT, alone or added to beta-blocker or flecainide therapy
- Mechanism-of-action data for amiodarone in calcium-handling arrhythmias
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the HSA to complete the safety review
- Comparison against standard-of-care therapy, with a safety plan for QT prolongation, thyroid, lung and liver effects
