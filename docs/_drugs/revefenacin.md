# Revefenacin: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Revefenacin is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) nebulized bronchodilator, originally developed and approved (in the US, per the literature evidence in this pack) for the maintenance treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Bronchitis**, but currently only **1 clinical trial** and **0 dedicated publications** support this specific direction — evidence is preliminary.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not provided in the structured evidence fields (flagged as a data gap, DG002). However, based on the literature evidence collected for this drug, revefenacin is a **lung-selective, long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA)**. Acetylcholine binding to muscarinic (M3) receptors on airway smooth muscle triggers bronchoconstriction; revefenacin blocks this binding, producing sustained bronchodilation with once-daily nebulized dosing (PMID 30803279, 29736245).

Chronic bronchitis is one of the two classic clinical phenotypes of COPD (alongside emphysema), sharing the same underlying pathophysiology of airway smooth muscle hyperreactivity and mucus hypersecretion. Since revefenacin's established efficacy in COPD is driven by M3-receptor blockade and bronchodilation, the same mechanism is plausible in chronic bronchitis specifically.

That said, the only trial linked directly to "bronchitis" in this evidence pack (NCT01704404) is an early Phase 2 pharmacodynamics/safety/PK study conducted in a general COPD population, not a confirmatory trial designed around a bronchitis-specific endpoint. The evidence for bronchitis is therefore mechanistically plausible but clinically preliminary — weaker than the extensive Phase 3 evidence base that exists for revefenacin's approved COPD indication.
