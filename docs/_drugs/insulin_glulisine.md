# Insulin Glulisine: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue. It binds the insulin receptor and lowers blood glucose by promoting peripheral glucose uptake and suppressing hepatic glucose production. It is used as mealtime insulin within basal-bolus or insulin-pump regimens. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description relies on general pharmacological knowledge.

Type 1 diabetes is a disease of absolute insulin deficiency, and mealtime insulin replacement is the standard of care. The original indication (diabetes mellitus) already covers this condition. The high TxGNN score therefore reflects a well-established use rather than a new discovery.

On grading: the trial list contains Phase 2 and Phase 4 trials and several Phase 3 studies. Many of the Phase 3 trials have not been individually appraised for relevance. The level was assigned conservatively as L2 from the data supplied. Pivotal trials such as PMID 16308840 and 19614947 appear in the literature list, and a fuller review could justify a higher level.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs; 20 were reviewed here. Most are of moderate severity, and a few are minor. Main examples:
  - Other glucose-lowering agents (metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide): additive blood-glucose-lowering effects.
  - Beta-agonists and sympathomimetics (salbutamol, formoterol, epinephrine, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine): may blunt glucose control.
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, with topical forms rated minor), estrogens (estradiol, ethinylestradiol), and thiazides (hydrochlorothiazide): may raise glucose levels.
  - Beta-blocker (acebutolol), acetazolamide, doxycycline, alpelisib, and ethanol: moderate-level interactions that can alter glucose response or mask hypoglycaemia.
- **Standard insulin precautions**: Monitor for hypoglycaemia, injection-site lipodystrophy, and dose titration needs. In pump use, watch for pump failure.

Warnings and contraindications from the package insert were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Type 1 diabetes is a core use of insulin glulisine, backed by many completed Phase 2 to Phase 4 trials and published RCTs, systematic reviews and pump studies. The drug is already marketed in Singapore. The main gaps are in labelling and safety documentation, not in evidence of efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert from the Health Sciences Authority (HSA) with warnings and contraindications, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- The approved indication text for SIN13432P, which is blank in the registration data
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A formal review of the Phase 3 trials, to confirm whether the evidence level can be raised above L2
- A safety monitoring plan covering hypoglycaemia, injection-site reactions, and pump-related risks

**Other predictions (all Hold):** The other nine predicted indications have no trials or literature (evidence level L5) and should not proceed. Examples are thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, stiff person syndrome and pancreatic agenesis. Several likely reflect comorbidity artifacts. The lipodystrophy-related predictions probably reflect adverse effects of injected insulin rather than a treatment benefit.
