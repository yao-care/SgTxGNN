# Triamterene: From Potassium-Sparing Diuretic to Malignant Hypertensive Renal Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Triamterene is a potassium-sparing diuretic. The registration record in Singapore does not state an approved indication, so this class-level description is used instead.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant hypertensive renal disease**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.
The evidence is model-only, so the recommendation is **Hold**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on known pharmacology, triamterene blocks the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) in the distal nephron. This promotes sodium excretion while sparing potassium, and it has a modest blood-pressure-lowering effect, mainly as an add-on to thiazide diuretics.

This makes a link to hypertensive renal disease plausible, since both involve sodium and volume handling in the kidney. The TxGNN score is the only support, however. No trials or literature were retrieved. Malignant hypertension with renal impairment is also a high-risk setting for hyperkalaemia with a potassium-sparing diuretic.

A closely related prediction, malignant renovascular hypertension, has an identical score. The RAAS is strongly activated in that condition, and there is no clinical evidence for triamterene there either.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hyperkalaemia risk**: A potassium-sparing diuretic carries a hyperkalaemia risk in renal impairment, which is expected in malignant hypertensive renal disease. The risk is higher with concurrent ACE inhibitor or ARB use.

Please refer to the package insert for further safety information, including warnings, contraindications and drug interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score but no trials or publications behind it (L5). The target population is also at high risk of hyperkalaemia with this drug class.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A targeted literature and trial search specific to triamterene in malignant hypertension with renal involvement
- A hyperkalaemia risk assessment for patients with severe renal impairment

Among the other predictions, only **potassium deficiency disease** (L4, stage S1) has a strong rationale. It largely overlaps with established potassium-sparing pharmacology, so its novelty is limited.
