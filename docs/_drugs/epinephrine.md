## Epinephrine: From Asthma and Anaphylaxis to Obstructive Lung Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Epinephrine is a non-selective adrenergic agonist, originally used for asthma, anaphylaxis, bronchitis, emphysema, hypotensive disorders and open-angle glaucoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, with **16 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction.
Much of this prediction rediscovers an existing use, and the strongest evidence is in asthma (Phase 3 trials of an inhaled epinephrine formulation).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report. From established pharmacology, epinephrine is a non-selective alpha/beta-adrenergic agonist. Beta-2 receptor activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and causes bronchodilation, while alpha-1 activation reduces mucosal oedema.

Obstructive lung disease covers asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Asthma, emphysema and bronchitis are already among epinephrine's original indications, so this prediction is largely a rediscovery of an existing use rather than a wholly new one. The inhaled HFA metered-dose formulation (E004 / Primatene Mist) has Phase 3 efficacy and safety data in asthma. Evidence in bronchiolitis is mixed and is best treated as a separate sub-question.

Among the other predictions, **laryngotracheitis (croup)** has the next strongest support (L2). Nebulised epinephrine reduces subglottic oedema, and this is established clinical practice. Food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis (L4) is a subtype of anaphylaxis, an existing indication. Predictions such as Rienhoff syndrome, anorectal stricture, dry eye syndrome, headache disorder and trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia have no clinical trials or no therapeutic rationale and should be held.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials of inhaled epinephrine in asthma, in adults and children, support this prediction, and asthma, emphysema and bronchitis are already established uses. The new element is mainly the inhaled route, as the Singapore licences are injectable only. The bronchiolitis evidence is mixed and the cardiovascular safety burden is notable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Singapore package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A regulatory assessment of whether an inhaled epinephrine product could be registered in Singapore, since current licences are injectable only
- A review of the interaction and contraindication burden, especially cardiovascular disease and use with beta-blockers or tricyclic antidepressants
- Treatment of bronchiolitis and croup as separate sub-questions with their own evidence reviews
