# Fluoxetine: From Antidepressant Use to Schizotypal Personality Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Fluoxetine is an SSRI antidepressant. The HSA records supplied do not state its approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Schizotypal Personality Disorder**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **10 publications**. Only one of these is a small, uncontrolled fluoxetine study in this population, so the prediction is largely model-driven.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Fluoxetine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). Its efficacy in depression is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to schizotypal personality disorder.

Serotonergic modulation may reduce affective instability, impulsivity, and obsessional or anxious features that overlap with schizotypal traits. This link is plausible but indirect. The core psychotic-like features of the disorder (odd beliefs, perceptual distortions) are not clearly serotonin-responsive.

The 99.92% TxGNN score is a model prediction only. The evidence available is a small, uncontrolled 12-week trial and several reviews stating that no agent of choice exists for this disorder.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One literature report (PMID 9664779) describes transient psychosis with psychogenic polydipsia in a schizotypal patient taking fluoxetine. This is a single case report, but it is relevant to this specific population and should be considered in any study design.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is limited to one small, non-blind, 12-week study plus reviews. The reviews state that no agent of choice exists for this disorder. There are no registered trials. The mechanistic link is indirect, and the high TxGNN score reflects graph proximity to related personality disorders. Package insert safety data are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking data gap)
- Confirmed approved indication text for the Singapore licenses, and mechanism of action data
- A randomized, controlled trial of fluoxetine in schizotypal personality disorder, or a more focused literature review on cluster A personality disorders
- Assessment of the psychosis-related case report as a safety signal for this population

Other predictions in the same Evidence Pack, such as agoraphobia and phobic disorder, have stronger supporting evidence and may be better candidates to prioritise.
