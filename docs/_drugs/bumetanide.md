# Bumetanide: From Congestive Heart Failure to Acute Pulmonary Heart Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Bumetanide is a loop diuretic originally used to treat fluid overload (oedema) in congestive heart failure.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acute pulmonary heart disease**, but **no clinical trials** and only **5 publications** (mostly general reviews of heart failure and diuretics) currently relate to this prediction.
The evidence is indirect, and the prediction largely reflects the drug's similarity to its existing heart failure use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, bumetanide is a loop diuretic that inhibits the NKCC2 transporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle. This reduces preload and relieves volume overload.

Acute right-sided heart failure (acute cor pulmonale) involves congestion that diuretics may relieve. However, this is the same mechanism as the approved heart failure indication, not a genuinely new one. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects this similarity to heart failure rather than independent evidence in acute cor pulmonale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by general heart failure and loop diuretic literature and small, old haemodynamic studies, with no trials in acute cor pulmonale. Its mechanism overlaps with the already approved heart failure use, so it offers little new indication value.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
