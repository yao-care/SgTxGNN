## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Gatifloxacin belongs to the fluoroquinolone class. Fluoroquinolones inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, which is how they kill bacteria.

Polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome is a protein-driven disorder of blood rheology, not an infection. No plausible mechanistic link between the two was identified. The high score (0.976) most likely reflects proximity in the knowledge graph rather than biology, and no clinical or literature evidence supports it.

Septicemic plague is the only candidate with a biological rationale. It is caused by *Yersinia pestis*, a Gram-negative bacterium that fluoroquinolones are expected to inhibit. The only support is one mouse study (PMID [15555886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15555886/), 2004). It found that gatifloxacin, moxifloxacin and ciprofloxacin protected mice in systemic and pneumonic plague models. This is indirect, non-human evidence.
