# Calcium Chloride: From Fluid and Electrolyte Products (Registered Indication Not Stated) to Dyspepsia

## One-Sentence Summary

Calcium chloride is a calcium salt used in Singapore-registered fluid and electrolyte products such as lactated Ringer's solutions, and in an eye-drop product.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dyspepsia**.
Although **9 clinical trials** and **5 publications** were retrieved, none of them tests calcium chloride for dyspepsia, so the prediction is **not supported by evidence**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, calcium chloride is a calcium salt used for fluid and electrolyte replacement. Its efficacy in those uses is established, but there is no established mechanism linking it to dyspepsia.

Calcium salts are sometimes associated with antacid-like or acid-secretion effects. However, calcium chloride is acidifying rather than buffering, so it does not neutralise stomach acid. A 1982 review notes that calcium chloride contact with the gastric mucosa can trigger gastrin release and acid rebound, which would work against a dyspepsia benefit.

The high TxGNN score is therefore likely a knowledge-graph association rather than a signal of real therapeutic activity. The only calcium chloride items retrieved for dyspepsia are old veterinary and compounding references.

## Safety Considerations

- **Corrosive injury risk**: A 2021 case report describes corrosive gastritis with gastric stenosis and penetration after accidental calcium chloride ingestion (PMID 34897144). This is a harm signal for GI use.
- **Calcium-alkali syndrome**: This is the TxGNN rank-2 prediction. It reflects an adverse effect of excess calcium, not a treatment, and should be treated as a safety signal.
- **Drug interactions**: No interaction records were found.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The 97.47% TxGNN score is not backed by any clinical or mechanistic evidence. Calcium chloride is acidifying rather than buffering, and the only relevant human report is a harm case. The other nine predicted indications are also rated Hold, and calcium-alkali syndrome should be read as a safety signal.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from HSA (a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any direct preclinical or clinical study of calcium chloride in dyspepsia
- Clarification of the registered indications for the Singapore products
- Route compatibility assessment, since the registered forms are injection, infusion, and eye solution, not oral
