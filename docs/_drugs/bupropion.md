## Bupropion: norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor → attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is an antidepressant and smoking-cessation agent that inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder**, with **6 clinical trials** (including one completed Phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trial) and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not compiled for this report. Published reviews describe bupropion's main action as inhibition of norepinephrine and dopamine reuptake, without serotonergic activity.

These are the same catecholaminergic pathways targeted by established ADHD treatments such as stimulants and atomoxetine. Reduced catecholamine reuptake in the brain is a plausible route to better attention and less impulsivity. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this mechanistic overlap. Reviews of non-stimulant ADHD medications also discuss bupropion as an option, mainly for adults who do not respond to or cannot tolerate stimulants.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Interaction screening returned 100 records. Major-level interactions include fentanyl, lidocaine, dihydrocodeine, codeine, tramadol, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, alfentanil and amantadine. Moderate-level interactions include ethanol, nifedipine, hydrochlorothiazide, cetirizine, alprazolam and chlorpheniramine, among others.
- **Points for guardrails**: Dose-dependent seizure risk, blood pressure monitoring, and caution in patients with comorbid substance use or eating disorders.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trial and a Cochrane review directly address bupropion in ADHD, and the mechanism is plausible. Only one Phase 3 trial is completed, and several studies involve patients with comorbid substance use. Seizure and blood pressure risks require safeguards.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Phase 3 trial results (NCT00048360) and the Cochrane review for effect size and tolerability
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Singapore products
- A safety plan covering seizure risk, blood pressure monitoring, drug interactions and patients with substance use or eating disorders
