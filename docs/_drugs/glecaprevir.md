## Glecaprevir: HIV infectious disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Glecaprevir is an oral NS3/4A viral protease inhibitor that is marketed in Singapore. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, but the evidence level is **L5 (model prediction only)**. The 7 clinical trials and the literature retrieved for this prediction involve patients who are co-infected with HIV and do not test glecaprevir as a treatment for HIV itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Glecaprevir is a viral NS3/4A protease inhibitor with no established direct antiretroviral activity against HIV.

The high score most likely reflects network proximity between viral protease inhibitors and HIV-related targets. It is not evidence of an anti-HIV effect. The retrieved trials and publications show that glecaprevir-based therapy can be used safely and effectively in people who also have HIV. They do not show that it treats HIV. Any HIV-related benefit is therefore unsupported at this time.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence level is L5. No trial or publication studies glecaprevir for treating HIV itself, and the available studies concern patients with HIV co-infection.
- The drug has no known direct activity against HIV, so the high model score is not backed by a plausible mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical or in vitro data showing any antiretroviral activity
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert safety information (warnings and contraindications)
- A review of interactions with antiretroviral regimens, including the major interactions listed above
