## Glecaprevir: HIV infectious disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glecaprevir is an antiviral that blocks the hepatitis C virus NS3/4A protease and is marketed in Singapore as part of a glecaprevir/pibrentasvir tablet.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**, but the evidence level is **L5 (model prediction only)**: the 7 listed clinical trials and the literature all concern hepatitis C treatment in people who also have HIV, not treatment of HIV itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Glecaprevir is known to be an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor, and it has no established direct antiretroviral activity against HIV.

The high TxGNN score probably reflects network proximity between viral protease inhibitors and HIV-related targets, rather than a demonstrated pharmacological link. The clinical studies show that glecaprevir/pibrentasvir can cure hepatitis C safely in people co-infected with HIV, but none tests whether it treats HIV. Any HIV-related benefit is therefore unsupported at present.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** 57 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include:
- Rosuvastatin
- Simvastatin
- Edoxaban

Moderate-level interactions include dexamethasone, omeprazole, esomeprazole, apixaban, cobicistat and deferasirox, among others.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level for HIV infectious disease is L5. No study examines glecaprevir as a treatment for HIV; the trials and publications show only that hepatitis C can be treated effectively and safely in people with HIV co-infection. There is also no plausible direct antiretroviral mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Laboratory or preclinical evidence of anti-HIV activity
- Mechanism-of-action data for glecaprevir
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- A dedicated HIV-directed study, if preclinical data support one
