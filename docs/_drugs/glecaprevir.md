# Glecaprevir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Glecaprevir is an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor, originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, but the **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved for this prediction all concern treating HCV (including in people who also have HIV), not HIV itself.
The prediction is therefore best read as a research question rather than supported efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Glecaprevir is known to be an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor, and it is used together with pibrentasvir (an NS5A inhibitor) as a fixed-dose combination for HCV.

Mechanistically, the prediction is hard to justify. Glecaprevir has no established direct antiretroviral activity. HIV is a retrovirus and does not have an NS3/4A-like protease target. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity between viral protease inhibitors and HIV-related targets in the knowledge graph.

The clinical link that does exist is about coinfection. Many people with HIV also have HCV, and the trials show glecaprevir/pibrentasvir can cure HCV in that group. That is a benefit for the HCV infection, not an HIV treatment effect.

The other nine predictions (hepatitis B, feline and simian immunodeficiency, hepatitis E, animal viral hepatitis, hepatitis A, Omsk hemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease, and a genetic neurodevelopmental disorder) all have even weaker support and are rated Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 57 interactions were identified. Those shown below are the Major-level ones in the retrieved data:
  - Rosuvastatin (Major)
  - Simvastatin (Major)
  - Edoxaban (Major)
  - Moderate-level interactions include dexamethasone, omeprazole, esomeprazole, cobicistat, apixaban, betrixaban, deferasirox, lusutrombopag, metreleptin, naldemedine, revefenacin, griseofulvin and troglitazone.
  - Minor-level interactions include fidaxomicin, naloxegol, prucalopride and indacaterol.
  - The cobicistat interaction deserves attention in any HIV setting, since cobicistat is a common antiretroviral booster.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Although the trial count would formally reach L1, every trial and publication tests glecaprevir/pibrentasvir for HCV, including in people with HIV coinfection. None tests HIV treatment. Glecaprevir has no plausible direct anti-HIV mechanism, so the high TxGNN score appears to be a network-based artifact. Its value in people with HIV is curing their HCV, which is already its approved use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- In vitro evidence of anti-HIV activity, or a plausible mechanism, before any clinical exploration
- Mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulator
- Drug interaction review for antiretroviral regimens, especially cobicistat-boosted ones and statins
- Consideration of whether the better framing is "HCV treatment in HIV/HCV coinfection", which is already within the existing indication
