## Eltrombopag: HIV infectious disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is an oral thrombopoietin receptor agonist that raises platelet counts, and it is currently marketed in Singapore.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**, but no study in the data compiled for this report tests it as a treatment for HIV infection itself.
The supporting material is **1 clinical trial** (in thrombocytopenia from chronic liver disease, not HIV) and **8 publications**. These are mostly case reports, reviews and one preclinical screen on HIV-associated blood-count problems, so the evidence level is **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Eltrombopag acts on the thrombopoietin receptor and stimulates platelet production. In people with HIV who develop low platelet counts, including immune-mediated and immune-reconstitution-related forms, it can raise platelet counts whatever the underlying viral process. Any benefit is therefore supportive, treating the blood-count problem rather than the virus.

Case reports describe platelet responses in refractory HIV-associated thrombocytopenia. One report describes a blood-count response in aplastic anaemia associated with HIV infection, with changes in T-cell subsets that suggest an immunomodulatory effect. A 2020 in vitro screen of approved drugs (PMID 32977702) suggests possible modulation of HIV-1 proviral transcription. That finding is preclinical and only generates hypotheses.

These studies concern patients who happen to have HIV, not the treatment of HIV infection. The model's prediction is therefore best read as an HIV-associated haematological use, not an antiviral one. Detailed mechanism-of-action text is also missing from the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications**: Package-insert warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 records. The first 20 reviewed include one major and many moderate interactions.
  - Major: eluxadoline.
  - Moderate: polyvalent-cation products, including calcium salts, magnesium salts, aluminium hydroxide, iron, kaolin, attapulgite and magaldrate, as well as glyburide. The cation products can bind the drug and are commonly managed by separating administration times.
- **Conditions flagged for caution** (not label text; confirm against the package insert): liver and gallbladder disease, acute liver failure, hepatitis, kidney disease, hyperbilirubinaemia and Gilbert syndrome, anaemia, leukopenia, myelodysplastic syndrome, thrombocytosis, and hepatic vein thrombosis including Budd-Chiari syndrome.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study directly tests eltrombopag as a treatment for HIV infection, so the evidence level is L5 despite the high model score of 0.9926. The available reports describe supportive treatment of low blood counts in people with HIV. The one preclinical antiviral signal is hypothesis-generating only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies that directly assess eltrombopag in HIV infection, or a clear decision to define the use as an HIV-associated thrombocytopenia indication
- Mechanism-of-action data and confirmation of the preclinical proviral-transcription finding
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications from the Singapore regulatory source
- A safety plan covering liver function, interactions with antiretrovirals and cation-containing products, and thrombosis risk
