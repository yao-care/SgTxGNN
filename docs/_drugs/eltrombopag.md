# Eltrombopag: From Thrombocytopenia Treatment to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is an oral thrombopoietin receptor (TPO-R) agonist that raises platelet counts. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**, most plausibly as supportive care for HIV-related low blood counts rather than as an antiviral. Five clinical trials were retrieved, but none is confirmed as an HIV study. The literature is limited to reviews, one in vitro screen, and case reports or small case series.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Eltrombopag stimulates the thrombopoietin receptor on megakaryocytes and their precursors, which increases platelet production. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the input, so this description relies on its known drug class.

The plausible link to HIV is supportive care, not viral suppression. HIV infection can cause immune thrombocytopenia and other cytopenias, including immune reconstitution-related thrombocytopenia and aplastic anaemia. Published case reports and a small case series describe eltrombopag raising counts in these settings. Reviews also list HIV as a recognised cause of chronic secondary thrombocytopenia.

One in vitro screen of FDA-approved drugs reported that eltrombopag may modulate HIV-1 proviral transcription. This is preclinical only and does not show antiviral benefit in patients. The 99.26% score is a model output and should not be read as evidence of efficacy.

TxGNN also proposed other indications, including AIDS (same rationale as HIV), female breast carcinoma (preclinical only), and rheumatoid arthritis (supportive care for secondary thrombocytopenia only). Several lower-ranked predictions have no trials or literature.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The HIV prediction rests on a model score, one in vitro screen, and case-level reports of thrombocytopenia and cytopenia management. None of the retrieved trials is confirmed as an HIV study. The package insert safety information is also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- HSA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Confirmation on the trial registry of whether any listed trial enrolled HIV patients
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Prospective or controlled data in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia
- The approved indication text for the two Singapore registrations, to confirm the original indication
